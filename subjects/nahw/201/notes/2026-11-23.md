## Grammar Rules Covered

### 1. What is a Tabi' (تابع)?

A **Tabi'** is a noun whose I'rab is derived from the noun that precedes it (the **Matbu'**), not from a direct Amil acting upon it.

- **Asli I'rab:** The I'rab of a noun upon which the Amil directly acts.
  - Example: *Ja'a akhuka* — "akhuka" is Fa'il; its I'rab is Asli because Ja'a (the Amil) acts directly on it.
- **Taba'i I'rab:** The I'rab a noun receives because it follows (is associated with) a Matbu'.
  - Example: *Ja'a akhuka Zaydun* — "Zaydun" is Tabi'; it is Marfu' not because Ja'a acts on it, but because it follows Akhuka.

> **Rule:** Every Mu'rab noun that takes the I'rab of a preceding noun from one direction is called a **Tabi'**.

---

### 2. The Five Types of Tawabi' (التوابع الخمسة)

1. **النعت (An-Na't)** — The Adjective/Descriptor
2. **العطف بالحروف (Al-'Atf bil-Huruf)** — Conjunction with particles
3. **التأكيد (At-Ta'kid)** — Corroboration/Emphasis
4. **البدل (Al-Badal)** — Substitution
5. **عطف البيان ('Atf al-Bayan)** — Appositional conjunction

---

### 3. Na't / Sifat (النعت / الصفة) — Definition

**Na't** (also called Sifat) is a Tabi' that indicates a meaning in its Matbu' (Mawsuf/Man'ut).

- The noun being described = **Mawsuf (الموصوف)** / **Man'ut (المنعوت)** / **Matbu' (المتبوع)**
- The descriptor = **Sifat (الصفة)** / **Na't (النعت)** / **Tabi' (التابع)**

---

### 4. Two Types of Na't

#### Type 1 (القسم الأول): Sifat describes the Mawsuf directly

The Sifat describes the Mawsuf itself.

**Example:** *Ja'ani rajulun 'alimun* — "A learned man came to me."

In this case, **Sifat follows Mawsuf in 10 things:**

| # | Category | Arabic | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Raf' | رفع | If Mawsuf is Marfu', Sifat is Marfu' |
| 2 | Nasb | نصب | If Mawsuf is Mansub, Sifat is Mansub |
| 3 | Jarr | جر | If Mawsuf is Majrur, Sifat is Majrur |
| 4 | Ma'rifah | معرفة | If Mawsuf is Ma'rifah, Sifat is Ma'rifah |
| 5 | Nakirah | نكرة | If Mawsuf is Nakirah, Sifat is Nakirah |
| 6 | Ifrad | إفراد | If Mawsuf is singular, Sifat is singular |
| 7 | Tathniyah | تثنية | If Mawsuf is dual, Sifat is dual |
| 8 | Jam' | جمع | If Mawsuf is plural, Sifat is plural |
| 9 | Tazkir | تذكير | If Mawsuf is masculine, Sifat is masculine |
| 10 | Ta'nis | تأنيث | If Mawsuf is feminine, Sifat is feminine |

**Examples across I'rab:**
- *Ja'ani rajulun 'alimun* — both Marfu' (Nakirah, Mufrad, Muzakkar)
- *Ra'aytu rajulan 'aliman* — both Mansub
- *Marartu bi rajulin 'alimin* — both Majrur
- *Ja'at imra'atun 'alimatun* — both Marfu', Muannas
- *Ja'a ar-rajulu al-'alimu* — both Ma'rifah, Marfu'

> **Cannot say:** *Ja'a ar-rajulu 'alimun* — Mawsuf is Ma'rifah (ar-rajul), Sifat cannot be Nakirah ('alimun).

---

#### Type 2 (القسم الثاني): Sifat describes the Muta'alliq al-Mawsuf (متعلق الموصوف)

The Sifat does not describe the Mawsuf directly, but rather something **associated with** the Mawsuf.

**Example:** *Ja'ani rajulun 'alimun abuhu* — "A man came to me whose father is learned."

Here:
- **Mawsuf** = Rajul (the man)
- **Sifat** = 'Alim (learned)
- **Muta'alliq al-Mawsuf** = Abuhu (his father) ← this is what 'Alim actually describes

The Sifat is Alim, but it describes Abu (father), who is associated with Rajul (son). The father is not the Mawsuf; the father is the Muta'alliq al-Mawsuf.

In this case, **Sifat follows Mawsuf in only 5 things:**

| # | Category |
|---|---|
| 1 | Raf' |
| 2 | Nasb |
| 3 | Jarr |
| 4 | Ma'rifah / Nakirah |
| (5 combined) | Ta'rif / Tankir |

**It does NOT follow Mawsuf in:** Ifrad/Tathniyah/Jam' (number) or Tazkir/Ta'nis (gender) — instead it follows the **Muta'alliq** in those.

**Quranic Example:**
> *min hādhihi al-qaryati al-ẓālimi ahluhā* — "from this town whose people are wrongdoers" (An-Nahl: 59 / similar usage)

- **Mawsuf** = Al-Qaryah (القرية) — feminine
- **Sifat** = Al-Zalim (الظالم) — masculine ← follows Muta'alliq (Ahluha = people, masculine)
- Al-Qaryah is Majrur → Al-Zalim is Majrur (follows Mawsuf in I'rab)
- Al-Qaryah is Ma'rifah → Al-Zalim is Ma'rifah (follows Mawsuf in Ta'rif/Tankir)
- Al-Qaryah is feminine, but Al-Zalim is masculine → follows Muta'alliq (Ahl) in gender

**Further Example:**
*Ja'a ar-rajulu al-ẓālimatun zawjatuhu* — "The man whose wife is oppressive came."
- Mawsuf: Ar-Rajul (masculine, Ma'rifah, Marfu')
- Muta'alliq: Zawjatuhu (his wife — feminine)
- Sifat: Al-Zalamitu — feminine (follows Muta'alliq in gender)
- Sifat: Marfu', Ma'rifah (follows Mawsuf in I'rab and Ta'rif)

**Example with number disagreement:**
*Fi al-fasli tulabun ghāfilun abuhu* — "In the class there are students whose fathers are heedless."
- Mawsuf: Tullab — Jama' (plural)
- Sifat: Ghafil — Mufrad (singular) ← follows Muta'alliq (Abu, singular) in number

---

### 5. The Five Purposes of Sifat (فوائد النعت)

| # | Purpose | Condition | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Takhsis (تخصيص)** — Specification | Mawsuf is Nakirah | *Ja'ani rajulun 'alimun* — narrows down which man |
| 2 | **Tawdih (توضيح)** — Clarification | Mawsuf is Ma'rifah | *Ja'a Zaydun al-tawilu* — distinguishes between two Zayds |
| 3 | **Madh (مدح)** — Praise | Mawsuf is well-known | *Bismillah ir-Rahman ir-Rahim* — Allah is known; Sifat brings praise |
| 4 | **Dhamm (ذم)** — Condemnation | Mawsuf is well-known | *A'udhu billahi min ash-shaytani ar-rajim* — Sifat brings condemnation |
| 5 | **Ta'kid (تأكيد)** — Corroboration | Meaning already implied | *Nafkhatun wahidatun* — "one blast"; Wahidah reinforces what Nafkhah already implies |

---

### 6. Sifat as a Jumlah (Sentence)

Sifat does not have to be a single word — it can be an entire **Jumlah Khabariyyah (جملة خبرية)**.

**Examples:**

1. *Marartu bi rajulin abuhu 'alimun*
   - Mawsuf: Rajul (Majrur, Nakirah)
   - Sifat = entire sentence: *Abuhu 'alimun*
     - Abuhu = Mubtada (Muzaf + Muzaf ilayh)
     - 'Alimun = Khabar
     - Together they form the Sifat of Rajul

2. *Marartu bi rajulin qama abuhu*
   - Mawsuf: Rajul (Majrur, Nakirah)
   - Sifat = Jumlah Fi'liyyah: *Qama abuhu*
     - Qama = Fi'l
     - Abuhu = Fa'il
     - Together form Sifat of Rajul

> **Note:** A Jumlah acting as Sifat must have a **Dhamir (pronoun)** linking it back to the Mawsuf (e.g., *abu**hu*** — the "hu" refers back to Rajul).

| Word | I'rab |
|---|---|
| جاءني | Fi'l + Fa'il (Dhamir) + Maf'ul bih (Ya') |
| رجلٌ | Fa'il, Marfu', 'alamatuhu al-damma, Nakirah, Mufrad, Muzakkar |
| عليمٌ | Sifat (Na't), Marfu', 'alamatuhu al-damma — Tabi' li-Rajul in I'rab, Ta'rif/Tankir, 'Adad, Jins (all 10) |

| Word | I'rab |
|---|---|
| من | Harf Jarr |
| هذه | Ism Isharah, Mabni, fi mahall Jarr |
| القريةِ | Majrur, Ma'rifah, Muannas |
| الظالمِ | Na't, Majrur (follows Mawsuf in I'rab), Ma'rifah (follows Mawsuf in Ta'rif), Muzakkar (follows Muta'alliq: Ahl — masculine) |
| أهلُها | Muta'alliq al-Mawsuf; Mubtada (or Fa'il of implied verb); Muzaf + Muzaf ilayh |

| Word | I'rab |
|---|---|
| جاء | Fi'l Madi |

| زوجتُهُ | Muta'alliq al-Mawsuf; Mubtada; Muzaf + Muzaf ilayh; Muannas |

## Practice Examples

**Q: Choose the correct Sifat:**

1. *Ra'aytu mu'minatan ___* (What I'rab does the Maf'ul take?)
   - Fathah (Nasb) — Maf'ul bih is Mansub

2. *Sallamtu 'ala 'Uthman* — Is Kasrah correct on 'Uthman here?
   - **No** — 'Uthman is Majrur (after 'ala) but 'Uthman is Ghayru Munsarif (doesn't take Tanwin or Kasrah); it takes Fathah as sign of Jarr.

3. *Fi al-fasli tulabun ghāfilun abuhu* — Why is Ghafil Mufrad when Tullab is Jama'?
   - Because Sifat is describing Muta'alliq (Abu, singular father), not Tullab directly. In Adad (number), it follows Muta'alliq.

- Given a sentence, choose the correct I'rab marker: (A) Kasrah (B) Fathah (C) Jazm/Sukun
- Given a sentence, is [this I'rab] correct? (A) Yes (B) No

## Key Takeaways

1. **Tabi' derives its I'rab from its Matbu'**, not from a direct Amil — this is I'rab Taba'i, as opposed to I'rab Asli.

2. **There are five Tawabi':** Na't, 'Atf bil-Huruf, Ta'kid, Badal, 'Atf al-Bayan. This session covered Na't in full.

3. **Na't/Sifat has two types:**
   - Describes Mawsuf directly → follows in **10 things** (I'rab × 3, Ta'rif/Tankir, 'Adad × 3, Jins × 2)
   - Describes Muta'alliq al-Mawsuf → follows Mawsuf in **5 things only** (I'rab × 3, Ta'rif/Tankir); follows Muta'alliq in gender and number

4. **The key diagnostic** for Type 2: when the adjective semantically describes something *related to* the Mawsuf (child, spouse, father, people) rather than the Mawsuf itself, check the Muta'alliq for gender/number agreement.

5. **Sifat serves five purposes:** Takhsis (for Nakirah), Tawdih (for Ma'rifah), Madh (praise), Dhamm (condemnation), Ta'kid (reinforcement).

6. **Sifat can be a Jumlah** (full sentence), provided it contains a returning Dhamir back to the Mawsuf.
