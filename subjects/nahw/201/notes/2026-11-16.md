## Grammar Rules Covered

### 1. The Seven Types of Maf'ool (المفاعيل)

All seven Maf'ool types fall under the broader category of Mansubaat — they are Mansub (accusative).

| Maf'ool Type | Arabic | Function | Key Identifier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Maf'ool Bihi | مفعول به | Direct object (who/what the action is done to) | Receives the action of the verb directly |
| Maf'ool Mutlaq | مفعول مطلق | Absolute/cognate object | **Must share the same root (Masdar) as its verb** |
| Maf'ool Lahu / Lih | مفعول له / لأجله | Object of reason/purpose | Answers "why?" — gives the سبب (Sabab) of the action |
| Maf'ool Fih | مفعول فيه | Adverbial of time or place (Zarf) | Answers "when?" (Zarf Zamaan) or "where?" (Zarf Makaan) |
| Maf'ool Ma'ahu | مفعول معه | Object of accompaniment | Indicates what accompanied the subject/verb |
| Hal | حال | Circumstantial accusative | Answers "how?" — describes the state/condition when the action occurred |
| Tamiz | تمييز | Specification/distinction | Removes ambiguity (إبهام Ibhaam) from a noun or sentence |

---

### 2. Hal (الحال)

- Hal is **not** called Maf'ool — it has its own distinct name.
- However, it **falls under Mansubaat** — it is Mansub.
- Hal answers the question **"how?"** — it describes the state or condition in which something occurred.
- **Key distinction from Maf'ool Mutlaq:** Maf'ool Mutlaq must come from the same Masdar as the verb. Hal does not have this requirement.

**Example:**
> اسْتَيْقَظَتْ فَاطِمَةُ بَاكِيَةً
> *Fatima woke up crying.*
> → بَاكِيَةً = Hal (describes the state in which she woke up)

> لِمَ صَلَّيْتَ جَالِسًا؟
> *Why did you pray sitting?*
> → جَالِسًا = Hal

---

### 3. Maf'ool Mutlaq — Key Rule

Maf'ool Mutlaq **must** share the same root (Masdar) as the verb. If the word after the verb is not from the same root, it cannot be Maf'ool Mutlaq.

**Example:**
> نِمْتُ نَوْمًا جَمِيلًا
> *I slept a beautiful sleep.*
> → نَوْمًا = Maf'ool Mutlaq (نام → نَوْم, same root)

**Bayaan al-'Adad (بيان العدد)** — specifying number/quantity — is a subcategory of Maf'ool Mutlaq (even if it does not appear to be a Masdar of the verb, the quantity specifies the degree/extent of the action).

---

### 4. Tamiz (التمييز)

Tamiz removes **إبهام (Ibhaam)** — ambiguity — from a sentence or noun.

- The **Mumayyaz** (مميَّز) is the ambiguous element being clarified.
- The **Tamiz** is the word that removes the ambiguity.
- The Mumayyaz can be a noun **or** a pronoun (even a Mutakallim pronoun — ياء المتكلم).

**Examples:**
> اللَّهُمَّ زِدْنِي عِلْمًا
> *O Allah, increase me in knowledge.*
> → نِي = Maf'ool Bihi (also functions as the Mumayyaz)
> → عِلْمًا = Tamiz (clarifies: increase in what? In knowledge)

> اللَّهُمَّ زِدْهُ مَرَضًا
> *O Allah, increase him in illness.*
> → مَرَضًا = Tamiz

### 5. Istithna — Exception (الاستثناء)

#### Kalaam Mujab vs. Kalaam Ghair Mujab

| Type | Arabic | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| Kalaam Mujab | كلام موجب | Affirmative speech — no negation, prohibition, or interrogation |
| Kalaam Ghair Mujab | كلام غير موجب | Contains نفي (Nafi — negation), نهي (Nahi — prohibition), or استفهام (Istifhaam — interrogation) |

#### Rule for Mustasna in Kalaam Ghair Mujab

If the Mustasna (المُستثنى — the excepted item) comes after **إلا** in Kalaam Ghair Mujab, **يجوز فيه الوجهان** — two options are both valid:

1. **النصب** (Nasb) — treat it as Mustasna (accusative)
2. **البدل عما قبله** (Badal of what precedes it) — treat it as a Badal (substitute) of the Mubdal Minhu

**Critical rule for Badal:** The Badal must match the I'rab of the Mubdal Minhu (what it substitutes). If the Mubdal Minhu is Marfu', the Badal is Marfu'. If the Mubdal Minhu is Mansub, the Badal must also be Mansub.

**Example:**
> مَا قَامَ الْقَوْمُ إِلَّا حَامِدٌ / حَامِدًا
> *The people did not stand except Hamid.*
> → Both حَامِدٌ (Badal of الْقَوْمُ, which is Marfu') and حَامِدًا (Nasb as Mustasna) are correct.

---

#### Mustasna Mufarraq (المستثنى المُفَرَّق)

**Definition:** When the **Mustasna Minhu** (المُستثنى منه — what is being excepted from) is **not mentioned** in the sentence.

**Rule:** When Mustasna Minhu is absent, the Mustasna takes its I'rab **according to the 'Amil** (العامل) in the sentence — it is as if إلا is not there.

**Reasoning:** The two essential elements of Istithna are Mustasna and Mustasna Minhu. When one pillar is absent, the construct does not truly function as Istithna. The word after إلا should therefore be treated as if إلا is absent, and its I'rab follows whatever the 'Amil demands.

**Examples:**
> مَا نَامَ إِلَّا زَيْدٌ → (compare with: مَا نَامَ زَيْدٌ — Fa'il, Marfu')
> مَا رَأَيْتُ إِلَّا زَيْدًا → (compare with: مَا رَأَيْتُ زَيْدًا — Maf'ool Bihi, Mansub)

## I'rab (Sentence Analysis) Examples

### Example 1: Maf'ool Bihi + Maf'ool Lahu
> أَطْعَمَ أَحْمَدُ الْمَسَاكِينَ رَغْبَةً فِي ثَوَابِ اللهِ
> *Ahmad fed the poor people out of desire for Allah's reward.*

| Word | I'rab | Type |
|---|---|---|
| أَطْعَمَ | Fi'l Maadi | Verb |
| أَحْمَدُ | Marfu' | Faa'il (subject) |
| الْمَسَاكِينَ | Mansub | Maf'ool Bihi |
| رَغْبَةً | Mansub | Maf'ool Lahu (reason: desire for reward) |

---

### Example 2: Hal
> اسْتَيْقَظَتْ فَاطِمَةُ بَاكِيَةً
> *Fatima woke up crying.*

| Word | I'rab | Type |
|---|---|---|
| اسْتَيْقَظَتْ | Fi'l Maadi | Verb |
| فَاطِمَةُ | Marfu' | Faa'il |
| بَاكِيَةً | Mansub | Hal (describes her condition when waking) |

---

### Example 3: Maf'ool Mutlaq
> نِمْتُ نَوْمًا جَمِيلًا
> *I slept a beautiful sleep.*

| Word | I'rab | Type |
|---|---|---|
| نِمْتُ | Fi'l Maadi + Fa'il | Verb + subject |
| نَوْمًا | Mansub | Maf'ool Mutlaq (نَوْم = Masdar of نام) |
| جَمِيلًا | Mansub | Sifah/Na't of نَوْمًا |

---

### Example 4: Multiple Mansubaat in one sentence
> اسْتَغْفَرَ زَيْدٌ بَاكِيًا لَيْلًا خَوْفًا لِلْعَذَابِ
> *Zaid sought forgiveness, crying, at night, out of fear of punishment.*

| Word | I'rab | Type |
|---|---|---|
| اسْتَغْفَرَ | Fi'l Maadi | Verb |
| زَيْدٌ | Marfu' | Faa'il |
| بَاكِيًا | Mansub | Hal (his state: crying) |
| لَيْلًا | Mansub | Maf'ool Fih — Zarf Zamaan (time: at night) |
| خَوْفًا | Mansub | Maf'ool Lahu (reason: fear of punishment) |

**Total Mansubaat in this sentence: 3** (Hal, Maf'ool Fih, Maf'ool Lahu)

---

### Example 5: Tamiz
> اللَّهُمَّ زِدْنِي عِلْمًا
> *O Allah, increase me in knowledge.*

| Word | I'rab | Type |
|---|---|---|
| زِدْ | Fi'l Amr | Verb (command) |
| نِي | Mansub | Maf'ool Bihi (also functions as Mumayyaz) |
| عِلْمًا | Mansub | Tamiz (removes ibhaam: increase in what?) |

---

### Example 6: Maf'ool Fih — Zarf Makaan
> إِنَّهُ يَجْلِسُ يَمِينًا فِي الْمَسْجِدِ
> *Indeed he sits to the right in the mosque.*

| Word | I'rab | Type |
|---|---|---|
| يَجْلِسُ | Marfu' | Verb (mudaari') |
| يَمِينًا | Mansub | Maf'ool Fih — Zarf Makaan (place: to the right side) |

---

### Example 7: Istithna in Kalaam Ghair Mujab (Two valid readings)
> مَا قَامَ الْقَوْمُ إِلَّا حَامِدٌ / حَامِدًا
> *The people did not stand except Hamid.*

| Reading | Word | I'rab | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| Option 1 | حَامِدًا | Mansub | Mustasna — Nasb in Kalaam Ghair Mujab |
| Option 2 | حَامِدٌ | Marfu' | Badal of الْقَوْمُ (Mubdal Minhu is Marfu') |

**Both are grammatically correct.** The Nasb reading (Mustasna) is considered slightly more common.

---

### Example 8: Mustasna Mufarraq (Mustasna Minhu absent)
> مَا رَأَيْتُ إِلَّا زَيْدًا
> *I saw no one except Zaid.*

Here Mustasna Minhu (e.g., أحدًا) is not mentioned. The 'Amil (رَأَيْتُ) governs Nasb, so زَيْدًا takes Nasb — as if إلا is not there: مَا رَأَيْتُ زَيْدًا.

> مَا نَامَ إِلَّا زَيْدٌ
> *No one slept except Zaid.*

Here the 'Amil governs Rafa' (Faa'il), so زَيْدٌ takes Rafa'.

   - نَوْمًا → Maf'ool Mutlaq (Masdar shares root with verb)

5. **اسْتَغْفَرَ زَيْدٌ بَاكِيًا لَيْلًا خَوْفًا لِلْعَذَابِ**
   - بَاكِيًا → Hal | لَيْلًا → Maf'ool Fih (Zarf Zamaan) | خَوْفًا → Maf'ool Lahu

6. **إِنَّهُ يَجْلِسُ يَمِينًا فِي الْمَسْجِدِ**
   - يَمِينًا → Maf'ool Fih (Zarf Makaan)

7. **اللَّهُمَّ زِدْنِي عِلْمًا**
   - عِلْمًا → Tamiz

8. **اللَّهُمَّ زِدْهُ مَرَضًا**
   - مَرَضًا → Tamiz

9. **مَا قَامَ الْقَوْمُ إِلَّا حَامِدٌ / حَامِدًا**
   - Both valid — Nasb (Mustasna) or Rafa' (Badal)

## Key Takeaways

1. **All Maf'ool types are Mansub**, but not everything Mansub is called a Maf'ool — Hal and Tamiz are also Mansub but are distinct categories.

2. **Maf'ool Mutlaq requires the same Masdar root as its verb.** A word cannot be Maf'ool Mutlaq unless it shares the root of the verb — this is its defining feature and distinguishes it from Hal.

3. **Hal answers "how?" and Tamiz removes ambiguity.** These are frequently confused — Hal describes the state/condition during an action, while Tamiz specifies what is meant by an otherwise vague word or pronoun.

4. **In Kalaam Ghair Mujab (نفي / نهي / استفهام), the Mustasna after إلا has two valid I'rab options:** Nasb as Mustasna, or Rafa'/Nasb/Jarr as Badal (matching the Mubdal Minhu's I'rab).

5. **Mustasna Mufarraq (absent Mustasna Minhu) follows the 'Amil**, not the rules of Istithna — because without both pillars (Mustasna + Mustasna Minhu), the exception construct is essentially void, and the word takes normal I'rab from whatever governs it.

6. **Badal must match the Mubdal Minhu's I'rab exactly.** A common error is making the Badal Marfu' when the Mubdal Minhu is Mansub — this is incorrect.
