## Grammar Rules Covered

### 1. Classification of Ism
Ism (noun) is divided into two categories:
- **Mu'rab** — its ending changes according to the governing factor (Aamil)
- **Mabni** — its ending does not change; it is fixed

---

### 2. Definition of Ism al-Mu'rab
> *Huwa kullu ismin rukkiba ma'a ghairihi wa la yushbihu mabniyya al-asl*

**Every noun that is compounded with another word and does not resemble Mabniyya al-Asl.**

---

### 3. Three Conditions for a Word to be Ism al-Mu'rab

| # | Condition | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Must be an **Ism** | Harf is permanently Mabni; Fil Madi and Fil Amr are permanently Mabni — so only Ism qualifies |
| 2 | Must be **Murakab** (compounded) | It must appear in a sentence with another word (e.g., with a Fa'il or another noun) |
| 3 | Must **not resemble Mabniyya al-Asl** | It must not have similarity to words that are Mabni by nature (i.e., Huruf al-Jar and Asma' al-Ishara) |

**Example of condition 2 failing:** *Zaid* alone (isolated) — not Murakab, so it is Mabni in that state.

**Example of condition 3 failing:** *Ha'ula'i* — it is an Ism and it is Murakab, but it is Mushabih (similar) to Mabniyya al-Asl, so it is Mabni, not Mu'rab.

---

### 4. Mabniyya al-Asl — What It Is and Why Asma' al-Ishara Resemble It

**Mabniyya al-Asl** = that which is Mabni by birth/nature. These are the **Huruf**: إِلَى، عَلَى، مِن، فِي

**Why Asma' al-Ishara are Mushabih to Mabniyya al-Asl:**
- Huruf al-Jar require another word to convey their meaning (e.g., *fi al-dar*, *ila al-masjid*) — they are dependent.
- Similarly, Asma' al-Ishara require a **Mushaar ilayhi** (the thing being pointed at) to convey meaning. If you say *ha'ula'i* without any context or pointing, the listener does not know who is meant. Just as *fi* needs a word after it, *ha'ula'i* needs a physical or contextual gesture.
- Because of this dependency/resemblance, all Asma' al-Ishara are Mabni, not Mu'rab.

**Asma' al-Ishara (all Mabni):**

| هَذَا | هَذِهِ | ذَلِكَ | تِلْكَ | هَؤُلَاءِ | أُولَئِكَ |

---

### 5. Hukm (Ruling) of Ism al-Mu'rab
> *Wa hukmahu an yakhtalifa akhiruhu bikhtilafi al-awamili ikhtilafan lafziyyan aw taqdirayyan*

The **ending** of Ism al-Mu'rab changes as the Awamil (governing factors) change. That change is either:

- **Lafzi (لَفْظِي)** — visible/audible; we can see and hear the change
  - Example: جَاءَنِي زَيْدٌ ← Dammah | رَأَيْتُ زَيْدًا ← Fathah | مَرَرْتُ بِزَيْدٍ ← Kasrah
- **Taqdiri (تَقْدِيرِي)** — hidden/invisible; the change exists grammatically but is not pronounced
  - Categories of Taqdiri I'rab will be covered in upcoming sessions

Lafzi I'rab is the default/original (asl); Taqdiri is the exception (approximately 10–15% of cases).

---

### 6. The Three Types of I'rab for Ism
> *Wa i'rabu al-ismi 'ala thalathati anwa'*

1. **Rafa'** (رَفْع)
2. **Nasb** (نَصْب)
3. **Jarr** (جَرّ)

Note: Ism does not have Jazm. Jazm is specific to Fil Mudhari'.

---

### 7. Aamil — The Governing Factor
**Aamil** = that which causes I'rab (Rafa', Nasb, or Jarr) in a word.

| Sentence | Aamil |
|---|---|
| جَاءَ زَيْدٌ | جَاءَ (caused Rafa') |
| رَأَيْتُ زَيْدًا | رَأَيْتُ (caused Nasb) |
| مَرَرْتُ بِزَيْدٍ | الْبَاء (caused Jarr) |

---

### 8. Mahallu al-I'rab — The Site of I'rab
> *Wa mahall al-i'rabi min al-ismi huwa al-harfu al-akhir*

I'rab is located on the **last letter** of the word.

**Example breakdown of جَاءَ زَيْدٌ:**
- **Aamil:** جَاءَ
- **Ism al-Mu'rab:** زَيْدٌ
- **I'rab:** الضَّمَّة (Dammah — indicating Rafa')
- **Mahallu al-I'rab:** الدَّال (the Dal, the last letter of زَيْد)

---

### 9. Which Words Receive I'rab?
Only two categories in Arabic receive I'rab:

1. **Ism al-Mu'rab** (with the exception of Ism that resembles Mabniyya al-Asl)
2. **Fil al-Mudhari'** (present/future tense verb)

**What does NOT receive I'rab (always Mabni):**
- Harf — never receives I'rab
- Fil Madi (past tense verb) — always Mabni
- Fil Amr (command verb) — always Mabni

---

### 10. Primary vs. Secondary I'rab Signs

| Type | Signs | Analogy |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary (Asli)** | ضَمَّة، فَتْحَة، كَسْرَة | President |
| **Secondary (Niyabi)** | وَاو، أَلِف، يَاء | Vice President — steps in when primary is unavailable |

The secondary signs (Waw, Alif, Ya) replace the primary signs in specific grammatical contexts (to be detailed in upcoming sessions on the nine categories of I'rab).

---

### 11. The Nine Categories of I'rab (Preview)
The three I'rab types (Rafa', Nasb, Jarr) can each appear as either **Lafzi** or **Taqdiri**, and in some cases through **secondary signs** — giving rise to a total of **nine categories**. These will be covered in the next two sessions.

## Key Takeaways

1. **Ism is either Mu'rab or Mabni.** Mu'rab changes its ending; Mabni does not.

2. **Three conditions must all be met** for an Ism to be Mu'rab: (1) it must be an Ism, (2) it must be Murakab, and (3) it must not resemble Mabniyya al-Asl (Huruf).

3. **Asma' al-Ishara are Mabni** because they depend on a Mushaar ilayhi (context/gesture) to convey meaning — just as Huruf depend on a following word — making them Mushabih to Mabniyya al-Asl.

4. **Only two categories receive I'rab:** Ism al-Mu'rab and Fil al-Mudhari'. All Huruf, Fil Madi, and Fil Amr are permanently Mabni.

5. **I'rab always resides on the last letter** (Mahallu al-I'rab = Harf al-Akhir).

6. **I'rab is either Lafzi (visible) or Taqdiri (hidden).** Lafzi is the default; Taqdiri cases will be studied in coming sessions.

7. **Primary I'rab signs** are Dammah/Fathah/Kasrah. **Secondary signs** (Waw/Alif/Ya) substitute for them in certain noun types — forming the basis of the **nine categories of I'rab** to be covered next session.

8. **Second year is a turning point** — the teacher emphasized that the material requires more focus and dedication, but mastery of this level gives a firm foundation for Years 3 and beyond.
