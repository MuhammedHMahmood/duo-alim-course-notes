## Session Overview

This session continued the tafseer of Surah Al-Baqarah, focusing on a series of Quranic events narrating the history of Bani Israel and Allah's blessings upon them. The session opened with a brief grammar clarification on the structure of *ta'ajjub* (expressing wonder/astonishment), then moved into tafseer covering four major incidents: (1) the thunderbolt at Mount Tur following their demand to see Allah, (2) their resurrection after death, (3) the blessings of shade, manna, and quail in the field of Tih, and (4) the command to enter Bayt al-Maqdis with humility and istighfar — and their deliberate mockery of that command. The session concluded with a brief mention of the miracle of water springing from the rock (Ayah 60).

## Key Themes and Topics

1. **Ta'ajjub (Grammatical Wonder Construction)** — Brief review of the two patterns of expressing astonishment in Arabic
2. **The Demand to See Allah Openly** — Bani Israel's refusal to believe in Musa's words without directly seeing Allah
3. **The Thunderbolt (As-Sa'iqah)** — Divine punishment for their audacious demand; their death and subsequent resurrection
4. **Musa's Supplication After Their Death** — Musa's weeping and calling upon Allah on behalf of Bani Israel
5. **Resurrection of the 70** — Allah reviving the 70 chosen men after they remained dead for a day and night
6. **Punishment in the Field of Tih** — 40-year wandering as a consequence of refusing to fight the tyrants of Palestine
7. **Blessings in the Field of Tih** — Shade from clouds, manna and quail provided without effort
8. **The Command to Enter Bayt al-Maqdis** — Instructions to enter prostrating, saying *Hittah*, and eating abundantly
9. **Their Mockery and Disobedience** — The wrongdoers changed the word and entered crawling; the plague as punishment
10. **The Miracle of the Rock and 12 Springs** — Introduction to Ayah 60

## Main Tafseer Points and Explanations

### Opening Grammar Question (Pre-Session)
Before beginning the tafseer, a student raised a question about a grammatical structure from the previous session's *munāsabah*. The teacher clarified that the structure in question is a *ta'ajjub* (expression of wonder), not *nāfi* (negation). The two recognized Arabic patterns for *ta'ajjub* are:
- **مَا أَفْعَلَهُ** — e.g., *mā akramahu* (How honourable he is!), *mā a'ẓamahu* (How magnificent He is!)
- **أَفْعِل بِهِ** — e.g., *an'im bihi*, *akrim bihi*, *akrimbih*

In context, the *ta'ajjub* conveys: "How wicked they are! How disgraceful they are!"

---

### Ayah 55 — The Demand to See Allah and the Thunderbolt

**Context:** The 70 chosen men of Bani Israel went with Musa عليه السلام to the mountain of Tur to apologize to Allah for having worshipped the Golden Calf (*al-'ijl*) during Musa's 40-night absence.

**Their Demand:** Upon arriving at Tur, they said:
> *"لَن نُّؤْمِنَ لَكَ حَتَّىٰ نَرَى اللَّهَ جَهْرَةً"*
> "We will never believe in you until we see Allah openly."

They had already heard Allah's speech but refused to accept it as sufficient proof of faith.

**The Punishment:** Allah سبحانه وتعالى sent a thunderbolt (*as-sā'iqah*) — a fire from above — which struck and killed them while they watched it happen with their own eyes.

> *فَأَخَذَتْكُمُ الصَّاعِقَةُ وَأَنتُمْ تَنظُرُونَ*
> "Then the thunderbolt seized you while you were looking on."

---

### The Weeping of Musa عليه السلام

After the 70 died, Musa عليه السلام began weeping intensely and supplicating to Allah:
> *"رَبِّ مَاذَا أَقُولُ لِبَنِي إِسْرَائِيلَ؟"*
> "O Lord, what shall I say to Bani Israel? I selected the best 70 among them and You have caused them to die."

He continued supplicating without ceasing. This demonstrates the depth of Musa's compassion for his people and his reliance on Allah.

---

### Ayah 56 — Resurrection After Death

In response to Musa's prolonged supplication, Allah revived the 70 men after they had remained dead for a full day and night:

> *ثُمَّ بَعَثْنَاكُم مِّن بَعْدِ مَوْتِكُمْ*
> "Then We revived you after your death."

After being resurrected, they stood up and looked at one another in amazement as each person came back to life. This was an immense blessing — the greatest possible in this world, since death is the last and most final thing. Allah stated the purpose:

> *لَعَلَّكُمْ تَشْكُرُونَ*
> "That you may be grateful."

---

### Ayah 57 — The Field of Tih: Shade, Manna, and Quail

**Why the Punishment of Tih:** Bani Israel had been commanded to enter the land of Palestine (Bayt al-Maqdis) and fight its unjust rulers (*jabbarīn* — tyrants). They refused, saying:
> "Go, you and your Lord, and fight — we will not go."

This disobedience led to their being punished by being confined to the field of Tih for **40 years**. In this area, they would walk all morning to cross it, only to find themselves back at the same starting point by evening — unable to exit.

**Blessings During Punishment:**
Despite being in a state of punishment, Allah blessed them with:

1. **Shade from clouds** — Allah covered them with clouds like a canopy/umbrella to protect them from the intense heat of the sun in the open desert.

2. **Al-Mann (مَنّ)** — A honey-like liquid that descended upon the leaves of trees. They would mix it with water and drink it. This provided their sustenance without effort.

3. **As-Salwā (سَلْوَى)** — A quail-like bird of delicious taste, sent down to them for food.

Allah commanded them:
> *كُلُوا مِن طَيِّبَاتِ مَا رَزَقْنَاكُمْ*
> "Eat of the good things We have provided for you."

**Key Lesson:** Even in punishment, Allah's mercy and provision did not cease. These blessings were provided *without effort* — something that in ordinary life requires great exertion to obtain.

---

### Ayah 57 — Allah Did Not Wrong Them

> *وَمَا ظَلَمُونَا وَلَٰكِن كَانُوا أَنفُسَهُمْ يَظْلِمُونَ*
> "They did not wrong Us, but they wronged themselves."

The teacher explained: the bad consequence (*wabāl*) of disobedience returns to the one who disobeys — not to Allah. Their rejection of Allah's blessings harmed only themselves.

---

### Ayah 58 — The Command to Enter Bayt al-Maqdis

After the punishment in Tih, Bani Israel were commanded to enter the holy land:

> *ادْخُلُوا هَٰذِهِ الْقَرْيَةَ*
> "Enter this town [Bayt al-Maqdis]."

They were given three instructions:
1. **Eat from it wherever you wish** (*fakulū minhā ḥaythu shi'tum raghadan*) — abundantly and with enjoyment
2. **Enter the gate prostrating** (*wadhkulū al-bāba sujjadan*) — in a state of sajdah, as a gesture of gratitude to Allah for their salvation from the Tih
3. **Say "Ḥiṭṭah"** (*qūlū ḥiṭṭah*) — i.e., "O Allah, remove our sins and forgive us" — repeating it as they entered

**Allah's Promise in Return:**
> "We will forgive your sins (*nughfir lakum khaṭāyākum*) and We will increase the reward of the *Muḥsinīn*."

The *Muḥsin* is one who performs every action with excellence (*iḥsān*). Even among those all performing the same act of worship, the quality of their performance differs — and Allah increases the reward of those who do it best.

---

### Ayah 59 — Their Mockery and the Plague

The wrongdoers (*alladhīna ẓalamū*) among Bani Israel did the opposite of what was commanded:

- **Changed the word:** Instead of saying *ḥiṭṭah* (seeking forgiveness), they said *ḥabbatun fī sha'īratin* ("a grain in barley") — a meaningless, mocking substitution.
- **Changed the manner:** Instead of entering prostrating, they entered crawling on their backsides (*yazḥafūna 'alā astāhihim*).

This was done *sakhiran min awāmiri Allāh* — as an act of outright mockery of Allah's commands.

**The Punishment:**
> *فَأَنزَلْنَا عَلَى الَّذِينَ ظَلَمُوا رِجْزًا مِّنَ السَّمَاءِ*
> "So We sent down upon those who wronged a punishment from the sky."

This punishment was a **plague (ṭā'ūn)**. According to a narration cited in the lesson, **70,000 people died in a single hour** from this plague.

---

### Ayah 60 — The Miracle of the Rock (Brief Introduction)

The session began introducing Ayah 60 before ending. While in the field of Tih, Bani Israel suffered extreme thirst. Musa عليه السلام supplicated to Allah, who instructed him to strike a rock with his staff (*'aṣā*). When he struck it:

> *فَانفَجَرَتْ مِنْهُ اثْنَتَا عَشْرَةَ عَيْنًا*
> "12 springs gushed forth from it."

Since Bani Israel were divided into **12 tribes**, each tribe received its own dedicated spring (*jadwal khāṣṣ*), shared with no other tribe. The full tafseer of this ayah was deferred to the next session.

## Grammar and Language Notes

- **Ta'ajjub patterns** — Two recognized forms in Arabic:
  - *Mā af'alahu* → e.g., *mā a'ẓamahu* (How magnificent He is!)
  - *Af'il bihi* → e.g., *an'im bihi*, *akrim bihi*
  - In context: expressing how wicked/disgraceful they are

- **تِيه (Tīh)** — Derived from the verb *tāha yatīhu*, meaning to wander aimlessly. The field of Tih is literally "the field of wandering."

- **جَهْرَةً (*jahratan*)** — An adverb meaning "openly/manifestly," as opposed to something hidden. Used to describe their demand to see Allah directly and openly.

- **سُجَّدًا (*sujjadan*)** — Plural of *sājid*, one who prostrates. They were to enter as a group all in prostration.

- **رَغَدًا (*raghadan*)** — Indicates abundance combined with ease and happiness; connotes enjoying blessings in a pleasant, carefree manner.

- **بَعَثَ (*ba'atha*)** — Used for resurrection and also for sending/dispatching a prophet. Context determines meaning.

## Lessons and Practical Takeaways

1. **Iḥsān in worship matters** — Allah specifically promises increased reward for the *Muḥsinīn*. Performing every act of worship to the best of one's ability is not optional excellence — it is the goal.

2. **Disobedience harms only oneself** — As Allah states, they did not wrong Allah; they wronged themselves. The *wabāl* (evil consequence) of any sin returns to its doer.

3. **Allah's blessings do not stop even in punishment** — During 40 years of wandering in the Tih, Bani Israel still received shade, manna, and quail from Allah. This reflects the all-encompassing nature of Allah's *raḥmah*.

4. **The power of Musa's supplication** — Musa's weeping and persistence in dua led to the resurrection of 70 people. This is a lesson in the importance of sincere, sustained supplication and interceding for one's community.

5. **Gratitude must be active** — Allah commanded them to enter prostrating as an act of *shukr* (gratitude). Gratitude is not merely a feeling but an act of submission and recognition.

6. **The danger of mocking divine commands** — The wrongdoers among Bani Israel changed both the word and the action out of mockery. The punishment was immediate and devastating — 70,000 dead in one hour. Treating Allah's commands lightly is among the gravest of sins.

7. **Resurrection is the ultimate sign of divine power** — *Iḥyā' al-mawtā* (bringing the dead back to life) is entirely in Allah's hands. When Bani Israel were revived, it served as proof: *yuḥyī wa yumītu wa huwa 'alā kulli shay'in qadīr*.

## Historical Context and Stories Mentioned

### The Golden Calf Incident (Background)
While Musa عليه السلام was at Mount Tur for 40 nights, Bani Israel in his absence began worshipping the Golden Calf (*al-'ijl*). Upon his return, after discussions, they repented. Musa then selected 70 of the best and most chosen among Bani Israel to go to Tur to formally apologize to Allah.

### The 70 at Mount Tur
The 70 chosen representatives heard Allah's speech directly but still refused to accept it as proof of His existence/message without seeing Him *openly* (*jahratan*). This demand resulted in the thunderbolt. Musa's grief at their death — having brought the best of his community only to see them struck down — led to his prolonged, tearful supplication.

### The Field of Tih (40-Year Punishment)
Referenced in Surah Al-Ma'idah as well. Bani Israel were commanded to enter Palestine and fight the *jabbarīn* (tyrants/oppressors) who had unjustly occupied it. Their response was: "Go, you and your Lord, and fight — we are sitting here." As punishment for this refusal to obey Allah's military command, they were confined to the field of Tih for 40 years, unable to leave despite walking all day.

### Entering Bayt al-Maqdis
After the 40 years concluded, Bani Israel were commanded to enter Bayt al-Maqdis with humility, prostration, and seeking forgiveness. The wrongdoers among them mocked this command entirely — changing the prescribed word and the prescribed posture — and were punished with a plague that killed 70,000 in one hour.

### The Miracle of the Rock
In the field of Tih, when they faced severe thirst, Musa struck a rock with his staff at Allah's command and 12 springs emerged — one dedicated to each of the 12 tribes of Bani Israel. *(Full tafseer deferred to next session.)*

## Q&A Highlights

**Q: Was the word "mā" in the munāsabah a negation (nāfi) or something else? And what is the grammatical structure?**
A: It is a *ta'ajjub* (expression of wonder/astonishment), not *nāfi*. The structure *mā af'alahu* or *af'il bihi* expresses "how much" something is the case — here, how wicked and disgraceful they are.

**Q: Did Bani Israel have any reason for their disobedience, or were they just trying to disobey?**
A: The teacher clarified that there was no valid reason — it was purely the nature of disobedience that characterized them throughout their history. In this specific case (entering the gate), they did it *sakhiran* — out of mockery of Allah's commands. The student noted it "doesn't make sense" that they would put active effort into disobeying, to which the teacher affirmed: this was precisely the pattern seen across every event involving Bani Israel — blessed abundantly, yet disobeying at every turn.
