## Session Overview

This session covered the tafseer of Surah Al-Baqarah, Ayah 24 (page 46 of the textbook), focusing on the challenge (Tahhaddi) posed by Allah to those who deny the Qur'an's divine origin. The lesson examined the linguistic dimensions of the Qur'anic inimitability (I'jaz), the description of Hellfire and its fuel, and concluded with a grammatical exercise on the verb اتَّقُوا. Students were assigned homework on the conjugation of the verb اتَّقى.

---

## Key Themes and Topics

1. **The Eternal Challenge of the Qur'an** — Allah's declaration that mankind can never produce a surah comparable to the Qur'an, neither in the past nor in the future.
2. **I'jaz al-Qur'an (Inimitability of the Qur'an)** — Multiple dimensions of Qur'anic miracle: apparent (zahira) and hidden (batina), linguistic and beyond.
3. **The Role of لَن (Lan)** — Its grammatical function as a particle negating an action forever into the future.
4. **Description of Hellfire** — Its fuel (waqud), those for whom it is prepared, and the nature of its punishment.
5. **Passive Voice in Arabic** — Analysis of أُعِدَّت (u'iddat) as a passive past-tense verb form.
6. **Classroom Grammar Exercise** — Conjugation and I'rab of the verb اتَّقُوا.

## Main Tafseer Points and Explanations

### Ayah 24 — فَإِن لَّمْ تَفْعَلُوا وَلَن تَفْعَلُوا فَاتَّقُوا النَّارَ الَّتِي وَقُودُهَا النَّاسُ وَالْحِجَارَةُ أُعِدَّتْ لِلْكَافِرِينَ

**"But if you do not — and you never will — then fear the Fire whose fuel is people and stones, prepared for the disbelievers."**

---

#### 1. فَإِن لَّمْ تَفْعَلُوا — "If you do not do that"

- This refers to the challenge in the preceding ayah: produce a surah like the Qur'an.
- وَعَجَزْتُمْ فِي الْمَاضِي — "You were incapable in the past."
- Even when the Arabs — the most eloquent people of the world, masters of Arabic rhetoric and language — could not meet this challenge, it proves the Qur'an is beyond human capacity.
- The challenge was open to all: linguists, poets, rhetoricians, and even all of humanity and jinn combined.
- Reference to another ayah: لَا يَأْتُونَ بِمِثْلِهِ وَلَوْ كَانَ بَعْضُهُمْ لِبَعْضٍ ظَهِيرًا — "They cannot produce its like even if they helped one another."

---

#### 2. وَلَن تَفْعَلُوا — "And you will never do it"

- This is a **parenthetical clause** (جُمْلَةٌ اعْتِرَاضِيَّة) inserted to emphasize the point.
- The particle **لَن** indicates **permanent, eternal negation** — not merely "you did not" but "you never will, ever."
- وَلَن تَقْدِرُوا فِي الْمُسْتَقْبَلِ أَيْضًا — "In the future you also will not be capable of it."
- This is a **qati'an jaziman** — a decisive, conclusive, confirmed divine statement.
- It is made غَيْرَ خَائِفٍ وَلَا مُشْفِقٍ — "without fear and without apprehension," meaning Allah makes this statement with complete certainty.
- Ibn Kathir confirms: كَذَلِكَ وَقَعَ الْأَمْرُ — "And so it has come to pass in history." From the time of the Prophet ﷺ until today, the Qur'an has never been successfully challenged (لَمْ يُعَارَض).

---

#### 3. The Miracle of the Qur'an (I'jaz al-Qur'an)

- Whoever contemplates the Qur'an (مَن تَدَبَّرَ الْقُرْآنَ) will find many dimensions of miracle (وُجُوهُ الإِعْجَاز).
- Some miracles are **apparent** (ظَاهِرَة) and some are **hidden** (بَاطِنَة).
- Some miracles were not apparent in the time of the Prophet ﷺ but have become clear throughout history and in our era.
- The entire Qur'an is eloquent (جَمِيعُهُ فَصِيحٌ) — at the highest level of eloquence and clarity (فِي غَايَةِ نِهَايَاتِ الْفَصَاحَةِ وَالْبَيَانِ).
- This eloquence is recognizable by anyone who knows Arabic language and the full range of Arabic speech.

---

#### 4. فَاتَّقُوا النَّارَ — "So fear the Fire"

- Since you cannot meet the challenge, the logical conclusion is: accept the Qur'an and fear the punishment of Allah.
- خَافُوا عَذَابَ اللَّهِ عَزَّ وَجَلَّ — Fear the punishment of Allah.
- وَاحْذَرُوا نَارَ الْجَحِيم — Beware and protect yourselves from the fire of Hell.

---

#### 5. الَّتِي وَقُودُهَا النَّاسُ وَالْحِجَارَة — "Whose fuel is people and stones"

- **وَقُود** = the fuel/substance by which a fire is kindled and kept burning.
- The fuel of Hellfire consists of **people** (the disbelievers) and **stones**.
- Cross-reference: إِنَّكُمْ وَمَا تَعْبُدُونَ مِن دُونِ اللَّهِ حَصَبُ جَهَنَّمَ — "You and what you worship besides Allah are the firewood of Hell." (Surah Al-Anbiya)
- This means both the disbelievers themselves and the idols they worshipped will be fuel for Hellfire.
- **Mujahid's interpretation of الْحِجَارَة (stones):** These are stones of **sulfur** (كِبْرِيت). Sulfur stones are more foul-smelling and more pungent than carrion (جِيفَة — decaying flesh of a dead animal). The people of Hellfire will be tormented alongside this unbearable stench.

---

#### 6. أُعِدَّتْ لِلْكَافِرِينَ — "Prepared for the disbelievers"

- الَّتِي جَعَلَهَا اللَّهُ جَزَاءً لِلْكَافِرِينَ — "Which Allah has made as a recompense for the deniers."
- The disbelievers — those who deny the Qur'an, deny Allah's signs, worship idols — will face in Hellfire: عَذَابًا مُحِيطًا مُهِينًا — an all-encompassing, humiliating punishment.

## Grammar and Language Notes

### 1. لَن (Lan) — Eternal Negation
- **لَن** is used to negate an action in the future **permanently and forever**.
- In وَلَن تَفْعَلُوا, it means: "You will never — not now, not ever — be able to do this."
- It is stronger than لَا (la) and specifically projects into the indefinite future.

---

### 2. جُمْلَةٌ اعْتِرَاضِيَّة — Parenthetical Clause
- وَلَن تَفْعَلُوا is identified by the commentator as a **parenthetical clause** (جُمْلَة اعْتِرَاضِيَّة).
- It is inserted within the flow of the ayah to reinforce the point about mankind's permanent incapacity.

---

### 3. فَاتَّقُوا — Grammatical Analysis (Classroom Exercise)
- **Sentence:** فَاتَّقُوا النَّارَ
- **Words:** فَ (particle) + اتَّقُوا (verb) + النَّارَ (noun)
- **Form:** اتَّقُوا is **Amr** (imperative/command form), plural masculine.
- **Root verb conjugation** (assigned as homework):
  - **Madhi (past):** اتَّقَى
  - **Mudari' (present/future):** يَتَّقِي
  - **Masdar (verbal noun):** اتِّقَاءً
  - **Amr (command):** اتَّقِ / اتَّقُوا
  - **Nahi (prohibition):** لَا تَتَّقِ / لَا تَتَّقُوا

> **Homework:** Write the full conjugation of اتَّقَى (Madhi, Mudari', Amr, Nahi) with complete I'rab on each form.

---

### 4. أُعِدَّتْ — Passive Past Tense (Madhi Majhul)
- **أُعِدَّتْ** is from the root أَعَدَّ — يُعِدُّ — إِعْدَادًا (to prepare).
- Active form: أَعَدَّ (he prepared)
- Passive form (Madhi Majhul): أُعِدَّت (it was prepared)
- Students were asked to identify its Madhi and Mudari' forms in class.

---

### 5. ظَهِيرًا — From ظَهَرَ
- In the ayah لَا يَأْتُونَ بِمِثْلِهِ وَلَوْ كَانَ بَعْضُهُمْ لِبَعْضٍ ظَهِيرًا:
- **ظَهِيرًا** = Mu'in / Nasir = a supporter, helper, backer.
- Even if all of humanity and jinn backed each other up as helpers, they still could not produce a surah like the Qur'an.

## Lessons and Practical Takeaways

1. **The Qur'an is a living miracle.** Its inimitability is not a historical claim — it is an ongoing, present-tense reality. No one has met the challenge, and Allah declares no one ever will.

2. **Contemplating the Qur'an reveals its depth.** The more one reflects (تَدَبُّر), the more dimensions of miracle one discovers — some apparent, some hidden, some only uncovered as history unfolds.

3. **The challenge of the Qur'an demands a response.** If you cannot produce its like, the logical and only appropriate response is: fear Allah, accept the Qur'an, and guard yourself from Hellfire.

4. **Hellfire is real and prepared.** It is not a metaphor. It has been created and prepared specifically as a recompense for those who deny. Its description — fuel of people and sulfur stones, stench worse than carrion — is meant to awaken a visceral fear.

5. **Arabic mastery unlocks Qur'anic appreciation.** The teacher repeatedly emphasized that recognizing the Qur'an's eloquence requires familiarity with Arabic rhetoric (balāgha). This is a motivation to study Arabic deeply.

6. **Grammar is a tool for understanding.** The in-class analysis of اتَّقُوا and أُعِدَّت shows how knowing Arabic morphology (sarf) and syntax (nahw) directly enhances comprehension of Qur'anic meaning.

## Historical Context and Stories Mentioned

- **The Arab Challenge:** The audience of this challenge were the Arabs of the Prophet's ﷺ time — the most eloquent people of the world, unrivaled in poetry and rhetoric. Despite their motivation to disprove the Qur'an and their mastery of language, they failed utterly.
- **Ibn Kathir's observation:** He notes كَذَلِكَ وَقَعَ الْأَمْرُ — this is exactly how history played out. From the Prophet ﷺ to Ibn Kathir's own time, the Qur'an was never successfully challenged. The same holds true today.
- **Mujahid's tafseer of الحجارة:** The great Tabi'i mufassir Mujahid interpreted the "stones" of Hellfire as sulfur stones, which are extremely foul-smelling — more so than the carrion of dead animals.
- **Cross-reference — Surah Al-Anbiya:** The ayah إِنَّكُمْ وَمَا تَعْبُدُونَ مِن دُونِ اللَّهِ حَصَبُ جَهَنَّمَ establishes that both the polytheists and their idols are fuel for Hellfire.
