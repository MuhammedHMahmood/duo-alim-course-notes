## Key Themes and Topics

1. **Refutation of the Jews' False Claim** — Their assertion that the Fire would only touch them for a limited period is invalidated by Allah with the word *balā*.
2. **Condition for Eternal Punishment** — Whoever earns evil and whose sin encircles them from all sides will be a permanent resident of the Fire.
3. **Contrast: Believers vs. Transgressors** — Those who combine imān with righteous deeds will reside eternally in the gardens of Paradise.
4. **The Covenant of Banu Isra'eel (Mīthāq)** — A comprehensive covenant was taken from them in the Torah covering tawheed, parental rights, care for relatives/orphans/poor, kind speech, salah, and zakah.
5. **Crimes of the Jews Enumerated** — Breaking the covenant, unlawful killing, unjust seizure of wealth, aggression against their own brethren, and expelling them from their homes.
6. **Consequence of Covenant-Breaking** — They earned la'nat (damnation), khizya (shame), and damār (destruction).

## Main Tafseer Points and Explanations

### Ayah 81 — Refutation of the Jews' Claim

The Jews claimed: *"The Fire shall not touch us except for a few numbered days"* (لَنْ تَمَسَّنَا النَّارُ إِلَّا أَيَّامًا مَعْدُودَةً). Allah subhānahu wa ta'ālā exposes this as a false claim (*kidhb*) with no evidence.

Allah responds with **balā** — a powerful Arabic particle that:
- Negates what came before (their false claim)
- Affirms what follows (the true reality)

The true condition: **whoever earns evil deeds AND whose sin encircles him from all sides** — such a person is a permanent resident of the Fire.

The phrase *aḥāṭat bihi khaṭī'atuh* means:
- His sin has surrounded him from every direction
- It has blocked all pathways of salvation (*masālik al-najāh*) for him
- He is overwhelmed by sin on all sides with no escape

These are the *aṣḥāb al-nār* — they will remain in the Fire forever, just as a disbeliever (*kāfir*) who committed major sins (*kabā'ir*) remains there forever.

### Ayah 82 — The Believers' Reward

In contrast, **those who combine two things**:
1. Īmān (belief)
2. ʿAmal ṣāliḥ (righteous deeds)

Their outcome:
- The Fire will **not** touch them
- They will be in *rawḍāt al-jannāt* — the gardens of Paradise
- They will be *yuḥbarūn* — rejoicing and delighted
- They are *aṣḥāb al-jannah* — eternal residents of Paradise

The teacher made du'ā: *Allāhumma ij'alnā minhum yā Raḥmān* — O Allah, make us among them.

### Ayaat 83–86 — Crimes of Banu Isra'eel and Their Broken Covenant

**Overview of this passage (pp. 74 onward):** These verses enumerate clear (*ṣārīkhah*) and blatant examples of the Jews' aggression (*ʿudwān*), tyranny (*ṭughyān*), and corruption (*ifsād*) on earth.

**The Mīthāq (Covenant) taken from Banu Isra'eel** — Allah reminded them of a solemn, most emphatic covenant taken from their forefathers. The covenant contained the following obligations:

| # | Obligation |
|---|---|
| 1 | Worship none but Allah (pure tawḥīd) |
| 2 | Be good/kind to parents (*walidayn iḥsānan*) |
| 3 | Do good to relatives (*dhī al-qurbā*) |
| 4 | Care for orphans (*yatāmā*) — those whose fathers died while they were young |
| 5 | Care for the needy (*masākīn*) — those unable to earn for themselves |
| 6 | Speak kind words to people (*qawlan ḥasanan*) |
| 7 | Be gentle and humble in manner (*khafd al-janāḥ*) |
| 8 | Establish prayer (*iqāmat al-ṣalāh*) |
| 9 | Give zakāh (*ītā' al-zakāh*) |

**Crimes committed in violation of this covenant:**
- Broke the covenant (*naqaḍū al-mīthāq*)
- Killed those Allah forbade killing — innocent, unlawful killings
- Took people's wealth unjustly
- Attacked their own religious brothers (fellow Jews)
- Expelled them from their homes and homelands

**Result:** They deserved *la'nah* (damnation), *khizy* (shame), and *damār* (destruction) from Allah.

**Address to the Jews of the Prophet's ﷺ time:** When Allah says "you turned away," He is addressing the Jews of the Prophet's era — reminding them that the crimes of their forefathers are a warning for them, as they too followed the same path of rejection.

## Grammar and Language Notes

- **بَلَى (balā):** A response particle used to negate a previously stated negative claim and affirm the positive truth. Different from *naʿam*, which simply confirms. Here it directly refutes the Jews' claim.
- **أَحَاطَ (aḥāṭa):** Verb on the pattern *افعل* (*af'ala*), from root ح-و-ط. *Aḥāṭa bihi* = to surround something completely.
- **مَسَالِك (masālik):** Plural of *maslak* (مَسْلَك) — a path, a route, a way.
- **نَجَاة (najāh):** Salvation, deliverance — from root ن-ج-و.
- **يُحْبَرُون (yuḥbarūn):** From *ḥabara* — to make joyful, to cause to rejoice. Passive form: they are made to rejoice.
- **تَوَلَّيْتُمْ (tawallaytum):** Past tense, second person plural from *tawallā* — to turn away, to abandon. The *tā* at the end indicates the second person plural (*antum*).
- **مُعْرِضُون (muʿriḍūn):** Active participle from *aʿraḍa* — one who turns away. The phrase *wa antum muʿriḍūn* emphasizes the manner of their turning away: they did so completely and deliberately.
- **أَشْلَاف (ashlāf):** Plural of *shalf* — forefathers, ancestors, predecessors.

## Lessons and Practical Takeaways

1. **Sin has a surrounding effect** — When a person accumulates evil deeds without repentance, those sins gradually encircle him, closing off all routes to salvation. This is a warning to take sin seriously before it overwhelms us.

2. **Imān and ʿAmal must go together** — Allah pairs belief (*āmanū*) with righteous deeds (*ʿamilū al-ṣāliḥāt*) as the condition for Paradise. One without the other is incomplete.

3. **The covenant obligations are timeless** — Tawheed, honoring parents, caring for orphans and the poor, kind speech, salah, and zakah were obligated upon every nation. These are not unique to Islam; they are universal divine commands.

4. **Salah and Zakah are the twin pillars of worship** — The greatest act of physical worship is salah; the greatest act of financial worship is zakah. Together they represent the full commitment of the believer.

5. **Aggression against fellow Muslims is a major crime** — The Jews are rebuked specifically for attacking and expelling their own brethren. This is a reminder that harming fellow believers is among the gravest violations.

6. **A few remain steadfast** — *Illā qalīlan minkum* — even in corrupt communities, there are always those who remain firm. We should strive to be among that small righteous group.

7. **Consequences of covenant-breaking** — Breaking agreements with Allah leads to *la'nah*, *khizy*, and *damār* — a complete loss of dignity, blessing, and standing in this world and the next.

## Historical Context and Stories Mentioned

- **The Covenant in the Torah (Mīthāq):** Allah took a binding covenant from Banu Isra'eel through their scripture, the Torah. This covenant enumerated the core moral and religious obligations they were required to fulfill. Their systematic violation of this covenant is the central accusation in this passage.

- **Jewish Intra-tribal Warfare:** The Qur'an references a historical reality in which Jewish tribes in Arabia would fight against one another despite belonging to the same religious community — attacking their own brethren, expelling them from their homes, and taking their wealth. This was particularly notable in Medina, where Jewish tribes aligned with different Arab factions and fought against each other.

- **Jews of the Prophet's ﷺ Time:** The address in *wa-idh akhadhnā mīthāqa banī isrā'īl* is directed at the Jews contemporary with the Prophet ﷺ, holding them accountable for the crimes of their forefathers and their own continuation of that pattern of rejection.
