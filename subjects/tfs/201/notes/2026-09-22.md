# Tafseer 201 - Study Notes
**Date:** 2026-09-22
**Surah Covered:** Surah Al-Baqarah, Ayat 44–48

---

## Session Overview

This session covered the tafseer of Surah Al-Baqarah, Ayat 44–48 (from page 54 of the class text). The verses address Bani Israel — specifically the Jewish scholars (Ahbar/Rabbis) — scolding and rebuking them for the contradiction between what they preached and what they practiced. Allah ﷻ then pivots to prescribe the means of overcoming worldly desires (patience and prayer), describes the qualities of the truly humble believers, and closes by reminding Bani Israel of the many favors Allah ﷻ bestowed upon their forefathers. The session concludes with a description of the Day of Judgment in Ayah 48.

---

## Key Themes and Topics

1. **Hypocrisy of the Ahbar (Jewish scholars):** Commanding others to righteousness and belief in the Prophet ﷺ while not following it themselves.
2. **The contradiction exposed:** The Torah itself contained descriptions of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, yet they recited it without accepting him.
3. **Means of overcoming desires:** Seeking Allah's help through Sabr (patience) and Salah (prayer).
4. **The quality of Khushu' (humility/submissiveness):** A key condition for benefiting from prayer.
5. **Firm belief in the Day of Judgment:** The Khashi'in believe with certainty that they will meet their Lord and be held accountable.
6. **Reminder of Allah's favors upon Bani Israel:** Multiple prophets, revealed books, and rulers/kings sent to and from among them.
7. **The gravity of the Day of Judgment:** No intercession, no ransom, and no helper for the disbelievers.

## Main Tafseer Points and Explanations

### Ayah 44 — Commanding Others While Neglecting Oneself
- Allah ﷻ addresses the **Ahbar** (Jewish rabbis and scholars) in a tone of rebuke and scolding (taqri'a and tawbiq).
- The charge: **"Atamuruna al-Nasa bil-Birri"** — Do you command people to righteousness?
- Specifically, they called people to believe in Prophet Muhammad ﷺ (as his coming was foretold in the Torah), yet they themselves refused to believe in him.
- They recited the Torah — which contained descriptions of the Prophet ﷺ — yet still did not accept him.
- Allah ﷻ rebukes them: **"Afala ta'qilun"** — Do you not understand? This behavior is irrational and incoherent.

### Ayah 45 — The Remedy: Patience and Prayer
- After the rebuke, Allah ﷻ prescribes the cure: **"Wasta'inu bil-Sabri wal-Salah"** — Seek help through patience and prayer.
- **Sabr** here means: bearing the burden of Shari'ah obligations (takalif shar'iyyah) — enduring what the self finds difficult in fulfilling religious duties.
- **Salah** is described as the fundamental pillar of religion. Performing it can be difficult, yet it is a means of drawing near to Allah.
- Salah is **easy only for the Khashi'in** — the humble and submissive.

### Description of the Khashi'in
- The Khashi'in are those whose inner souls and hearts are pure, devoted solely to Allah ﷻ.
- They are described as those who perform salah with true humility and submission.

### Ayah 46 — The Quality of Certainty (Yaqeen)
- **"Alladhina yadhunnuna annahum mulaqun rabbihim"** — Those who are certain they will meet their Lord.
- Note on **yadhunnun**: The word ظَنّ (dhann) ordinarily means "to suppose" or "to think." Here, however, it carries the meaning of **firm, certain belief** — **i'tiqadan jaziman la yukhalijah shakk** (a decisive belief without any doubt). This is a known usage in Arabic where context elevates dhann to certainty.
- They believe they will stand before Allah ﷻ, be held accountable for their deeds (**fa-yuhasibuhum 'ala a'malihim**), and that their final return is to Him (**wa annahum ilayhi raji'un**).

### Ayah 47 — Reminder of Favors Upon Bani Israel
- Allah ﷻ again addresses: **"Ya Bani Isra'il, adhkuru ni'matiya allati an'amtu alaykum"** — O Children of Israel, remember My favors which I bestowed upon you.
- The proper way to remember these favors is through **shukr (gratitude) expressed via ta'ah (obedience)**.
- **"Wa anni fadhdhaltu-kum 'ala al-'alamin"** — And I preferred you above the worlds (of your time).
- Important clarification: The address is to the Jews of Medina at the time of revelation, but it refers to the history and honors given to their **forefathers** — not to the contemporaries directly.
- The preference/tafdhil of Bani Israel came through:
  1. **Sending many Prophets and Messengers** from among them (e.g., Musa, Dawud, Sulayman ﷺ)
  2. **Revealing divine books** to them
  3. **Making them rulers and kings** (e.g., Dawud and Sulayman ﷺ)
- Principle stated: **"Tafdhilu al-aba'i sharafun lil-abna'"** — The honoring of the fathers is an honor for the sons/children as well. Their forefathers' distinction is a source of pride and dignity for their descendants.

### Ayah 48 — The Severity of the Day of Judgment
- **"Wattaqu yawman la tajzi nafsun 'an nafsin shay'an"** — Fear the Day when no soul shall avail another soul anything.
- Four conditions of that Day for the disbelievers:
  1. **No soul shall help another** — no mutual benefit
  2. **No intercession shall be accepted** for a disbelieving soul — **"wa la yuqbalu minha shafa'ah"**
  3. **No ransom/compensation shall be taken** — **"wa la yu'khadhu minha 'adlun"** (la yuqbalu minha fida'un)
  4. **No helper** — **"wa la hum yunsarun"** — they will have no one to protect them from Allah's punishment (laysa lahum man yamna'uhum wa yunajjihim min 'adhab Allah)

## Grammar and Language Notes

- **Tatluuna** — from تَلَا يَتْلُو تِلَاوَةً; second person plural present tense. "You (all) recite."
- **Taqri'a** — verbal noun (masdar) from كَرَّعَ يُقَرِّعُ; means a formal, pointed rebuke.
- **Tawbiq** — verbal noun from وَبَّخَ يُوَبِّخُ; a close synonym to taqri'a, meaning to scold or censure.
- **Yadhunnun / Yadh-dhunnun** — The root ظَنَّ typically implies supposition. Its use here for *certain* belief is a contextual usage (qarīnah) — the context (meeting Allah on the Day of Judgment) demands certainty, so the scholars interpret it as **yaqeen** (certainty) rather than mere conjecture.
- **Yu'khadhu** — passive form (mabni lil-majhul) of أَخَذَ يَأْخُذُ; "it shall be taken."
- **'Adl** — in this ayah means compensation or ransom (فِدَاء), not the common meaning of "justice." Context determines meaning.
- **Atamuruna** — second person plural present tense of أَمَرَ يَأْمُرُ; "Do you (all) command?" The hamzat al-istifham (interrogative hamzah) at the beginning signals a rhetorical rebuke.

## Lessons and Practical Takeaways

1. **Practice what you preach.** The gravest contradiction is calling others to good while neglecting it yourself. This is explicitly condemned in the Quran.
2. **Knowledge without action is a liability.** The Ahbar knew the truth from their scripture yet rejected it — knowing is not enough; one must act.
3. **Use Sabr and Salah as tools against nafs.** When desires, love of leadership, or love of wealth tempt you, the Quran prescribes patience and prayer as the antidote.
4. **Khushu' in Salah is essential.** Salah without humility loses much of its benefit. Strive to cultivate internal submission (khushu') in every prayer.
5. **Yaqeen in the Akhirah is transformative.** Firm belief in meeting Allah ﷻ and being held accountable is what drives righteous behavior.
6. **Gratitude for Allah's blessings must be expressed through obedience**, not merely words.
7. **The Day of Judgment offers no shortcuts.** No wealth, no intercession (for disbelievers), no connections — prepare now.

## Historical Context and Stories Mentioned

- **Bani Israel and the Ahbar:** The Jewish rabbis and scholars of Medina at the time of the Prophet ﷺ are the primary addressees of these verses. They were aware of prophecies regarding Prophet Muhammad ﷺ in the Torah but refused to accept him out of pride, love of power, and love of wealth.
- **The Torah's testimony:** The Tawrah contained descriptions of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ. Despite reciting these passages, the scholars of Bani Israel chose not to act on them.
- **Prophets among Bani Israel:** Allah ﷻ sent an extraordinary number of prophets to and from Bani Israel — including Musa, Dawud, and Sulayman ﷺ. Dawud and Sulayman ﷺ were also kings, representing Allah's favor of combining prophethood with kingship.
- **The principle of ancestral honor:** The honor given to the forefathers of Bani Israel — through prophecy, divine books, and kingship — was also a source of dignity for their descendants, making their rejection of the Prophet ﷺ all the more inexcusable.
