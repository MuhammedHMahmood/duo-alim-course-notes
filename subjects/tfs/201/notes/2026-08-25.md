## Session Overview

This session continued the tafseer of the munafiqeen (hypocrites) passages in Surah Al-Baqarah, working through the textual commentary (likely from a classical tafseer text, referenced page 36 onward). The instructor explained the grammatical and rhetorical structures emphasizing the corruption of the munafiqeen, their mockery of the believers, Allah's response to their mockery, the parable of the fire, and concluded with three descriptors (deaf, dumb, blind). References were made to Ibn Abbas (رضي الله عنه) and Ibn Katheer (رحمه الله) throughout.

---

## Key Themes and Topics

1. **Inna + Hum + Al-Mufsidoon** — The grammatical structure of emphasis confirming the munafiqeen are the true corruptors
2. **Lack of Iman as spiritual blindness** — Their failure to perceive their own corruption stems from having no light of Iman in their hearts
3. **The munafiqeen's mockery of the Sahabah** — They called believers (Bilal, Suhaib, Ammar, رضي الله عنهم) foolish and illiterate
4. **Allah's reversal** — Allah restricts and limits foolishness (safaha) to the munafiqeen themselves
5. **Their double-faced behavior (nifaq and musana'ah)** — Showing Iman to believers, returning to their shayateen when alone
6. **Allahu Yastahzi'u bihim** — Allah's retribution mirrors their mockery in wording but is actual punishment
7. **Extending them in transgression (yamudduhum fi tughyanihim)** — Allah's giving respite as a form of punishment
8. **The trade metaphor** — They exchanged guidance for misguidance, a profitless transaction
9. **The parable of the fire** — Their condition like one who kindles a fire, then is left in total darkness
10. **Summun Bukmun Umyun** — Deaf, dumb, blind — they will not return to guidance

## Main Tafseer Points and Explanations

### 1. The Grammatical Emphasis in "Inna Humul Mufsidoon" (Ayah 12)
The construction uses multiple layers of emphasis:
- **إِنَّ** — particle of affirmation (for confirmation)
- **هُمْ** — a separating pronoun (dameer fasl) creating a Mubtada-Khabar structure
- **الْمُفْسِدُونَ** — the predicate with the definite article (for restriction/hasr)

All three elements together serve to *restrict and emphasize*: it is they — and none other — who are the corruptors. Yet they do not perceive this because the light of Iman has been extinguished in their hearts (intimas noor al-iman).

### 2. "Wa Lakin La Yash'uroon" — They Do Not Perceive (Ayah 12)
The reason for their blindness: they have no light of Iman. A person with Iman can feel when they are doing wrong — the light of faith illuminates the conscience. Without it, there is no internal check. This is spiritual blindness at its core.

### 3. The Munafiqeen's Response to Being Called to Sincere Iman (Ayah 13)
When told: *"Aminu kama amana al-naas"* (Believe as the people — i.e., the Sahabah — believed):
- They responded with **أَنُؤْمِنُ كَمَا آمَنَ السُّفَهَاءُ** — "Should we believe as the fools believed?"
- The hamza (أ) here is not a genuine question but an **interrogative of mockery** (hamzat al-inkaar / al-istihza')
- They called the Sahabah — Bilal, Suhaib, Ammar (رضي الله عنهم) — sufaha' (fools), juhal (ignorant), and ahl al-faqr (poor/slaves)
- Their motive: **تحقير شأنهم** — to belittle, disgrace, and degrade the status of Muslims publicly
- Most early believers were poor or slaves, giving the munafiqeen social cover to demean them

### 4. Allah's Response: Restricting Foolishness to the Munafiqeen (Ayah 13)
**أَلَا إِنَّهُمْ هُمُ السُّفَهَاءُ** — "Indeed, they are the fools."
- **أَلَا** — particle for alerting attention: "Beware!" / "Listen!"
- **هُمُ** — again a dameer fasl for restriction (hasr)
- Allah uses **حَصْر** (restriction) to limit foolishness exclusively to the munafiqeen

Justification cited: *"Whoever rides upon falsehood (batil) is undoubtedly a fool — without any doubt."*

**وَلَكِنْ لَا يَعْلَمُونَ** — They do not know (about their own condition), due to their misguidance and spiritual ignorance.

### 5. Their Double-Faced Behavior — Nifaq and Musana'ah (Ayah 14)
**وَإِذَا لَقُوا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا قَالُوا آمَنَّا**
- In front of believers: "We have believed (amanna)"
- They showed **Iman and muwalah (alliance)** to the Muslims — out of nifaq and musana'ah (flattering/duplicity), not conviction

**وَإِذَا خَلَوْا إِلَى شَيَاطِينِهِمْ**
- **خَلَا إِلَى** (khala ila) = to be alone in a place with someone
- When alone with their leaders (described as shayateen — devils) — the elders of misguidance and nifaq
- They said: *"We are with you. We are only mocking the Muslims (innama nahnu mustahzi'un)."*
- They clarified: they follow the same belief as their leaders; the display of Iman is pure mockery and imitation

### 6. "Allahu Yastahzi'u Bihim" — Divine Retribution in Kind (Ayah 15)
This is a profound stylistic feature:
- They said: **نَحْنُ مُسْتَهْزِئُونَ** (we are mockers)
- Allah responds: **اللَّهُ يَسْتَهْزِئُ بِهِمْ** (Allah mocks them)

The same root word is used, but:
- Their istihza' = mere words/actions of mockery
- Allah's istihza' = **actual punishment** — specifically through **imla'** (giving respite, delaying punishment) followed by a final severe punishment

**Ibn Abbas (رضي الله عنه):** Allah's yaskhar (mocking) of them = taking revenge (linikma minhum), by giving them respite (wayumli lahum).

**Ibn Katheer (رحمه الله):** This is Allah informing us He will retribute the munafiqeen for their mockery — punishing them with the punishment of deception (uqubat al-khida'), because they deceived the believers.

Quranic parallel: **وَأُمْلِي لَهُمْ إِنَّ كَيْدِي مَكِين** — "I give them respite, for My plan is very strong."

### 7. Quranic Style: Same Word, Two Different Meanings
The instructor highlighted this as a recurring Quranic device:
- **جَزَاءُ سَيِّئَةٍ سَيِّئَةٌ مِثْلُهَا** — "The recompense of an evil is an evil like it"
  - First **sayyi'ah** = an evil act (Zulm / injustice)
  - Second **sayyi'ah** = the punishment for it (Adl / justice)
- **فَمَنِ اعْتَدَى عَلَيْكُمْ فَاعْتَدُوا عَلَيْهِ**
  - First i'tada = aggression/injustice
  - Second i'tada = justified retaliation (not injustice)

Conclusion: The word is the same; the meaning and moral status differ entirely.

### 8. "Wa Yamudduhum fi Tughyanihim Ya'mahoon" (Ayah 15)
- **يَمُدُّ** (yamuddu) — to extend, to stretch out
- **طُغْيَان** (tughyan) — transgression
- **يَعْمَهُونَ** — wandering blindly, floundering in confusion

Allah extends them in their transgression *by way of giving them respite* (imhal). Their hearts are sealed, their eyes blinded — they cannot find the right path. They wander like a blind person in a street, unable to determine direction.

Reason stated: **لِأَنَّ اللَّهَ طَبَعَ عَلَى قُلُوبِهِمْ وَأَعْمَى أَبْصَارَهُمْ** — Allah has sealed their hearts and blinded their sight.

### 9. The Profitless Trade (Ayah 16)
**اشْتَرَوُا الضَّلَالَةَ بِالْهُدَى**
- They exchanged (istabdalu) kufr for Iman — disbelief in place of belief
- They took misguidance (dalalah) and paid for it with guidance (huda)
- **فَمَا رَبِحَتْ تِجَارَتُهُمْ** — their trade earned no profit
- Result: they lost the happiness of *both* worlds — dunya and akhirah

### 10. The Parable of the Fire (Ayah 17) — First of Two Parables
**مَثَلُهُمْ كَمَثَلِ الَّذِي اسْتَوْقَدَ نَارًا**

The example:
- A person kindles a fire to get warmth and light
- The fire illuminates everything around him — he feels secure and comfortable
- Suddenly **ذَهَبَ اللَّهُ بِنُورِهِمْ** — Allah takes away their light, extinguishes the fire entirely
- The person is left in **ظُلُمَات كَثِيفَة** (dense darkness) and **خَوْف شَدِيد** (extreme fear)
- He flounders (yatakhabbatoon), unable to find any way out

**Application to the munafiqeen (Ibn Katheer رحمه الله):**
- The fire = their Iman when they first entered Islam
- The light they enjoyed = the benefit and security of living among Muslims
- The extinguishing = Allah taking away their light of Iman after they returned to kufr
- The darkness they are left in = the darkness of doubt, kufr, and hypocrisy
- Indication in this parable: **annahum amanu thumma kafaru** — they believed, then disbelieved

The parable illustrates: *misguidance chosen after insight becomes absolute blindness.*

### 11. "Summun Bukmun Umyun Fahum La Yarji'oon" (Ayah 18)
Three conditions of the munafiqeen:
- **صُمٌّ** — Deaf: they do not hear / heed guidance
- **بُكْمٌ** — Dumb/Mute: they cannot speak anything beneficial to themselves
- **عُمْيٌ** — Blind: they cannot see the right way

**فَهُمْ لَا يَرْجِعُونَ** — They will not return from their state of disobedience and misguidance.

## Grammar and Language Notes

### Singular and Plural Forms (Isma Tafdil Pattern — أَفْعَل)
The three descriptors at the end of the passage follow the pattern of **اسم التفضيل / صفة مشبهة** on the **وزن أَفْعَل**, with broken plurals:

| Singular | Plural (used in ayah) | Form |
|---|---|---|
| أَصَمّ (asam) — deaf | صُمٌّ (summun) | فُعْل |
| أَبْكَم (abkam) — dumb | بُكْمٌ (bukmun) | فُعْل |
| أَعْمَى (a'ma) — blind | عُمْيٌ (umyun) | فُعْل |

The plural form confirms these are referring to a group — the munafiqeen collectively.

### Key Verb Patterns Introduced
| Root / Pattern | Verb | Masdar | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| فَطِنَ يَفْطَنُ | يَفْطَنُ | فِطْنَة | To be alert/perceptive |
| أَحَسَّ يُحِسُّ | يُحِسُّ | إِحْسَاس | To feel/sense |
| اسْتَوْقَدَ يَسْتَوْقِدُ | اسْتَوْقَدَ | اسْتِيقَاد | To kindle a fire |
| اسْتَبْدَلَ يَسْتَبْدِلُ | اسْتَبْدَلَ | اسْتِبْدَال | To exchange/substitute |
| رَبِحَ يَرْبَحُ | رَبِحَ | رِبْح | To profit |
| أَمْلَى يُمْلِي | يُمْلِي | إِمْلَاء | To give respite/delay |
| مَدَّ يَمُدُّ | يَمُدُّ | مَدّ | To extend/stretch |
| عَمِهَ يَعْمَهُ | يَعْمَهُ | عَمَه | To wander blindly |
| أَعْمَى يُعْمِي | يُعْمِي | إِعْمَاء | To blind someone |

### Rhetorical Device: Hamzat al-Istifham lil-Inkaar
**أَنُؤْمِنُ كَمَا آمَنَ السُّفَهَاءُ** — The hamza is not a genuine question but carries the meaning of **mockery and contempt** (istihza' and inkaar). This is a well-known Quranic rhetorical device.

### Hasr (Restriction) Technique
**أَلَا إِنَّهُمْ هُمُ السُّفَهَاءُ**:
- **أَلَا** (alerting particle) + **إِنَّ** (emphasis) + **هُمْ** (dameer fasl for hasr) + **السُّفَهَاءُ** (definite noun)
= Complete restriction of foolishness to the munafiqeen alone

### خَلَا إِلَى vs. خَلَا بِـ
**خَلَا إِلَى** — to be alone with someone *in a place* (implying going/returning to them); different from merely being secluded with someone.

## Lessons and Practical Takeaways

1. **Iman is a light that enables self-awareness.** Without it, a person cannot recognize their own corruption. The first casualty of spiritual blindness is insight into one's own state.

2. **The munafiqeen's defining trait is inconsistency.** They perform Iman publicly and abandon it privately. Consistency between public and private is a mark of true faith.

3. **Calling believers foolish is itself a sign of foolishness.** The munafiqeen mocked the poor Sahabah; Allah turned the description back on them. Social status, wealth, and lineage do not determine wisdom — Iman does.

4. **Allah's punishment can take the form of apparent ease.** Respite (imla') — being left to continue in sin without immediate punishment — is itself a form of divine retribution. Not being corrected is not the same as being approved.

5. **A profitless trade: choosing dunya over deen.** The munafiqeen lost both worlds by trading guidance for misguidance. The metaphor of business is deliberate — they made a calculated choice, and that calculation was catastrophically wrong.

6. **Insight given and then abandoned becomes a greater darkness.** The parable of the fire is not about someone who never had light — it is about someone who *had* it and lost it. Apostasy and nifaq after knowledge is worse than never having known.

7. **The Quran uses the same vocabulary to distinguish an act from its just consequence.** Justice is not vengeance — when Allah uses the same word for an evil act and its punishment, the first is wrong and the second is right.

## Historical Context and Stories Mentioned

- **Bilal ibn Rabah, Suhaib al-Roomi, Ammar ibn Yasir (رضي الله عنهم):** Named explicitly as examples of early Muslims the munafiqeen mocked. Bilal and Suhaib were former slaves; Ammar was among the poor early converts. The munafiqeen used their social and economic status to belittle their faith.

- **Most early believers were among the poor (fuqara').** This social reality is why the munafiqeen's taunt of "sufaha'" carried social weight — they were leveraging class prejudice to discredit Islam.

- **Ibn Abbas (رضي الله عنه):** Cited in explaining *Allahu yastahzi'u bihim* — he interpreted it as Allah taking revenge (nikma) and giving them respite before punishment.

- **Ibn Katheer (رحمه الله):** Cited twice: (1) on Allah's retribution mirroring their mockery in wording but constituting real punishment; (2) on the parable of the fire as illustrating the munafiqeen's trajectory of believing then disbelieving.

## Q&A Highlights

- **Q: Does "fi" in "fi dalalihim wa jahilihim" indicate causality (because of their misguidance) or state (being in a state of misguidance)?**
  A: The instructor clarified it refers to their *state* — that they are *in* misguidance and ignorance, and because of that state they do not know about themselves that they are doing wrong. He noted this is in fact the more severe description: they are so deep in blindness they cannot perceive their own condition. This makes the blindness more profound — not just lacking guidance, but unaware that guidance is absent.

- **Q (implicit — on the plural forms صُمٌّ، بُكْمٌ، عُمْيٌ):** A student asked about the grammatical category (sighah) of these words.
  A: The instructor confirmed they are all plurals on the وزن فُعْل. Singulars are أَصَمّ (asam), أَبْكَم (abkam), أَعْمَى (a'ma) — all on the pattern of اسم تفضيل / صفة مشبهة (أَفْعَل). The plural context is confirmed by **فَهُمْ لَا يَرْجِعُونَ** which uses a plural pronoun.
