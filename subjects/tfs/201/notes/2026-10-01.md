# Tafseer 201 - Study Notes
**Date:** 2026-10-01
**Surah Covered:** Surah Al-Baqarah, Ayat 60–62 (Page 62 of Tafseer text)

## Session Overview

This session covered the tafseer of three ayat from Surah Al-Baqarah. The class began with Ayah 60, which describes Musa (AS) striking the rock to produce water for Bani Isra'il in the Tih (wilderness), including a discussion on whether the rock was specific or general. The session then moved to Ayah 61, covering Bani Isra'il's ingratitude — their complaint about the food (mann and salwa) and their request for earthly vegetables — followed by Musa's (AS) rebuke and the divine consequences of their transgressions. The session concluded with the beginning of Ayah 62, in which Allah invites all religious communities (Muslims, Jews, Christians, Sabians) to true faith and righteous deeds, with the promise of reward and no fear or grief in the Hereafter.

---

## Key Themes and Topics

1. **The Miracle of the Twelve Springs** — Allah's power manifested through Musa (AS) striking the rock; water gushed forth for each of the 12 tribes of Bani Isra'il.
2. **Divine Provision Without Labor** — Mann and salwa provided without physical exertion; a pure bounty (ni'mah) from Allah.
3. **Ingratitude of Bani Isra'il** — Despite extraordinary blessings, they complained about food and requested inferior earthly alternatives.
4. **Consequences of Transgression** — Humiliation (dhull), wretchedness (maskanah), and the wrath of Allah were imposed on Bani Isra'il due to their disbelief, killing of prophets, and disobedience.
5. **Universal Invitation to True Faith (Ayah 62)** — Allah addresses all religious communities; salvation is conditional on sincere belief in Allah and the Last Day, and righteous deeds.
6. **Lesson on Gratitude and Corruption** — The prohibition "do not spread corruption on the earth" applies universally; bounties of Allah must be met with obedience, not sin.

## Main Tafseer Points and Explanations

### Ayah 60 — The Miracle of the Twelve Springs

- **Context:** Bani Isra'il were in the Tih (wilderness) as a form of punishment and became extremely thirsty. Musa (AS) supplicated to Allah on their behalf.
- **The Command:** Allah commanded Musa (AS) to strike *the rock* (اَلْحَجَر) with his staff. Water would gush forth by Allah's power (بِقُدْرَتِنَا).
- **Scholarly Difference on "the rock":** The use of اَلْحَجَر (with the definite article الـ) raises a question:
  - **Some scholars (including the mufassir being studied):** The الـ here is not for specific identification (ta'rīf); it could be any rock — the miracle was that from *any* rock struck, springs would gush. The power belonged entirely to Allah, not to any particular stone.
  - **Majority of mufassiroon:** There was a *specific* rock in the Tih that Musa (AS) was directed to strike. Evidence: other ayat may use اضرب بعصاك الحجر with indications of specificity (e.g., use of ذَلِك or هَذِهِ in related narrations).
  - The instructor noted this remains an open question and will revisit if additional evidence is found.
- **Result:** إِثْنَتَا عَشْرَةَ عَيْنًا — 12 springs gushed forth, corresponding to the 12 tribes (asbāṭ) of Bani Isra'il.
- **Purpose of 12 separate springs:** To prevent quarreling over water. Each tribe (sibṭ) knew its own designated spring. Social order was maintained through divine provision.
- **Divine Command accompanying the miracle:** كُلُوا وَاشْرَبُوا مِن رِّزْقِ اللَّهِ — "Eat and drink from the provision of Allah." This food and drink came without كَدّ (exhausting labor) or تَعَب (tiredness). It was pure, effortless bounty.
- **Accompanying prohibition:** وَلَا تَعْثَوْا فِي الْأَرْضِ مُفْسِدِينَ — "Do not spread corruption on earth." When given a blessing, the response must be gratitude and obedience, not transgression.

---

### Ayah 61 — Bani Isra'il's Ingratitude Over Food

- **Their complaint:** While eating mann and salwa in the Tih, Bani Isra'il said to Musa (AS): لَن نَّصْبِرَ عَلَىٰ طَعَامٍ وَاحِدٍ — "We will never be patient upon one (type of) food." The use of لَنْ (never/emphatic negation) indicates their total rejection and impatience.
- **Their request:** They asked Musa (AS) to supplicate to Allah: فَادْعُ لَنَا رَبَّكَ يُخْرِجْ لَنَا مِمَّا تُنبِتُ الْأَرْضُ — "Call upon your Lord to bring forth for us what the earth grows." They specifically named:
  - بَقْل — herbs/greens
  - قِثَّاء — cucumber
  - فُوم — garlic
  - عَدَس — lentils
  - بَصَل — onions
  - (Also mentioned in discussion: mint, celery, leeks)
- **Musa's (AS) rebuke:** He responded with وَيْحَكُمْ ("Woe to you!") — an expression of strong rebuke and disbelief at their attitude. He said: أَتَسْتَبْدِلُونَ الَّذِي هُوَ أَدْنَىٰ بِالَّذِي هُوَ خَيْرٌ — "Would you exchange that which is better for that which is worse?" Mann and salwa are superior; vegetables, lentils, and onions are inferior by comparison.
- **The solution offered:** اهْبِطُوا مِصْرًا — "Go down to any town/city." The word مِصْر here means any settlement, not specifically Egypt. The linguistic root means "a town or settlement." Since mann and salwa are miraculous and unique, they cannot be found in any marketplace — but vegetables can be found anywhere. If they wanted earthly food, they must return to earthly living.
- **Lesson from this passage:** Bani Isra'il repeatedly rejected and disliked the blessings of Allah. This is a pattern throughout their history — every extraordinary favor was eventually denied, rejected, or found insufficient.

**Consequences imposed (end of Ayah 61):**
- وَضُرِبَتْ عَلَيْهِمُ الذِّلَّةُ وَالْمَسْكَنَةُ — Humiliation and wretchedness were imposed upon them permanently (أبدي — eternal, unceasing).
- وَبَاءُوا بِغَضَبٍ مِّنَ اللَّهِ — They returned carrying/incurring the wrath and anger of Allah.

**Reasons for these consequences:**
1. بِأَنَّهُمْ كَانُوا يَكْفُرُونَ بِآيَاتِ اللَّهِ — They disbelieved in the signs and miracles of Allah out of جُحُود (denial) and استِكبار (arrogance), not out of ignorance.
2. وَيَقْتُلُونَ النَّبِيِّينَ بِغَيْرِ الْحَقِّ — They killed the prophets of Allah unjustly and aggressively (ظُلْمًا وَعُدْوَانًا).
3. ذَٰلِكَ بِمَا عَصَوا وَّكَانُوا يَعْتَدُونَ — This was the result of their disobedience (عِصيان) and their transgression (اِعتداء) — crossing the limits of Allah, rebellion against His rulings.

### Ayah 62 — The Universal Invitation to True Faith

- **Who is being addressed:** Allah addresses all people of all religious communities:
  - المُؤمِنُون — Believers (followers of Muhammad ﷺ)
  - الَّذِينَ هَادُوا — Jews (followers of Musa AS)
  - النَّصَارَى — Christians (followers of Isa AS)
  - الصَّابِئُون — Sabians: a group who broke away from Judaism and Christianity and began worshipping the angels. (As explained in the tafseer text.)
- **Form of address:** Notably, Allah presents this as a khabar (report/statement), not as a direct command (amr). It is a declaration of a principle rather than a direct imperative.
- **The conditions for salvation (three):**
  1. مَنْ آمَنَ بِاللَّهِ — Sincere, true belief in Allah (إيمان صادق)
  2. وَالْيَوْمِ الْآخِرِ — Belief in the Last Day/Hereafter (يَقِين بالآخِرة)
  3. وَعَمِلَ صَالِحًا — Performance of righteous deeds; obedience to Allah in this worldly life
- **The promise for those who fulfill these conditions:**
  - فَلَهُمْ أَجْرُهُمْ عِندَ رَبِّهِمْ — Their reward is with their Lord; not an atom's weight (مِثْقَال ذَرَّة) of their deeds will be lost or wasted.
  - وَلَا خَوْفٌ عَلَيْهِمْ — No fear shall come upon them on the Day of Judgment, when disbelievers will be in terror of punishment.
  - وَلَا هُمْ يَحْزَنُونَ — Nor shall they grieve — unlike those who wasted their lives in sin and missed the reward of the Hereafter.

## Grammar and Language Notes

- **عَيْن (spring):** A feminine noun (مُؤَنَّث). Its femininity is سَمَاعِي (heard/irregular), not derived from a grammatical rule. This is why اِثْنَتَا عَشْرَةَ (the feminine form of twelve) is used.

- **اَلْحَجَر — الـ not necessarily for ta'rīf (specification):** The definite article الـ is not always used for specific identification. Here, some hold that اَلْحَجَر means "a rock" (any rock), with الـ serving as the definite article of the مفعول به (direct object) without specifying which rock. This is a known grammatical phenomenon in Arabic.

- **لَنْ نَّصْبِرَ:** لَنْ is used for emphatic negation of the future — "we will absolutely never." Stronger than لَا. The use here shows the finality and extremity of Bani Isra'il's complaint.

- **عَسَا يَعْسُو (وَلَا تَعْثَوْا):** This verb follows the pattern of سَمِعَ يَسْمَعُ (i.e., فَعَلَ يَفْعُلُ with a و pattern). The ta'asū form is second person plural. Meaning: to go to extremes in corruption, to transgress utterly.

- **اِسْتَبْدَلَ يَسْتَبْدِلُ اِسْتِبْدَالًا:** Form X (استفعل) verb meaning "to exchange/replace one thing with another."

- **دَعَا يَدْعُو دُعَاءً / دَعْوَةً:** The imperative is اُدْعُ, with the fa' prefix giving فَادْعُ. Both verbal nouns (مصدر) are valid: دُعَاء and دَعْوَة.

- **عَصَى يَعْصِي عِصْيَانًا:** Pattern of فَعَلَ يَفْعِلُ (سَمِعَ يَسْمَعُ category). To disobey.

- **اِقْتَرَفَ يَقْتَرِفُ:** Form VIII (افتعل). To commit or earn (especially used for committing sins).

- **مِصْر:** The linguistic meaning is "a town, city, or settlement" — not restricted to Egypt. The Quran's use here (اهْبِطُوا مِصْرًا) is indefinite (no الـ), meaning *any* town wherever they wished to go.

- **أَدْنَى / خَيْر:** Comparative forms — أَدْنَى (lower/worse) is the opposite of خَيْر (better). These are اِسم التفضيل.

## Lessons and Practical Takeaways

1. **Gratitude for provision is obligatory.** When Allah provides rizq (provision) — however simple it may seem — the proper response is شُكر (gratitude) and obedience, not complaint or desire for something "better." Mann and salwa were miraculous; Bani Isra'il's dissatisfaction with them is a lesson against ingratitude.

2. **Divine blessings come with moral responsibility.** The command "do not spread corruption on the earth" was paired directly with the gift of water and food. When Allah provides, He also commands how to live with that provision. This principle is universal: our rizq is not a blank check for sin.

3. **Ingratitude is a pattern, not an incident.** The instructor drew attention to how Bani Isra'il repeatedly disliked or rejected Allah's blessings. Muslims should examine themselves: do we recognize the blessings of Allah in our lives, or do we habitually find them insufficient?

4. **The conditions of salvation are universal and clear.** Ayah 62 establishes that Jannah is not reserved by name or lineage. Regardless of one's religious label, what matters is: (a) true belief in Allah, (b) belief in the Hereafter, and (c) righteous deeds. Labels without substance do not save.

5. **Rebellion against Allah's rulings leads to real consequences.** The humiliation and wrath imposed on Bani Isra'il were not arbitrary — they were the direct result of كفر, killing prophets, and willful transgression. This is a warning for all communities: divine blessings withdrawn and replaced with disgrace are the consequence of sustained disobedience.

6. **Fear and grief on the Day of Judgment are for those who wasted their lives.** Those who neglected their duties, missed opportunities for reward in the Hereafter, and spent their time in sin will grieve on that Day. True believers — having fulfilled the three conditions — will have no fear and no grief.

## Historical Context and Stories Mentioned

- **Bani Isra'il in the Tih (Wilderness):** The Tih was a specific barren field/plateau where Bani Isra'il were made to wander as a divine punishment. It was elevated terrain, which is why "going down" (اهْبِطُوا) to a town was mentioned. The period of wandering lasted 40 years according to other narrations.

- **The 12 Tribes of Bani Isra'il:** Descended from the 12 sons of Ya'qub (AS). Each tribe was a distinct community (قِبَط/سِبَط). The 12 springs assigned one per tribe prevented conflict and maintained social order.

- **Mann and Salwa:** Mann — a sweet substance (like honey or dew) that fell from the sky; Salwa — quails provided as meat. These were miraculous provisions that required no agricultural labor, hunting, or cooking preparation in the traditional sense. They were pure divine gifts.

- **The Sabians (الصَّابِئُون):** As explained in this tafseer, they were a group who broke away from both Judaism and Christianity and took to worshipping the angels. They are mentioned in the Quran three times (Al-Baqarah 62, Al-Ma'idah 69, Al-Hajj 17). Their precise identity has been debated by scholars historically.

- **Killing of the Prophets:** A recurring crime attributed to segments of Bani Isra'il in the Quran. This is mentioned here as one of the direct causes of divine wrath — prophets were killed unjustly (بِغَيْرِ الْحَقِّ), aggressively (عُدوانًا), and oppressively (ظُلمًا).

## Q&A Highlights

**Q: Why is it "al-ḥajar" (the rock) with the definite article if any rock would do?**
A: The instructor explained that الـ in Arabic is not always for ta'rīf (making something specific). Here, some scholars read it as referring to any rock — the miracle was entirely in Allah's power, not in a particular stone. The majority view, however, holds that a specific rock was intended, supported by indications in related narrations. The instructor acknowledged both positions and committed to investigating further.

**Q (student asking for page number):** Confirmed: page 62 of the Tafseer text, Ayah 60.

**Q (implicit, regarding عَسَا):** The instructor clarified that وَلَا تَعْثَوْا follows the pattern of سَمِعَ يَسْمَعُ — specifically عَسَا يَعْسُو. It means to go to the extreme in corruption or transgression, and the instruction not to do so applies universally beyond Bani Isra'il.
