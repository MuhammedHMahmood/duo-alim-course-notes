# Tafseer 201 - Study Notes
**Date:** 2026-11-19
**Surah Covered:** Surah Al-Baqarah, Ayat 111–115 (page 87–88)

---

## Session Overview

This session covered Surah Al-Baqarah ayat 111–115, focusing on the falsehoods of Ahlul Kitab (People of the Book). The main themes were the exclusive paradise claims of Jews and Christians against one another, Allah's rebuttal of these claims, the true criteria for entering Jannah, and the grave injustice of preventing worship in the Masajid of Allah. The session also touched on the ayah regarding the direction of Qibla (verse 115) and included a grammar discussion on the use of the feminine verb form (sigha mu'annas) with collective nouns like Yahud and Nasara.

---

## Key Themes and Topics

1. **Mutual Takfeer of Jews and Christians** — Each group declared the other to be disbelievers and claimed paradise exclusively for themselves.
2. **Allah's Rebuttal: Both Groups Are Liars** — Allah falsified the claims of both groups and clarified that Jannah is earned through Iman and good deeds, not ethnic or religious group membership.
3. **Asbab al-Nuzul (Occasion of Revelation)** — The dispute between Jewish scholars and Christian delegates of Najran in front of the Prophet ﷺ directly triggered the revelation of the relevant ayah.
4. **True Criteria for Paradise** — Submitting oneself to Allah (Aslama wajhahu lillah), being a true believer (Mu'min), and doing good deeds (Muhsin).
5. **The Mushrikeen Made the Same False Claim** — The pagans similarly declared the Prophet ﷺ's religion baseless, paralleling the Jews' and Christians' mutual rejection.
6. **Injustice of Preventing Worship in the Masajid of Allah** — The most unjust act is preventing people from remembering Allah in His houses and destroying them, as the Romans did to Bayt al-Maqdis.
7. **Ayah of the Qibla (verse 115)** — Allah owns the East and the West; wherever one turns, Allah is there — revealed in the context of a traveler unable to find the Qibla direction.

## Main Tafseer Points and Explanations

### Ayah 111 — Exclusive Paradise Claims of Jews and Christians

- Jews declared: Christians are disbelievers (kafir) because they believe in Isa and the Injil, which Jews reject. They deny Isa's prophethood and reject the Injil entirely.
- Christians declared: Jews are disbelievers because they reject Isa al-Masih, who came — in the Christian belief — to complete their Shari'ah. Therefore Jews are kafir for rejecting him.
- This mutual rejection created severe enmity between the two groups: **صَارَ كُلُّ فَرِيقٍ يَطْعَنُ فِي دِينِ الْآخَرِ** — each group attacked the religion of the other.
- Each group then claimed: **الْجَنَّةُ وَقْفٌ عَلَيْنَا** — paradise is reserved for us alone.
- **Allah's response:** He falsified (أَكْذَبَ) both groups and declared that Jannah belongs to the pious believer who does good deeds — regardless of ethnic or communal identity.

### Asbab al-Nuzul (Occasion of Revelation)

- Narrated by Ibn Abbas: The Christians of Najran (led by their leaders) and the Jewish scholars both came to the Prophet ﷺ at the same time.
- In his presence, they began disputing:
  - **Rafi' ibn Harmala** (a Jewish scholar) said to the Christians: *"You are upon nothing"* — he rejected the Injil and denied Isa's prophethood.
  - A Christian from Najran responded: *"You are upon nothing"* — he denied Musa's prophethood and disbelieved in the Tawrat.
- This live dispute in front of the Prophet ﷺ was the direct occasion for the revelation of this ayah.

### Ayah 111 — Allah's Challenge: Bring Your Proof

- **قُلْ هَاتُوا بُرْهَانَكُمْ إِنْ كُنْتُمْ صَادِقِينَ** — "Say: Bring your proof if you are truthful."
- Allah commands the Prophet ﷺ to challenge them: if only Jews or only Christians enter paradise, produce the evidence.
- The word **هَاتُوا (Hatu)** is unique: it is used only in the imperative (amr), and only in two forms — **هَاتِ** (singular) and **هَاتُوا** (plural). There is no madi (past) or mudari (present) form.
- The result: they had no proof. Their claims were nothing but **أَمَانِيّ** — wishes, fantasies, baseless desires.

### Ayah 112 — The True Criterion for Paradise

- **بَلَى** — a particle that negates the preceding false statement and affirms the opposite.
- **مَنْ أَسْلَمَ وَجْهَهُ لِلَّهِ وَهُوَ مُحْسِنٌ** — Whoever submits himself entirely to Allah while being a doer of good.
- Three qualities of the person who earns paradise:
  1. **Aslama wajhahu lillah** — Full surrender and dedication to Allah with humility.
  2. **Muhsin** — Does good deeds in the best manner (Ihsan).
  3. **Mu'min** — A true believer: affirms the truth (Musaddiq) and follows the Sunnah of Rasulullah ﷺ (Muttabi').
- **فَلَهُ أَجْرُهُ عِنْدَ رَبِّهِ** — Such a person will have their reward with their Lord.
- **وَلَا خَوْفٌ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا هُمْ يَحْزَنُونَ** — There will be no fear upon them, and they shall not grieve on the Day of Judgment. They will be in eternal bliss.

### Ayah 113 — Each Group Rejects the Other

- **وَقَالَتِ الْيَهُودُ لَيْسَتِ النَّصَارَى عَلَى شَيْءٍ** — The Jews said the Christians are upon nothing.
- **وَقَالَتِ النَّصَارَى لَيْسَتِ الْيَهُودُ عَلَى شَيْءٍ** — The Christians said the Jews are upon nothing.
- Allah's rebuttal: **وَهُمْ يَتْلُونَ الْكِتَابَ** — Yet both groups recite the Book (the Tawrat and the Injil).
  - Both books are Books of Allah, undistorted in their original form.
  - Musa ﷺ and Isa ﷺ are both Prophets of Allah.
  - Both groups know this truth, yet they deliberately reject one another — a rejection done with full knowledge.
- **كَذَلِكَ قَالَ الَّذِينَ لَا يَعْلَمُونَ مِثْلَ قَوْلِهِمْ** — Similarly, those without knowledge (the Mushrikeen) said the same kind of statement: *"Muhammad is upon nothing; his religion is baseless."*
  - Jews and Christians were considered "learned" peoples (they had Books from Allah), while the Mushrikeen were considered unlearned. Yet all three groups made the same baseless rejection.
- **فَاللَّهُ يَحْكُمُ بَيْنَهُمْ يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ فِيمَا كَانُوا فِيهِ يَخْتَلِفُونَ** — Allah will judge between them on the Day of Resurrection in all matters over which they differed, with His just (fair) judgment.

### Ayah 114 — The Injustice of Preventing Worship in the Masajid of Allah

- **وَمَنْ أَظْلَمُ مِمَّنْ مَنَعَ مَسَاجِدَ اللَّهِ أَنْ يُذْكَرَ فِيهَا اسْمُهُ وَسَعَى فِي خَرَابِهَا**
- **أَظْلَم** is an Ism al-Tafdhil (superlative form of ظَلَمَ): "Who is more unjust than…?"
- The rhetorical question establishes: **the most unjust person in the world is the one who prevents people from worshipping Allah in His Masajid and works to destroy them.**
- Historical reference: The Romans (specifically Bukht Nassar) destroyed Bayt al-Maqdis and disabled it for worship. Similarly, the Kuffar of Quraish prevented worship at the Ka'bah/Baytullah.
- Allah combines all such acts: whoever — from among Jews, Christians, Mushrikeen — did this to the Houses of Allah (Bayt al-Maqdis, Baytullah/Ka'bah) has committed the gravest crime.
- Those who entered these houses should have done so only with fear and submission to Allah, let alone that they destroyed them.
- **Consequence:**
  - **In this world:** لَهُمْ فِي الدُّنْيَا خِزْيٌ — They will have disgrace and humiliation.
  - **In the Hereafter:** وَلَهُمْ فِي الْآخِرَةِ عَذَابٌ عَظِيمٌ — They will have a great punishment, which is the punishment of Hellfire.

### Ayah 115 — Allah Owns the East and the West; The Qibla

- **وَلِلَّهِ الْمَشْرِقُ وَالْمَغْرِبُ** — To Allah belongs the East and the West; the entire earth is under His power.
- **فَأَيْنَمَا تُوَلُّوا فَثَمَّ وَجْهُ اللَّهِ** — Wherever you turn, there is the Face (presence) of Allah.
- Context/application: This ayah was revealed regarding a traveler who exhausted all efforts to find the Qibla direction but was unable to. Allah affirmed: you may pray in any direction, for I am in every direction.
- Also indicates the history of the Qibla: the Qibla was originally Bayt al-Maqdis, then it was changed to the Ka'bah.
- **وَاسِعٌ عَلِيمٌ:**
  - **Wasi':** يَسَعُ الْخَلْقَ بِالْجُودِ وَالْإِفْضَالِ — He encompasses His creation with His generosity and favor.
  - **Alim:** عَلِيمٌ بِتَدْبِيرِ شُؤُونِهِمْ — He knows how to manage the affairs of His creation; nothing of their condition is hidden from Him.

## Grammar and Language Notes

### Sigha Mu'annas with Collective Nouns (Yahud and Nasara)

- **Question raised in class:** Why is the verb **قَالَتْ** (feminine form) used with **الْيَهُودُ** and **النَّصَارَى**, which are not biologically feminine?
- **Answer:** Yahud and Nasara are collective nouns (groups of people). In Arabic grammar, when the subject is a collective noun representing a group, the hidden/implied noun is a feminine word such as **جَمَاعَة** (group) or **فِئَة** (faction) — both of which are grammatically feminine (mu'annas ghayir haqiqi — non-biological femininity).
- Full implicit reading: **قَالَتْ جَمَاعَةٌ مِنَ الْيَهُودِ** / **قَالَتْ جَمَاعَةٌ مِنَ النَّصَارَى**
- Same rule applies to **لَيْسَتِ الْيَهُودُ** — the implied subject is **جَمَاعَةٌ مِنَ الْيَهُودِ**.
- **Rule:** Wherever you see a feminine verb (sigha mu'annas) used with a collective noun of people, the hidden word is **جَمَاعَة** or **فِئَة**.

### Key Morphological Notes

- **هَاتُوا (Hatu):** Imperative verb meaning "bring!" — only exists in two forms: هَاتِ (singular) and هَاتُوا (plural). No madi or mudari form exists for this verb.
- **أَظْلَمُ (Athlam):** Ism al-Tafdhil on the pattern أَفْعَلُ — superlative of ظَلَمَ, meaning "most unjust."
- **بَلَى (Bala):** A particle used to negate a preceding negative statement and affirm the positive. Here it negates the false claim that only Jews/Christians enter paradise and affirms the true criterion.
- **أُمْنِيَّة / أَمَانِيّ:** Singular/plural — wish, desire. Used to describe the baseless fantasies of Jews and Christians regarding paradise.

## Lessons and Practical Takeaways

1. **Group membership does not earn paradise.** Being Jewish, Christian, Muslim by name or ethnic identity alone is not the criterion. What counts is sincere Iman, full submission to Allah, and doing good deeds (Ihsan).

2. **Beware of baseless communal pride (Amani).** Claiming Allah's favor based on lineage, community, or label without substance is a form of self-delusion explicitly condemned in the Quran.

3. **The Masajid of Allah are sacred.** One of the gravest crimes is to prevent people from worshipping in Allah's houses or to destroy them. This principle applies universally — it is not tied only to historical events.

4. **Allah will settle all disputes on the Day of Judgment.** Inter-religious and intra-religious disputes that remain unresolved in this world will receive a final, just verdict from Allah on Yawm al-Qiyamah.

5. **The requirement for Jannah has three pillars:** (1) Aslama wajhahu — surrender and dedication to Allah; (2) Iman — true belief; (3) Ihsan — doing good deeds in the best manner. All three are necessary.

6. **Mutual rejection without knowledge is a sign of ignorance.** Both the "learned" Ahlul Kitab and the "unlearned" Mushrikeen fell into the same trap of baseless rejection — showing that having a Book or tradition does not protect from going astray if one rejects truth willfully.

7. **Allah is Wasi' (All-Encompassing) and Alim (All-Knowing).** His generosity covers all His creation, and His knowledge encompasses every affair. This should instill trust in His mercy and his ultimate management of all things.

## Historical Context and Stories Mentioned

### Dispute Between Najrani Christians and Jewish Scholars Before the Prophet ﷺ
- The Christians of Najran (a region in the Arabian Peninsula) sent their leaders to Medina. Jewish scholars were also present with the Prophet ﷺ.
- In this gathering, Rafi' ibn Harmala (a Jewish scholar) openly told the Christians their religion has no basis, denying Isa's prophethood and the Injil.
- A Christian from Najran responded in kind: he denied Musa's prophethood and rejected the Tawrat.
- This live confrontation in front of the Prophet ﷺ was the direct occasion (Sabab al-Nuzul) for the revelation of Ayah 113.

### Bukht Nassar and the Destruction of Bayt al-Maqdis
- Bukht Nassar was a Roman king who destroyed Bayt al-Maqdis (the Temple in Jerusalem, also referred to as the first Qibla of the Muslims).
- He not only physically destroyed it but rendered it completely non-functional for worship for a prolonged period.
- This event is referenced in the tafsir of verse 114 as a prime example of the injustice of preventing worship in the Masajid of Allah.

### The Kuffar of Quraish and Baytullah (the Ka'bah)
- Mentioned alongside Bayt al-Maqdis as another historical instance: the disbelievers of Makkah prevented the Muslims from worshipping at Baytullah/Ka'bah, rendering the house of Allah inaccessible for a period.
- Both incidents are cited together to show the breadth of Allah's statement in verse 114.

### The Qibla Change (verse 115 context)
- Originally, the Qibla for prayer was Bayt al-Maqdis. It was later changed to the Ka'bah (Masjid al-Haram in Makkah).
- Verse 115 is understood to have been revealed for the specific case of a traveler who cannot determine the Qibla direction despite making every effort — such a person may pray in any direction, as Allah is present in all directions.

## Q&A Highlights

**Q: Why is the feminine verb form (قَالَتْ / لَيْسَتْ) used with الْيَهُودُ and النَّصَارَى?**

A student suggested it might be because they are a "group of people" and collective nouns take feminine. The teacher confirmed this reasoning is essentially correct but asked for greater precision. The full explanation: in Arabic, the implicit noun behind collective nouns like Yahud and Nasara is a feminine word — **جَمَاعَة** (group) or **فِئَة** (faction). Both are grammatically mu'annas (feminine), not because the people are female, but because the collective word itself is feminine (mu'annas ghayir haqiqi — non-biological). The full reading is: **قَالَتْ جَمَاعَةٌ مِنَ الْيَهُودِ**. This same rule applies wherever a mu'annas verb appears with a masculine collective noun.
