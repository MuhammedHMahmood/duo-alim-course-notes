# Tafseer 201 - Study Notes
**Date:** 2026-09-15
**Surah Covered:** Surah Al-Baqarah, Ayats 30–33 (Page 74)

---

## Session Overview

This session covered the tafseer of Surah Al-Baqarah, ayats 30–33, focusing on the story of the creation of Adam (alayhi salam) and his appointment as Allah's Khalifah on earth. The class examined the munasabah (connection) to previous ayats, the angels' inquiry about the creation of humanity, Allah's response demonstrating Adam's superiority through knowledge, and concluded with a grammar discussion on the word "Subhanaka."

---

## Key Themes and Topics

1. **Munasabah (Contextual Connection):** The ayats follow naturally from the discussion of Allah's blessings upon His creation — bringing human beings from non-existence into existence, subjugating the heavens and earth for them, and honoring their forefather Adam (a.s.).

2. **The Appointment of Adam as Khalifah:** Allah informed the angels of His intention to place a representative (Khalifah) on earth — a continuous line of humanity, generation after generation, implementing Allah's rulings.

3. **The Angels' Inquiry:** The angels asked, out of astonishment and desire for information, how Allah could appoint as Khalifah a creation that would spread corruption and shed blood.

4. **Adam's Superior Knowledge:** Allah taught Adam the names of all things, then tested the angels, demonstrating that Adam's knowledge surpassed theirs.

5. **The Angels' Humility and Acknowledgment:** Unable to name anything, the angels acknowledged their limitation and glorified Allah.

6. **Allah's Omniscience:** Allah affirmed that He knows what is hidden in the heavens and earth, what is revealed, and what is concealed — including the hidden assumption of the angels that no creation would surpass them.

7. **Grammar of "Subhanaka":** A detailed grammatical breakdown of the word *Subhanaka* as a maf'ul mutlaq with a hidden verb.

## Main Tafseer Points and Explanations

### Munasabah — Connection to Previous Ayats

Allah's earlier blessings upon humanity included:
- **Al-Ijad** — bringing human beings from non-existence (*al-'adam*) into existence (*al-wujud*)
- Subjugating everything in the heavens and earth for them: *وَأَنَّهُ سَخَّرَ لَهُمْ مَا فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَمَا فِي الْأَرْضِ جَمِيعًا*
- Honoring their forefather Adam (a.s.) — *تَشْرِيف أَبِيهِمْ وَتَكْرِيمِهِ*
- Making Adam the Khalifah on earth
- Accommodating Adam in Jannah — *دَار الْكَرَامَة* (the house of honor)
- Commanding the angels to prostrate before Adam in his honor

**Key principle:** Blessing the *asl* (origin/root) is a blessing to the *far'* (branch). Since Adam was honored, all of humanity shares in that honor. This is why Allah reminds all human beings of these blessings.

---

### Ayat 30 — إِنِّي جَاعِلٌ فِي الْأَرْضِ خَلِيفَةً

**وَإِذْ** — "Remember the time when..." This word appears throughout the Qur'an referring to a specific occasion or event.

**قَالَ رَبُّكَ لِلْمَلَائِكَةِ** — "Your Lord said to the angels." Allah announced to the angels His intention to create a Khalifah on earth.

**The Khalifah — two interpretations:**
1. Adam (a.s.) specifically as the first representative
2. A continuous nation — humanity as a whole, with each generation succeeding the one before, implementing Allah's rulings century after century

**The Angels' Response — on two levels:**
- **Ta'ajjub (astonishment):** They were amazed at this new creation
- **Isti'lam (inquiry):** They genuinely wanted more information, asking: *كَيْفَ تَسْتَخْلِفُ هَؤُلَاءِ وَفِيهِمْ مَنْ يُفْسِدُ فِي الْأَرْضِ بِالْمَعَاصِي*

The angels raised two specific concerns about humanity:
1. **Spreading corruption** (*fasad*) through disobedience and sin (*ma'asi*)
2. **Shedding blood** (*safk al-dima'*) through transgression (*ta'addi*) and aggression, violating the boundaries of Shari'ah

The angels contrasted humanity with themselves: *وَنَحْنُ نُسَبِّحُ بِحَمْدِكَ وَنُقَدِّسُ لَكَ* — "We glorify You with praise and sanctify You." They implied they were sufficient for worshipping Allah.

**Taqdis explained:** To magnify Allah's command (*nu'azzimu amraka*) and to purify His name from whatever atheists (*mulhidun*) attribute to Him.

**Allah's Reply:** *إِنِّي أَعْلَمُ مَا لَا تَعْلَمُونَ* — "Indeed, I know what you do not know." Allah has wisdom behind this creation hidden from the angels — reasons, interests, and purposes they could not perceive.

---

### Ayat 31 — وَعَلَّمَ آدَمَ الْأَسْمَاءَ كُلَّهَا

**وَعَلَّمَ آدَمَ الْأَسْمَاءَ كُلَّهَا** — "And He taught Adam all the names."

- *'Allama yu'allimu ta'leeman* — Bab Taf'eel (Form II verb)
- *Al-asma' kullaha* = *asma' al-musammayyat kulliha* — the names of every single named thing

**Ibn Abbas (r.a.) interpreted this** as Allah teaching Adam the name of everything — every object, animal, utensil, and creature. Examples given: *qas'ah* (bowl), *miqrafah* (ladle/spoon).

**Scope of this knowledge:** Not limited to Adam's time, but extending to everything that would exist on earth until the Day of Resurrection. This is why human knowledge and science continue to expand — it is the fulfillment of what was placed in human nature (*fitrah*) from the time of Adam. The ongoing development of science and technology reflects this God-given capacity.

**Purpose of teaching the names:** To demonstrate Adam's superiority over the angels and to justify his appointment as Khalifah.

---

### Ayat 31 (continued) — ثُمَّ عَرَضَهُمْ عَلَى الْمَلَائِكَةِ

**ثُمَّ عَرَضَهُمْ عَلَى الْمَلَائِكَةِ** — "Then He presented them to the angels."

Allah presented the named things (creatures, animals, birds, insects, objects) before the angels and challenged them: *أَنْبِئُونِي بِأَسْمَاءِ هَؤُلَاءِ إِنْ كُنْتُمْ صَادِقِينَ* — "Tell me the names of these, if you are truthful."

This was done *'ala sabeel al-tabkit* — **by way of rebuking and scolding**, because the angels had raised objections about the human being's fitness for khilafah.

The implicit challenge: If you claim to be more deserving of khilafah than this new creation, prove it by naming these things.

The angels fell silent, unable to name anything.

---

### Ayat 32 — قَالُوا سُبْحَانَكَ لَا عِلْمَ لَنَا إِلَّا مَا عَلَّمْتَنَا

The angels acknowledged their inability and said: "Glory be to You. We have no knowledge except what You have taught us. Indeed, You are the All-Knowing, the All-Wise."

- **Al-'Alim** — nothing is hidden from Allah; He knows all things
- **Al-Hakim** — the Wise; One who does nothing except according to what wisdom requires

---

### Ayat 33 — قَالَ يَا آدَمُ أَنْبِئْهُمْ بِأَسْمَائِهِمْ

Allah then ordered Adam: "O Adam, inform them of their names." Adam named each and every thing and further mentioned:
- The **wisdom** (*hikmah*) behind the creation of each thing
- The **purpose** for which it was created

*فَلَمَّا أَنْبَأَهُمْ بِأَسْمَائِهِمْ* — When Adam informed them of all the names, Allah said to the angels: "Did I not tell you that I know the unseen of the heavens and earth? And I know what you reveal and what you conceal."

**What the angels had concealed (*ma taktumun*):** A hidden assumption in their minds — that no creation of Allah would ever be more honorable or superior to them. A hadith mentions that when the angels witnessed the wondrous nature (*taratahu al-'ajeebah*) of Adam's creation, they said to themselves: *لَيَكُنْ مَا شَاءَ فَلَنْ يَخْلُقَ رَبُّنَا خَلْقًا إِلَّا كُنَّا أَكْرَمَ عَلَيْهِ* — "Whatever he will be, Allah will never create a creation except that we would be more honorable to Him." Allah exposed and refuted this hidden claim through Adam's demonstration of superior knowledge.

## Grammar and Language Notes

### إِذْ (*Idh*)
- Appears throughout the Qur'an as a temporal marker
- Translated as "Remember the time when..." / "Remember the occasion when..."
- Always refers to a specific past event or occasion

### وَعَلَّمَ (*wa'allama*)
- From *'allama yu'allimu ta'leeman* — **Bab Taf'eel** (Form II)
- Denotes intensive/causative action — thorough teaching

### أَنْبَأَ (*anba'a*)
- *Anba'a yunbi'u inba'an* — to inform, to tell
- The imperative *Anbi'hum* = inform them

### سُبْحَانَكَ (*Subhanaka*) — Detailed Grammatical Analysis

**The Tarkib (grammatical breakdown):**
- *Subhan* + *ka* (second person possessive pronoun) = *mudhaf + mudhaf ilayhi*
- The word *Subhana* is **mansub** (accusative) — it carries a *fathah*
- It is a **Maf'ul Mutlaq** (absolute object/cognate accusative)
- The verb (*fi'l*) and subject (*fa'il*) are **hidden/implied** before it

**Full implied sentence:**
- One person saying: *Usabbihu Subhanaka* — "I glorify You with glorification"
- Multiple people saying: *Nusabbihu Subhanaka* — "We glorify You with glorification"

In the ayat, the angels are speaking, so it is *Nusabbihu* (plural).

**Therefore:** *Nusabbihu* is the *fi'l* and *fa'il*, and *Subhanaka* is the **maf'ul mutlaq**.

**Parallel examples from du'a:**
- *Subhanakal-lahumma wa bihamdik* — full implied sentence: *Nusabbihuka subhanak*
- *Subhanaka la ilaha illa anta inni kuntu minal-zalimin* (du'a of Yunus a.s.)
- *Subhanallah* = *Nusabbihu subhana Allah*

**Meaning of Tasbih:** To glorify Allah and declare Him free (*munazzah*) from all defects, imperfections, and anything the atheists (*mulhidun*) falsely attribute to Him.

## Lessons and Practical Takeaways

1. **Knowledge is humanity's greatest distinction.** Adam's superiority over the angels was established not through physical strength or prior worship, but through *'ilm* — knowledge. This is a reminder of the immense value Islam places on learning.

2. **Allah's wisdom surpasses our understanding.** When we question why Allah ordains something that appears harmful or difficult, we must remember: *إِنِّي أَعْلَمُ مَا لَا تَعْلَمُونَ* — Allah sees the full picture; we do not.

3. **The honor of humanity is real and Divinely-granted.** Being a human being is itself a blessing from Allah — our forefather was honored by Allah, appointed as Khalifah, accommodated in Jannah, and had the angels prostrate before him. We inherit that dignity.

4. **The Khalifah carries responsibility.** Humanity's role as khalifah on earth means implementing Allah's rulings, maintaining justice, and being accountable for how we steward what Allah has placed in our care.

5. **Humility before Allah's knowledge.** The angels — the greatest of creation in worship — acknowledged: *لَا عِلْمَ لَنَا إِلَّا مَا عَلَّمْتَنَا*. This is the proper stance of every scholar and student: all knowledge ultimately comes from Allah.

6. **Allah knows the hidden.** He knows not only what we say and do, but what we hide in our hearts. The angels thought they had concealed their assumption; Allah exposed it. This is a warning to guard one's inner thoughts and intentions (*niyyat*).

7. **Science and technology are expressions of Adam's inheritance.** The ongoing expansion of human knowledge in every field is the fulfillment of Allah teaching Adam *al-asma' kullaha* — it is encoded in human nature.

## Historical Context and Stories Mentioned

### Creation of Adam (a.s.) and the Prostration of the Angels
- Allah honored Adam (a.s.) by:
  - Making him the Khalifah on earth
  - Accommodating him in *Dar al-Karamah* — Jannah
  - Commanding all angels to prostrate (*sujud*) before Adam in his honor (*isjad lil-ikram*, not worship)

### The Angels' Hidden Assumption (from Hadith)
When the angels witnessed the extraordinary nature of Adam's creation (*taratuhu al-'ajeebah*), they said to each other: *لَيَكُنْ مَا شَاءَ فَلَنْ يَخْلُقَ رَبُّنَا خَلْقًا إِلَّا كُنَّا أَكْرَمَ عَلَيْهِ* — "Whatever this new creation will be, we will always be more honorable to Allah than him." This hidden pride was what Allah referred to by *وَأَعْلَمُ مَا تَكْتُمُونَ*.

### Ibn Abbas (r.a.) on the Meaning of "All the Names"
Ibn Abbas (r.a.) explained that *al-asma' kullaha* means Allah taught Adam the name of everything in existence — every creature, utensil, and object — citing *qas'ah* (bowl) and *miqrafah* (ladle) as examples, indicating the comprehensiveness of this knowledge down to the most ordinary items.

## Q&A Highlights

**Q: Was the Khalifah specifically Adam (a.s.) or humanity as a whole?**
Both interpretations are valid: Adam was the first Khalifah, but the institution of khilafah refers to all of humanity — each generation succeeding the last, implementing Allah's rulings on earth, century after century.

**Q: How did the angels know in advance that humans would cause corruption and shed blood?**
Two possibilities discussed: either Allah had informed the angels about the nature of this new creation beforehand, or the angels perceived it from the wondrous (yet also potentially destructive) nature of Adam's creation when they saw him.

**Q: What is the grammatical analysis of *Subhanaka*?**
*Subhana* is mansub (accusative) and functions as a **maf'ul mutlaq** (cognate accusative) with an implied verb (*usabbihu/nusabbihu*) before it. The full sentence is: *Nusabbihu subhanaka* — "We glorify You with glorification."
