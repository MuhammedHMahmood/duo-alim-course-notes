# Tafseer 201 - Study Notes
**Date:** 2026-08-27
**Surah Covered:** Surah Al-Baqarah — Ayaat covering the Munafiqeen parable (rain/storm example) and transition to Ayah 21–25 (Ya Ayyuhan-Nas)

---

## Session Overview

This session covered two major sections of Surah Al-Baqarah. The first part completed the extended parable (second example) of the munafiqeen — the storm/rain parable — explaining its imagery in detail, its application to the state of the hypocrites, and Allah's declaration of power over them. The second part introduced the next set of ayaat (21–25), examining their rhetorical connection to the preceding section and beginning the tafseer of the address "Ya Ayyuhan-Nas," which calls all of humanity to worship Allah and enumerates His blessings (earth as a resting place, sky as a roof, rain and provision).

---

## Key Themes and Topics

1. **The second parable of the munafiqeen** — the storm/heavy rain scenario as a metaphor for their confusion and hesitation between iman and kufr.
2. **Imagery of the storm** — darkness, thunder, lightning, thunderbolts, and the reaction of putting fingers in ears.
3. **Parenthetical statement (jumlah mu'taridah)** — "Wallahu muhitun bil-kafirin" inserted mid-parable as a warning of Allah's encompassing power.
4. **Allah's omnipotence** — He could take away their hearing and sight if He willed; no one can escape Him.
5. **The munafiqeen's wavering nature** — oscillating between Muslims and disbelievers based on perceived power, paralleled with the traveler walking only when lightning flashes.
6. **Rhetorical transition (munasabah)** — how the shift from describing the three groups (mu'mineen, kafireen, munafiqeen) to presenting proofs of tawheed is logically connected.
7. **Iltifat (direct address)** — the rhetorical shift from third-person description to direct second-person address ("Ya Ayyuhan-Nas") and its purpose.
8. **Proofs of Allah's oneness** — creation of humanity from nothing, the earth as a resting place, the sky as a canopy, rain, and provision from fruits.

## Main Tafseer Points and Explanations

### The Second Parable of the Munafiqeen (The Storm)

- Allah gives a **second example** of the munafiqeen's state — intended as an increase in clarification (زيادة في الكشف والإيضاح) of their nifaq.
- The example: *their condition in their confusion and hesitation about accepting Islam is like the condition of people struck by a torrential rain* (مَطَر شَدِيد), in which:
  - The earth is plunged into darkness (أَظْلَمَتْ الأَرْض)
  - The sky thunders (أَرْعَدَتْ السَّمَاء)
  - Thunderbolts (صَوَاعِق) fall
  - Lightning (بَرْق) flashes dangerously

- In this state, the people **put their fingers in their ears** out of extreme fear (فَرْط الدَّهْشَة والفَزَع) to ward off (لِدَفْعِ) the danger of the thunderbolts — as if they believe this will save them from death.

- **Wallahu Muhitun bil-Kafirin** — inserted as a parenthetical sentence (جُمْلَة مُعْتَرِضَة) mid-narrative. This is a Qur'anic rhetorical device: in the middle of describing a scene, Allah inserts a warning or reminder. Here it means: Allah surrounds and encircles the disbelievers from all sides with His power; they cannot escape Him, just as one cannot escape when surrounded by an enemy from all directions.

- The lightning (بَرْق) is described as so intense it is **about to take away their eyesight** (يَكَادُ الْبَرْقُ يَخْطَفُ أَبْصَارَهُمْ) due to its intensity and strength.

- **Walking in the flash, stopping in the dark:** Whenever the lightning illuminates the path, they walk. When it disappears and darkness returns, they stop and stand still — out of fear of falling into a hole (خَشْيَة التَّرَدِّي فِي الحُفَر).

#### Application to the Munafiqeen

- This precisely describes the munafiqeen's spiritual condition: whenever they saw Islam and the Muslims in a position of strength, they walked with them. Whenever they saw the kuffar in a stronger position, they inclined toward them.
- As Allah says elsewhere: مُذَبْذَبِينَ بَيْنَ ذَٰلِكَ لَا إِلَىٰ هَٰؤُلَاءِ وَلَا إِلَىٰ هَٰؤُلَاءِ — "Wavering between this and that, belonging neither to these nor to those."
- They would say to the believers: أَلَمْ نَكُنْ مَعَكُمْ ("Were we not with you?") — and to the disbelievers similarly.
- There is a full Surah dedicated to them: **Surah Al-Munafiqun**.

### Allah's Power Over Them

- **وَلَوْ شَاءَ اللَّهُ لَذَهَبَ بِسَمْعِهِمْ وَأَبْصَارِهِمْ** — "Had Allah willed, He would have taken away their hearing and their sight."
- Allah could intensify the thunder and deafen them; He could intensify the lightning and blind them. He has full power (قُدْرَة) over all things.
- Ibn Jarir al-Tabari's explanation: Allah described His own power here specifically to **warn the munafiqeen** that they are under His total control. He surrounds them (مُحِيط بِهِمْ) and is fully capable of taking their senses — no one in the heavens or earth can make Allah incapable (لَا يُعْجِزُهُ أَحَدٌ).
- Ends with: **إِنَّ اللَّهَ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَيْءٍ قَدِيرٌ** — Allah has power over all things.

### Transition to Ayaat 21–25: Proofs of Tawheed

**Munasabah (rhetorical connection):**
- Having described the three groups (mu'mineen, kafireen, munafiqeen) and their characteristics, and having given parables, Allah now moves to presenting **evidences and proofs (دَلَائِل وَبَرَاهِين) for His oneness (وَحْدَانِيَّة)**.
- Purpose: to introduce His great blessings so people may be thankful (لِيَشْكُرُوا عَلَيْهَا), and to highlight the **miracle of the Qur'an** (بِأَنْصَعِ بَيَانٍ — with the clearest exposition), to uproot the roots of shirk and doubt from hearts (لِيَقْتَلِعَ جُذُورَ الشِّرْكِ مِنَ الْقُلُوبِ).

### Tafseer of "Ya Ayyuhan-Nas Ubudu Rabbakum"

- **يَا أَيُّهَا النَّاسُ** addresses ALL people of the world — believers, disbelievers, munafiqeen, mushrikeen alike. Not restricted to any one group.
- Command: **اعْبُدُوا رَبَّكُمُ** — Worship your Lord. This means: tawheed (making Allah one), thanking Him, and obeying Him.
- **الَّذِي خَلَقَكُمْ** — Who created you (brought you from non-existence/عَدَم into existence by His power).
- **وَالَّذِينَ مِنْ قَبْلِكُمْ** — And who created those before you (all prior generations and nations).
- Purpose: **لَعَلَّكُمْ تَتَّقُونَ** — So that you may be among the righteous (زُمْرَة الْمُتَّقِين) — the winners of guidance and success.

#### Imam Baydawi's Explanation of Iltifat

- In the preceding ayaat, the three groups were described in **third person** ("there are those who believe," "among the people are those who say…").
- Here Allah shifts to **direct second-person address** (Ya Ayyuhan-Nas). This rhetorical technique is called **iltifat** — literally "turning toward," i.e., drawing the listener's full attention.
- **Benefit:** it activates the listener, stimulates them, and signals that what follows is of the **utmost importance**. It is used whenever Allah wishes to address people about something critical, since most people are heedless (غَافِلُون) of that matter. The address commands their complete hearts and attention.
- Allah uses this throughout the Qur'an: "يَا أَيُّهَا النَّاسُ", "يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا" — each time signaling a weighty command or reminder.

### Allah's Blessings Enumerated

1. **Earth as a Firash (bed/resting place):**
   - Allah made the earth like a **فِرَاش** — a furnished bed, like a cradle (مِهَاد) or stable resting place (قَرَار).
   - Notably, the earth is **spherical (كَرَوِيَّة)** — it would normally be impossible to settle on a moving, spherical body. Yet Allah made it possible for humanity to live, sit, sleep, and settle upon it.
   - Imam Baghdadi's point: this does not require the earth to be literally flat (مُسَطَّح). Because the earth's size (حَجْم) is so immense, despite its spherical shape, its curvature is imperceptible to us and does not prevent settlement on it.

2. **Sky as a Canopy (Bina'):**
   - Allah raised the sky above as a **roof/dome** (كَهَيْئَةِ الْقُبَّة) — a built structure over the earth.

3. **Rain from the sky:**
   - **وَأَنزَلَ مِنَ السَّمَاءِ مَاءً** — Allah sent down water from the sky, extracted as **fresh and sweet water (عَذْب)** from the clouds by His power.

4. **Fruits and provision:**
   - **فَأَخْرَجَ بِهِ مِنَ الثَّمَرَاتِ رِزْقًا لَكُمْ** — With this water, Allah brought forth various fruits, vegetables, and food as sustenance for humanity.

## Grammar and Language Notes

- **صَيِّب** — noun meaning a heavy rain shower; used as a simile marker here.
- **أَظْلَمَ يُظْلِمُ** (bab إِفْعَال) — to become dark/to darken. **أَرْعَدَ يُرْعِدُ إِرْعَادًا** (same bab) — to thunder.
- **مُحِيط** — ism al-fa'il from أَحَاطَ يُحِيطُ إِحَاطَةً (bab إِفْعَال).
- **كَادَ يَكَادُ** — a "verb of approximation" (فِعْل مُقَارَبَة); means "to be about to" do something.
- **جُمْلَة مُعْتَرِضَة** — a parenthetical sentence: inserted between two parts of a continuous statement to add a warning, lesson, or emphasis, without breaking the grammatical structure of the surrounding speech. This is a well-recognized device in Arabic rhetoric (بَلَاغَة) and is frequently employed in the Qur'an.
- **جَعَلَ** — discussed as having multiple meanings; among them is the meaning of **وَضَعَ** (to place/put). Student asked whether يَجْعَلُونَ in the context of placing fingers could mean "to put" — confirmed yes, جَعَلَ carries this meaning as one of its uses.
- **إِبَاد (abad) vs. عِبَاد (ibad)** — corrected in class: the plural of **عَبْد** (servant/worshipper) is **عِبَاد** (ibad), not **أَبَاد** (abad). Teacher corrected the student's reading.
- **نَجَّى يُنَجِّي تَنْجِيَةً** and **أَنْجَى يُنْجِي إِنْجَاءً** — both valid verb forms meaning "to save/rescue."
- **مُنَبِّه** (munabbih) — ism al-fa'il from نَبَّهَ يُنَبِّهُ تَنْبِيهًا — one who warns or draws attention.

## Lessons and Practical Takeaways

1. **The danger of nifaq (hypocrisy):** The munafiqeen are in a state of perpetual confusion — they lack the clarity, courage, and conviction of true faith. Their condition is like a person stranded in a violent storm with no fixed direction.

2. **No one escapes Allah:** The parenthetical warning — *Wallahu muhitun bil-kafirin* — reminds us that despite apparent freedom, the disbelievers and hypocrites are entirely within Allah's grasp. True safety lies only with Allah, not in worldly calculations.

3. **Gratitude as a foundation of worship:** The transition to "Ya Ayyuhan-Nas" teaches that recognizing Allah's blessings (earth, sky, rain, food) is the rational and spiritual foundation for worshipping Him alone.

4. **Heedlessness requires direct address:** The reason Allah uses "Ya Ayyuhan-Nas" is precisely because people tend to be heedless of the most important things. The louder and more direct the call, the more heedless the audience being addressed.

5. **Taqwa is the goal of worship:** The command to worship is connected to **لَعَلَّكُمْ تَتَّقُونَ** — the ultimate aim of all ibadah is becoming among the muttaqeen (people of God-consciousness and righteousness).

6. **Preparation matters:** The teacher reminded students to always prepare their reading beforehand — coming unprepared leads to mistakes and a weaker learning experience.

## Historical Context and Stories Mentioned

- **The context of Madinah and the munafiqeen:** The parable was revealed in the context of the early Madinan period when Islam was gaining strength. A group of people — the munafiqeen — were neither committed Muslims nor open disbelievers. They calculated their allegiances based on which side appeared stronger at any given time.

- **Surah Al-Munafiqun:** Referenced as the dedicated Qur'anic surah exposing the characteristics of the hypocrites in detail.

- **Ayah from Surah Al-Nisa (or Al-Ma'idah — referenced as such in class):** مُذَبْذَبِينَ بَيْنَ ذَٰلِكَ لَا إِلَىٰ هَٰؤُلَاءِ وَلَا إِلَىٰ هَٰؤُلَاءِ — describing the wavering character of the munafiqeen, oscillating between the believers and the disbelievers.

## Q&A Highlights

**Q: Can جَعَلَ mean "to put/place" (like وَضَعَ)?**
A: Yes. جَعَلَ has many meanings. One of them is synonymous with وَضَعَ (to place). So يَجْعَلُونَ أَصَابِعَهُمْ فِي آذَانِهِمْ is correctly understood as "they place their fingers in their ears."

**Q: What does يُنْجِيهِمْ refer to — is it that the putting of fingers will save them?**
A: Yes. The idea is that the people in the storm think that plugging their ears will save them from dying due to the deafening sound of the thunderbolt. In reality it cannot truly save them from death, but extreme noise can physically damage the ear system and in some cases even cause death — so the impulse to block one's ears is understandable in that state of terror.

**Q: What are the two verb forms for "to save"?**
A: Both **نَجَّى يُنَجِّي تَنْجِيَةً** and **أَنْجَى يُنْجِي إِنْجَاءً** are valid. Both carry the meaning of saving or rescuing.

**Q: Does the earth have to be flat (مُسَطَّح) for the ayah's description of it as a "bed" to be valid?**
A: No. Imam Baghdadi explains that because the earth's size is so immense, its spherical curvature does not prevent humans from settling and resting on it. The description of the earth as a فِرَاش (bed) refers to its function — it serves as a resting place for humanity — not its literal geometric shape.
