# Tafseer 201 - Study Notes
**Date:** 2026-10-08
**Surah Covered:** Surah Al-Baqarah, Ayat 76–77 (Page 71)

---

## Session Overview

This session covered the tafseer of Surah Al-Baqarah, Ayaat 76–77, focusing on Allah's address to the believers regarding their hope that the Jews would accept Islam. The ayaat describe two major crimes of a group among the Jews: (1) the deliberate distortion of the Torah after fully understanding it, and (2) their duplicitous behavior — speaking favorably to Muslims in public while privately warning their own community not to share information about the Prophet ﷺ that was recorded in the Torah. The session also included an extended grammar lesson on conjugating weak verbs (أفعال معتلة) that end in alif maqsurah, with practical tips for determining whether the plural form takes a fathah or dhammah.

---

## Key Themes and Topics

1. **Allah's rebuke of the believers' optimism** — Allah rhetorically questions whether the Muslims genuinely expect the Jews to accept Islam, given what those Jews were doing.
2. **Deliberate distortion of the Torah** — A group of Jewish scholars (rabbis/ulama) altered the verses of the Torah knowingly and intentionally, not out of mistake or forgetfulness.
3. **Hypocrisy of some Jews toward Muslims** — When meeting Muslims, certain Jews claimed belief; when alone among themselves, other Jews scolded them for sharing Quranic/Tawraic information with Muslims.
4. **The argument on the Day of Resurrection** — Sharing knowledge of the Prophet ﷺ's descriptions with Muslims would become evidence against those Jews on the Day of Judgment.
5. **Allah's all-encompassing knowledge** — Allah rebukes those who think their secret scheming is hidden from Him.
6. **Grammar: conjugation of weak verbs with alif maqsurah** — Practical tips to distinguish plural forms (فتحة vs. ضمة) in verbs like مَشَى، لَقِيَ، سَعَى.

## Main Tafseer Points and Explanations

### Ayah 76 — أَفَتَطْمَعُونَ أَن يُؤْمِنُوا لَكُمْ

**"Do you [believers] then hope that they will believe in you?"**

- Allah addresses the Sahabah (the believers), who sincerely wished for the Jews to enter Islam.
- The question is rhetorical — it introduces a contrast (واو الحال) between the believers' hope and the reality of what those Jews were actually doing.
- **وَقَدْ كَانَ فَرِيقٌ مِّنْهُمْ:** "While there is a group among them..." — the وَ here carries the meaning of a condition/contrast (حال).
- That group (rabbis and scholars) would hear the very words of Allah in the Torah — clearly, openly, without ambiguity.
- **يَسْمَعُونَ كَلَامَ اللَّهِ:** They heard it and understood it fully (عَقَلُوهُ — their intellect grasped it).
- **ثُمَّ يُحَرِّفُونَهُ:** Yet after fully understanding it, they would deliberately distort it.
  - Distortion took two forms:
    1. **تَبْدِيل** — outright substitution/changing of words
    2. **تَأْوِيل** — false interpretation, reading their own meaning into the text
  - This was done **عَلَى بَصِيرَة** — with complete awareness — not by mistake (لَا عَن خَطَأ) and not by forgetfulness (وَلَا عَن نِسْيَان).
- **Implication:** If these scholars, with full knowledge, committed the crime of distorting Allah's word, how can you realistically hope they will now accept Islam?

---

### Ayah 76 continued — وَإِذَا لَقُوا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا قَالُوا آمَنَّا

**"And when they meet those who believe, they say: 'We have believed.'"**

- This describes a second crime: outward hypocrisy.
- A sub-group among the Jews — the **munafiqeen** among them — would tell Muslims: "We believe, you are right, and Muhammad ﷺ is indeed the messenger whose coming was foretold in our books (مُبَشَّرٌ بِهِ)."
- **لَّذِينَ آمَنُوا** is اسم موصول; **آمَنُوا** is its صِلَة الموصول and acts as مَفْعُول بِهِ for لَقُوا.

---

### Ayah 76 continued — وَإِذَا خَلَا بَعْضُهُمْ إِلَى بَعْضٍ

**"But when they are alone with one another..."**

- When those munafiq Jews were away from Muslims and in private with other (non-munafiq) Jews, the non-munafiq group would rebuke them:
  - **"Do you tell the Muslims what Allah has revealed to you [in the Torah]?"** — i.e., the descriptions and glad tidings of the Prophet ﷺ.
  - **"So that they will have an argument against you before your Lord on the Day of Resurrection?"** — لِتَكُونَ الْحُجَّةُ لِلْمُؤْمِنِينَ عَلَيْكُمْ فِي الْآخِرَة
  - Their fear: if they acknowledge to Muslims that the Torah confirms Muhammad ﷺ, then on the Day of Judgment Muslims will use that acknowledgment as evidence against them — "You knew, yet you did not follow."

---

### Ayah 77 — أَوَلَا يَعْلَمُونَ أَنَّ اللَّهَ يَعْلَمُ مَا يُسِرُّونَ وَمَا يُعْلِنُونَ

**"Do they not know that Allah knows what they conceal and what they reveal?"**

- Allah responds to their scheming with a rebuke (رَدًّا وَتَوْبِيخًا).
- Whether they hide something or declare it openly, Allah knows all of it — **لَا تَخْفَى عَلَيْهِ خَافِيَة** — nothing whatsoever is hidden from Him.
- The rebuke sharpens: they claim to have faith (يَزْعُمُونَ الإِيمَانَ) yet conduct themselves as though Allah is unaware of their scheming — a profound contradiction.

## Grammar and Language Notes

### Verb: يُخَاطِبُ
- From **خَاطَبَ** — بَاب مُفَاعَلَة (Form III). Meaning: to address someone.

### Verb: طَمِعَ يَطْمَعُ طَمَعًا
- To hope/desire eagerly; also carries connotation of greed in some contexts.

### Verb: خَلَا يَخْلُو خَلَاءً
- To be secluded/alone with someone.

### Verb: أَسَرَّ يُسِرُّ إِسْرَارًا
- To conceal, to keep secret.

### Key Grammar Lesson: Conjugating Weak Verbs (أفعال معتلة) — Plural Vowel Rule

The teacher provided a practical shortcut for determining whether the plural (جمع مذكر غائب) form takes **فتحة** (fathah → مَشَوْا) or **ضمة** (dhammah → لَقُوا).

**The Tip:** Look at the **first sighah** (either the مَاضِي base form or the مُضَارِع). If that first form ends in **alif maqsurah (الف مقصورة — ى)**, then the plural takes **فتحة**. If the first form does NOT end in alif maqsurah, the plural takes **ضمة**.

| Verb | مَاضِي | مُضَارِع | Alif Maqsurah in Madi? | Alif Maqsurah in Mudari'? | Plural (جمع) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| مَشَى | مَشَى (✓ alif maqsurah) | يَمْشِي (✗) | ✓ | ✗ | مَشَوْا (فتحة) |
| لَقِيَ | لَقِيَ (✗ regular ya) | يَلْقَى (✓ alif maqsurah) | ✗ | ✓ | لَقُوا (ضمة) in madi / يَلْقَوْنَ (فتحة) in mudari' |
| سَعَى | سَعَى (✓ alif maqsurah) | يَسْعَى (✓ alif maqsurah) | ✓ | ✓ | سَعَوْا / يَسْعَوْنَ (فتحة both) |

**Examples worked in class:**

- **مَشَى / يَمْشِي:**
  - مَاضِي جمع: مَشَوْا (فتحة — because مَشَى has alif maqsurah)
  - مُضَارِع جمع: يَمْشُونَ (ضمة — because يَمْشِي has no alif maqsurah)

- **لَقِيَ / يَلْقَى:**
  - مَاضِي جمع: لَقُوا (ضمة — because لَقِيَ has no alif maqsurah)
  - مُضَارِع جمع: يَلْقَوْنَ (فتحة — because يَلْقَى has alif maqsurah)
  - Application in ayah: **وَإِذَا لَقُوا** → لَقُوا with ضمة ✓

- **سَعَى / يَسْعَى:**
  - Both مَاضِي and مُضَارِع have alif maqsurah
  - مَاضِي جمع: سَعَوْا (فتحة); مُضَارِع جمع: يَسْعَوْنَ (فتحة)
  - Quranic example: **يَسْعَوْنَ فِي الْأَرْضِ فَسَادًا**

**Note from teacher:** This is a practical memorization tip, not the formal morphological rule (which involves تَعْلِيل and deeper sarf principles). The formal rules will be covered in dedicated sarf study.

**Assignment:** Write out the full conjugation of three verb patterns (covering مَشَى، لَقِيَ، سَعَى) with all vowel markings and send to the teacher on WhatsApp for correction.

### Sighah Analysis: آمَنَّا
- مَاضِي، مُتَكَلِّم جمع (first person plural past)
- Root: أَمَنَ يُؤْمِنُ إِيمَانًا
- Pattern: آمَنَ / آمَنَا / آمَنُوا / آمَنَتْ — with نَّا for first person plural

## Lessons and Practical Takeaways

1. **Knowledge without action is not enough.** The Jewish scholars knew the truth — they heard the words of Allah clearly and understood them — yet their knowledge did not lead to obedience. This is a warning for all students of knowledge: understanding an obligation intensifies one's accountability.

2. **Deliberate deception is a grave sin.** The rabbis distorted the Torah **after** fully understanding it — on full insight (عَلَى بَصِيرَة). Changing the word of Allah knowingly is described here as one of the greatest crimes.

3. **Hypocrisy that serves worldly interests leads to disaster in the Akhirah.** The munafiq Jews thought they were being strategic — appearing friendly to Muslims to avoid conflict. But their own community warned them that this would only create a stronger argument against them on the Day of Judgment.

4. **Allah's knowledge is total and unconditional.** Secret councils, private conversations, hidden intentions — none of these are outside Allah's knowledge. True faith requires behaving consistently whether in public or private.

5. **Studying sarf (Arabic morphology) is essential for Tafseer.** The teacher emphasized that without understanding verb forms and conjugations, one cannot fully grasp the meanings embedded in the Quranic text. Students should invest in learning sarf patterns systematically.

6. **Practical approach to sarf:** Rather than reading an entire morphology textbook, focus on the verbs that appear in the Tafseer text being studied. Write out five conjugations per pattern, memorize them with vowel markings, and get them checked by the teacher.

## Historical Context and Stories Mentioned

- **The Rabbis of the Jews in Madinah:** At the time of the Prophet ﷺ, the Jewish scholars of Madinah possessed the Torah, which contained descriptions (أوصاف) and glad tidings (بِشَارَات) about the coming of the final Prophet. Some among them acknowledged this privately or semi-publicly to Muslims, while the mainstream scholarly establishment suppressed this information to protect their communal authority and identity.

- **The munafiqeen among the Jews:** This ayah points to a distinct sub-group — Jews who outwardly expressed sympathy with or even acceptance of the Muslim position when in Muslim company, without formally converting. They were not the same as the Arab munafiqeen (hypocrites) described elsewhere in the Quran; they were a separate phenomenon specific to the Jewish community of Madinah.

- **Distortion of the Torah (تحريف):** Islamic scholarship distinguishes between تحريف لفظي (literal textual alteration of words) and تحريف معنوي (distortion of meaning through false interpretation). Both forms are referenced in this ayah — tabdeel (substitution) and ta'weel (misinterpretation).

**Q: Is there a book that comprehensively explains the rules for verb conjugation changes?**
> The teacher confirmed that such a book was recommended at the start of the course and covers all conjugation patterns comprehensively. The full theoretical rules (تعليل) for why weak verb letters change are advanced-level content. For now, the recommended approach is: (1) identify the pattern of a verb, (2) write out five full conjugations for each pattern with all vowel marks, (3) memorize them by heart. The teacher offered to review student work submitted via WhatsApp.

**Q: How do you determine whether لَقُوا is with ضمة or فتحة?**
> Apply the alif maqsurah tip: لَقِيَ (مَاضِي) does not end in alif maqsurah — it ends in a regular ya (ي). Therefore the plural مَاضِي takes ضمة → لَقُوا. The teacher confirmed: **وَإِذَا لَقُوا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا** — لَقُوا is correct with ضمة.

**Q (implicit): Which sigha is لَقُوا, and what is اسم موصول الَّذِينَ doing grammatically?**
> لَقُوا is مَاضِي جمع مذكر غائب. الَّذِينَ is اسم موصول. آمَنُوا is its صِلَة الموصول. The entire phrase الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا functions as مَفْعُول بِهِ (direct object) of لَقُوا.
