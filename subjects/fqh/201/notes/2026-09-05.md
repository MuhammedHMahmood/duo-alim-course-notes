# Fiqh 201 - Study Notes
**Date:** 2026-09-05

---

## Session Overview

This session covered two major topics. The first half completed the chapter on Masa (wiping) by examining the rules for different types of footwear (Khuffayn, Jorabayn, Jarmouk), the prohibition of Masa on head coverings, and the special rules for Masa on bandages and casts (Jabair), including four key differences between Masa on socks and Masa on Jabair. The second half began the chapter on Haydh (menstruation), covering the linguistic and Shari' definitions of Haydh, its minimum and maximum durations according to the four madhabs, the colors of blood that constitute Haydh, and an introduction to the rulings that apply to a woman in the state of Haydh — including restrictions on salah, sawm, entering the masjid, tawaf, marital relations, and recitation and touching of the Quran.

---

## Key Themes

1. **Differentiated rulings for types of footwear** — Masa is not a blanket permission; it depends on the material, construction, and functional properties of what is being wiped over.
2. **Conditions for Masa on Jorabayn** — Three specific conditions must all be met for regular socks to qualify; the thin dress/office socks commonly worn today do not meet these conditions.
3. **Jabair (bandage/cast) as a special category** — Unlike socks, the bandage ruling does not require prior wudu and has no time limit; the ruling is tied to the underlying medical need.
4. **Haydh as a Shari' category with precise parameters** — Haydh has defined minimum/maximum durations, specific colors that qualify, and a clear endpoint (white discharge), with differences of opinion among the four Imams.
5. **Restrictions during Haydh are specific and bounded** — Haydh removes/suspends specific ibadah obligations (salah is saqit; sawm is delayed with qada required) and imposes specific prohibitions, but does not render the woman socially untouchable or restrict ordinary daily life.
6. **Distinction between hadas asghar and janabah/haydh** — A person without wudu (hadas asghar) is restricted only from touching the Quran; a person in janabah or haydh is also prohibited from oral recitation, because the impurity in those states encompasses the entire body including the mouth.

## Fiqh Rulings & Evidence

### Masa on Jarmouk
- **Ruling:** Permissible. If a person is wearing a Jarmouk over his Khuffayn, he may wipe over the Jarmouk directly. He does not need to remove it to wipe the Khuff beneath.
- **Rationale:** The Jarmouk is worn on top of valid footwear; wiping the outer layer suffices.

### Masa on Regular Jorabayn (without leather reinforcement)
- **Ruling (Imam Abu Hanifa — general position):** Not permissible unless three conditions are met (see Conditions section below).
- **Ruling (Sahibayn — Imam Abu Yusuf and Imam Muhammad):** Permissible if the socks are thick enough that water cannot seep through.
- **Evidence for Muna'al permissibility:** A hadith in Sunan al-Bayhaqi grants permissibility to Jorabayn wrapped with leather.

### Masa on Caps, Turbans, Burqa, and Gloves
- **Ruling:** Not permissible. Imam Quduri explicitly states: "Masa on the Amamah, Qalansuwa, Burqa, and Quffazayn is not valid."
- **Note on Amamah — scholarly positions:**
  - *Some Sahabah and Tabi'in:* gave permissibility for Masa on the Amamah.
  - *Imam Ahmad (rahimahullah):* Permissible under certain conditions.
  - *Imam Shafi'i (rahimahullah):* Not permissible; however, if one slides the Amamah back to expose the front hairline and wipes even a very small portion of hair (he holds even three hairs sufficient), the Masa of the head is valid.
  - *Imam Muhammad (Hanafi):* Masa on Amamah was permissible in early Islam but was later abrogated (Mansookh).
  - *Hadith (Abu Dawud, narrated by Abu Hurayra):* The Prophet ﷺ slid his hand under his Amamah and wiped the front of his head. The Hanafi position is that sliding the hand under the Amamah to wipe one-quarter of the head is the correct method.

### Masa on Jabair (Bandages and Casts)
- **Ruling:** Permissible, even if the bandage/cast was applied when the person was not in wudu.
- **Key point:** Unlike socks, being in wudu when the bandage is applied is not a condition.
- **If the bandage falls during salah:** The Masa remains valid; wudu is not broken — provided the wound underneath is still not healed.
- **If the bandage falls and the wound is fully healed:** The Masa becomes batil (invalid) and wudu is broken, because the underlying Shari' dispensation (rukhsa) was tied to the existence of the injury.

### Prohibition of Entering the Masjid for a Ha'idah and a Junub
- **Ruling:** A woman in Haydh and a person in Janabah may not enter the masjid.
- **Evidence:** Hadith in Abu Dawud and Ibn Majah.
- **Scope:** Mutlaqan — no condition qualifies this prohibition.
- **Exception (Imam Shafi'i only):** Mere passage through the masjid is permissible if there is no alternative route (a unique edge case).

### Prohibition of Tawaf
- **Ruling:** All forms of Tawaf — fard, wajib, nafl, Tawaf al-Ziyarah — are absolutely impermissible for a Ha'idah. There is no exception and no scholarly disagreement on this.

### Prohibition of Marital Relations
- **Ruling:** Husband and wife may not have physical marital relations during Haydh.
- **Evidence:** Quranic ayah commanding to keep away until the woman becomes pure.
- **Imam Abu Hanifa, Imam Abu Yusuf, Imam Shafi'i, Imam Malik:** The entire area from the navel to the knees of the woman becomes haram; all else is permissible (sitting together, eating, hugging, etc.).
- **Imam Muhammad (rahimahullah):** Only the private part itself is impermissible; everything else including the area between navel and knees is halal.
- **Note:** Islam does not render a Ha'idah socially untouchable. She lives as a normal family member in all respects except the specific prohibitions listed.

### Prohibition of Qira'at al-Quran for Ha'idah and Junub
- **Ruling:** Recitation of the Quran — whether from memory or from the text — is not permissible for a Ha'idah or a person in Janabah.
- **Evidence:** Hadith in Tirmidhi and Ibn Majah: the Prophet ﷺ said the Ha'idah and the Junub may not recite the Quran.
- **Distinction with person lacking wudu (Muhdith):** A person who only lacks wudu (hadas asghar) may recite from memory; they may not touch the written text. The Ha'idah/Junub are restricted from recitation itself because the impurity of Janabah and Haydh encompasses the entire body including the interior of the mouth.

### Prohibition of Touching the Quran Without Wudu
- **Ruling:** Touching the written text of the Quran is not permissible without wudu.
- **Evidence:** Quranic ayah and hadith in Nasa'i, Tabrani, Bayhaqi, Musnad Ahmad, Musnad Hakim: "Only a pure person may touch the Quran."
- **Workaround:** Holding the Quran via a piece of cloth, a cover, or a sleeve as a barrier is permissible, provided the writing itself is not touched directly.
- **Digital Quran / Phone:** Scrolling through a Quran app without physically touching the written ayaat on screen is permissible without wudu. The restriction targets touching the writing itself, regardless of medium.
- **Holding the closed Mushaf:** Permissible without wudu provided no Quranic ayah on the cover is touched. If the cover has an ayah on it, caution is required.
- **For Ha'idah / Junub specifically:** Touching the Quran in any form is impermissible; recitation is also impermissible.

### Salah During Haydh
- **Ruling:** Salah is **saqit** (completely lifted/dropped) — it does not need to be made up. The missed prayers during Haydh are permanently forgiven; there is no qada.

### Sawm (Fasting) During Haydh
- **Ruling:** Fasting is **haram** during Haydh. It is not permanently dropped — **qada is required.** Whatever days of Ramadan are missed must be made up after the period ends.

## Conditions & Categories

### Three Conditions for Masa on Jorabayn (Regular Socks)

All three must be met simultaneously:

1. **Water impermeability:** The socks must be thick enough that water cannot seep through them when wet hands are passed over them. Thin dress/office/professional socks that allow water to penetrate do not meet this condition and Masa on them is not valid.

2. **Self-supporting on the shin:** The socks must be capable of staying up on the shin on their own, without being tied, laced, or held in place. If the socks would fall down the moment the wearer lets go, this condition is not met.

3. **Able to withstand walking one mile without tearing:** The socks must be durable enough that a person can walk a full mile (approximately 1.6 km) without any tears appearing. If tears begin before the mile is completed, they do not qualify.

### Masa on Mujallad vs. Muna'al Jorabayn
- **Mujallad:** The entire outer surface of the Jorab has been covered/wrapped with leather → treated like Khuffayn; Masa is valid without the three conditions above.
- **Muna'al:** Only the sole (bottom) of the Jorab has leather applied → also qualifies for Masa; evidence from Sunan al-Bayhaqi.

### Types of Footwear — Comparative Summary

| Feature | Khuffayn | Jorabayn | Jarmouk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Material | Thick, durable leather | Cotton, wool, synthetic | Leather or any material |
| Worn on | Feet directly | Feet directly | On top of Khuff or boots |
| Fit | Well-fitted | Well-fitted | Made larger to fit over inner footwear |
| Durability | Very sturdy | Varies by material | Flimsy; purpose is only to protect inner footwear |
| Masa ruling | Always permissible | Conditional (3 conditions) | Permissible (wipe over the Jarmouk directly) |

### Four Differences Between Masa on Socks (Jorabayn) and Masa on Jabair

| # | Jorabayn (Socks) | Jabair (Bandage/Cast) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | If one sock is removed, both socks must be removed and both feet washed | If the bandage comes off and the wound is healed, only that area needs to be washed; wudu remains intact otherwise |
| 2 | Removing one sock for any reason requires washing both feet | If the wound is unhealed and the bandage falls, simply retie the bandage — wudu remains valid |
| 3 | Has a time limit: 24 hours for a resident (mukeem); 72 hours for a traveler | No time limit — the Masa remains valid for as long as the bandage/cast is medically necessary |
| 4 | Must be put on while in wudu — this is a prerequisite for valid Masa | Can be applied at any time, with or without wudu — prior wudu is not a condition |

### Duration of Haydh — Positions of the Four Madhabs

| Imam | Minimum | Maximum |
|---|---|---|
| **Imam Abu Hanifa (Ahnaf)** | 3 days and 3 nights | 10 days and 10 nights |
| **Imam Shafi'i** | 1 day and 1 night | 15 days and 15 nights |
| **Imam Ahmad ibn Hanbal** | 1 day | Not specified above (follows Hanbali sources) |
| **Imam Malik** | No minimum | 10 days |

- **Evidence for Ahnaf position:** Hadith in Dar al-Qutni and Tabrani — the Prophet ﷺ stated the minimum is 3 days and 3 nights and the maximum is 10 days and 10 nights.
- **Evidence for Imam Shafi'i's 15-day maximum:** Dar al-Qutni.
- Blood lasting fewer than 3 days (Ahnaf) = Istihada. Blood lasting more than 10 days (Ahnaf) = Istihada.

### Colors of Blood That Constitute Haydh

During the established days of a woman's cycle, the following colors are all considered Haydh:

1. Red (الحُمْرَة)
2. Yellow (الصُّفْرَة)
3. Dark/black (very dark red)
4. Dusty/dirty color (القُدْرَة — the color of soil)
5. Light orange
6. Greenish (rare; usually indicates infection or dietary cause)

**End of Haydh:** A woman's Haydh ends when she sees a completely white or clear discharge with absolutely no trace of any other color. Even a single thread or strand of color in the discharge means Haydh continues.

- **Opinion of Imam Abu Hanifa and Imam Muhammad:** The discharge must be fully clear/white with no color at all.
- **Fatwa of Sayyidah Aisha (ra):** "Do not hurry — wait until you see complete whiteness." This is reported as her fatwa based on what she heard directly from the Prophet ﷺ.

### Istihada — Definition and Scope
- Blood outside the valid Haydh window (less than 3 days or more than 10 days per Ahnaf) is classified as Istihada.
- Istihada can arise from illness, a medical condition, or other physical causes.
- Istihada does not carry the same rulings as Haydh — the woman continues to pray, fast, and fulfill ibadah obligations.

## Scholarly Opinions

### Masa on Jorabayn
- **Imam Abu Hanifa (general Hanafi position):** Not permissible unless the Jorab is Mujallad or Muna'al (wrapped fully or on the sole with leather).
- **Sahibayn (Imam Abu Yusuf and Imam Muhammad):** Permissible on regular socks if they are thick enough that water cannot penetrate.

### Masa on Amamah (Turban)
- **Some Sahabah and Tabi'in:** Permissible.
- **Imam Ahmad:** Permissible under certain conditions.
- **Imam Shafi'i:** Not permissible; valid Masa requires exposing and wiping actual hair (even the minimal amount he requires — down to three hairs — suffices if the Amamah is slid back).
- **Imam Muhammad (Hanafi):** Was permissible initially in Islam, then became Mansookh (abrogated).
- **Hanafi practical guidance:** Slide the hand beneath the Amamah and wipe at least one-quarter of the head.

### Marital Restrictions During Haydh
- **Imam Abu Hanifa, Imam Abu Yusuf, Imam Shafi'i, Imam Malik:** The area from the navel to the knees is haram during Haydh; all else is permissible.
- **Imam Muhammad:** Only the private part itself is impermissible; the rest — including the navel-to-knee area — is halal.

### Duration of Haydh
- See the comparative table in Conditions & Categories above.

### Entering the Masjid During Haydh / Janabah
- **Imam Abu Hanifa, Imam Malik, Imam Ahmad, and the Hanafi fuqaha:** Absolutely prohibited; no conditions qualify this.
- **Imam Shafi'i:** Passage through the masjid is permissible when no alternative route exists; otherwise prohibited.

## Lessons & Practical Application

1. **Thin dress socks do not qualify for Masa.** The three conditions — water impermeability, self-support on the shin, and ability to withstand a mile of walking — are not met by the thin cotton/polyester professional or office socks most people wear today. Performing Masa on these socks and then praying is not valid per the mainstream Hanafi position. People should be aware of this common mistake in local masajid.

2. **Leather-soled or leather-wrapped socks are the practical solution.** If one wants to perform Masa on regular socks, ensuring they are Mujallad (fully wrapped) or Muna'al (leather-soled) brings them into the valid category.

3. **For Jabair: do not delay wudu out of concern about bandage timing.** Since the bandage does not require prior wudu and has no time limit, a person with a cast or wound can make wudu normally, wiping over the bandage without fear of the clock running out.

4. **Women in Haydh planning for Hajj or Umrah must plan carefully.** They cannot enter Masjid al-Haram or perform any type of Tawaf during Haydh. This is a matter with no scholarly disagreement and must be factored into travel planning. Further rulings will be addressed when Kitab al-Hajj is covered.

5. **Salah is saqit; sawm requires qada.** A practical distinction for women: missed prayers during Haydh are permanently dropped — do not attempt to make them up. Missed fasts of Ramadan must be made up. Confusing these two rulings is a common error.

6. **Maintain wudu when interacting with the Quran as a matter of adab.** Even where technically permissible (e.g., reciting from memory without wudu, scrolling a digital Quran without touching the text), the adab (etiquette) of Quran demands that one strive to always be in wudu. The Salaf would not even utter the name of the Prophet ﷺ without wudu — this reflects the level of reverence that should be aimed for.

7. **Digital Quran apps:** Scrolling or reading a Quran app on a phone without physically pressing the text itself is permissible without wudu. However, if one physically touches the text on the screen, it falls under the restriction. Striving to use the Quran in the state of wudu remains the recommended adab regardless.

8. **Greenish discharge during the cycle period is still Haydh.** While uncommon, it may result from infection or diet. Women should not assume their Haydh has ended until they observe the completely white/clear discharge.

9. **Islam does not marginalize women during Haydh.** Aside from specific religious restrictions (salah, sawm, entering masjid, marital relations, Quran), a woman in Haydh lives completely normally — cooking, socializing, eating with family. The false notion of untouchability has no basis in Islamic law.
