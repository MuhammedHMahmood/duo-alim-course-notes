**Date:** 2026-08-12

## Session Overview

This session opened Kitab al-Tahara (Book of Purification) from Mukhtasar al-Quduri. Before beginning the kitab, the teacher reviewed the seven categories of Shari'a rulings from the previous session. The class then covered the structure of Islamic legal texts (Kitab, Bab, Fasl), the linguistic and technical meanings of Tahara, the Quranic and hadith foundations for wudu, the four fara'id (obligatory elements) of wudu, the ikhtilaf (scholarly disagreement) regarding whether the elbows and ankle bones are included in washing, and the three opinions on the required amount of masa of the head. The session concluded with brief discussion on maintaining wudu and the merits of sleeping in a state of wudu.

---

## Key Themes

1. **Review of the seven categories of Shari'a rulings** — fard through makruh tanzih, with emphasis on proper usage of the term "haram"
2. **Structure of Islamic legal texts** — understanding Kitab, Bab, and Fasl as organizational levels
3. **Linguistic vs. Shar'i meaning of Tahara** — how the word changes meaning with vowelization
4. **The four fara'id of wudu** — derived from Surah al-Ma'idah with supporting ahadith
5. **Inclusion of the elbows and ankle bones** — Aima Thalasa of Ahnaf vs. Imam Zufar Rahimullah
6. **Masa of the head: how much is obligatory** — Hanafi, Shafi'i, and Maliki positions compared
7. **The principle of one-fourth (rub')** — a foundational usuli rule applied across multiple chapters of fiqh

## Fiqh Rulings & Evidence

### The Four Fara'id of Wudu

The Quranic basis for wudu is Surah al-Ma'idah (5:6):

> يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا إِذَا قُمْتُمْ إِلَى الصَّلَاةِ فَاغْسِلُوا وُجُوهَكُمْ وَأَيْدِيَكُمْ إِلَى الْمَرَافِقِ وَامْسَحُوا بِرُءُوسِكُمْ وَأَرْجُلَكُمْ إِلَى الْكَعْبَيْنِ
>
> *"O you who believe, when you stand for prayer, wash your faces and your hands up to the elbows, and wipe your heads, and wash your feet up to the ankle bones."*

The four fara'id are:

| # | Fard | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Washing the face | Once, covering the entire face |
| 2 | Washing the arms up to (and including) the elbows | Right then left; elbows are included per Aima Thalasa |
| 3 | Masa (wiping) of the head | Minimum: one-fourth (rub') of the head per Ahnaf |
| 4 | Washing the feet up to (and including) the ankle bones | Ankle bones are included per Aima Thalasa |

---

### The Eight Points of Surah al-Ma'idah 5:6 (per Sahib al-Durr al-Mukhtar)

The Sahib of Durr al-Mukhtar notes this one ayah points to **eight things in pairs**:

| Pair | Two Aspects |
|---|---|
| 1. Type of Tahara | Wudu or Ghusl |
| 2. Means of Tahara | Water or Tayammum |
| 3. Two Orders | Washing or Masah (for head) |
| 4. What makes wudu/ghusl wajib | Hadas (minor) or Janaba (major) |
| 5. Two Mubah relaxations | Sickness or Travel |
| 6. Two Dala'il | Ghusl is required; wudu is required |
| 7. Two Indications | Towards the ghaya (restroom use) and marital relations |
| 8. Two Rewards | Forgiveness of sins; completion of ni'mah (dying as shaheed if in state of wudu) |

### Inclusion of Elbows and Ankle Bones

**Position of Aima Thalasa of Ahnaf (Imam Abu Hanifa, Imam Abu Yusuf, Imam Muhammad — and the mufta bihi ruling):**
The elbows and ankle bones **are included** in the washing. If the elbow is not washed, the wudu is invalid and must be redone. Salah prayed without a valid wudu is also invalid.

**Dalil:** Hadith in Sahih Muslim narrated by Nu'aim ibn Abdullah al-Mujmir, who witnessed Abu Hurayra (radi Allahu anhu) perform wudu in the manner of Rasulullah ﷺ. Abu Hurayra:
- Washed his face completely
- Washed his right arm going **past** the elbow onto part of the upper arm
- Washed his left arm similarly
- Wiped his head
- Washed his right foot going past the ankle up to the shank
- Washed his left foot similarly

Abu Hurayra then stated: *"This is how I saw the Messenger of Allah ﷺ performing wudu."* He also added that Rasulullah ﷺ said those who perform wudu well shall have bright faces, hands, and feet on the Day of Judgment, and whoever can, should increase that brightness.

**Secondary dalil (rational — ghaya and mughayyah):** When something and its boundary share the same *genus* (jins), the boundary is included. The fingers, palm, and elbow are all part of the same limb (the hand/arm); similarly the foot and ankle bone belong to the same limb. Hence the boundary is included in the washing.

---

**Position of Imam Zufar Rahimullah:**
The elbows and ankle bones are **not** included. Washing up to the elbow is sufficient; the elbow itself need not be washed.

**Dalil (qiyas):** Surah al-Baqarah (2:187):
> أَتِمُّوا الصِّيَامَ إِلَى اللَّيْلِ

*"Complete the fast until the night."* — No one includes the night in the fast. The boundary (night) is not included in the bounded thing (the fast day). Imam Zufar applies the same logic: washing *up to* the elbow means stopping at the elbow, not including it.

**Ahnaf's response to Imam Zufar:** Day and night are two different genera (jins). The boundary (night) does not share the same genus as the bounded period (day), so it is excluded. But the elbow shares the same genus as the rest of the arm, so it is included. The rational principle only excludes a boundary when it belongs to a different genus.

### Masa of the Head: Minimum Quantity

Imam Quduri states: *"The obligatory amount for masa of the head is the quantity of the nasiya, which is one-fourth of the head."* — Based on the hadith of Mughira ibn Shu'ba (radi Allahu anhu) in Bukhari and Muslim.

**Background of the hadith:** During a journey, Mughira ibn Shu'ba poured water for Rasulullah ﷺ after he relieved himself. Rasulullah ﷺ performed wudu: he washed his face and arms, performed masa over the nasiya of his head, and wiped over his two khuffain (leather socks).

Note: This hadith does not mention washing the bare feet — it mentions masa over the khuffain, which is a separate ruling (to be covered in Bab al-Masa ala al-Khuffain).

## Conditions & Categories

### Three Opinions on the Meaning of "Nasiya" (Minimum Masa of the Head)

| Opinion | Minimum Required | Held By |
|---|---|---|
| One-fourth (rub') of the head — the top portion | Wiping the top of the head, which constitutes approximately one-fourth | **Ahnaf (dominant/mufta bihi position)** |
| The forehead only | Placing the wet hand on the hairline and wiping that area | Second Hanafi opinion |
| Three fingers' width | Wiping an area equivalent to three fingers from the hairline | Third Hanafi opinion |
| Three hairs | Wetting even three hairs on any part of the head | **Imam al-Shafi'i Rahimullah** |
| The entire head | Full masa of the entire head is obligatory | **Imam Malik Rahimullah** (based on ahadith describing Rasulullah ﷺ wiping his full head) |

---

### The Usuli Principle of One-Fourth (Rub')

**Principle:** If one-fourth of a required action is performed, it counts as the entire action being completed. If one-fourth is left out, the entire action is considered omitted.

**Applications across chapters of fiqh:**

| Chapter | Application |
|---|---|
| Wudu | Masa of one-fourth of the head fulfills the fard of masa |
| Salah (woman's satr) | If less than one-fourth of the satr opened during salah and was covered before a full rukun passed, salah is not void |
| Hajj (prohibited acts) | If a muhrim shaved less than one-fourth of his head, dam is not wajib (though penalty still applies) |

### Structure of Islamic Legal Texts

| Level | Arabic | Equivalent | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| Highest | كِتَاب (*Kitab*) | Volume / Major Book | Covers an entire major subject area (e.g., Kitab al-Tahara, Kitab al-Zakat) |
| Middle | بَاب (*Bab*) | Chapter | Groups masa'il related to a specific topic (e.g., Bab al-Tayammum) |
| Lowest | فَصْل (*Fasl*) | Section | Breaks a bab into finer sub-topics; not always present in Quduri |

### Linguistic Meanings of Tahara by Vowelization

| Vowelization | Pronunciation | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| فَتْحَة | *Tahara* | To purify (verbal noun / act of purifying) |

| كَسْرَة | *Tihara* | The instrument/vessel of purification |

**Shar'i meaning:** Removal of impurity (hadas or janaba) that stands as an obstacle to acts of worship.

## Scholarly Opinions

### On Inclusion of Elbows/Ankle Bones in Wudu

| Scholar/School | Position |
|---|---|
| Imam Abu Hanifa, Imam Abu Yusuf, Imam Muhammad (Aima Thalasa of Ahnaf) | Elbows and ankle bones **must** be included; not washing them invalidates wudu |
| Imam Zufar Rahimullah (Hanafi) | Elbows and ankle bones **not** required; washing up to them is sufficient |
| Other madhahib | Various positions; some lean toward exclusion |

### On Minimum Masa of the Head

| Scholar/School | Position |
|---|---|
| Ahnaf (Imam Abu Hanifa, Abu Yusuf, Muhammad) | One-fourth (rub') of the head — top portion — is the minimum fard |
| Imam al-Shafi'i Rahimullah | Three hairs anywhere on the head suffice |
| Imam Malik Rahimullah | Masa of the **entire head** is obligatory |

### On Why Imam Quduri Began with Kitab al-Tahara

Scholars note that Imam Quduri began with tahara because:
1. The first obligation on a Muslim after accepting Islam is salah.
2. Salah requires valid wudu.
3. Wudu is performed at least five times daily, making it the most frequently performed act of worship.
4. Beginning with Bismillah and an ayah of Quran is a means of barakah and indicates the author's intention for acceptance of his work.

## Lessons & Practical Application

1. **Never label something haram without definitive dalil.** Declaring something haram requires proof from Quran, Sunnah, Ijma, or Qiyas. Calling things haram carelessly is itself a serious matter in the deen.

2. **Wash the elbows and ankle bones.** The mufta bihi (operative) ruling in the Hanafi madhab is that the elbows and ankle bones must be included in wudu. If uncertain whether the elbow was washed, go back and wash it — the rest of the wudu need not be restarted if minimal time has passed.

3. **Wiping one-fourth of the head fulfills the fard.** The minimum for masa of the head in the Hanafi madhab is the top portion of the head (approximately one-fourth). Wiping the full head is sunnah and should be done whenever possible.

4. **Do not waste water in wudu.** Even if standing in a river, one should not wash limbs more times than the sunnah requires. When performing a voluntary (mustahab) wudu on top of an existing valid wudu, perform only the fara'id to conserve water and still receive the reward.

5. **Wudu on top of existing wudu:** If wudu is still intact and one wishes to renew it for extra reward, it is sufficient to perform only the fara'id. The sunnah amount (washing three times) is for wudu that has become obligatory (e.g., after hadas).

6. **Sleep in the state of wudu.** A person who dies in the state of wudu is considered a shaheed. It takes only two minutes. If wudu breaks immediately after making it, one is still considered to have made the intention and the reward is not lost. Making the intention alone has value in the sight of Allah.

7. **Staying in wudu brings barakah.** The story of Bilal (radi Allahu anhu) — Rasulullah ﷺ heard his footsteps in Jannah and asked his secret; Bilal said he renews wudu every time it breaks and prays two raka'at. Staying in wudu makes good deeds easier, protects from sin, and brings barakah in one's rizq (sustenance).

8. **Merits of wudu and light on the Day of Judgment.** Rasulullah ﷺ said that the Muslim Ummah will be recognized on the Day of Judgment by the light (ghurra) on the parts of the body washed in wudu — like the white markings on a dark horse. Whoever wishes to increase that light should do more wudu.
