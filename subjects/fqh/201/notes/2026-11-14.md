## Session Overview

This session completed Bab al-Janazah (Chapter on the Deceased) from Imam al-Quduri's *Mukhtasar*. The chapter spans the entire process from the moment a person is on the verge of death through burial. Topics covered include: actions at the time of death, ghusl of the deceased, kafan (shrouding) for men and women, Salatul Janazah — including who leads it and its method — carrying the janazah, burial rites, and rulings on stillborn children. The teacher noted the goal is to finish Kitab al-Salah by Wednesday and begin Kitab al-Zakat.

---

## Key Themes

1. **Preparing the dying person** — turning toward the qibla and reminding them of the Shahada
2. **Immediate post-death actions** — closing the eyes and tying the jaw
3. **Ghusl al-Mayyit** — the specific method and conditions for washing the deceased, including differences from living person's ghusl
4. **Kafan** — the masnoon (recommended) and kifaya (minimum) shrouding for men and women, materials, and wrapping method
5. **Salatul Janazah** — the order of precedence for who leads, the structure of the prayer (four takbirat), and when it may be repeated or performed at the grave
6. **Carrying and burial** — proper conduct when transporting the body and placing it in the grave
7. **Rulings on infants** — distinction between a live birth and stillbirth with respect to ghusl and janazah

## Fiqh Rulings & Evidence

### At the Time of Death
- **Ruling:** Turn the dying person to face the Qibla — the masnoon position is on their right side, face toward the Qibla. If that is impossible, laying flat on the back is permissible.
- **Ruling:** Remind the dying person to say the Shahada (Shahadatayn). Do not force it; say it yourself once or twice nearby. Forcing can cause the person in the agony of death to deny it, which is spiritually dangerous.
- **Sleeping positions:** Best to begin sleep on the right side. Sleeping on the stomach is makrooh; acceptable if necessary due to pain.

### Immediately After Death
- **Ruling:** Close the eyes of the deceased — **Dalil:** Hadith of Umm Salama (RA) that Rasulullah ﷺ closed the eyes of Abu Salama (RA) upon his death and said: *"When the soul is seized and leaves the body, the sight follows it."*
- **Ruling:** Tie the jaw shut with a cloth immediately after death, before rigor mortis sets in. The body stiffens once blood flow stops and warmth leaves — this must be done within approximately 30 minutes.
- **Ruling:** Do not comb the hair, beard, or cut the nails of the deceased — **Dalil:** Statement of Aisha (RA): "Why do you pull the forelock of your deceased?"

### Ghusl of the Deceased
- **Ruling:** The body is placed on a raised plank (*takhta*) with drainage underneath so water does not pool.
- **Ruling:** Cover the private parts (*satr*) before removing the clothing — the actual private parts (not the full *awra*) must remain covered throughout.
- **Ruling:** Vudu is performed first, but gargling and inserting water into the nose are **omitted** (unlike the living person's ghusl) to prevent water entering the body internally.
- **Ruling:** Recommended to burn incense (luban/bakhur) under the plank during the washing.
- **Ruling:** Water may be mixed with Sidr (lote leaves) or Khitmi (saltwort) to increase cleaning power. If unavailable, plain water suffices.
- **Ruling:** The body is washed **left side first**, then right side — the opposite of the living person's ghusl. This matches the tarteeb (order) of the kafan wrapping.
- **Ruling:** The body is raised to a sitting position very gently and the abdomen wiped slowly. If anything exits the body, it is cleaned, but the ghusl is **not restarted** from the beginning.
- **Ruling:** After ghusl, dry the body completely with a clean cloth before placing it on the kafan. Drying prevents the kafan from becoming wet.
- **Ruling (Hanafi):** Clothes of the deceased are removed before ghusl; covering the private parts with a cloth is sufficient.
- **Dalil for Hanafi position vs. Imam Shafi'i:** Imam Shafi'i holds the Prophet ﷺ was washed over his shirt (Kamees), citing Aisha (RA). Hanafi response: This was specific to the Prophet ﷺ (a khasiyyah), not a general ruling. The Sahaba did not continue this practice on others, which confirms it was not intended as a universal Sunnah.
- **Hanut/Camphor:** Applied to the head, beard, and the seven bones of sajda after drying — **Dalil:** Hadith of Umm Atiyya (RA) that Rasulullah ﷺ, at the ghusl of his daughter Zaynab (RA), instructed: *"Wash her three times or five times, then put camphor."* Also narrated from Ali (RA) pointing to the use of hanut.

### Tajmir (Perfuming the Shroud with Smoke)
- **Ruling:** The kafan is perfumed with incense smoke in **odd numbers** (three times) before the body is placed inside — **Dalil:** Rasulullah ﷺ ordered it in odd numbers at the ghusl of his daughter.

### Kafan (Shrouding)

**For Men — Kafan Masnoon (Sunnah): 3 pieces**
1. Izar (head-to-toe wrap)
2. Qamees (shirt with diamond head opening)
3. Lifafa (outermost envelope wrap)

**Dalil:** Hadith of Aisha (RA): *"The Prophet ﷺ was shrouded in three white Sahuliyya cloths."* Also, Jabir ibn Sumra (RA) narrated the Prophet ﷺ was shrouded in three: Qamees, Izar, and Lifafa of Sahuliyya. (Sahuliyya = a village in Yemen known for white cloth.)

**For Men — Kafan Kifaya (Minimum): 2 pieces**
- Izar + Lifafa (Qamees may be omitted)
- **Dalil:** Abu Bakr (RA) instructed his family to use the two sheets he owned: *"The living need clothing more than the dead."* (Narrated in Binaya — a commentary on Hidaya.)

**For Women — Kafan Masnoon (Sunnah): 5 pieces**
1. Qamees
2. Izar
3. Khirqah (chest band)
4. Khimar (head covering)
5. Lifafa

**Dalil:** Hadith of Umm Atiyya (RA): When the daughter of Rasulullah ﷺ, Zaynab (RA), passed away, he gave five pieces of cloth and instructed they be placed on her.

**For Women — Kafan Kifaya (Minimum): 3 pieces**
- Izar + Khimar + Lifafa (Qamees and Khirqah may be omitted)
- The Khimar is never omitted even at minimum — this underscores the importance of head covering for women, even in death.

### Salatul Janazah
- **Ruling:** The prayer is performed **outside the masjid** — it is not prayed inside the masjid where the five daily prayers are held (Imam Quduri's text).
- **Ruling:** The Imam stands at the level of the **chest** (*sadr*) of the deceased.
- **Ruling:** Hands are raised only for the **first takbir** — no raising of hands for subsequent takbirat. (Hanafi position; other madhabs differ.)
- **Ruling:** Four takbirat total. After the fourth, the Imam says salam and the muqtadi (followers) follow.
- **Ruling on 7 takbirat:** A hadith of Ibn Haytham (RA) from Umar (RA) mentions seven takbirat were done for the Shuhada of Badr. However, in his later practice, Rasulullah ﷺ consistently prayed with four takbirat — this is the relied-upon position (mu'tamad).
- **Ruling:** Salatul Janazah may be **repeated** only if the Wali requests it after a Sultan or local Imam led it. Once the Wali himself has led it, it cannot be repeated.
- **Ruling:** If a person was buried without Salatul Janazah, it may be performed **at the grave for up to three days** — provided the body has not begun to swell and disintegrate.
- **Dalil for three-day limit:** Rasulullah ﷺ prayed at the grave of an Ansari woman who used to clean the masjid; he had not been informed of her death at the time of burial (the grave where she is buried near Masjid al-Qaba is visited during Umra).
- **Relied-upon opinion (Hanafi):** Imam Abu Yusuf (RA) — Salatul Janazah may be prayed up to three days, not after.
- **Body lost at sea:** The body is lowered into the water (facing Qibla); those on the ship pray Salatul Janazah. If no Muslims were present and three days have not passed, family may pray when informed.
- **Minimum number for Salatul Janazah:** No specific minimum number of people is stipulated, though rows should be in odd numbers.
- **Ruling on woman's janazah:** The father of the deceased woman takes precedence over her son in leading Salatul Janazah — this is the opposite of the nikah wali order (where the son has precedence over the father).

### Carrying the Body
- **Ruling:** The body is carried on a *sareer* (bier) held from **all four corners** (*qawa'im al-arba'*).
- **Ruling:** Walk **quickly but do not run** (*muthri'een duna al-khabab*).
- **Ruling:** It is makrooh for people to sit down at the graveside before the body has been placed in the grave.

### Burial
- **Ruling:** The preferred type of grave is the **lahd** — a niche carved into the side of the grave wall toward the qibla.
- **Ruling:** When lowering the body into the lahd, the person doing so says: *"Bismillahi wa ala millati Rasulillah."*
- **Ruling:** The body is turned to face the Qibla; the coffin ties are loosened inside the grave.
- **Ruling:** The lahd is sealed with **labin** (unbaked mud bricks). It is makrooh to use ajurr (baked bricks) or well-finished, cut wooden planks inside. Rough wood pieces are acceptable.
- **Ruling:** The grave mound should be slightly raised — not flat, and not raised excessively like a camel's hump.

### Rulings on Infants
- **Ruling:** If a child was born alive and showed signs of life (cried, was named), ghusl, kafan, and Salatul Janazah are all performed even if the child died minutes later.
- **Ruling:** If it was a **stillbirth** (no signs of life, did not cry), the child is wrapped in kafan but there is **no ghusl** and **no Salatul Janazah**.

## Conditions & Categories

### Wrapping Order (Tarteeb al-Kafan)

**For Men:**
1. Lay Lifafa flat on the table first
2. Lay Izar on top of Lifafa
3. Lay Qamees on top of Izar (Qamees has a diamond-shaped head opening — lay it flat, unfolded)
4. Place the bathed, dried body onto the Qamees
5. Fold the Qamees over the body (head comes through the opening)
6. Wrap Izar: left side first, then right over left
7. Wrap Lifafa: left side first, then right over left
8. Tie only if necessary (body too large or kafan too small and there is risk of it opening)

**For Women:**
1. Lay Lifafa flat on the table
2. Lay Izar on top
3. Place Khirqah approximately where the chest will be (on top of Izar, before Qamees)
4. Lay Qamees on top
5. Place the body on the Qamees; a cloth is also placed where the hair will rest
6. Fold the Qamees over the body (head through the opening)
7. Divide the hair into two, bring forward, place on the chest
8. Place the Khimar over the head
9. Secure the Khirqah over the chest area
10. Wrap Izar: left side first, then right over left
11. Wrap Lifafa: left side first, then right over left

> **Important correction noted in class:** Some videos and demonstrations show wrapping from right-to-left first. This is incorrect per the Hanafi madhab (or may reflect another madhab's practice). The correct Hanafi order is **left side first, then right over left**, both in ghusl and in kafan wrapping.

### Structure of Salatul Janazah (Four Takbirat)

| Takbir | Action After It |
|---|---|
| 1st Takbir (hands raised) | Recite Thana (praise of Allah) |
| 2nd Takbir (no hand raising) | Recite Salawat on Rasulullah ﷺ |
| 3rd Takbir (no hand raising) | Make du'a for the deceased and all Muslims |
| 4th Takbir (no hand raising) | Imam says salam; followers follow |

### Precedence for Leading Salatul Janazah (Order)
1. Sultan (ruler/appointed authority) — if present
2. Imam al-Hayy (imam of that locality/masjid) — if sultan is absent
3. Wali of the deceased (legal guardian, based on the tarteeb of wali in Kitab al-Nikah)
   - Exception: for a deceased woman, her **father** takes precedence over her **son**

## Scholarly Opinions

| Issue | Hanafi Position | Other Position |
|---|---|---|
| **Clothing during ghusl** | Clothing is removed; private parts covered with a cloth | Imam Shafi'i: Clothing remains on; washed over the shirt — based on how the Prophet ﷺ was washed. Hanafi response: This was specific (khas) to the Prophet ﷺ, not for the Ummah. |
| **Covering during ghusl (awra)** | Only the actual private parts need to be covered (not the full awra from navel to knee) | Shafi'i: The full awra should be covered |
| **Maximum takbirat in Salatul Janazah** | Four takbirat (relied-upon, mu'tamad) based on Rasulullah ﷺ's later practice | Some narrations indicate seven takbirat (done for Shuhada of Badr); a minority view |
| **Time limit for grave-side janazah** | Imam Abu Yusuf (RA): up to three days (mu'tamad Hanafi position) | Sahib al-Hidaya: based on when the body begins to swell/disintegrate (place-dependent) |
| **Bricks for sealing the lahd** | Labin (unbaked mud bricks) preferred; ajurr (baked) and well-cut wood planks are makrooh inside | — |

## Lessons & Practical Application

1. **Know the full process.** These rulings are not everyday occurrences but every Muslim should know them. If a family member is in their final moments, knowing the ahkam is essential. Ignorance at that moment can lead to incorrect actions.

2. **At the time of death: be gentle, not forceful.** A dying person is in extreme pain and may be in a semi-conscious state. Reminding them of the Shahada once or twice by saying it aloud nearby is better than forcing them to repeat it.

3. **Tie the jaw and close the eyes promptly.** These actions must be done immediately after death — within roughly 30 minutes — before rigor mortis makes it impossible.

4. **The living need clothing more than the dead.** Abu Bakr (RA)'s instruction to use his existing two sheets as his kafan is a powerful reminder that the material quality of the kafan is spiritually irrelevant. Once placed in the grave, what matters is one's deeds (*amal*), not one's shroud.

5. **Covering the head in death reflects its importance in life.** The bare minimum kafan for a woman (Kafan Kifaya) always includes the Khimar. If a woman's head must be covered even after death, it is a reflection of how essential the hijab is during life.

6. **The wrapping direction matters — correct a common mistake.** Many demonstrations (and some madhabs) wrap right-to-left. The Hanafi order is left-side first in both ghusl and kafan. If you witness an incorrect wrapping, it is a responsibility to correct it.

7. **Encourage participation in ghusl al-mayyit.** The teacher strongly encouraged students to participate in washing a deceased person if the opportunity arises — it is a profound experience that revives iman and reminds one of the akhirah.

8. **Know who leads the janazah.** Proper adherence to the order of precedence (Sultan → Imam → Wali) is a Sunnah, as demonstrated by Hussain (RA) yielding the imamat to the Amir of Medina at the janazah of Hassan (RA), saying: *"If this were not the Sunnah, I would never let you lead."*

9. **Seek out correct instructional resources.** The teacher committed to sharing a correct video demonstrating the kafan wrapping method, noting that widely available videos often contain madhab errors.
