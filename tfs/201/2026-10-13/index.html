    Tafseer 201 - Study Notes

    Date: 2026-10-13 Surah Covered: Surah Al-Baqarah — Ayat 78–79 (Tafseer of the illiterate followers of the Jews and the crime of their misleading scholars)

    Session Overview

    This session continued the tafseer of Surah Al-Baqarah from page 71 of the tafseer text, focusing on Ayat 78–79. The session examined two connected groups among the Children of Israel: (1) the illiterate masses (ummiyyun) who blindly followed their scholars with no knowledge of the Torah, relying only on false wishful thinking instilled by their rabbis; and (2) the corrupt scholars themselves who deliberately distorted the Torah for worldly gain and then attributed their fabrications to Allah. The session also included substantial sarf (morphology) and nahw (grammar) exercises on two key verbs from the ayat: massa/yamassu and ittakhaza/yattakhizu.

    Key Themes and Topics

    1. The illiterate masses among the Jews (al-ummiyyun) — their lack of knowledge of the Torah and their dependence on fabricated wishful thinking from their rabbis.
    2. False beliefs instilled by the rabbis — that Allah would certainly pardon them, that the hellfire would only touch them for a few days, that their prophets would intercede for them, and that they were the sons and beloved of Allah.
    3. The crime of the corrupt scholars (Ayah 79) — deliberate distortion (tahrif) of the Torah with their own hands, attributing the fabrication to Allah, and earning forbidden (haram) income through this deception.
    4. The double crime — combining tahrif al-kitab (distorting the Book) with al-kidhb 'ala Allah (lying against Allah).
    5. Rhetorical challenge to the Jews — Allah commands the Prophet ﷺ to ask them whether they have taken a covenant with Allah to support their false claims.
    6. Morphology exercise: massa/yamassu — identifying the sighah, bab, fa'il, maf'ul bihi in lan tamassana al-naru.
    7. Morphology exercise: ittakhaza/yattakhidhu — identifying the bab (Bab Ifti'al), the hamzah al-istifham, and hamzah al-wasl in attakhaztum.

    Main Tafseer Points and Explanations

    Ayah 78 — The Illiterate Masses (al-Ummiyyun)

    • After mentioning the corrupt Jewish scholars who distorted the Torah, Allah now turns to describe their followers — the illiterate masses.
    • وَمِنْهُمْ أُمِّيُّونَ لَا يَعْلَمُونَ الْكِتَابَ إِلَّا أَمَانِيَّ وَإِنْ هُمْ إِلَّا يَظُنُّونَ
    • Ummiyyun here refers to a group from among the Jews who were juhhala al-'awam — ignorant members of the public who could neither read nor write, and therefore could not verify the contents of the Torah themselves.
    • They had no knowledge of the actual Tawrat; all they possessed were amaniyy — wishful thinkings and false hopes given to them by their rabbis, such as:
    • Allah will certainly pardon them and have special mercy on them.
    • The hellfire will not touch them except for a few days (lan tamassana al-naru illa ayyaman ma'dudah).
    • Their prophets (forefathers/ancestors) will intercede for them on the Day of Judgment.
    • They are the sons (abna') and beloved (ahibba') of Allah.
    • وَإِنْ هُمْ إِلَّا يَظُنُّونَ — The in here is nafiyyah (meaning laysa/la). They have no certainty (yaqin) in their religion. Rather, they follow their forefathers in blind imitation (taqlid a'ma) — like a blind or foolish person follows their elders without understanding.

    Ayah 79 — The Crime of the Corrupt Scholars

    • فَوَيْلٌ لِلَّذِينَ يَكْتُبُونَ الْكِتَابَ بِأَيْدِيهِمْ — Woe to those who write the book with their own hands.
    • Yaktubuna al-kitab here means yuharrifuna al-kitab — they distort the book with their own hands.
    • Meaning: halaak wa 'adhab (destruction and torment) for those who distorted the Torah.
    • They wrote the distorted verses with their own hands and added them to the Torah.
    • ثُمَّ يَقُولُونَ هَذَا مِنْ عِنْدِ اللَّهِ — Then they told their illiterate followers: "What you find in this book is from the original Torah that was revealed to Musa ﷺ by Allah" — even though they wrote it with their own hands. This is a false attribution (nasabaha ila Allah kadhiban wa zhuran).
    • Motivation: They did this only to gain worldly profit — li-yashtaru bihi thamanan qalilan — for a paltry, contemptible price of this world.
    • وَيْلٌ لَهُمْ مِمَّا كَتَبَتْ أَيْدِيهِمْ — Severe punishment for what their hands have written (i.e., the act of distortion).
    • وَوَيْلٌ لَهُمْ مِمَّا يَكْسِبُونَ — And woe to them for what they earn from it — the haram and forbidden income (al-haram wal-suht) received from the public as a result of this deception.

    Double Crime of the Corrupt Scholars

    The mufassir highlights that these scholars committed two grave sins simultaneously: 1. تَحْرِيفُ كَلَامِ اللَّهِ — Distorting the word of Allah in the Tawrat. 2. الْكِذْبُ وَالْبُهْتَانُ عَلَى اللَّهِ — Lying against and slandering Allah by attributing their own fabrications to Him.

    The Rhetorical Challenge (End of Ayah 79 / Transition)

    • Allah commands the Prophet ﷺ: قُلْ — Say, O Muhammad ﷺ, to those who claim the fire will only touch them for a few days:
    • أَتَّخَذْتُمْ عِنْدَ اللَّهِ عَهْدًا — Have you taken a covenant with Allah (to this effect)?
    • This is not a genuine question but a rebuke — 'ala sabili al-inkar wal-tawbikh — a rhetorical scolding that exposes the falsity of their claims.
    • فَلَنْ يُخْلِفَ اللَّهُ عَهْدَهُ — If Allah had truly made such a promise, He would not break it (because Allah never breaks His promise). But since no such promise was made, their claim is a lie.

    Grammar and Language Notes

    Verb 1: مَسَّ يَمَسُّ (to touch)

    • Bab: Fa'ala — Yaf'alu (فَعَلَ — يَفْعُلُ), samia form — though the sheikh noted it is mostly used in the fatha-damma pattern.
    • Madi: مَسَّ — Massani — Massa — Massataa — Massuu — Massna
    • Mudari': يَمَسُّ — يَمَسَّانِ — يَمَسُّونَ — تَمَسُّ — تَمَسَّانِ — يَمَسْسْنَ

    Analysis of lan tamassana al-naru: - Original form: yamassu → becomes tamassu because the fa'il (al-naru) is mu'annas (feminine); Allah uses the feminine sighah for the subject. - Lan makes the fi'l mudari' mansub, so tamassu → tamassa. - Na after tamassa is maf'ul bihi (pronoun: "us"), not part of the sighah. - Al-naru is the fa'il (subject). - Full i'rab: lan (nafia + nasib) + tamassa (fi'l mudari' mansub, sighah mu'annas ghayibah) + na (maf'ul bihi, damir) + al-naru (fa'il marfu'). - Students are assigned to write the full conjugation (madi + mudari') of massa/yamassu in their notebooks.

    Verb 2: اِتَّخَذَ يَتَّخِذُ (to take / to make) — Bab Ifti'al

    • Bab: Ifti'al (اِفْتَعَلَ — يَفْتَعِلُ)
    • Original root: akhaza ya'khudhu (أَخَذَ يَأْخُذُ) → moved to Bab Ifti'al → ittakhaza yattakhidhu ittikhadhan
    • Madi: اِتَّخَذَ — اِتَّخَذَا — اِتَّخَذُوا — اِتَّخَذَتْ — اِتَّخَذَتَا — اِتَّخَذْنَ
    • Mudari': يَتَّخِذُ — يَتَّخِذَانِ — يَتَّخِذُونَ — تَتَّخِذُ — تَتَّخِذَانِ — يَتَّخِذْنَ

    Analysis of attakhaztum: - The alif at the beginning is hamzah al-istifham (interrogative hamzah), not part of the verb. - What remains — ittakhaztum — is the verb in Bab Ifti'al. - Originally ittakhaztum begins with hamzah al-wasl, which is dropped when the hamzah al-istifham precedes it. - Result: أَتَّخَذْتُمْ — the hamzah al-wasl of ittakhaztum merges/drops after the interrogative hamzah. - Sighah: Jama' al-mudhakkar al-mukhatab (plural masculine, second person) — "you (all) have taken." - I'rab of the full phrase: a (istifham) + ttakhaztum (fi'l madi, fa'il = antum) + 'inda Allah (jar-majrur) + 'ahdan (maf'ul bihi mansub). - Students assigned: write full madi, mudari', amr, and nahi conjugations of ittakhaza from Bab Ifti'al in notebooks and share via WhatsApp.

    General Morphology Reminder from the Sheikh

    • All verbs appearing in the Qur'anic ayat must be noted in the notebook with full conjugations.
    • Students must be able to identify: which sighah, which bab, where is the fa'il, maf'ul bihi.
    • Failure to know the morphology leads to misunderstanding the meaning of the Quran — just as the illiterate followers of the Torah could not verify what their scholars told them.

    Lessons and Practical Takeaways

    1. Scholars bear a greater responsibility — Those who possess knowledge and misuse it to mislead the public face a double punishment: for the distortion itself (mimma katabat aydihim) and for the forbidden earnings they gain from it (mimma yaksibun).

    2. Blind following (taqlid a'ma) is a spiritual danger — The ummiyyun among the Jews are criticized not merely for being illiterate, but for accepting false claims without any verification. Muslims must seek knowledge to verify what they are told about the Deen.

    3. Wishful thinking is not a substitute for knowledge — The amaniyy (false hopes: "we are sons of Allah," "hellfire won't touch us") gave the masses a false sense of security. Religious certainty (yaqin) must be grounded in the actual Book of Allah and authentic scholarship.

    4. Attributing falsehood to Allah is among the gravest sins — Combining distortion of scripture with kidhb 'ala Allah (lying against Allah) is an enormity. Statements about what Allah has or has not promised must be traceable to revelation.

    5. The Quran challenges false certainty — The rhetorical question attakhaztum 'inda Allah 'ahdan is a model for how to dismantle false religious claims: demand evidence of a divine covenant, and when none exists, the claim collapses.

    6. Morphology is an act of worship — The sheikh emphasized that mastering the sarf of Quranic verbs is not merely academic; it protects the student from misunderstanding the Quran, just as ignorance of the Torah led its followers to accept distortions.

    Historical Context and Stories Mentioned

    • Worship of the Calf (Ibadah al-'Ijl): The claim that the hellfire would only touch them for ayyaman ma'dudah (a few days) was interpreted by the mufassir as referring to the period during which the Israelites worshipped the calf while Musa ﷺ was at Mount Tur (Sinai). This was a recognized grave sin, and they rationalized their punishment would be limited to that episode alone.

    • The 7,000-year calculation: Another interpretation of ayyaman ma'dudah mentioned in the tafseer: they believed the age of this world is 7,000 years, and that for each 1,000 years only one day of punishment applies — totaling only seven days in Hellfire. This was a fabricated theological position with no basis in revelation.

    • Descriptions of the Prophet ﷺ in the Torah: The sheikh noted that the distortion of the Torah was not a one-time historical event but an ongoing pattern, including during the time of the Prophet ﷺ himself — particularly the distortion of the descriptions and attributes (awsaf) of Muhammad ﷺ that were present in the original Tawrat.

    • Context of surrounding ayat: A student asked about the 70 men who went up the mountain with Musa ﷺ and were subsequently killed and resurrected.     Morphology Q&A (in-class):

    Q: What is the sighah of tamassana in lan tamassana al-naru? A: This is from massa/yamassu (Bab Fa'ala-Yaf'alu). The sighah is tamassu — third person singular feminine (mu'annath ghayibah) — because the fa'il (al-naru) is mu'annas. Lan makes it tamassa (mansub). Na is maf'ul bihi (pronoun "us"), not part of the sighah.

    Q: What is the sighah and bab of attakhaztum? A: The alif is hamzah al-istifham. The verb is ittakhaztum from Bab Ifti'al, originally from akhaza. The sighah is jama' al-mukhattab (antum). The hamzah al-wasl of ittakhaztum is dropped after the interrogative hamzah, giving attakhaztum.
