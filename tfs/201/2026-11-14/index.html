    Tafseer 201 - Study Notes

    Date: 2026-11-14 Surah Covered: Surah Al-Baqarah, Ayat 99–103 (Page 82)

    Session Overview

    This session covered Surah Al-Baqarah ayat 99–103, focusing on the nature of the Jews (Bani Isra'eel) in their historical relationship with the books and messengers of Allah. The teacher explained the connection (munasabah) between this passage and the preceding ayat, then gave a detailed tafseer of the story of Sulaiman alayhis salam and the false attribution of black magic to him, the sending of the two angels Harut and Marut to Babylon, and the distinction between miracles (mu'jizah) and sorcery (sihr). The session closes with the sababun nuzul (reason for revelation) of the ayah clearing Sulaiman alayhis salam of all association with magic.

    Key Themes and Topics

    1. Munasabah (Connection to Previous Ayat): The preceding passage described the ingrained traits of the Jews — evil hearts, breaking covenants, denying messengers, and showing enmity toward Jibreel alayhis salam. This passage continues by exposing their adoption of sorcery in place of the Book of Allah.

    2. Comfort to the Prophet ﷺ: Allah reassures Prophet Muhammad ﷺ that the Jews' rejection of him follows the same pattern they used with all messengers — none of this is new.

    3. The Jews' Abandonment of the Torah: Rather than following the Torah — which contained clear prophecies of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ — the Jewish rabbis and scholars threw it behind their backs and replaced it with satanic literature and black magic.

    4. False Attribution to Sulaiman alayhis salam: The Jews falsely claimed that Sulaiman's kingdom was built on black magic. Allah refutes this clearly.

    5. Harut and Marut: Two angels were sent by Allah to Babylon as a test (fitnah) for the people — to teach magic only so as to distinguish it from divine miracles, not to spread it.

    6. Mu'jizah vs. Sihr: A core theological distinction: miracles are exclusively from Allah, while magic is a human craft.

    7. Consequences of Sihr: Whoever practices or trades in black magic will have no share in the Hereafter.

    Main Tafseer Points and Explanations

    Ayah 99 — Clear Verses Sent to the Prophet ﷺ

    "We have sent down to you clear verses (ayatin bayyinat) that prove your prophethood. None deny them except the fasiqoon."

    • Allah sent clear, unambiguous ayat to Prophet Muhammad ﷺ as proof of his prophethood.
    • Whoever denies them is not doing so out of genuine doubt but out of transgression and rebellion (fisq).
    • الْخَارِجُونَ عَنِ الطَّاعَةِ — those who have stepped outside obedience to Allah.

    Ayah 100 — The Jews' Pattern of Breaking Covenants

    "Whenever they made a covenant, a group of them cast it aside."

    • This describes a recurring, historical pattern: every time Bani Isra'eel made a covenant with Allah or His messengers, a faction broke it.
    • بَلْ أَكْثَرُهُمْ لَا يُؤْمِنُونَ — most of them do not believe in the Torah with true, sincere faith, which is why covenant-breaking is natural for them.

    Ayah 101 — Rejecting the Prophet ﷺ Who Confirmed Their Book

    • When Prophet Muhammad ﷺ came confirming the Torah (not the altered version, but the original), and confirming the prophethood of Musa alayhis salam, a party of the People of the Book threw the Book of Allah behind their backs.
    • The specific reason: لِأَنَّهَا تَدُلُّ عَلَىٰ نُبُوَّةِ مُحَمَّدٍ ﷺ — the Torah contained clear prophecies of the coming of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, and the rabbis suppressed it deliberately.
    • They denied (jahadoo) and insisted on rejecting his prophethood as if they knew nothing of the evidence in their own scripture.

    Ayah 102 — The Story of Sulaiman, Harut, and Marut

    Three actions of the Jews described: 1. They threw away the Book of Allah (the Torah) behind their backs. 2. They completely denied Prophet Muhammad ﷺ. 3. They followed what the devils used to recite during the reign of Sulaiman alayhis salam — i.e., the books and methods of black magic.

    Clearing Sulaiman alayhis salam:

    "Sulaiman did not disbelieve (ma kafara Sulayman), but the devils disbelieved — they taught people magic."

    • The Jews spread the slander that Sulaiman's kingdom was built on black magic, not divine miracles.
    • Allah refutes this completely: مَا كَفَرَ سُلَيْمَانُ — Sulaiman was entirely innocent of magic. It was the shayateen who taught people sihr.
    • The gifts given to Sulaiman alayhis salam were pure divine miracles (mu'jizah):
    • Winds subjugated to him — travel that took a month done in hours (like a wooden aircraft)
    • Jinn subjugated to him by Allah's command
    • Knowledge of the languages of animals, birds, and insects
    • A vast kingdom over jinn, humans, animals, and birds

    Harut and Marut — the Two Angels of Babylon: - Black magic was widespread in Babylonian communities (in the area of Kufa, Iraq). - Allah sent two angels — Harut and Marut — specifically to that area as a test (fitnah) for the people. - Their mission: to demonstrate the difference between sihr (magic, a human craft) and mu'jizah (a divine miracle). - Every time they taught anyone magic, they gave this warning: "We are only a test from Allah — do not disbelieve (فَلَا تَكْفُرْ)." - Despite the warning, people began learning it en masse.

    Two categories of learners: 1. Those who learned it to remove harm (e.g., to treat someone afflicted by magic) — they were saved. 2. Those who learned it to cause harm to others — they went astray and were destroyed.

    The most common harmful use:

    "They learned from them (the angels) what causes separation between a man and his wife." - Using magic to cause discord, hatred, and divorce between spouses became widespread in Babylon.

    An important caveat:

    "They could not harm anyone with it except by Allah's permission." - Black magic has no independent power. Any harm it causes is only by Allah's will and permission.

    The ultimate loss: - The Jews knew full well that whoever makes a profession/trade out of black magic has no share in the Hereafter. - They preferred sihr over the Book of Allah — آثَرُوا السِّحْرَ عَلَىٰ كِتَابِ اللَّهِ — and thus sold themselves for the worst possible bargain.

    Ayah 103 — The Better Alternative

    "If they had believed and had taqwa, the reward from Allah would have been far better — if only they knew."

    • Had the magicians and those who followed this path believed in Allah and feared His punishment, Allah would have given them a reward far superior to any worldly gain from their profession of magic.

    Grammar and Language Notes

    • مَا in مَا كَفَرَ سُلَيْمَانُ — this is maa an-nafiyah (the negating maa), giving a complete and emphatic negation: Sulaiman absolutely did not disbelieve.
    • نَبَذَ — يَنْبِذُ — نَبْذًا: root meaning is to throw something away with force and contempt; used to describe how they discarded the Book of Allah.
    • اشْتَرَى — يَشْتَرِي — شِرَاءً: means both to buy and to sell; used metaphorically here for trading away one's soul for black magic.
    • أَصَرَّ — يُصِرُّ — إِصْرَارًا: to insist, persist — used for their stubborn denial of the Prophet's prophethood.
    • تَتْلُو: from تَلَا — يَتْلُو — to recite; used here for what the shayateen used to recite (their satanic scriptures of magic).
    • مَثُوبَة: reward or good place — from the same root as ثَوَاب (spiritual reward).

    Lessons and Practical Takeaways

    1. Clear proofs do not benefit the stubborn. The ayat sent to Prophet Muhammad ﷺ were unambiguous — yet the fasiqoon still denied. Rejection of truth often stems from arrogance and vested interest, not lack of evidence.

    2. History repeats itself with the enemies of truth. The pattern of breaking covenants, denying prophets, and following falsehood is a recurring trait documented across generations — recognizing this pattern provides comfort and clarity.

    3. Scholars bear a greater responsibility. The rabbis and scholars of the Jews actively hid the truth about Prophet Muhammad ﷺ despite knowing it. Those with knowledge who conceal it for worldly gain bear a heavier sin.

    4. Mu'jizah and sihr are fundamentally different. Miracles are exclusively from Allah — no human craft, effort, or training can produce them. Magic is a learnable human skill with real but limited effects, subject entirely to Allah's permission.

    5. Black magic is real but bounded. It can cause genuine harm, including separating spouses, but it cannot harm anyone except by Allah's leave. This should neither make one complacent nor paranoid — seek protection in Allah.

    6. Preferring falsehood over the Book of Allah is the ultimate losing trade. Those who abandon the Quran and Sunnah for other systems — whether ideological, philosophical, or superstitious — are making the same exchange the Jews of Babylon made.

    7. Belief and taqwa are the superior trade. The ayah closes by affirming: had these people simply believed and feared Allah, their reward would have been incomparably better. This is the formula for the best outcome in both worlds.

    Historical Context and Stories Mentioned

    • The Kingdom of Sulaiman alayhis salam: One of the most remarkable kingdoms in human history — winds subjugated to travel great distances rapidly, jinn and animals under his command, knowledge of animal and bird languages (referenced in Surah An-Naml and Surah Saba'), and extraordinary construction projects carried out by jinn.

    • Babylonian Sorcery (بَابِل — Babylon, near Kufa, Iraq): Black magic was rampant in the Babylonian civilization. It had spread so extensively that people were being harmed by it. This was the context into which Allah sent Harut and Marut.

    • Harut and Marut: Two angels sent specifically to Babylon (in the area of modern-day Kufa, Iraq) — not to spread magic but to demonstrate the distinction between divine miracle and human magic, and to test whether people would follow guidance or follow sorcery. Their teaching always came with the explicit warning not to use it for harm and not to fall into disbelief.

    • Sababun Nuzul of "مَا كَفَرَ سُلَيْمَانُ": When Prophet Muhammad ﷺ mentioned Sulaiman alayhis salam as a great messenger of Allah, some Jewish rabbis and leaders came to their people and said mockingly: "Do you not find it amazing what Muhammad claims — that the son of Dawood was a prophet? He was nothing but a magician!" In response, Allah revealed these ayat defending Sulaiman alayhis salam and clarifying the full context of magic in his era.

    Q&A Highlights

    • Q (implied via transcript): What was the purpose of Harut and Marut being sent if they were going to teach people magic? A: They were sent as a divine test (fitnah) — to separate those who would remain faithful to Allah's messengers from those who would pursue magic. Every lesson came with a clear warning not to use it harmfully and not to disbelieve. The angels' role was clarification and testing, not propagation.

    • Audio interruption noted during the section on people beginning to learn magic from the angels; the teacher confirmed the point upon reconnection: they began learning from Harut and Marut as a test, with the angels always issuing the caveat "فَلَا تَكْفُرْ" (do not disbelieve).
