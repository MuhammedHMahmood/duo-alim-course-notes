    Tafseer 201 - Study Notes

    Date: 2026-08-18 Surah Covered: Surah Al-Baqarah — Ayahs 7–20 (focus on Ayah 7 and Ayahs 8–20, Munafiqeen section)

    Session Overview

    This session covered the tafseer of Surah Al-Baqarah, beginning with Ayah 7 (خَتَمَ اللَّهُ عَلَى قُلُوبِهِمْ — "Allah has sealed their hearts") and transitioning into the opening ayahs of the Munafiqeen section (Ayahs 8–20). The sheikh explained the theological meaning of the "sealing of the hearts," the three categories of people introduced in the surah's opening, the definition and characteristics of the Munafiqeen, the Sabab al-Nuzul (occasion of revelation) for the Munafiqeen ayahs, and began the detailed tafseer of Ayah 8 (وَمِنَ النَّاسِ مَنْ يَقُولُ آمَنَّا بِاللَّهِ). The session concluded with a discussion of Ayah 9 (يُخَادِعُونَ اللَّهَ) before prayer time.

    Key Themes and Topics

    1. Khatm al-Qulub (Sealing of the Hearts) — the meaning, mechanism, and cause of Allah sealing the hearts, ears, and eyesights of the disbelievers.
    2. Three Categories of Humanity — Mu'mineen, Kafireen, and Munafiqeen as the structural framework of Surah Al-Baqarah's opening.
    3. Al-Munasabah (Contextual Transition) — how the surah moves from qualities of believers → qualities of disbelievers → qualities of hypocrites.
    4. Definition and Danger of Nifaq — who the Munafiqeen are, what makes them the most dangerous and most hated group.
    5. Sabab al-Nuzul — the historical occasion behind the Munafiqeen ayahs, specifically named individuals.
    6. Tafseer of Ayah 8 — the verbal declaration of faith by hypocrites versus the reality of their hearts.
    7. Tafseer of Ayah 9 — the act of deception (khida') attributed to the Munafiqeen toward Allah and the believers.

    Main Tafseer Points and Explanations

    Ayah 7 — خَتَمَ اللَّهُ عَلَى قُلُوبِهِمْ وَعَلَى سَمْعِهِمْ وَعَلَى أَبْصَارِهِمْ غِشَاوَةٌ وَلَهُمْ عَذَابٌ عَظِيمٌ

    "Allah has sealed their hearts and their hearing, and over their vision is a veil. And for them is a great punishment."

    The Meaning of Sealing (al-Khatm)

    • The mufassireen explain خَتَمَ as التَّغْطِيَة (covering) and الطَّبْع (imprinting/sealing).
    • This sealing occurs as a consequence of accumulated sins: when a heart is weighed down by many sins (الذُّنُوب), the نُورُ الْبَصِيرَة (light of inner insight and Iman) becomes obscured within it.
    • The result: there is no مَسْلَك (pathway or opening) for Iman to enter the heart, and no way for kufr to exit. Kufr is locked in; Iman is locked out.

    Why Were Their Hearts Sealed?

    • Another Quranic ayah (referenced without citation) states: "Allah sealed their hearts because of their kufr (disbelief)." Thus kufr is the root cause of the sealing — the sealing is Allah's response to their persistent rejection.

    The Three Sealed Faculties

    The sealing covers three things: 1. القلوب (Hearts) — sealed from following the truth 2. السمع (Hearing) — sealed from truly perceiving the call to guidance (دَاعِي الْفَلَاح) 3. الأبصار (Eyesight) — sealed from perceiving the نُورُ الْهِدَايَة (light of guidance)

    The mufassir (referenced in text) compared all three to a sealed vessel: a container sealed on all sides from which nothing can enter and nothing can exit.

    Important Clarification

    • This sealing is not a physical disease of the heart, ears, or eyes. Physically, these people are completely healthy and functional.
    • Despite full physical capacity, they refuse to accept the truth — as if their faculties are covered with thick veils (حُجُب كَثِيفَة).
    • This explanation employs Arabic rhetorical metaphor (majaz) to describe their spiritual condition.

    The Punishment

    • وَلَهُمْ عَذَابٌ عَظِيمٌ — They will have a severe and unending punishment in the hereafter.
    • Reasons: their كُفْر (disbelief), their إِجْرَام (crimes), and their تَكْذِيب بِآيَاتِ اللَّه (denial of Allah's verses).

    Al-Munasabah — Transition to the Munafiqeen Section (Ayahs 8–20)

    The structural logic of the surah's opening is: - Ayahs 1–5: Qualities of the Mu'mineen (believers) — the righteous - Ayahs 6–7: Qualities of the Kafireen (disbelievers) — those warned or unwarned - Ayahs 8–20: Qualities of the Munafiqeen (hypocrites) — the third and most dangerous group

    Allah devoted 13 full verses to the Munafiqeen — more than either other group — to highlight: 1. The great danger (خَطَر عَظِيم) they pose to the Muslim community 2. The great harm (ضَرَر كَبِير) they can inflict from within 3. Following the 13 verses, Allah gives two parables (مَثَلَان) to further clarify the nature of nifaq — to expose the inner darkness (ظُلْمَة) of their souls and the destination (مَآل) of their hypocrisy, which is destruction (هَلَاك وَدَمَار).

    Sabab al-Nuzul — Occasion of Revelation (Ayahs 8–20)

    • Reported by Ibn Abbas (رضي الله عنه): these verses were revealed about the hypocrites from Ahl al-Kitab (Jews and Christians who outwardly joined the Muslim community).
    • Named leaders of the Munafiqeen:
    • Abdullah ibn Ubayy ibn Salul — the most prominent leader of the hypocrites in Madinah
    • Mu'tab ibn Qushayr — another named leader
    • Their behavior: when meeting Muslims, they would openly declare belief in Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, in Allah, and in the Quran — even claiming to find the Prophet's description in their own scriptures — while concealing kufr in their hearts.

    Ayah 8 — وَمِنَ النَّاسِ مَنْ يَقُولُ آمَنَّا بِاللَّهِ وَبِالْيَوْمِ الْآخِرِ وَمَا هُم بِمُؤْمِنِينَ

    "And of the people are some who say, 'We believe in Allah and the Last Day,' but they are not believers."

    Tafseer Explanation

    • وَمِنَ النَّاسِ فَرِيقٌ — Among people there is a group (فَرِيق)
    • يَقُولُونَ بِأَلْسِنَتِهِمْ — who say with their tongues (not their hearts): صَدَّقْنَا بِاللَّه — "We have affirmed belief in Allah"
    • They also claim: وَبِمَا أُنزِلَ عَلَى رَسُولِهِ مِنَ الْآيَاتِ الْبَيِّنَات — belief in the clear verses revealed to the Prophet ﷺ
    • They claim: وَبِالْيَوْمِ الْآخِر — belief in the Last Day, meaning the البَعْث وَالنُّشُور (resurrection and revival)
    • But Allah declares: وَمَا هُمْ عَلَى الْحَقِيقَةِ بِمُصَدِّقِينَ وَلَا مُؤْمِنِينَ — in reality, they are neither sincere affirmers nor true believers
    • The reason: لِأَنَّهُمْ يَقُولُونَ ذَلِكَ قَوْلًا دُونَ اعْتِقَاد وَكَلَامًا دُونَ تَصْدِيق — they say these words as mere speech without inner conviction, and as mere utterance without sincere belief.

    Imam al-Baydawi's Commentary

    Imam al-Baydawi describes the Munafiqeen as: - الْقِسْم الثَّالِث الْمُزَبْزَب بَيْن الْقِسْمَيْن — the third category, wavering/confused between the two groups (neither fully Mu'min nor straightforward Kafir) - They are: أَلَّذِينَ آمَنُوا بِأَفْوَاهِهِمْ وَلَمْ تُؤْمِنْ قُلُوبُهُمْ — those who believed with their mouths but whose hearts did not believe - They are described as: أَخْبَث الْكَفَرَة — the most vile of all disbelievers — and أَبْغَضُهُمْ إِلَى اللَّه — the most hateful to Allah - Why? Because they mixed their kufr with خِدَاع (deception) and مَكْر (scheming/mockery of the believers) — making their disbelief more harmful and more reprehensible than open kufr. - Because of this, Allah exposed their malice and ignorance in 13 detailed verses, rebuked them, warned them, and recorded their misguidance and tyranny against them.

    Ayah 9 — يُخَادِعُونَ اللَّهَ وَالَّذِينَ آمَنُوا

    "They [think they] deceive Allah and those who believe..."

    • يُخَادِعُونَ — from خَادَعَ يُخَادِعُ مُخَادَعَةً: to deceive, to try to trick
    • The Munafiqeen attempt to deceive Allah and deceive the Mu'mineen (وَالَّذِينَ آمَنُوا) by displaying outward Iman while hiding kufr.
    • This is the essential act of nifaq: showing belief to gain safety and belonging among Muslims while concealing their true rejection.
    • The session ended here due to prayer time; continuation scheduled for next class.

    Grammar and Language Notes

    • خَتَمَ — Past tense (māḍī), Form I verb: خَتَمَ يَخْتِمُ خَتْمًا
    • التَّغْطِيَة — verbal noun (maṣdar) from Form II verb: غَطَّى يُغَطِّي تَغْطِيَة
    • مُصَدِّق — ism al-fā'il (active participle) from Form II: صَدَّقَ يُصَدِّقُ تَصْدِيقًا — therefore مُصَدِّق means "one who sincerely verifies/affirms"
    • صَدَّقْنَا — Form II māḍī, jamā' mutakallim (first-person plural): "we have affirmed/believed"
    • آمَنَ vs. آمَنُوا — corrected in class: الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا (plural past, "those who believed"), not آمَنُوا as improperly inflected
    • آلَ يَؤُولُ — مَآل — "to lead to / result in"; مَآل is the maṣdar meaning "outcome" or "destination." The phrase وَمَا يَؤُولُ إِلَيْهِ حَالُهُمْ = "whatever their condition leads to"
    • خَادَعَ — Form III verb (mufā'alah), indicating a mutual or attempted action between two parties: يُخَادِعُونَ = "they attempt to deceive (Allah and the believers)"
    • أَخْبَث — superlative (ism tafḍīl) from خَبِيث: "most vile/wicked"
    • أَبْغَض — superlative from بَغِيض: "most hateful"

    Lessons and Practical Takeaways

    1. Sins seal the heart progressively. Accumulated sins obscure the نُور الْبَصِيرَة (light of insight). Every sin is a layer of covering; the heart does not become sealed overnight — it is a gradual process tied directly to moral choices.

    2. Kufr is the primary cause of the seal. The sealing is Allah's response to willful and sustained rejection of truth. This should inspire fear of persistent rejection of what one knows to be true.

    3. Protect all three faculties. The heart, hearing, and sight are all mentioned together — guarding what we expose our hearts to, what we listen to, and what we look at are all equally important for preserving Iman.

    4. Supplicate consistently for a sound heart. The sheikh emphasized specific du'as:

    5. رَبِّ اشْرَحْ لِي صَدْرِي وَيَسِّرْ لِي أَمْرِي — "My Lord, open my chest and ease my affair"
    6. رَبَّنَا لَا تُزِغْ قُلُوبَنَا بَعْدَ إِذْ هَدَيْتَنَا وَهَبْ لَنَا مِنْ لَدُنْكَ رَحْمَةً إِنَّكَ أَنْتَ الْوَهَّاب — "Our Lord, do not let our hearts deviate after You have guided us; grant us mercy from Your presence — You are the Bestower."

    7. اللَّهُمَّ إِنَّا نَعُوذُ بِكَ مِنْ زَوَالِ نِعْمَتِكَ وَتَحَوُّلِ عَافِيَتِكَ وَفَجَاءَةِ نِقْمَتِكَ وَجَمِيعِ سَخَطِكَ — seeking refuge from the removal of blessings, the change of well-being, sudden punishment, and all causes of Allah's anger.

    8. Nifaq is worse than open kufr. The Munafiq is described as more vile and more hateful to Allah than the open disbeliever. The combination of outward Iman with inward kufr, plus deception and mockery, makes nifaq the most dangerous spiritual state.

    9. Tongue without heart is worthless. Verbal declaration of Iman (شَهَادَة بِاللِّسَان) is meaningless without تَصْدِيق بِالْقَلْب (sincere conviction of the heart). Faith must be a reality in the heart, not merely a social identity.

    10. The Muslim community must be alert to internal threats. The detailed 13-verse treatment of Munafiqeen signals that the danger from within — from those who appear to be insiders — is greater than external opposition.

    Historical Context and Stories Mentioned

    • Abdullah ibn Ubayy ibn Salul: The foremost leader of the Munafiqeen in Madinah during the Prophet's ﷺ time. He publicly displayed Islam while actively working against the Muslim community. His name is one of the most referenced figures in the Munafiqeen context throughout Quranic tafseer.
    • Mu'tab ibn Qushayr: Another named leader of the hypocrites, mentioned by Ibn Abbas as among those about whom these ayahs were revealed.
    • Both figures represent the Munafiqeen from Ahl al-Kitab — Jews and Christians who outwardly affiliated with the Muslim community while maintaining their kufr internally.
    • Their specific tactic: telling Muslims "we find the description of your Prophet in our scriptures" — a pretense designed to appear as sincere converts while retaining their internal rejection.

    Q&A Highlights

    Q: Does the sealing of hearts in this ayah mean a physical affliction of the heart, ears, and eyes? A: No. The sheikh clarified that these people are physically healthy and fully functional. Despite having intact hearing, sight, and cognitive ability, they see the truth and do not follow it, hear the call to guidance and do not accept it. The "sealing" is a Quranic metaphor (majaz) describing their spiritual and moral condition — not a physical disability.

    Q: What is the meaning of مَآل (ma'al)? A: From آلَ يَؤُولُ — to lead to, to result in. The maṣdar مَآل means the destination or outcome of something. In context: وَمَا يَؤُولُ إِلَيْهِ حَالُهُمْ مِنَ الْهَلَاكِ وَالدَّمَار — "whatever condition of destruction their situation leads them to" — i.e., the inevitable ruin that nifaq produces.
