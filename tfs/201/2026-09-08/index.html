    Tafseer 201 - Study Notes

    Date: 2026-09-08 Surah Covered: Surah Al-Baqarah (Ayat on the Fasiqeen, followed by Ayat 28–29)

    Session Overview

    This session continued the tafseer of Surah Al-Baqarah, focusing on two major sections: (1) the concluding ayah of the parable passage, specifically the phrase "وَمَا يُضِلُّ بِهِ إِلَّا الْفَاسِقِينَ" and the detailed enumeration of the qualities of the Fasiqeen (transgressors), and (2) the subsequent ayah "وَكَيْفَ تَكْفُرُونَ بِاللَّهِ" (Ayah 28) and the proof of resurrection derived from Allah's creation of the earth and seven heavens (Ayah 29). The session also included a notable student Q&A on the specific groups the ayat apply to.

    Key Themes and Topics

    1. Who are the Fasiqeen? — Allah defines the Fasiqeen (sinful transgressors) and enumerates their specific characteristics.
    2. Breaking of Covenants (Naqd al-'Ahd) — The Fasiqeen break the covenants Allah took from them in the divine books (Torah, Injeel), especially belief in the final Prophet ﷺ.
    3. Severing of Connections (Qat' ma amarallahu bihi an yusala) — They sever what Allah commanded to be connected: ties of kinship, bonds between messengers, and friendship among believers.
    4. Fasad fil-Ard (Corruption on Earth) — The Fasiqeen spread corruption through sins, kufr, shirk, and raising doubts about the Qur'an.
    5. The Ultimate Loss — The Fasiqeen are described as the true losers who traded guidance for misguidance and forgiveness for punishment.
    6. Proof of Resurrection (Ayah 28) — Allah presents the stages of human existence as evidence that He who created can also resurrect.
    7. Creation of the Earth and Seven Heavens (Ayah 29) — Allah's power over creation of the entire universe is the daleel (proof) for the ease of resurrection.

    Main Tafseer Points and Explanations

    1. "وَمَا يُضِلُّ بِهِ إِلَّا الْفَاسِقِينَ" — Allah does not lead astray by it except the Fasiqeen

    • "By it" refers to the parable (al-mathal) or the ayat of the Holy Qur'an.
    • Allah does not lead astray by this except those who are:
    • Al-Kharijeen 'an ta'at Allah — those who have stepped outside Allah's obedience.
    • Al-Jahideen bi-ayatihi — those who deny and reject Allah's signs.

    2. Characteristics of the Fasiqeen (ثُمَّ عَدَّدَ تَعَالَى أَوْصَافَ هَوْلَاءِ الْفَاسِقِينَ)

    Allah enumerates the ill characteristics of the Fasiqeen in the following ayah:

    A. Breaking of Allah's Covenant — "الَّذِينَ يَنْقُضُونَ عَهْدَ اللَّهِ مِنْ بَعْدِ مِيثَاقِهِ"

    • Naqd al-'Ahd = breaking/violating a covenant or agreement.
    • "Min ba'di mithaqihi" = after it had been affirmed with proofs, evidences, and witnesses — making it a Mithaq (a strongly binding, witnessed covenant).
    • What were these covenants?
    • Belief in Prophet Muhammad ﷺ — the divine books (Torah, Injeel) explicitly commanded that if the final messenger comes in their lifetime, they must believe in him.
    • More broadly: Iman billah (belief in One Allah), belief in all messengers, and acting upon the Sharia.
    • Multiple Qur'anic ayat confirm that Allah took these agreements from them.
    • Important distinction (from Q&A): This characteristic is specific to Ahl al-Kitab (People of the Book — Jews and Christians), as the Mushrikeen (polytheists) did not have a divine book with such covenants.

    B. Severing What Allah Commanded to be Connected — "وَيَقْطَعُونَ مَا أَمَرَ اللَّهُ بِهِ أَنْ يُوصَلَ"

    • This is a general (jami') characteristic applying to all types of severed connections.
    • Examples include:
    • Qat' al-Arham — Severing ties of kinship and blood relations (which Allah commanded to be maintained).
    • Breaking the connection between the prophets — accepting some, rejecting others (e.g., believing in Musa but rejecting Muhammad ﷺ).
    • Tark Muwalat al-Mu'mineen — Abandoning the bonds of friendship and loyalty with believers.
    • All of these connections are ordered by Allah to be maintained; the Fasiqeen sever them.
    • Applicability: Some aspects are specific to Ahl al-Kitab; others apply to all (Mushrikeen, Munafiqeen, Ahl al-Kitab).

    C. Spreading Corruption on Earth — "وَيُفْسِدُونَ فِي الْأَرْضِ"

    • They spread Ifsad (corruption) through:
    • Sins and transgressions.
    • Spreading temptations (Fitnah).
    • Preventing people from accepting Iman and Islam.
    • Raising doubts about the Holy Qur'an.
    • Spreading kufr and shirk — the most serious form of Ifsad.
    • Key Qur'anic reference: "الْفِتْنَةُ أَشَدُّ مِنَ الْقَتْلِ" (Fitnah is worse than killing) — from the same surah (Al-Baqarah). All Mufassireen explain that "Fitnah" here means the spreading of kufr and shirk.
    • Applicability: General — applies to both Ahl al-Kitab and Mushrikeen.

    D. The Ultimate Loss — "أُولَئِكَ هُمُ الْخَاسِرُونَ"

    • These are the actual/true losers.
    • They exchanged guidance for misguidance and forgiveness for punishment.
    • The emphasis on "هُمُ" (they themselves) indicates that these are the real losers — not those who lose worldly things, but those who lose their akhira.

    3. Ayah 28 — "وَكَيْفَ تَكْفُرُونَ بِاللَّهِ وَكُنتُمْ أَمْوَاتًا فَأَحْيَاكُمْ ثُمَّ يُمِيتُكُمْ ثُمَّ يُحْيِيكُمْ ثُمَّ إِلَيْهِ تُرْجَعُونَ"

    • "Kayfa" (How) here — This is not a genuine question. It is an interrogative of rebuke and denial (Inkaar) — used to scold, rebuke, and deny the disbelievers their stance.
    • Allah presents the stages of human existence as proof of His power and as a rebuke to those who deny Him:
    Stage Arabic Explanation
    1. Death (non-existence) أَمْوَاتًا You were like dead — sperm in the loins of your fathers, in the wombs of your mothers
    2. Life (in this world) فَأَحْيَاكُمْ Allah brought you into this life
    3. Death (end of life) ثُمَّ يُمِيتُكُمْ Allah will cause you to die at your appointed time (ajal)
    4. Resurrection ثُمَّ يُحْيِيكُمْ Allah will revive you from your graves (Ba'th min al-Quboor)
    5. Return to Allah ثُمَّ إِلَيْهِ تُرْجَعُونَ You will be returned to Allah for reckoning, reward, and punishment

    4. Ayah 29 — Creation of the Earth and Seven Heavens as Proof of Resurrection

    • Allah created everything on earth for humanity's benefit, so that people recognize Allah as Creator and Sustainer.
    • Allah then rose to (Istawa ila) the heavens — directing His divine will toward the upper realm.
    • Allah formed (Sawwa) seven heavens — well-built, firm, not collapsing.
    • "وَذَلِكَ دَلِيلُ الْقُدْرَةِ الْبَاهِرَةِ" — This is the evidence of Allah's overwhelming, amazing power.
    • "وَهُوَ بِكُلِّ شَيْءٍ عَلِيمٌ" — Allah is All-Knowing of everything He created and produced (Zara'a).

    The Logical Proof for Resurrection:

    "The One who is able to create this entire universe — the earth, the seven heavens, all planets, galaxies, and stars — is certainly able to bring back a single human being."

    • The creation of the universe is far greater than recreating a human. If Allah did the greater, the lesser (resurrection) is even easier.
    • On the Day of Judgment, the "samples" (the framework of creation) already exist — resurrection is simply re-assembling what has already been created once.
    • "اللَّهُ عَلَى كُلِّ شَيْءٍ قَدِيرٌ" — Allah has power over all things.

    Grammar and Language Notes

    • يَنْقُضُونَ — Present tense verb from the root ن-ق-ض, meaning to break or unravel (like breaking rope or an agreement). "Naqd al-'Ahd" specifically means to break a covenant.
    • أَمَاتَ / يُمِيتُ / إِمَاتَةً — Form IV (باب إفعال) verb meaning "to cause to die." The Form IV denotes causativity.
    • أَحْيَا / يُحْيِي / إِحْيَاءً — Form IV verb meaning "to give life / to revive."
    • الْخَارِجِينَ — Active participle (ism fa'il), plural, meaning "those who exit/step outside."
    • الْجَاهِدِينَ — Active participle, meaning "those who deny/reject."
    • ذَرَأَ / يَذْرَأُ — Means to produce/create (especially living creatures); "Allah knows everything He created."
    • Balagha (Rhetoric): The teacher noted that there are rhetorical devices (balagha) present in these ayat but declined to cover them in depth, as the students have not yet studied a balagha text. This will be addressed in future study.

    Lessons and Practical Takeaways

    1. Misguidance comes from within: The Qur'an does not mislead anyone — it is the Fasiq's own rejection that leads to misguidance. The same rain that causes flowers to bloom causes swamps to stagnate.
    2. The danger of breaking covenants: One of the hallmark signs of a Fasiq is breaking agreements — with Allah, with people, and with family. Muslims must be people of their word.
    3. Maintain ties of kinship: Qat' al-Arham (severing family ties) is listed among the qualities of the Fasiqeen — a serious warning for believers to actively maintain family relationships.
    4. Kufr and Shirk are the worst corruption: Spreading disbelief is classified as the most severe form of Ifsad fil-Ard — worse than physical killing, as the Qur'an states.
    5. Reflect on the stages of your existence: The progression from non-existence → life → death → resurrection is a powerful reminder of Allah's complete authority over us. Ingratitude (kufr) is irrational given this reality.
    6. Contemplation of the universe reinforces Iman: Reflecting on the creation of the earth and seven heavens should strengthen conviction in both Allah's existence and the certainty of resurrection.
    7. The true losers are those who lose their akhira: Worldly loss is recoverable; the loss of the Day of Judgment is absolute.

    Historical Context and Stories Mentioned

    • The Covenants in the Torah and Injeel: Allah took firm covenants (Mithaq) from Ahl al-Kitab in their divine books to believe in all messengers, including the final Prophet Muhammad ﷺ. The breaking of this covenant — by accepting Musa but rejecting Muhammad ﷺ — is specifically cited as an example of "Naqd al-'Ahd."
    • Applicability of the Ayat: The teacher clarified through Q&A that while the Fasiqeen ayat are addressed generally, specific characteristics apply to specific groups:
    • Breaking covenants (Naqd al-'Ahd) → specific to Ahl al-Kitab.
    • Severing connections → general, some aspects specific to Ahl al-Kitab (rejecting certain prophets), others to all.
    • Spreading corruption → general, applying to both Ahl al-Kitab and Mushrikeen.
    • The surah was revealed in Madinah (Madani), at which time the primary groups were Ahl al-Kitab, Mushrikeen, and Munafiqeen.

    Q&A Highlights

    Q (Student): What exactly is the "Fasad" (corruption) the Fasiqeen spread? Can you repeat that?

    A (Teacher): The Fasad includes: Fitnah (temptations) — specifically here, spreading kufr and shirk. The Qur'anic ayah "al-Fitnatu ashaddu min al-Qatl" (Fitnah is worse than killing) confirms that Fitnah here means spreading kufr and shirk. It is a greater crime than physical killing. All Mufassireen agree on this meaning in context. Fitnah has many meanings (temptation from Shaytan, testing people), but in this context of corruption, it specifically refers to kufr and shirk.

    Q (Student): This surah was revealed in Madinah. At that time, wouldn't the Fasiqeen refer to both Ahl al-Kitab and Mushrikeen? And is the first characteristic (breaking covenants) specifically for Ahl al-Kitab only?

    A (Teacher): Yes, you are correct. The first characteristic — breaking the covenant with Allah — is specific to Ahl al-Kitab, because the Mushrikeen did not have a divine book from which such a covenant was taken. However, the word "Fasiqeen" is general — it encompasses all. Some characteristics are specific to Ahl al-Kitab, some to Mushrikeen and Munafiqeen, and some are general. For example, Iman billah (belief in Allah) is a requirement for all people of the world; Iman in the later messengers (al-rusul al-akhireen) is specifically relevant to Jews.

    Q (Student): Will we be covering Balagha (rhetoric) in these ayat?

    A (Teacher): There are indeed balagha elements in these ayat, and balagha is my favorite subject. However, since the students have not yet studied a balagha textbook, covering it now would take too much time and we would not cover the required tafseer content. The focus will remain on tafseer and vocabulary meanings.
