    Tafseer 201 - Study Notes

    Date: 2026-09-19 Surah Covered: Surah Al-Baqarah (Ayat addressing Bani Israel — verses on covenant, belief in the Quran, prayer, and zakat)

    Session Overview

    This session continued the tafseer of Surah Al-Baqarah, focusing on the series of divine commands addressed to Bani Israel (the Jews of Madinah). The instructor covered several consecutive ayat containing instructions: fulfilling the covenant with Allah, believing in the Quran, avoiding the sale of divine verses for worldly gain, fearing Allah alone, refraining from mixing truth with falsehood, not concealing truth, and establishing salah and zakat. Significant time was also devoted to Arabic morphology (sarf) — conjugating key verbs from the ayat in their full paradigms (madi, mudari', amr, nahi).

    Key Themes and Topics

    1. Fulfilling the covenant (ʿahd) with Allah — Bani Israel are commanded to honor their agreement with Allah; in return, Allah will fulfill His promise of reward.
    2. Belief in the Quran — The Quran is presented as a confirmation (musaddiq) of the Tawrah, particularly regarding tawheed and prophethood; Jews are told it is most befitting for them to be among the first to believe in it, not the first to disbelieve.
    3. Not selling the ayat for a small price — A condemnation of the Jewish religious scholars who altered and exchanged the verses of the Tawrah for worldly/material gain.
    4. Fearing Allah alone — Exclusive fear and reverence belongs to Allah, not to anyone or anything else.
    5. Not mixing truth with falsehood — Prohibition against blending divine revelation with fabricated content.
    6. Not concealing the truth — Prohibition against hiding the descriptions of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ that were present in the Tawrah.
    7. Establishing salah and giving zakah — Commands to perform prayer (with congregation) and give zakah; ruku' mentioned as representing the whole salah.
    8. Sarf drill — Conjugation of the verbs أَوْفَى، آمَنَ، اتَّقَى، كَتَمَ، أَقَامَ، آتَى، رَكَعَ in full paradigms.

    Main Tafseer Points and Explanations

    1. أَوْفُوا بِعَهْدِي أُوفِ بِعَهْدِكُمْ — Fulfill My covenant, I will fulfill yours

    • Allah commands Bani Israel to fulfill their agreement with Him: to believe in Him and obey His commands.
    • Allah's corresponding pledge: to give them reward and blessings for their obedience.
    • The mutakallim (first person) form: أُوفِي بِعَهْدِك — "I will fulfill what I have with you."

    2. وَآمِنُوا بِمَا أَنزَلْتُ مُصَدِّقًا لِمَا مَعَكُمْ — Believe in what I have sent down, confirming what is with you

    • The Quran confirms the Tawrah, especially in matters of tawheed (oneness of Allah) and nubuwwah (prophethood of Muhammad ﷺ).
    • Since Jews already believe in the Tawrah, they are told: the Quran carries the same message — so believe in it.
    • Command: آمِنُوا (amr from آمَنَ يُؤْمِنُ إِيمَانًا).
    • Warning: Do not be the first to disbelieve in the Quran — on the contrary, being first to believe in it is what befits them.

    3. وَلَا تَشْتَرُوا بِآيَاتِي ثَمَنًا قَلِيلًا — Do not sell My verses for a small price

    • This refers specifically to the ayat of the Tawrah, not the Quran (because the Quran could not be tampered with in this way).
    • The Jewish religious scholars had a "religious trade" — they would alter verses of the Tawrah in exchange for material payment from those who wanted rulings changed.
    • People would pay them to modify religious rulings, and they would oblige by altering the text.
    • Full meaning: لَا تَسْتَبْدِلُوا بِالْآيَاتِ الْبَيِّنَاتِ حُطَامَ الدُّنْيَا الْفَانِيَة — Do not exchange the clear signs Allah revealed with the debris of this perishing world.
    • وَإِيَّايَ فَاتَّقُونِ — And fear Me alone (not your patrons, not your clients).

    4. وَلَا تَلْبِسُوا الْحَقَّ بِالْبَاطِلِ — Do not mix truth with falsehood

    • Prohibition against blending divine revelation with fabricated content.
    • Full interpretation: لَا تُحَرِّفُوا مَا فِي التَّوْرَاةِ بِالْبُهْتَانِ الَّذِي تَفْتَرُونَهُ — Do not distort what is in the Tawrah with the slander you fabricate.
    • This refers to the additions, deletions, and alterations they made to the Tawrah.

    5. وَتَكْتُمُوا الْحَقَّ وَأَنْتُمْ تَعْلَمُونَ — Do not conceal the truth while you know

    • Truth being concealed = the descriptions and characteristics of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ that were present in the original Tawrah.
    • وَأَنْتُمْ تَعْلَمُونَ carries two meanings:
    • (i) You know it is the truth and that other things are false.
    • (ii) You know what the consequence of this concealment will be (and it will not be good).
    • This concealment was done knowingly and deliberately — making it a graver sin.

    6. وَأَقِيمُوا الصَّلَاةَ وَآتُوا الزَّكَاةَ وَارْكَعُوا مَعَ الرَّاكِعِينَ

    • Three commands: establish salah, give zakah, and bow with those who bow.
    • رَكَعَ here does not mean only the physical act of ruku'. Mufassirun explain that ruku' stands for the complete salah — just as sajdah (prostration) is sometimes used to represent the whole prayer.
    • Full meaning: Perform complete, proper salah.
    • وَارْكَعُوا مَعَ الرَّاكِعِينَ — "Do ruku' with those who do ruku'" — specifically addressed to the Jews of Madinah, commanding them to go to the masjid and pray with the companions of Muhammad ﷺ, which would visibly demonstrate their acceptance of Islam.
    • Fiqh implication: Some scholars derive from this ayah that salah with jama'ah (congregation) is wājib (obligatory), because the command (amr) indicates wujūb.

    Grammar and Language Notes

    Verb Conjugation Drill — Key Verbs from the Ayat

    1. أَوْفَى — to fulfill (bāb: faʿala)

    Form Conjugation
    Māḍī (3rd m. sg.) أَوْفَى
    Mudāriʿ يُوفِي، يُوفِيَانِ، يُوفُونَ، تُوفِي، تُوفِيَانِ، يُوفُونَ
    Amr (sg.) أَوْفِ
    Amr (dual) أَوْفِيَا
    Amr (plural) أَوْفُوا (with ḍammah on the fāʾ)
    Mutakallim mudāriʿ أُوفِي (I will fulfill)

    Note: The plural of the amr أَوْفِيَا is أَوْفُوا — not أَوْفَى (which is māḍī). This was the key error some students made.

    2. آمَنَ — to believe

    Form Conjugation
    Māḍī آمَنَ، آمَنَا، آمَنُوا، آمَنَتْ
    Amr آمِنْ (sg.) → آمِنُوا (pl.)

    Note: آمَنُوا (they believed) vs. آمِنُوا (believe! — amr) — the distinction is critical. The hamzah and voweling differ.

    3. اتَّقَى — to fear Allah (ittaqā)

    Form Conjugation
    Amr (sg.) اِتَّقِ
    Amr (dual) اِتَّقِيَا
    Amr (plural) اِتَّقُوا
    In ayah فَاتَّقُونِ / وَإِيَّايَ فَاتَّقُونِ (the yāʾ is dropped; nūn with kasrah indicates the original yāʾ)

    4. خَشِيَ / رَهِبَ — to fear (with reverence)

    Term Notes
    خَشِيَ يَخْشَى خَشْيَةً To fear with awe; amr: اِخْشَوْنِي (fear Me)
    رَهِبَ يَرْهَبُ رَهَبًا/رَهَبَةً To fear; same meaning as خَشِيَ; amr form in ayah: فَرْهَبُونِ
    فَرْهَبُونِ Fāʾ added to رَهَبُونِ; the yāʾ is dropped, nūn with kasrah indicates original yāʾ

    5. كَتَمَ — to conceal (bāb: naṣara)

    Form Conjugation
    Māḍī / Mudāriʿ كَتَمَ يَكْتُمُ كِتْمَانًا
    Command لَا تَكْتُمُ (do not conceal)

    6. أَقَامَ — to establish (bāb: ifʿāl)

    Form Conjugation
    Māḍī أَقَامَ
    Mudāriʿ يُقِيمُ
    Maṣdar إِقَامَةً
    Amr (pl.) أَقِيمُوا

    7. آتَى — to give

    Form Conjugation
    Amr (sg.) آتِ
    Amr (dual) آتِيَا
    Amr (plural) آتُوا

    8. رَكَعَ — to bow

    Form Conjugation
    Māḍī رَكَعَ
    Mudāriʿ يَرْكَعُ
    Maṣdar رُكُوعًا
    Ism al-fāʿil رَاكِع → رَاكِعِين (pl.)
    Amr اِرْكَعُوا

    General Grammar Rules Reviewed

    • Alif maqsūrah at the end of a verb (e.g., أَوْفَى، اتَّقَى) affects conjugation patterns; only a few patterns in mujārrad (5–6) and mazīd fīh (9) — manageable with practice.
    • Amr vs. Māḍī distinction is critical: e.g., أَوْفَى (he fulfilled — māḍī) vs. أَوْفِ (fulfill! — amr).
    • Dropped yāʾ in amr/nahi forms with attached pronouns: فَاتَّقُونِ, فَرْهَبُونِ — the nūn carries a kasrah to signal the missing yāʾ.
    • Amr indicates wujūb (obligation) — the command form in fiqh methodology signals that the action is obligatory.
    • Recommended reference: A sarf book that lists all conjugation patterns across Arabic — students to identify their verb's pattern and complete the full paradigm from the book.

    Lessons and Practical Takeaways

    1. Honor your commitments to Allah — The ayah on fulfilling the ʿahd is a reminder that our relationship with Allah is a two-way covenant: our obedience earns His promised reward.
    2. The Quran as universal confirmation — The Quran does not contradict the original divine scriptures; it confirms them. Those who already believe in earlier scriptures have the strongest reason to accept the Quran.
    3. Never compromise religious truth for worldly gain — The sin of the Jewish scholars was selling religious verdicts. This is a warning for all scholars and students of knowledge in every age.
    4. Fear Allah alone — Taqwa is exclusive to Allah; one must not fear the displeasure of people more than the displeasure of Allah.
    5. Do not mix truth with falsehood — Clarity and integrity in conveying religious knowledge is an obligation; mixing in falsehood or opinion presented as revelation is a grave sin.
    6. Do not conceal the truth — Knowing something is true and hiding it is worse than ignorance. Scholars bear a special responsibility to convey truth clearly.
    7. Pray in congregation — The command وَارْكَعُوا مَعَ الرَّاكِعِينَ indicates the strong (possibly obligatory) status of salah with jama'ah; do not pray alone when congregation is available.
    8. Sarf practice is essential — Students were assigned to complete full conjugation tables (māḍī, mudāriʿ, amr, nahi) for every new verb encountered, to be reviewed in the next class.

    Historical Context and Stories Mentioned

    Distortion of the Tawrah by Jewish Scholars

    • The Jewish religious scholars had a well-established practice of altering verses of the Tawrah for material compensation.
    • Specific content they concealed/altered: descriptions and prophecies of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ that were in the original Tawrah, as well as clear statements on tawheed.
    • This was not a minor or occasional sin — it was described as their "religious profession" (trade).
    • Method: People would approach them wanting a ruling changed; the scholars would alter the relevant Tawrah text accordingly, receiving payment.
    • Allah addresses this in multiple commands in these ayat: don't sell, don't mix, don't hide.

    Jews of Madinah Addressed

    • The ayat in this passage are specifically directed at the Jews of Madinah who were contemporary with the Prophet ﷺ.
    • The command to pray with the rākiʿīn was an invitation for them to physically join the Muslim congregation — an outward affirmation of faith.

    Q&A Highlights

    Q: Is the verse saying "don't exchange the Quran for the Tawrah" or "don't exchange [the Tawrah] for worldly benefit"?

    A: The exchange refers specifically to the ayat of the Tawrah, not the Quran. The Quran could not be altered. The Jewish scholars were exchanging/altering Tawrah verses for worldly material benefit — that is what Allah condemns here.

    Q: Would people actually come and pay them to change the rulings?

    A: Yes, exactly. That was the practice — people would approach the scholars wanting rulings or texts changed to their benefit, and the scholars would alter the Tawrah accordingly in exchange for payment.
