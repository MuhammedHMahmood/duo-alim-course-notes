    Tafseer 201 - Study Notes

    Date: 2026-09-05 Surah Covered: Surah Al-Baqarah — Ayah on Jannah (وَبَشِّرِ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ), Page 42

    Session Overview

    This session covered the tafseer of the ayah in Surah Al-Baqarah giving glad tidings to the believers about the rewards of Jannah. The teacher began by contextualizing the ayah within the Quranic style of Targheeb (encouragement) and Tarheeb (warning), showing how Allah contrasts the fate of His enemies with His friends. The session included detailed grammatical analysis of the phrase anna lahum jannatin with student participation, followed by tafseer of the qualities of Jannah: its palaces with flowing rivers, its identical-in-appearance but distinct-in-taste fruits, its purified wives, and the eternal nature of its blessings. The session concluded with notes on exam scope.

    Key Themes and Topics

    1. Targheeb and Tarheeb — The Quranic rhetorical style of motivating through hope (Jannah) and warning through fear (Jahannam), used to compare the states of the righteous (Abraar) and the unrighteous (Fujjaar).

    2. Transition in the Ayaat — After mentioning what Allah has prepared for His enemies (punishment), the text transitions to what He has prepared for His friends (reward), following the pattern of muqaarana bayna haal il-abraari wal-fujjaar (comparison between the righteous and unrighteous).

    3. Four Supreme Desires of the Human Being — The teacher structured the description of Jannah around the four highest worldly desires a person seeks:

    4. Best place of residence
    5. Best food and fruits
    6. Best spouse/companion

    7. Eternal life (never losing these blessings)

    8. Grammatical Analysis of Anna lahum jannatin — Deep dive into Inna/Anna grammar: muqaddam khabar (pre-posed predicate), mu'akhkhar ism (post-posed subject), and why jannatin is mansoob bil-kasra.

    9. Fruits of Jannah — Identical in color and appearance to familiar fruits, but entirely different in taste each time; angels bring the fruits to the residents.

    10. Wives of Jannah (Azwaajun Mutahharatun) — Purified from all physical and moral impurities; believing women of this world will be even more beautiful than the Hoor al-'Ayn.

    11. Eternal Abiding — The blessings of Jannah are permanent; residents will never lose them, constituting tamaam al-sa'aadah (complete happiness).

    Main Tafseer Points and Explanations

    1. Context: Transition from Punishment to Reward

    After previously mentioning the punishment prepared for the disbelievers (wa u'iddat lil-kaafireen), Allah now mentions the reward prepared for the believers. This is the signature Quranic method of Targheeb and Tarheeb — hope and fear placed side by side for maximum effect on the reader's heart.

    2. The Command to Give Glad Tidings

    وَبَشِّرِ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ

    "And give glad tidings, O Muhammad ﷺ, to those who believe and do righteous deeds."

    • Wabishir is the Amr (command form) addressed to the Prophet ﷺ.
    • The recipients are the mumineen who were muhsineen in the dunya — those who combined Iman with righteous action.

    3. Jannah: Best Residences

    The believers will have jannaatin tajri min tahtihal-anhaar — gardens and orchards with trees, palaces, and residences, under which the rivers of Paradise flow. This fulfills the highest worldly desire: the best place of living. The teacher noted that even the richest person in the world ultimately only aspires to the best earthly residence — and Jannah surpasses anything imaginable.

    4. Fruits of Jannah: Same Appearance, Different Taste Every Time

    كُلَّمَا رُزِقُوا مِنْهَا مِن ثَمَرَةٍ رِزْقًا قَالُوا هَذَا الَّذِي رُزِقْنَا مِن قَبْلُ

    "Whenever they are provided with fruit from it, they say: This is what we were provided before."

    Process: The angels of Allah bring fruits to the residents of Jannah. When the same fruit is brought again, the residents remark that they received this before.

    Two meanings of min qabl: - Immediate meaning: "before this occasion" (e.g. yesterday, or earlier today) - Broader meaning (also valid): "before this world" — i.e. similar to what we had in the dunya

    The reply of the angels:

    كُلْ يَا عَبْدَ اللَّهِ — "Eat, O servant of Allah" فَاللَّوْنُ وَاحِدٌ وَالطَّعَامُ مُخْتَلِف — "The color is the same, but the taste is entirely different."

    Scholarly opinions on mutashaabihan:

    Scholar View
    Ibn Jareer (رحمه الله) Similar in color (lawn) and appearance (mar'aa), but not in taste
    Majority of Mufassiroon Similar in shape, color, and appearance — different in taste and quality
    Ibn Abbas (رضي الله عنه) Only the names are similar (apple, grape, date, etc.); everything else — color, appearance, taste — is entirely different from this world

    5. Wives of Jannah: Azwaajun Mutahharatun

    وَلَهُمْ فِيهَا أَزْوَاجٌ مُطَهَّرَةٌ

    "And for them therein are purified wives."

    Mutahharatun distinguishes the wives of Jannah from the women of this world. They are purified from:

    Physical impurities (per Imam Mujahid): - Haydh (menstruation) - Nifaas (postpartum bleeding) - Ghaa'it (defecation) - Bawl (urine) - Nukhaam (sputum) - Discharge from any bodily opening

    Moral/spiritual impurities: Free from all moral filth, bad character, and spiritual corruption.

    Note on believing women of this dunya: A hadith states that the believing women of this world (mu'minaat) will be more beautiful in Jannah than the Hoor al-'Ayn. Allah says in Surah Al-Waqi'ah that He will recreate them — innaa ansha'naahunna inshaa'an — as a new, perfected creation: virgins, equal in age, and eternal companions.

    6. Eternal Abiding — Tamaam al-Sa'aadah

    The residents of Jannah will abide there forever (khaalidoon). The blessings will never be discontinued, interrupted, or taken away. This is called tamaam al-sa'aadah — complete and perfect happiness — because: - They have the best residence ✓ - They have the best food and fruits ✓ - They have the best purified companions ✓ - They will never die or lose any of these blessings ✓

    فَإِنَّهُمْ مَعَ هَذَا النَّعِيمِ فِي مَقَامٍ أَمِينٍ "They will be in this bliss in a secure, protected abode."

    Grammar and Language Notes

    Deep Dive: Anna lahum jannatin (أَنَّ لَهُمْ جَنَّاتٍ)

    Rule: حُرُوف مُشَبَّهَة بِالْفِعْل

    إنَّ and أنَّ belong to the category of huroof mushabbaha bil-fi'l — particles that resemble verbs. They govern a sentence by taking: - Ism (اسم): always mansoob (accusative) - Khabar (خبر): always marfoo' (nominative)

    Analysis of anna lahum jannatin:

    Word Analysis
    أنَّ Harf mushabbah bil-fi'l (particle of emphasis / harf tawkeed)
    لَهُمْ لِ = harf jaar; هُمْ = majroor — together they form a shibhu jumla (quasi-sentence) functioning as khabar Anna, placed muqaddam (pre-posed)
    جَنَّاتٍ Ism of Anna, placed mu'akhkhar (post-posed); mansoob — but since it is jam' mu'annath saalim, its sign of nasb is kasra (not fatha)

    Key points clarified: - Lam in lahum is a harf jaar, NOT a mudaaf. A harf jaar can never be a mudaaf — this is a permanent grammatical rule. - Jar + majroor (shibhu jumla) can function as khabar of Anna when placed in the khabar position. - The khabar here is muqaddam (came before the ism) — this is permissible with Anna/Inna. - Jannatin is mansoob bil-kasra because it is a sound feminine plural (jam' mu'annath saalim).

    Other Grammatical Notes

    • بَشِّرْ — Amr (command) from bashshara yubashshiru tabsheeran
    • كُلْ — Amr from akala ya'kulu; past tense akala, present ya'kulu, command kul
    • رَزَقَ — to provide/give; kullama ruziqoo = "whenever they were provided"
    • تَجْرِي — present tense verb, from jaraa yajree = to flow
    • خَالِدُون — active participle from khalada yakhludu = to abide forever; plural khaalidoon
    • مُطَهَّرَة — passive participle of tahhara yutahhiru = to purify; mutahharatun = that which has been purified

    Lessons and Practical Takeaways

    1. The Quran motivates through both hope and fear. The style of Targheeb and Tarheeb is deliberate — the description of Jannah immediately follows the description of Jahannam so the heart is moved in both directions. This is a pedagogical model for Islamic teaching.

    2. Jannah is designed to fulfill every deepest human desire — and exceed it. The teacher mapped the four blessings of Jannah directly to the four highest human aspirations (residence, food, spouse, immortality). This shows that Islam does not suppress human desire but redirects and fulfills it at its highest level.

    3. The blessings of Jannah are both familiar and transcendent. The fruits look like what we know but taste entirely different — there is enough familiarity to be comforting and enough difference to be wondrous. Ibn Abbas's view makes this even stronger: even the appearance is entirely unlike anything in this world; only the name is shared.

    4. Purity is a defining quality of Jannah. The description of the wives as mutahharatun signals that everything in Jannah is free from filth — physical, moral, and spiritual. This is a complete contrast to the impurities inherent to the human condition in this world.

    5. Eternity is what completes happiness. Tamaam al-sa'aadah is not merely having good things — it is having them forever without fear of loss. The fear of death, loss, and impermanence is the greatest anxiety of this life; Jannah removes it entirely.

    6. Grammar is a tool for Quranic understanding. The detailed grammatical discussion of anna lahum jannatin showed that a seemingly simple phrase contains layers of syntactic meaning — the pre-posing of the khabar (lahum) gives emphasis to the for them, and the post-posing of ism (jannatin) creates rhetorical anticipation. Arabic grammar is not abstract; it shapes meaning.

    Historical Context and Stories Mentioned

    • Ibn Jareer al-Tabari (رحمه الله): Cited for his tafseer that mutashaabihan means the fruits are similar in color and appearance (lawn wa mar'aa) but not in taste.
    • Ibn Abbas (رضي الله عنه): His tafseer states that nothing in Jannah resembles what is in the dunya — not in color, taste, or appearance — except the names. The apple of Jannah is called an apple, but it shares nothing else with the apple of this world.
    • Imam Mujahid (رحمه الله): Explained mutahharatun as specifically meaning freedom from haydh, nifaas, ghaa'it, bawl, and nukhaam — the physical impurities that are part of the human bodily condition.
    • Surah Al-Waqi'ah reference: The teacher referenced the ayah about Allah recreating the believing women as a new creation (innaa ansha'naahunna inshaa'an) — made virgins (abkaara), equal in age (atraaba), for the companions of the right hand.

    Q&A Highlights

    Q (implicit — grammatical exercise): In anna lahum jannatin, what is the grammatical role of lahum and jannatin?

    Discussion summary: - Students initially confused lam in lahum with a mudaaf or suggested jannatin was majroor. - Teacher clarified: lam is harf jaar (never a mudaaf); hum is majroor; together lahum is a jar + majroor shibhu jumla functioning as the muqaddam khabar of Anna. - Jannatin is the mu'akhkhar ism of Anna, mansoob — with kasra as its sign of nasb because it is a jam' mu'annath saalim. - Key rule reinforced: ism of Anna is always mansoob; khabar is always marfoo'; but their order can be inverted (khabar muqaddam, ism mu'akhkhar).

    Q (student, Huda): Does min qabl mean "before this time" or "what we had in the dunya"?

    Answer: Both are valid interpretations. The near/immediate meaning (min qabl hadhihi al-marra) is the primary one adopted by the mufassir in the text. The second meaning (referring to the dunya) is also noted as valid (la ba's).

    Exam scope clarification (end of session): The teacher clarified that for exams, students will be tested on: - Vocabulary (mufradaat): meaning of words in the ayaat - Verb conjugation (tasreef al-fi'l): past, present, command forms - Grammatical analysis (tarkeeb): irab of words within the Quranic text - Some tafseer content — but not exhaustive detail on every opinion; Quranic text is primary, tafseer opinions are for information/context only - Example given: a'oodhu in isti'aadha — students may be asked its conjugation and meaning, not full tafseer detail
