    Tafseer 201 - Study Notes

    Date: 2026-11-10 Surah Covered: Surah Al-Baqarah, Ayat 87–98

    Session Overview

    This session continued the ongoing tafseer of Surah Al-Baqarah, covering ayat 87 through approximately 93–98. The central theme is Allah's cataloguing of Bani Israel's persistent ingratitude and rebelliousness in the face of repeated divine blessings. The teacher walked through the tafseer of each ayah sequentially using a structured Arabic commentary, covering the following: Allah sending multiple messengers after Musa, the support given to Isa ibn Maryam, Bani Israel's pattern of rejecting and killing prophets, their claim that their hearts are "covered," their prior knowledge of and anticipation of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ followed by their rejection of him, the metaphor of a terrible transaction (selling themselves for disbelief), the wrath they accumulated, their insistence on only believing in the Torah, the covenant taken under the mountain of Tur, and their declaration of "Sami'na wa 'asayna" (we hear and we disobey).

    Key Themes and Topics

    1. Continuity of Divine Messengers — Allah sent a chain of messengers after Musa ﷺ, culminating in Isa ibn Maryam ﷺ, supported by clear miracles and Jibreel.
    2. Bani Israel's Ingratitude as a Pattern — Their repeated response to divine blessings was kufr (ingratitude/disbelief) and crimes; this is described as their established nature (ṭab' and 'ādah).
    3. Rejection and Murder of Prophets — Whenever a messenger brought something contrary to their desires, they either denied them or killed them.
    4. Hasad (Envy/Malice) as the Root Cause — Their rejection of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ and the Quran was driven entirely by envy that prophethood came to a nation other than the Jews.
    5. Prior Knowledge of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ — Before his arrival, the Jews used to supplicate to Allah for victory through the coming final prophet — yet when he came they recognized but rejected him.
    6. The "Covered Hearts" Claim — Bani Israel told the Prophet ﷺ their hearts were covered so as to make him despair of their ever accepting Islam.
    7. The Covenant Under Mount Tur — Allah raised the mountain of Tur over them to enforce the covenant of following the Torah, yet they later declared "Sami'na wa 'asayna."
    8. The Love of the Calf Penetrating Their Hearts — The worship of the golden calf became deeply embedded in their hearts.
    9. Accumulated Divine Wrath — They earned wrath upon wrath: first for distorting the Torah, then for rejecting the Quran and Prophet Muhammad ﷺ.

    Main Tafseer Points and Explanations

    Ayah 87 — وَلَقَدْ آتَيْنَا مُوسَى الْكِتَابَ وَقَفَّيْنَا مِنْ بَعْدِهِ بِالرُّسُلِ

    • Allah affirms that He gave Musa ﷺ the Torah (al-Kitāb).
    • قَفَّيْنَا: "We followed up" — meaning Allah sent many messengers in succession after Musa ﷺ among Bani Israel. The word originally means to follow someone's footsteps.
    • الْكَثِيرَ مِنَ الرُّسُلِ: "The many messengers" — the definite article (al-) on kathīra emphasizes the great and specific number of messengers sent.
    • The final messenger sent specifically among Bani Israel was Isa ibn Maryam ﷺ.

    Ayah 87 (continued) — Support of Isa ibn Maryam ﷺ

    • Allah gave Isa ﷺ al-bayyināt (clear evidences and miracles), which included healing the blind (al-akmah), healing lepers (al-abraṣ), and raising the dead (bi-idhnillāh).
    • Allah supported Isa ﷺ with Rūḥ al-Qudus — Jibreel ﷺ. The word ayyada means to strengthen and reinforce.

    Ayah 87 (continued) — Bani Israel's Rejection of Messengers

    • Whenever any messenger came to Bani Israel with something contrary to their desires (mā lā tahwā anfusuhum), they reacted in one of two ways:
    • Istikbār: They became arrogant and rejected/denied the messenger.
    • Qatl: They killed some messengers outright.
    • This is presented as an established pattern (ṭab' wa 'ādah) — responding to kindness with harm, and to blessings with kufr and juḥūd (denial).

    Ayah 88 — وَقَالُوا قُلُوبُنَا غُلْفٌ

    • Bani Israel told Prophet Muhammad ﷺ: "Our hearts are covered (ghulf)."
    • Ghulf is the plural of ghilāf (covering/sheath).
    • Their intent: "Our hearts cannot comprehend or receive what you say, O Muhammad."
    • Deeper meaning behind the claim: they were actually saying "We already have all the knowledge we need (from Torah); we do not need anything from you." This was designed to make the Prophet ﷺ despair of them ever accepting Islam.
    • Allah's response: بَلْ لَعَنَهُمُ اللَّهُ بِكُفْرِهِمْ — "No! Rather, Allah has cursed them for their disbelief." The hearts are not covered; they have been distanced from Allah's mercy due to their kufr.
    • La'nah = to be expelled and driven away (ab'adahum) from the mercy of Allah.
    • A minority among them (e.g., Abdullah ibn Salam and a few others) did accept Islam.
    • فَقَلِيلًا مَا يُؤْمِنُونَ has two interpretations:
    • Very few of them will believe.
    • They believe only a little — meaning they accept part of the scripture and reject the rest.

    Ayah 89 — وَلَمَّا جَاءَهُمْ كِتَابٌ مِنْ عِنْدِ اللَّهِ مُصَدِّقٌ لِمَا مَعَهُمْ

    • When the Quran came to them — a book that confirmed the original Torah in its fundamentals (Tawḥīd, Risālah, Ākhirah, good and evil) — they rejected it.
    • The Quran confirms the original, unamended Torah, not the distorted version.
    • Key point: The fundamentals of religion (Tawḥīd, prophethood, the afterlife, moral principles) are the same across all divine books; only some jurisprudential rulings differed according to time and people.

    Ayah 89 (continued) — The Jews' Prior Supplication for the Coming Prophet

    • وَكَانُوا مِنْ قَبْلُ يَسْتَفْتِحُونَ عَلَى الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا: Before Prophet Muhammad's ﷺ arrival, the Jews used to supplicate:

      "O Allah, give us victory through the prophet who will come at the end of times — the one whose description we find in our Torah."

    • They would use this anticipated prophet as a means of invoking victory over their enemies.
    • Yet when he came — and they recognized him with certainty (ma'rifah yaqīniyyah) through the Torah's descriptions — they rejected and denied his prophethood.
    • فَلَعْنَةُ اللَّهِ عَلَى الْكَافِرِينَ: The curse of Allah is upon those who disbelieved — specifically, the Jews who rejected the Seal of the Messengers.

    Ayah 90 — بِئْسَمَا اشْتَرَوْا بِهِ أَنْفُسَهُمْ

    • بِئْسَ is a verb of censure (fi'l dhamm), contrasting with نِعْمَ (fi'l madḥ — verb of praise).
    • "What a miserable transaction with which they sold themselves" — they sold their souls for the worst possible price: disbelief in the Quran.
    • Root cause of their rejection: Ḥasad (envy) — they resented that Allah sent His final revelation to a nation other than the Jews.
    • Baghī: Transgression/resentment — they did not want Allah's grace to descend upon anyone from outside their nation.
    • Result: فَبَاءُوا بِغَضَبٍ عَلَى غَضَبٍ — They incurred wrath upon wrath:
    • First wrath: For distorting and amending the Torah.
    • Second wrath: For rejecting Prophet Muhammad ﷺ and the Quran.
    • وَلِلْكَافِرِينَ عَذَابٌ مُهِينٌ: They will face a severe and humiliating punishment, appropriate because the root of their kufr was takabbur (arrogance) and ḥasad — therefore they are met with humiliation and degradation.

    Ayat 91 — نُؤْمِنُ بِمَا أُنْزِلَ عَلَيْنَا

    • When called to believe in the Quran, Bani Israel responded: "We believe only in what was revealed to us (the Torah). The Torah is sufficient."
    • وَيَكْفُرُونَ بِمَا وَرَاءَهُ: They disbelieve in everything beyond it — meaning the Quran and other books.
    • Allah's rebuttal: The Quran is al-ḥaqq and it confirms the original Torah — so their rejection of the Quran is internally contradictory.

    Ayah 91 (continued) — Challenge to Their Claim of Being True Believers

    • Allah commands Prophet Muhammad ﷺ to ask: فَلِمَ تَقْتُلُونَ أَنْبِيَاءَ اللَّهِ مِنْ قَبْلُ إِنْ كُنْتُمْ مُؤْمِنِينَ
    • "If you are true believers in the Torah, why did you kill Allah's prophets before?"
    • A true believer in a messenger follows him — not kills him. The claim of faith is therefore self-refuting.

    Ayah 92 — وَلَقَدْ جَاءَكُمْ مُوسَى بِالْبَيِّنَاتِ

    • Musa ﷺ came with clear proofs and miracles, yet in his absence (during his 40 days/nights on Mount Tur), Bani Israel worshipped the golden calf.
    • ثُمَّ اتَّخَذْتُمُ الْعِجْلَ مِنْ بَعْدِهِ وَأَنْتُمْ ظَالِمُونَ: They were wrongdoers who crossed all limits.
    • This event is also referenced in Surah Ṭā Hā and other places in the Quran.

    Ayat 93–98 — The Covenant Under Mount Tur

    • Allah reminded Bani Israel of the covenant taken when Mount Tur was physically lifted over their heads.
    • The command was: خُذُوا مَا آتَيْنَاكُمْ بِقُوَّةٍ — "Hold firmly to what We have given you."
    • They expressed outward acceptance (faaẓharū al-qabūl wal-ṭā'ah), but later returned to disbelief and disobedience.
    • Their infamous declaration: سَمِعْنَا وَعَصَيْنَا — "We heard and we disobeyed" — the ultimate expression of their defiance.
    • وَأُشْرِبُوا فِي قُلُوبِهِمُ الْعِجْلَ: The love of the calf's worship was infused/absorbed deep into their hearts. The verb ushriba (made to drink) conveys how completely this love permeated them.

    Grammar and Language Notes

    • الْكَثِيرَ مِنَ الرُّسُلِ vs. كَثِيرٌ مِنَ الرُّسُلِ: The definite article (al-) in الْكَثِيرَ makes it "the specific large number" — indicating a known, specific, and great quantity, rather than an indefinite "many."
    • قَفَّى (from the root ق-ف-و): Literally means to follow someone's footprints/tracks. Hence, "We followed Musa with messengers" means We sent messengers who traced/followed his path.
    • أَيَّدَ / يُؤَيِّدُ: Synonymous with قَوَّى (to strengthen) and شَدَّدَ (to fortify).
    • اسْتَكْبَرَ / يَسْتَكْبِرُ / اسْتِكْبَاراً: Form X verb — to consider oneself great; to become arrogant.
    • غُلْف: Plural of غِلَاف. The same pattern (CuCC) is a standard broken plural for certain word forms.
    • لَعَنَ / يَلْعَنُ / لَعْناً: To curse. Meaning technically: طَرَدَهُ وَأَبْعَدَهُ مِنْ رَحْمَتِهِ — to drive away and expel from His mercy.
    • بِئْسَ and نِعْمَ: Special verbs (af'āl al-madḥ wal-dhamm). نِعْمَ = verb of praise (fi'l madḥ); بِئْسَ = verb of censure (fi'l dhamm). They do not conjugate in the standard pattern.
    • أُشْرِبُوا: Passive form of أَشْرَبَ (to give someone to drink). Metaphorical: the love was "poured into" their hearts the way a drink is absorbed — it mixed thoroughly with them.
    • وَأَى / يَعِي / وَعْياً: To comprehend, to take in mentally. The verb is defective (nāqiṣ). تَعِي (feminine) agrees with قُلُوب (feminine plural). The student asked about this verb, and the teacher confirmed: وَأَى يَعِي means "to comprehend/understand" — the heart comprehends.
    • وَ in وَهُوَ الْحَقُّ: Here wāw functions as a wāw al-ḥāl (circumstantial wāw), meaning "while it is the truth" — expressing the irony that they reject it despite it being true.

    Lessons and Practical Takeaways

    1. Blessings demand gratitude, not ingratitude. The consistent lesson from Bani Israel's history is that meeting divine blessings with kufr and misconduct brings curses and punishment, not more blessing.
    2. Envy (ḥasad) corrupts the heart and leads to rejecting truth. The Jews' rejection of Islam was not intellectual — they had full knowledge of the Prophet ﷺ from their own scripture. The root was jealousy. Envy blinds a person to truth.
    3. Claiming to believe is not enough — actions must match. Bani Israel claimed belief in the Torah, yet killed prophets. A true believer follows the messenger; they cannot kill them. Faith without conformity of action is a contradiction.
    4. Agreements and covenants carry tremendous weight. Even when a covenant was taken forcibly (mountain over their heads), breaking it brought lasting consequences. We must honor our commitments to Allah.
    5. "Sami'na wa 'asayna" is the worst response a servant can give. Contrast with the Sahaba's declaration "Sami'na wa aṭa'na" (we hear and we obey). The manner in which we receive divine commands defines our character and our fate.
    6. Wrath accumulates when patterns of sin are not broken. The Jews did not receive one punishment — they accumulated wrath upon wrath because each generation repeated and deepened the sins of the previous ones.
    7. Allah's revelation is not the exclusive property of any ethnicity. Allah sends His mercy upon whomever He wills. Believing that divine guidance is exclusively for one group is a form of arrogance rejected by the Quran.
    8. Love of the dunya and attachment to false objects of worship can become deeply embedded. The image of calf-worship being "poured into" their hearts is a warning: what we repeatedly attach ourselves to spiritually becomes part of us.

    Historical Context and Stories Mentioned

    • The Chain of Prophets after Musa ﷺ: Allah sent many prophets among Bani Israel following Musa ﷺ. The last in this chain specifically sent to them was Isa ibn Maryam ﷺ, supported with miraculous signs and Jibreel ﷺ.
    • Isa ibn Maryam's Miracles ﷺ: Referenced in this lesson from elsewhere in the Quran — healing the congenitally blind (akmah), healing lepers (abraṣ), and raising the dead, all by Allah's permission.
    • The Jews' Pre-Islamic Supplication for Prophet Muhammad ﷺ: The Jews of Arabia, before the Prophet's ﷺ arrival, used to pray for victory against their enemies (mushrikeen of Arabia) by invoking the coming final prophet whose description they found in their Torah. This is documented in Seerah narrations — when the Prophet ﷺ came, the very people who had spoken of him recognized him but rejected him out of jealousy.
    • Abdullah ibn Salam: Mentioned as one of the rare Jewish scholars who did recognize and accept Islam — confirming that "few would believe."
    • The Golden Calf Incident: During Musa's ﷺ 40 days and nights on Mount Tur receiving the Torah, Bani Israel constructed and worshipped a golden calf. This major event of shirk is discussed in multiple surahs (Ṭā Hā, Al-A'rāf, here in Al-Baqarah).
    • The Covenant Under Mount Tur: Allah physically raised Mount Tur over the heads of Bani Israel as a coercive condition to accept the Torah. They expressed acceptance, but later returned to rebellion. This event illustrates how they had to be compelled — their acceptance was never from the heart.

    Q&A Highlights

    Q: Why is it الْكَثِيرَ مِنَ الرُّسُلِ (with the definite article) rather than just كَثِيرًا مِنَ الرُّسُلِ?

    A: The definite article (al-) on kathīra makes it refer to a specific and well-known large number — "the many (known) messengers" — rather than an indefinite "many." It emphasizes the precise magnitude of the number of prophets sent to Bani Israel.

    Q: What is the verb وَأَى / يَعِي (wa'ā / ya'ī) and why is it تَعِي (ta'ī) here?

    A: وَأَى / يَعِي means "to comprehend, to understand." It is a defective (nāqiṣ) verb. The form تَعِي (ta'ī) is the feminine singular imperfect, agreeing with قُلُوب (hearts), which is grammatically feminine. The phrase in context means: "our hearts do not comprehend what you say, O Muhammad."
