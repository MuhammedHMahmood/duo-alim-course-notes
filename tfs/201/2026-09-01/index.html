    Tafseer 201 - Study Notes

    Date: 2026-09-01 Surah Covered: Surah Al-Baqarah (Ayat on Tawheed, Challenge of the Quran — Ayat al-Tahhaddi)

    Session Overview

    This session covered two major Quranic themes from Surah Al-Baqarah. The first portion examined the ayat establishing Allah's Tawheed (oneness) through His blessings — specifically His sending down rain and producing sustenance — and the prohibition of Shirk. The second portion examined the ayat of Tahhaddi (challenge), wherein Allah challenges those who doubt the divine origin of the Quran to produce a single surah like it. The session also included significant grammatical instruction on verb conjugation (tasreef), particularly for the verbs أَتَى (ataa) and دَعَا (da'aa), with the teacher assigning conjugation exercises as homework.

    Key Themes and Topics

    1. Tawheed al-Uluhiyyah — Allah's exclusive right to worship, established through His role as Creator and Sustainer
    2. Allah's Blessings as Proof of Tawheed — Rain, crops, and sustenance as evidence that only Allah deserves worship
    3. Prohibition of Shirk (Nid/Andad) — Not making equals or partners with Allah in worship
    4. I'jaz al-Quran — The miraculous inimitability of the Quran as proof of Prophethood
    5. Ayat al-Tahhaddi (The Challenge) — The open challenge to produce a surah like the Quran, extended to all of humanity, jinn, and idols
    6. Arabic Verb Conjugation (Tasreef) — Practical grammatical work on فَأْتُوا (fa'tuu) from أَتَى, and وَادْعُوا (wad'uu) from دَعَا

    Main Tafseer Points and Explanations

    1. Allah Begins Establishing Tawheed al-Uluhiyyah

    شَرَعَ تَعَالَى فِي بَيَانِ وَحْدَانِيَّةِ أُلُوهِيَّتِهِ

    Allah subhanahu wa ta'ala began establishing proof of the oneness of His divinity by: - Bringing creation from nothingness (إِخْرَاجِهِمْ مِنَ الْعَدَمِ): Allah brought all people out of non-existence into existence - Abundantly bestowing blessings (إِسْبَاغِهِ عَلَيْهِمُ النِّعَمَ): Allah has poured His blessings upon His servants in full

    2. Rain as Evidence of Tawheed

    The word السَّماء in this ayah means السَّحاب (clouds), not the sky in general. Allah sends rain from the clouds: - At the right time (فِي وَقْتِهِ عِنْدَ احْتِيَاجِهِمْ إِلَيْهِ): Allah knows precisely when His creation needs water and sends it at the moment of need - With this water Allah produces أَنْوَاعَ الزُّرُوعِ وَالثَّمَرَاتِ (various kinds of crops and fruits) - This sustenance is رِزْقًا لَهُمْ وَلِأَنْعَامِهِمْ — provision for human beings and their livestock

    3. Prohibition of Shirk — فَلَا تَجْعَلُوا لِلَّهِ أَنْدَادًا

    أَيْ لَا تَجْعَلُوا مَعَهُ شُرَكَاءَ مِنَ الْأَصْنَامِ وَالْبَشَرِ

    • أَنْدَاد (Andaad): plural of نِدّ — meaning an equal, partner, or rival
    • Do not make anything — idols, human beings, or any created thing — equal to Allah in power, control, or worship
    • The mushrikeen of Arabia themselves acknowledged Allah as Creator when asked; their error was setting up partners alongside Him
    • Because Allah alone creates and sustains, He alone deserves to be worshipped — فَبِهَذَا يَسْتَحِقُّ أَنْ يُعْبَدَ وَحْدَهُ

    4. Transition: From Tawheed to Proof of Prophethood and I'jaz al-Quran

    ثُمَّ ذَكَرَ تَعَالَى بَعْدَ أَدِلَّةِ التَّوْحِيدِ الْحُجَّةَ عَلَى النُّبُوَّةِ وَأَقَامَ الْبُرْهَانَ عَلَى إِعْجَازِ الْقُرْآنِ

    After the proofs of Tawheed, Allah transitions to establishing: 1. Proof of the Prophethood of Muhammad ﷺ 2. Proof of the miraculous nature of the Quran

    5. The Challenge (Ayat al-Tahhaddi) — وَإِن كُنتُمْ فِي رَيْبٍ

    وَإِن كُنتُمْ فِي رَيْبٍ مِّمَّا نَزَّلْنَا عَلَى عَبْدِنَا فَأْتُوا بِسُورَةٍ مِّن مِّثْلِهِ

    • If you are in doubt about the Quran which was sent down to عَبْدِنَا (Our servant — Prophet Muhammad ﷺ), then:
    • Bring a single surah (بِسُورَةٍ وَاحِدَةٍ) similar to it
    • The Quran is miraculous (مُعجِز) in its: بَلاغَة (rhetoric), بَيان (clarity), نَظْم (composition and arrangement), words, meanings, and legislation
    • مِن مِّثْلِهِ — "like it": the pronoun هِ refers back to the Quran already mentioned in the context

    6. The Open Challenge — Seek All the Help You Can

    وَادْعُوا شُهَدَاءَكُم مِّن دُونِ اللَّهِ

    • Call upon all your supporters and helpers to assist you in opposing the Quran
    • The challenge is open to: الإِنس (humankind), الجِنّ (jinn), and الآلِهَة (idols and false gods)
    • The meaning: اسْتَعِينُوا بِمَن شِئْتُم غَيْرَهُ تَعَالَى — seek help from whomever you wish other than Allah

    7. The Implied Answer — جَوَابُهُ مَحْذُوف

    The grammatical answer to the conditional (إِن) is omitted (مَحذوف) because it is already clear from context: you cannot and will never produce anything like it. This is confirmed by the next ayah: وَلَن تَفْعَلُوا — "and you will never be able to do so."

    8. Consequence of Failing the Challenge

    Those who deny the Quran and cannot meet its challenge are warned of Hellfire. The rhetorical question is implied: why then do you not fear Allah, who has prepared punishment for those who reject His Book?

    Grammar and Language Notes

    Verb: أَتَى (ataa) — "to come"; with بِ — "to bring"

    Key rule: أَتَى alone = to come. أَتَى + بِ (sila) = to bring something. - Example in ayah: فَأْتُوا بِسُورَةٍ — "bring a surah" (not merely "come with")

    Form in ayah: فَأْتُوا — this is فِعل الأَمر (imperative), Jama' Mudakkar (masculine plural)

    Full conjugation of Maadi (أَتَى):

    Form Arabic
    Wahid Mudakkar Gha'ib أَتَى
    Tasniya Mudakkar Gha'ib أَتَيَا
    Jama' Mudakkar Gha'ib أَتَوا
    Wahid Mu'annas Gha'ib أَتَتْ
    Tasniya Mu'annas أَتَتَا
    Jama' Mu'annas أَتَيْنَ
    Wahid Mudakkar Hadir أَتَيْتَ
    Tasniya Hadir أَتَيْتُمَا
    Jama' Mudakkar Hadir أَتَيْتُمْ
    Wahid Mu'annas Hadir أَتَيْتِ
    Tasniya Mu'annas Hadir أَتَيْتُمَا
    Jama' Mu'annas Hadir أَتَيْتُنَّ
    Mutakallim Wahid أَتَيْتُ
    Mutakallim Jama' أَتَيْنَا

    Verb: دَعَا (da'aa) — "to call, to invite, to call upon"

    Form in ayah: وَادْعُوا — فِعل الأَمر, Jama' Mudakkar

    Assignment given: Students are to write the full conjugation of both أَتَى and دَعَا across all four forms: 1. فِعل الماضي (Maadi — past tense) 2. فِعل المُضارِع (Mudaari' — present/future) 3. فِعل الأَمر (Amr — imperative) 4. فِعل النَّهي (Nahi — prohibitive)

    For each: conjugate in the order — Gha'ib Mudakkar (6 forms) → Gha'ib Mu'annas → Hadir Mudakkar → Hadir Mu'annas → Mutakallim Wahid → Mutakallim Jama'

    Pronoun Reference (Zamir)

    Student question addressed: Why does مِن مِّثْلِهِ use a pronoun (هِ) referring to the Quran, rather than saying مِن مِثْلِ القُرآن explicitly?

    Answer: In Arabic, when a noun has already been mentioned in the discourse (here: the Quran sent down to the Prophet), subsequent references use a pronoun (zamir). The هِ refers back to what was already established — the Quran as a whole — not to any one surah. The challenge is to bring any surah comparable to the Quran in its qualities.

    Recommended Reference Book

    The teacher recommended a verb conjugation reference book (a PDF available for free download) that lists all conjugation patterns for Arabic verbs, including irregular verbs (verbs with و, ي, ء, or doubled letters). Students should use this to verify their conjugations.

    Lessons and Practical Takeaways

    1. Allah's blessings — rain, crops, sustenance — are not coincidental; they are deliberate, timed gifts from the Creator. Recognizing this should lead to Tawheed and gratitude, not Shirk.

    2. Knowing Allah created and sustains everything logically demands that He alone be worshipped. Even the Arab mushrikeen acknowledged Allah as Creator — their error was associating others with Him. True Tawheed means no partners in worship.

    3. The Quran's i'jaz (inimitability) is a living, ongoing proof. The challenge to produce a comparable surah has stood for over 1,400 years unanswered. This is a rational basis for certainty in the Quran's divine origin.

    4. For students of Arabic and Tafseer: mastering verb conjugation (tasreef) is non-negotiable. Misidentifying a verb's form (Maadi vs. Mudaari' vs. Amr, or Gha'ib vs. Hadir vs. Mutakallim) leads to mistranslation. Every verb encountered in the Quran or classical texts should be verified through the dictionary and sarf.

    5. The omitted (mahdhoof) answer in Arabic conditional sentences is a rhetorical device — the silence itself communicates the point powerfully: the challenge cannot be met.

    Historical Context and Stories Mentioned

    • The Arab Mushrikeen and their Shirk: The Quraysh and other Arab polytheists acknowledged Allah as the sole Creator of the heavens and earth (this is confirmed in multiple Quranic ayat). Despite this, they set up idols, human beings, and other created things as partners (andaad) alongside Allah in worship. The ayat here address this contradiction directly.

    • The Historical Challenge of the Quran: At the time of revelation, the Quraysh claimed the Quran was composed by a human being — by Prophet Muhammad ﷺ himself. Allah's challenge to produce a comparable surah was directed at native Arabic speakers who possessed the highest command of the language. Despite their eloquence, their poets, their orators, and their tribal pride, no individual or group across all centuries has produced anything matching the Quran's literary, legislative, and spiritual qualities.

    Q&A Highlights

    Q (Student): What does شَرِيعَة / شَرْع (shari') mean in this context?

    A (Teacher): Shara' / Shari'ah here refers to legislation — the laws, rulings, and regulations contained in the Quran. It is one of the dimensions in which the Quran is miraculous: its legislative system is complete and perfect, something no human could have composed.

    Q (Student): Why does the ayah say مِن مِّثْلِهِ (like it / like the Quran) rather than specifying "like the Quran's surah"? Since the Quran is not just one surah, wouldn't the comparison need to be to a specific surah?

    A (Teacher): The pronoun هِ refers back to the Quran as a whole entity already established in the discourse. The challenge is: bring any surah that is comparable to the Quran — i.e., equal to any surah of the Quran in its rhetoric, composition, meanings, and eloquence. The pronoun does not mean the Quran is one surah; it means "bring something resembling what has already been described."

    Q (Student - follow-up): So is it saying a surah similar to the Quran, rather than a surah similar to a surah of the Quran?

    A (Teacher): Yes — bring a surah that is like the Quran in its qualities. The standard being set is the Quran itself as a whole miraculous entity. Any surah you attempt must match the Quran's level — which no one has been able to do.

    Ongoing pedagogical note from teacher: Students were repeatedly reminded that conjugation errors prevent accurate translation. When reading any Arabic text — Quran, Hadith, or classical scholarship — every verb must be identified for: its root, its bab (pattern), its tense (Maadi/Mudaari'/Amr/Nahi), and its person/number/gender. This is the foundation without which tafseer study cannot proceed correctly.
