    Tafseer 201 - Study Notes

    Date: 2026-11-21 Surah Covered: Surah Al-Baqarah, Ayat 116–123 (Page 90)

    Session Overview

    This session covered Surah Al-Baqarah ayat 116–123, focusing on Allah's refutation of the false claims made by Jews, Christians, and Mushrikeen regarding Allah having a son or daughter. The session also addressed the stubbornness of the Quraysh in demanding direct speech from Allah, the warning to Prophet Muhammad ﷺ regarding the Jews and Christians, the description of true believers among Ahl al-Kitab, and the reminder to Bani Israel of Allah's favors and the gravity of the Day of Resurrection.

    Key Themes and Topics

    1. Refutation of false claims about Allah's offspring — Jews claimed Uzair was the son of God; Christians claimed Isa was the son of God; Mushrikeen claimed the angels were daughters of Allah. Allah refuted all three.
    2. Allah's absolute sovereignty and power — Everything in the heavens and earth belongs to Allah and is obedient to Him; creation occurs instantly by His command "Kun."
    3. Badie' — Allah as the Originator — Allah created the heavens and earth with no prior example or template.
    4. The arrogance of the Quraysh — Their demand for direct divine speech was rooted in arrogance, not genuine inquiry.
    5. Consolation for the Prophet ﷺ — Previous nations made the same demands of their prophets; the Prophet's duty is only to convey.
    6. Warning against following Jews and Christians — They will never be pleased until the Prophet follows their religion; the warning against following their desires.
    7. True believers among Ahl al-Kitab — Those who recite the Torah and Injil as they were truly revealed and accepted Islam.
    8. Address to Bani Israel — Reminder of Allah's favors, their status as a preferred nation in the time of Musa and Isa, and the terrors of the Day of Resurrection.
    9. Day of Resurrection — No soul will avail another, no ransom will be accepted, no intercession will benefit the disbelievers.

    Main Tafseer Points and Explanations

    Ayah 116 — وَقَالُوا اتَّخَذَ اللَّهُ وَلَدًا سُبْحَانَهُ

    • Three groups are referenced here:
    • Jews claimed Uzair (عليه السلام) was the son of God.
    • Christians claimed Isa/Masih (عليه السلام) was the son of God.
    • Mushrikeen claimed the angels were daughters of Allah.
    • Allah refutes all three claims with سُبْحَانَهُ — He is utterly free and exalted above all such falsehoods.
    • Context (Munasabah): This ayah follows the previous set of ayat that exposed the slanders of Jews and Christians (including their claim that Paradise is exclusive to them). These ayat now address the even greater slander of attributing a son or offspring to Allah.

    "بَلْ لَهُ مَا فِي السَّمَوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ"

    • The word بَلْ (bal) is a rhetorical device in Balagha known as إِضْرَاب (idhrab): it negates what came before it and strongly affirms what follows.
    • The truth established: everything in the heavens and the earth belongs to Allah — including Isa (عليه السلام), Uzair (عليه السلام), and the angels themselves. They are all His creation and under His dominion.
    • وَكُلٌّ لَهُ قَانِتُونَ — Every single thing is submissive and obedient to Allah. Nothing is beyond His creation, decree, or will.

    Ayah 117 — بَدِيعُ السَّمَوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ

    • بَدِيع is an attribute (sifah) of Allah meaning the Originator — He created the heavens and earth without any prior model or example.
    • Allah originated all of existence from nothing; there was no prior template for the universe.

    "وَإِذَا قَضَى أَمْرًا فَإِنَّمَا يَقُولُ لَهُ كُنْ فَيَكُونُ"

    • When Allah decrees a matter, He merely says "Be!" and it is.
    • Creation happens without impossibility, delay, or prevention — the thing comes into existence in the blink of an eye (بِلَمْحِ الْبَصَرِ).
    • Cross-reference: Surah Al-Qamar — "وَمَا أَمْرُنَا إِلَّا وَاحِدَةٌ كَلَمْحٍ بِالْبَصَرِ"
    • Implication: A being of such absolute power and self-sufficiency has absolutely no need for a son, daughter, or partner. The very concept of offspring implies need and dependency — attributes entirely inapplicable to Allah.

    Ayah 118 — وَقَالَ الَّذِينَ لَا يَعْلَمُونَ

    • "Those who do not know" = the Mushrikeen of Quraysh, the unlearned who have no revealed scripture.
    • They demanded: "Why does Allah not speak to us directly (مُشَافَهَةً), or why does a sign (آية) not come to us confirming your prophethood?"
    • This was said out of arrogance (استكبار) and stubbornness (عناد), not genuine searching for truth — many proofs were already available.
    • Allah's response: Those before them from previous nations said the exact same things to their prophets. Their hearts are alike — equal in blindness (عمى), stubbornness, and denial of prophets.
    • Consolation for the Prophet ﷺ: This parallel history reassures the Prophet that rejecting prophets is a repeated pattern — do not grieve over it.

    "قَدْ بَيَّنَّا الْآيَاتِ لِقَوْمٍ يُوقِنُونَ"

    • Allah has clarified His signs and established His proofs — but only those who seek truth and certainty (يَطْلُبُونَ الْحَقَّ وَالْيَقِينَ) will benefit from them.
    • All evidences speak clearly of the truth of what the Prophet ﷺ brought.

    Ayah 119 — إِنَّا أَرْسَلْنَاكَ بِالْحَقِّ بَشِيرًا وَنَذِيرًا

    • Allah reassures the Prophet ﷺ: "We have sent you with the truth — with the enlightened and upright Sharia."
    • His role: Bashir (bearer of glad tidings of Paradise for believers) and Nadhir (warner of Hell for disbelievers).
    • وَلَا تُسْأَلُ عَنْ أَصْحَابِ الْجَحِيمِ — The Prophet ﷺ will not be held accountable for those who refuse to believe. His duty is only tabligh (conveying the message); the reckoning belongs to Allah alone.

    Ayah 120 — وَلَنْ تَرْضَى عَنْكَ الْيَهُودُ وَلَا النَّصَارَى

    • Jews and Christians will never be pleased with the Prophet ﷺ unless he follows their religion.
    • Their wish: that he abandon Islam — the radiant, upright religion — and follow their crooked, deviated religion.
    • The Prophet ﷺ is commanded to declare clearly: "The true guidance is the guidance of Allah (هُدَى اللَّهِ هُوَ الْهُدَى)" — anything other than Islam is deviation (ضلال).
    • Warning: If the Prophet were to follow their desires after the truth has come to him, he would have no protector (وَلِيّ) and no helper (نَصِير) from Allah. This is a hypothetical warning, not an actual possibility — it establishes the severity of the matter.

    Ayah 121 — الَّذِينَ آتَيْنَاهُمُ الْكِتَابَ يَتْلُونَهُ حَقَّ تِلَاوَتِهِ

    • Refers to those among Ahl al-Kitab who accepted Islam.
    • They recite the Quran (and their original scriptures) as they were truly revealed — without addition, deletion, or distortion.
    • These are the true believers. In contrast, those who distorted the Torah and Injil by adding and removing from them are not among the true believers.
    • وَمَنْ يَكْفُرْ بِهِ فَأُولَٰئِكَ هُمُ الْخَاسِرُونَ — Whoever disbelieves in the Quran has lost both this world and the Hereafter.

    Ayat 122–123 — يَا بَنِي إِسْرَائِيلَ اذْكُرُوا نِعْمَتِيَ

    • Renewed direct address to Bani Israel (this address also appeared earlier in the surah).
    • Reminder of Allah's favors: Allah bestowed great blessings upon Bani Israel and their forefathers, and preferred them over all the nations of their time (in the era of Musa and Isa عليهما السلام).
    • وَأَنِّي فَضَّلْتُكُمْ عَلَى الْعَالَمِينَ — This preference was for the ummah of that specific time period, not a permanent or universal superiority.
    • Fear the Day of Resurrection, described as الْيَوْمَ الرَّهِيم (the terrible day), when:
    • No soul will avail another soul at all.
    • No ransom (عَدْل / فِدَاء) will be accepted.
    • No intercession (شَفَاعَة) will benefit the disbelievers — because they disbelieved in Allah and rejected the prophets.
    • No one can remove Allah's punishment from them.
    • Every soul is held in pledge (رَهِينَة) for what it has earned.

    Grammar and Language Notes

    • بَلْ (bal) — Idhrab (إِضْرَاب): A technical term in Arabic rhetoric (Balagha). When بَلْ appears in this usage, it negates the preceding statement and affirmatively establishes the opposing truth. Example: "Not as they claimed — but rather, to Allah belongs all that is in the heavens and earth."

    • بَدِيع: An ism (noun/adjective) functioning as a sifah (attribute) of Allah. Derived from the root meaning to originate something entirely new.

    • قَنَتَ / يَقْنُتُ / قُنُوتًا: Verb meaning to be obedient, submissive, devoted. The word قُنُوت in salah (the du'a in witr) comes from the same root.

    • كُنْ فَيَكُونُ: "كُنْ" is a command form (fi'l amr) of the verb كَانَ. "فَيَكُونُ" is the resultant action — "and it is/becomes." The sequence indicates immediate fulfillment upon divine command.

    • لَا تُسْأَلُ: This is in the majhool (passive) form — "you will not be questioned/asked." The active form would be يَسْأَلُ (yas'alu); the passive is يُسْأَلُ (yus'alu).

    • رَضِيَ / يَرْضَى / رِضًا: Verb meaning to be pleased or satisfied. لَنْ تَرْضَى = will never be pleased (لَنْ gives emphatic future negation).

    • كِتَاب — Mubtada and Khabar: In the structure of Ayah 121, الَّذِينَ آتَيْنَاهُمُ الْكِتَابَ is the mubtada (subject), and the khabar (predicate) describes the quality of these true believers — they recite it as it truly deserves.

    • عَدْل: In everyday Arabic means justice, but in this Quranic context it means ransom or compensation.

    Lessons and Practical Takeaways

    1. Tawhid is non-negotiable. The claims of Jews, Christians, and Mushrikeen regarding Allah having offspring represent the gravest form of falsehood (iftiraa'). A Muslim must be firm in the belief that Allah is absolutely free from any offspring, partner, or likeness.

    2. Allah's power requires no support. The concept of a son or daughter implies weakness and need. Allah's power is so absolute that the entire universe comes into existence by a single word. He needs nothing and no one.

    3. The Prophet's role is to convey, not to compel. The duty of Tabligh (conveying the message) is the Prophet's responsibility — but guidance is from Allah. This principle extends to da'wah workers today: do your part, but do not carry the burden of others' disbelief.

    4. Truth-seekers benefit from evidence; arrogance blinds. The Quraysh's demand for a "sign" was not genuine curiosity — it was arrogance. Evidence alone does not guide; the heart's orientation toward truth is what matters.

    5. Seeking approval from opponents of Islam is futile. Jews and Christians will not be satisfied until one abandons Islam. A Muslim must derive their sense of correctness from Allah's guidance, not from the approval of others.

    6. Don't compromise the deen for popularity. The ayah warns even the Prophet ﷺ in the strongest terms: following the desires of those who oppose Islam, after the truth has been made clear, would bring divine punishment with no helper.

    7. True recitation is faithful recitation. Those praised in Ayah 121 did not distort the Book. A lesson for Muslims today: preserve the Quran and its meanings faithfully; do not bend it to suit desires.

    8. Gratitude for divine favor carries responsibility. Bani Israel's status as a preferred nation came with obligations — and they failed them. Allah's blessings are not unconditional entitlements.

    9. Prepare for the Day of Resurrection. On that day, no wealth, lineage, intercession (for disbelievers), or connection will help. Each soul is held accountable solely for its own deeds.

    Historical Context and Stories Mentioned

    • Uzair (عليه السلام): The Jews claimed he was the son of God. He is mentioned in Surah At-Tawbah (9:30). His exact identity is debated by scholars, but the claim about him represents the same deviation of elevating a prophet to divine status.

    • Isa/Masih (عليه السلام): The Christians' claim that he was the son of God is a central point of refutation throughout the Quran.

    • Bani Israel's lineage: The name "Israel" is the name of Yaqub (عليه السلام). His children and descendants are "Bani Israel" — the children of Israel. Their favored status came from being the descendants of prophets (Yaqub, Ishaq, Ibrahim عليهم السلام) — this was Allah's choice and grace, not something they earned by their own merit. Despite this, they responded to Allah's favors with disobedience and ingratitude.

    • The Mushrikeen of Quraysh: Their demand for direct divine speech or a spectacular sign was an expression of their leadership arrogance — they considered themselves the elite of Arabia and expected special treatment even in prophethood.

    • Previous nations who rejected their prophets: Allah draws a direct parallel between the Quraysh and earlier rejecting nations, whose hearts were identical in blindness, stubbornness, and denial.

    • Surah Al-Qamar cross-reference (54:50): "وَمَا أَمْرُنَا إِلَّا وَاحِدَةٌ كَلَمْحٍ بِالْبَصَرِ" — cited to reinforce the concept of Kun fa-yakun and the immediacy of Allah's command.

    Q&A Highlights

    Q: Why were Bani Israel considered the best nation / preferred by Allah?

    A: Their status came specifically because they were the descendants of the Prophets — from Yaqub (عليه السلام) (whose name was Israel) through a lineage of prophets. Allah granted them this special position as a favor (فضل) — it was not something they earned by their own deeds, but a grace from Allah due to their prophetic lineage. This preference was specific to their time (the era of Musa and Isa عليهما السلام) and did not make them permanently or universally superior, especially since they subsequently responded to Allah's blessings with disobedience and rejection of the prophets.
