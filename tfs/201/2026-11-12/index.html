    Tafseer 201 - Study Notes

    Date: 2026-11-12 Surah Covered: Surah Al-Baqarah, Ayaat 93–98 (Page 79)

    Session Overview

    This session continued the tafseer of Surah Al-Baqarah, picking up at ayah 93. The class focused on two major condemnations of Bani Israel addressed directly to Prophet Muhammad ﷺ: (1) their deep-rooted attachment to worshipping the golden calf, described metaphorically as being "drunk into their hearts," and (2) their false claim of exclusive ownership of Paradise. The session examined the linguistic and rhetorical force of Quranic phrasing used to refute these claims.

    Key Themes and Topics

    1. Love of calf-worship penetrating the hearts of Bani Israel — the tafseer of وَشُرِبُوا فِي قُلُوبِهِمُ الْعِجْلَ
    2. The root cause: kufr (disbelief) — their calf-worship was enabled by pre-existing disbelief in their hearts
    3. Critique of Bani Israel's false claim of Iman — قُلْ بِئْسَ مَا يَأْمُرُكُمْ بِهِ إِيمَانُكُمْ
    4. Bani Israel's false claim of exclusive Paradise — refuted through a challenge: then wish for death
    5. Rhetorical style of the Quran — use of fi'l al-madh (praise verb) and fi'l al-dhamm (blame verb)

    Important Vocabulary

    Arabic Term Meaning

    اِمْتَزَجَ Was mixed, blended

    Main Tafseer Points and Explanations

    1. وَشُرِبُوا فِي قُلُوبِهِمُ الْعِجْلَ — "The calf was drunk into their hearts"

    • The verb شُرِبُوا is in the passive (majhool) form — meaning they were made to drink; i.e., the love of calf-worship was instilled deep within them.
    • The full meaning: أَنَّ حُبَّ عِبَادَةِ الْعِجْلِ اِمْتَزَجَ بِدِمَائِهِمْ وَدَخَلَ فِي قُلُوبِهِمْ — the love of worshipping the calf was mixed with their blood and entered into their hearts.
    • Two analogies given to illustrate this depth of penetration:
    • Dyeing a garment: dye enters and becomes inseparable from the fabric
    • Water entering the body: water mixes into one's very being when bathing
    • Just as these cannot be separated, the love of calf-worship had become inseparable from their hearts.

    2. The Role of Kufr (بِكُفْرِهِمْ)

    • Allah states this happened because of their disbelief (kufr).
    • The underlying disbelief was already in their hearts. When Prophet Musa ﷺ went to Mount Tur for 40 nights, they reverted to worshipping the calf precisely because that kufr never left them.
    • The sequence: pre-existing kufr → absence of Musa → return to calf-worship.

    3. قُلْ بِئْسَ مَا يَأْمُرُكُمْ بِهِ إِيمَانُكُمْ — "Say: Evil is what your faith commands you"

    • Allah commands Prophet Muhammad ﷺ: قُلْ لَهُمْ عَلَى سَبِيلِ التَّحَكُّمِ بِهِمْ — tell them by way of scolding and mockery.
    • Bani Israel / the Jews used to claim: We are the true believers; we alone are the Mu'mineen.
    • The Quran challenges this claim directly: if you are the true believers, how can your iman command you to worship a calf?
    • The implied conclusion: لَسْتُمْ بِمُؤْمِنِينَ — you are not true believers — لِأَنَّ الْإِيمَانَ لَا يَأْمُرُ بِعِبَادَةِ الْعِجْلِ — because true faith does not command worship of the calf.

    4. Refutation of the Claim of Exclusive Paradise

    • Bani Israel also claimed that Jannah (Paradise) is exclusively for them — no other nation or people would enter it.
    • Allah commands the Prophet ﷺ to challenge them: If the abode of the Hereafter is exclusively for you with Allah, with no one else sharing in its bliss — then wish for death.
    • This was a logical challenge: if you truly believe Paradise is yours alone and guaranteed, you should eagerly wish to leave this world.
    • The refutation is implicit — their unwillingness to wish for death exposes the falsity of their claim.

    Grammar and Language Notes

    • وَشُرِبُوا — Passive voice (فِعْل مَجْهُول, fi'l majhool). The doer is unspecified; the action is done to them. This construction emphasizes how thoroughly the love of calf-worship was instilled in them — it was not merely something they chose consciously but something that had permeated their very being.

    • بِئْسَ and نِعْمَ — These are two special fi'l (verbs) in Arabic grammar:

    • نِعْمَ = fi'l madh (فِعْل مَدْح) — verb of praise: "How excellent is…!"
    • بِئْسَ = fi'l dhamm (فِعْل ذَمّ) — verb of condemnation/blame: "How evil is…!"

    • Here بِئْسَ is used to express the utter contemptibility of an iman that leads to worshipping a calf.

    • خَالِصَةً — Accusative of state (حَال), meaning "exclusively" or "in a pure/unshared manner." Reinforces the exclusivity of their claim.

    Lessons and Practical Takeaways

    1. Sin can become habitual to the point of feeling natural. The image of kufr and love of falsehood being "drunk into the heart" and "mixed with the blood" warns believers to guard against allowing sins to take root — once deeply embedded, they are very hard to remove.

    2. True iman has standards — it cannot coexist with shirk. The Quran's challenge — "What evil faith is this that commands you to worship an idol?" — teaches that any claim of faith must be measured against one's actions. Faith that leads to shirk is no faith at all.

    3. Beware of false religious entitlement. The Jews' claim that Paradise belonged exclusively to them was exposed as self-deception. No group has a guaranteed claim to salvation by virtue of lineage or label — it is belief and righteous action that matter.

    4. The Quran uses rhetorical challenges to expose falsehood. The "wish for death" test is a divine rhetorical device: it did not require a miracle to refute their claim — it only required their honest self-examination.

    5. Absence of righteous leadership creates spiritual vulnerability. Bani Israel fell into calf-worship precisely when Prophet Musa ﷺ was absent. This underscores the importance of continuous guidance and not relying on faith that is externally dependent.

    Historical Context and Stories Mentioned

    • The 40 Nights at Mount Tur: Prophet Musa ﷺ left Bani Israel for 40 nights to receive the Torah at Mount Tur (Sinai). During his absence, the Samaritan (Al-Samiri) fashioned the golden calf and Bani Israel, whose kufr had never fully left them, reverted to worshipping it. This episode is what وَشُرِبُوا فِي قُلُوبِهِمُ الْعِجْلَ refers back to.

    • Jewish claim of exclusive Iman: The Jews of Madinah at the time of the Prophet ﷺ insisted they were the only true believers and that the blessings of the Hereafter were reserved solely for them — a claim the Quran systematically dismantles across multiple ayaat of Surah Al-Baqarah.
