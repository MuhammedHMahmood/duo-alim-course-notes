    Tafseer 201 - Study Notes

    Date: 2026-08-13 Surah Covered: Surah Al-Baqarah, Ayat 3–7 (Page 32 of the tafseer text)

    Session Overview

    This session continued the tafseer of Surah Al-Baqarah, focusing on the remaining qualities of the Muttaqeen (the God-fearing) described in Ayat 3–5, followed by an introduction to Ayat 6–7 concerning the disbelievers (Kuffar). The sheikh read and explained the Arabic text of the tafseer book directly, with students participating in reading. Key qualities covered included belief in the Unseen (Iman bil Ghayb), establishment of Salah (Iqamatus Salah), spending from provisions (Infaq), belief in revealed scriptures, and belief in the Hereafter. The session concluded partway through Ayah 7, with the full explanation of Khatamallahu deferred to the next class.

    Key Themes and Topics

    1. The qualities of the Muttaqeen — five attributes enumerated across Ayat 3–5
    2. Iman bil Ghayb — believing in what cannot be perceived through the senses
    3. Iqamatus Salah — what it truly means to "establish" prayer (not merely perform it)
    4. Infaq and Zakat — the scope of spending: obligatory vs. voluntary; Ibn Jarir vs. Ibn Abbas on the ayah's range
    5. Belief in all divine scriptures and all prophets — without differentiation
    6. Yaqeen in the Akhirah — the higher degree of certainty required for belief in the Hereafter
    7. Munasabah (contextual link) — why the Quran pairs descriptions of believers with descriptions of disbelievers
    8. The Kuffar's sealed hearts — the reason their Iman cannot enter (Khatamallahu ala qulubihim)
    9. Consolation to the Prophet ﷺ — the pedagogical function of Ayah 6

    Main Tafseer Points and Explanations

    Ayah 3 — The Three Qualities of the Muttaqeen

    1. الَّذِينَ يُؤْمِنُونَ بِالْغَيْبِ — Those who believe in the Unseen

    • Ghayb here is a theological/creedal term, not merely "absence."
    • It refers to everything that cannot be perceived through the five senses.
    • Examples: the Resurrection (al-Ba'th), Paradise (al-Jannah), Hellfire (an-Nar), the Bridge (as-Sirat), the Reckoning (al-Hisab), and everything else the Quran and the Prophet ﷺ have informed us of.
    • We affirm these things not by sense-experience but by the testimony of Allah and His Messenger.

    2. وَيُقِيمُونَ الصَّلَاةَ — And they establish the prayer

    • Iqamatus Salah means performing the prayer in the most complete and perfect manner — not merely going through the motions.
    • This includes:
    • Fulfilling all conditions (shurut) of prayer
    • Completing all pillars (arkan) of prayer
    • Maintaining khushu' (humility and submissiveness)
    • Observing the etiquettes (adab) of prayer
    • Ibn Abbas (رضي الله عنه) explained iqamatuha as: completing the ruku' (bowing), sujud (prostration), tilawah (recitation), and khushu' (humility).
    • Other mufassiroon extended the meaning further: managing the mosque, calling people to prayer, and organizing all affairs related to salah — all fall under "establishing" it.

    3. وَمِمَّا رَزَقْنَاهُمْ يُنْفِقُونَ — And they spend from what We have provided them

    • Razaqnahum = what We have given them (money, provisions, property).
    • Yunfiqoon = they spend from it; they give charity in all good and virtuous ways (fi wujuhil khayr wal ihsan).
    • The ayah is general ('ammah) — it encompasses all forms of giving.
    • Scholarly difference on the scope:
    • Ibn Jarir at-Tabari (رحمه الله): chose the view that the ayah includes all types of spending — obligatory Zakat, voluntary Sadaqah, and all other charitable expenditures.
    • Ibn Abbas (رضي الله عنه): understood the ayah to refer specifically to the obligatory Zakat al-Amwal only.
    • Ibn Kathir (رحمه الله): both the obligatory expenses (nafaqat wajibah) and the obligatory Zakat are included in this ayah. He notes that both Fard and Wajib giving fall under الَّذِينَ يُنفِقُونَ.

    Why does Allah frequently pair Salah and Infaq?

    Ibn Kathir (رحمه الله) explains: - As-Salah is the right of Allah (haqqullah); it contains tawhid, glorification (tamjid), and praise (thana') of Allah. - Al-Infaq is the right of the people/creation (haqqul makhluq); it expresses kindness (ihsan) toward the creations of Allah. - Together they represent the two fundamental axes of Islamic conduct: fulfilling one's duty to God and to fellow creation. This pairing appears throughout the Quran (e.g., أَقِيمُوا الصَّلَاةَ وَآتُوا الزَّكَاةَ).

    Ayah 4 — Two Further Qualities

    4. وَالَّذِينَ يُؤْمِنُونَ بِمَا أُنزِلَ إِلَيْكَ وَمَا أُنزِلَ مِنْ قَبْلِكَ And those who believe in what was revealed to you and what was revealed before you

    • Bima unzila ilayka = the Holy Quran.
    • Wa ma unzila min qablika = the earlier divine scriptures: the Torah (Tawrah), the Gospel (Injil), the Psalms (Zabur), and all other books revealed to previous prophets.
    • Belief is in these books in their original, unaltered form — not the distorted, amended versions that exist today.
    • The Muttaqeen do not differentiate between the books of Allah (la yufarriqoona bayna Kutubillah) nor between His messengers (wa la bayna Rusulihi).
    • This distinguishes the believer from Jews and Christians, who accept only their own prophet and reject others. Muslims believe in all prophets and all books.

    5. وَبِالْآخِرَةِ هُمْ يُوقِنُونَ — And in the Hereafter they have certainty (Yaqeen)

    • Yuqinoon from أَيقَنَ يُوقِنُ إِيقاناً — a firm, decisive belief.
    • Meaning: ya'taqidoona i'tiqadan jaziman la yulabisahu shakkun aw irtiyab — a firm belief without any doubt or suspicion.
    • Belief in the Hereafter includes all related matters: the Resurrection, the reward and punishment, the Balance (al-Mizan), and all that follows.
    • Why was Yaqeen used for the Akhirah rather than Iman?
    • Iman covers the beginning stages of belief through its various increasing levels.
    • Yaqeen is the highest, most firm stage of belief — the final, settled level of certainty.
    • Iman has degrees; Yaqeen is the apex. Using Yaqeen for the Akhirah emphasizes that true belief in the Hereafter must reach this level of unshakeable conviction.
    • Why is it called "الآخرة"? Because it comes after (ba'd) this present world (ad-Dunya) — it is the final abode.

    Ayah 5 — The Outcome

    أُولَٰئِكَ عَلَىٰ هُدًى مِّن رَّبِّهِمْ وَأُولَٰئِكَ هُمُ الْمُفْلِحُونَ Those are upon guidance from their Lord, and it is those who are the successful.

    • Those characterized by the above five qualities receive huda (guidance) — a clear light and insight directly from Allah.
    • They are the Muflihoon — the truly successful. Falah here does not refer only to worldly success; it refers specifically to final, everlasting success in the Hereafter.
    • They attain the highest ranks in Jannah — this is the ultimate, permanent victory that cannot be taken away.

    Munasabah — Link to Ayat 6–7

    The sheikh explained the munasabah (contextual connection) between the verses of the Muttaqeen (Ayat 1–5) and the verses about the Kuffar (Ayat 6–7):

    • After mentioning the qualities of believers, Allah follows with the qualities of disbelievers to make the distinction between the two groups clear (liyudhira al-fariq al-wadih bayna as-sinfayn).
    • This is a consistent method of the Quran: comparing the Abrar (righteous) with the Fujjar (wicked), and distinguishing Ahl as-Sa'adah (the fortunate) from Ahl ash-Shaqawah (the unfortunate).
    • An Arabic proverb captures the principle: بِضِدِّهَا تَتَمَيَّزُ الْأَشْيَاءُ — "By their opposites, things are distinguished."

    Ayah 6 — The Kuffar: Warning Makes No Difference

    إِنَّ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا سَوَاءٌ عَلَيْهِمْ أَأَنذَرْتَهُمْ أَمْ لَمْ تُنذِرُوهُمْ لَا يُؤْمِنُونَ Indeed, those who disbelieve — it is equal to them whether you warn them or you do not warn them — they will not believe.

    • Al-ladhina kafaru = those who denied the ayat of Allah and rejected the prophethood of Muhammad ﷺ.
    • Sawa'un alayhim = it is equal/the same for them.
    • Andhara yundhiru indharan = to warn; to give a warning.
    • Whether the Prophet ﷺ warned them from Allah's punishment and frightened them (khawwaftahum minhu), or did not warn them — in both cases: la yu'minoon — they will not believe.
    • This ayah is a consolation (tasliyah) to the Prophet ﷺ about the disbelief of his people. It does not mean he should stop inviting them; it means he should not grieve so much that it harms him. He has conveyed the message perfectly — their rejection is now their own responsibility.
    • Parallel examples from other prophets:
    • Nuh (عليه السلام): after exhausting all means of da'wah, supplicated: رَبِّ لَا تَذَرْ عَلَى الْأَرْضِ مِنَ الْكَافِرِينَ دَيَّارًا — "O Lord, do not leave a single disbeliever on earth."
    • Musa (عليه السلام): supplicated: رَبَّنَا اطْمِسْ عَلَىٰ أَمْوَالِهِمْ وَاشْدُدْ عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِهِمْ فَلَا يُؤْمِنُوا حَتَّىٰ يَرَوُا الْعَذَابَ — after performing all duties, when the prophet feels certain the people will not accept Iman.

    Ayah 7 — The Reason: Sealed Hearts (Begun, to be continued)

    خَتَمَ اللَّهُ عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِهِمْ وَعَلَىٰ سَمْعِهِمْ وَعَلَىٰ أَبْصَارِهِمْ غِشَاوَةٌ وَلَهُمْ عَذَابٌ عَظِيمٌ Allah has set a seal upon their hearts and upon their hearing, and over their vision is a veil. And for them is a great punishment.

    • Khatama = to seal; to place a seal. Same root as Khatamul Mursaleen (Seal of the Prophets).
    • Allah has sealed their hearts (qulubihim) — no light of Iman can enter; no faith illuminates within.
    • The reason (al-'illah) for their not believing is this seal — mentioned here to explain Ayah 6.
    • This section was not completed — the full explanation will continue in the next class.

    Grammar and Language Notes

    • يُؤمِنون — from أَمَنَ (Form I); يُؤمِن is Form IV (أَفعَلَ).
    • يُؤدّونها — from أَدَّى يُؤَدِّي (Form II doubled); means "to perform/fulfill" — used for rendering something to its proper recipient in complete form.
    • يُوقِنون — from أَيقَنَ يُوقِنُ (Form IV: أَفعَلَ); madi: أَيقَنَ; mudari': يُوقِنُ; masdar: إِيقان. The sheikh asked students to identify this bab and form during class.
    • أُنزِلَ — passive form (fi'l majhul) of أَنزَلَ يُنزِلُ إِنزالاً (Form IV).
    • أَنذَرَ يُنذِرُ إِنذاراً — Form IV verb meaning "to warn"; the sheikh identified it as a transitive verb taking a direct object.
    • خَتَمَ يَختِمُ خَتماً — Form I verb meaning "to seal."
    • سَوَاءٌ — predicate adjective meaning "equal/the same"; here used in the structure sawa'un alayhim... am... = "it is equal to them whether... or..."
    • الزكاة المفروضة — al-Zakat al-mafrudah: mafrudah is a passive participle (Form I) meaning "what has been made obligatory/prescribed."
    • Arabic proverb taught: بِضِدِّهَا تَتَمَيَّزُ الْأَشْيَاءُ / تُعرَفُ الأشياءُ بأضدادِها — "Things are known/distinguished by their opposites."
    • مِن in mimma razaqnahum — partitive min (indicating "from/out of"), making infaq an act of spending from a portion of what one has, not all of it.

    Lessons and Practical Takeaways

    1. Belief in the Unseen is the foundation — Iman in things we cannot see or touch is the first and most essential quality of a believer. Our creed does not rest on empirical proof alone but on the testimony of Allah and His Messenger.

    2. Salah must be "established," not merely "performed" — True iqamatus salah means fulfilling every condition, pillar, and etiquette with khushu'. A prayer done hastily or without focus does not meet the Quranic standard. Ibn Abbas specifically highlighted ruku', sujud, tilawah, and khushu' as its pillars of completion.

    3. Spending is an obligation, not an option — Infaq is listed alongside Salah as a defining quality of the Muttaqeen. Whether Fard Zakat or voluntary Sadaqah, giving in the way of Allah is central to Islamic identity.

    4. Salah = rights of Allah; Infaq = rights of creation — This is why they always appear together. A complete Muslim fulfills both: his vertical duty to Allah and his horizontal duty to people.

    5. Belief must not be selective — Accepting some prophets and rejecting others, or some books while rejecting others, is not valid Iman. Muslims believe in all prophets and all original scriptures without distinction.

    6. Yaqeen in the Akhirah should be our anchor — Our certainty about the Hereafter must be unshakeable — jazim (decisive), without doubt or suspicion. This firm conviction shapes behavior in this world.

    7. The da'i must not despair — The Prophet ﷺ was consoled that his duty is to convey, not to compel. A caller to Islam should not exhaust himself in grief over those who reject the message. Convey clearly, then leave the result to Allah.

    8. Contrast is a teaching tool — The Quran's method of pairing descriptions of believers and disbelievers side by side helps us see the stakes clearly and understand what is truly at risk. Studying both together deepens understanding of each.

    9. Hardened hearts are a consequence — The sealing of hearts in Ayah 7 is presented as the reason disbelievers cannot believe — a warning that persistent rejection can lead to a state where guidance no longer reaches the heart.

    Historical Context and Stories Mentioned

    • Nuh (عليه السلام) — After exhausting every means of da'wah with his people over a very long time, he supplicated against them: رَبِّ لَا تَذَرْ عَلَى الْأَرْضِ مِنَ الْكَافِرِينَ دَيَّارًا ("O Lord, leave not upon the earth any of the disbelievers"). This illustrates the stage a messenger reaches after fulfilling his duty completely and recognizing the people will not accept.

    • Musa (عليه السلام) — After Pharaoh's persistent rejection, Musa supplicated: رَبَّنَا اطْمِسْ عَلَىٰ أَمْوَالِهِمْ وَاشْدُدْ عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِهِمْ فَلَا يُؤْمِنُوا حَتَّىٰ يَرَوُا الْعَذَابَ ("Our Lord, obliterate their wealth and harden their hearts so that they will not believe until they see the painful punishment"). The sheikh cited this as parallel to the situation addressed in Ayah 6 — the consolation to the Prophet ﷺ.

    • The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ — The ayah is understood as addressing his grief over the persistent rejection by the Quraysh and other disbelievers, assuring him he had fulfilled his prophetic duty and should not harm himself with excessive sorrow.

    Q&A Highlights

    Q: What is the difference between Yaqeen (يقين) and Iman (إيمان)? Why did Allah use Yaqeen for the Akhirah and Iman for Ghayb?

    A (Sheikh's explanation): Iman covers a spectrum — it starts from the beginning stages of belief and increases over time. It has multiple levels and degrees. Yaqeen is not a separate category but rather the highest, most firm level that Iman can reach — a settled, unshakeable conviction. So Iman is the broader term encompassing all stages, while Yaqeen is the apex of that journey. Allah used Yaqeen for the Akhirah because belief in the Hereafter — since it is the foundation of all moral accountability and the ultimate reality — must reach this highest degree of certainty. A general belief (the starting point of Iman) is not sufficient for something as weighty as the Akhirah; it must be firm, decisive, and free of all doubt.

    Q (Clarification during reading): Is the word al-Zakat al-mafrudah in the genitive (majrur) form or is it a new sentence?

    A: The sheikh clarified that min here is a partitive min, and al-Zakat al-mafrudah is a continuation of the sentence as the object (maf'ul bihi), not a new sentence.
