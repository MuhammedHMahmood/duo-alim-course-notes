    Tafseer 201 - Study Notes

    Date: 2026-11-03 Surah Covered: Surah Al-Baqarah — Ayaat 84–86 (وَإِذْ أَخَذْنَا مِيثَاقَكُمْ... through أُولَٰئِكَ الَّذِينَ اشْتَرَوُا الْحَيَاةَ الدُّنْيَا بِالْآخِرَةِ)

    Session Overview

    This session covered the tafseer of Surah Al-Baqarah beginning at the ayah وَإِذْ أَخَذْنَا مِيثَاقَكُمْ لَا تَسْفِكُونَ دِمَاءَكُمْ وَلَا تُخْرِجُونَ أَنْفُسَكُمْ مِنْ دِيَارِكُمْ ثُمَّ أَقْرَرْتُمْ وَأَنْتُمْ تَشْهَدُونَ (page 74 of the text). The session explained Allah's reminder to Bani Isra'il of the firm covenants they had broken — specifically the prohibitions against killing one another and expelling one another from their homes. The historical background of Banu Qurayza and Banu Nadir's alliances with the Aws and Khazraj tribes was discussed at length to explain how these ayaat apply to the Jews of the Prophet's ﷺ time. The session concluded with the punishment declared for those who believe in part of the Book and disbelieve in another part, and for those who trade the Hereafter for worldly life.

    Key Themes and Topics

    1. The Mithaq (Covenant) of Bani Isra'il — Allah reminds the Bani Isra'il of the confirmed covenant He took from them, containing two core prohibitions: not shedding each other's blood and not expelling one another from their homes.

    2. Breaking the Covenant — Despite acknowledging and witnessing the covenant, Bani Isra'il violated both clauses: they killed each other and expelled each other from their homelands.

    3. The Double Standard of Bani Isra'il — They would kill their own brothers and expel them in the context of tribal warfare, then — after the war — cooperate to ransom those same brothers from captivity, citing the Torah. This selective application of their scripture is explicitly condemned.

    4. Iman Bi Ba'd Al-Kitab wa Kufr Bi Ba'dihi (Believing in Part of the Book, Disbelieving in Another Part) — Allah rebukes those who selectively apply scripture, declaring that disbelieving in any part of the Book is effectively disbelieving in the whole.

    5. Exchanging the Hereafter for the Dunya — The root cause of Bani Isra'il's transgressions is identified as preferring worldly life (alliances, tribal loyalties, material gains) over obedience to Allah and reward in the Akhirah.

    6. Punishment in This World and the Next — The punishment for selective belief in scripture is humiliation (khizy) in this world and severe, unending punishment in the Hereafter.

    7. Grammar Lesson (Arabic Morphology/Sarf) — Detailed discussion of:

    8. تَظَاهَرُونَ: omission of one تَاء in the form of تَتَظَاهَرُونَ
    9. تُفَادُوهُمْ: derivation from بَابُ الْمُفَاعَلَة (فَادَى يُفَادِي), and noon omission in شَرْط-جَزَاء sentences
    10. يَأْتُوكُمْ: noon omission in the conditional sentence

    Main Tafseer Points and Explanations

    Ayah: وَإِذْ أَخَذْنَا مِيثَاقَكُمْ لَا تَسْفِكُونَ دِمَاءَكُمْ وَلَا تُخْرِجُونَ أَنفُسَكُمْ مِنْ دِيَارِكُمْ ثُمَّ أَقْرَرْتُمْ وَأَنتُمْ تَشْهَدُونَ

    Translation: "And [remember] when We took your covenant: you shall not shed one another's blood, and you shall not expel one another from your homes. Then you acknowledged [the covenant] while you were witnesses."

    Two clauses of the covenant:

    1. لَا تَسْفِكُونَ دِمَاءَكُمْ — "You shall not shed your blood" = لَا يَقْتُلَ بَعْضُكُمْ بَعْضًا — you shall not kill one another. "Your blood" refers to the blood of your brothers; it is called "your blood" because Bani Isra'il are one community.

    2. لَا تُخْرِجُونَ أَنفُسَكُمْ مِنْ دِيَارِكُمْ — "You shall not expel yourselves from your homes" = none of you shall transgress against another by forcing them out of their homes or homelands (الْإِخْرَاجُ مِنَ الدِّيَارِ وَالْإِجْلَاءُ عَنِ الْأَوْطَانِ).

    ثُمَّ أَقْرَرْتُمْ وَأَنْتُمْ تَشْهَدُونَ — "Then you acknowledged it while you were witnesses." This means they explicitly confirmed and testified to the covenant — they did not enter it in ignorance. They accepted it and acknowledged the obligation of fulfilling it (بِوُجُوبِ الْمُحَافَظَةِ عَلَيْهِ).

    Ayah: ثُمَّ أَنتُمْ هَٰؤُلَاءِ تَقْتُلُونَ أَنفُسَكُمْ وَتُخْرِجُونَ فَرِيقًا مِّنكُم مِّن دِيَارِهِمْ

    Translation: "Then you are those [same people] killing one another and expelling a group of you from their homes."

    • ثُمَّ أَنْتُمْ هَؤُلَاءِ — "Then it is you" — a pointed, emphatic identification. Despite acknowledging the covenant, it is specifically you, O Bani Isra'il, who broke it.
    • They broke both clauses simultaneously: they killed their brothers AND expelled them from their homes.
    • كَمَا طَرَدْتُمْ مَنْ طَرَدْتُمُوهُ مِنْ دِيَارِهِ مِنْ غَيْرِ الْتِفَاتٍ إِلَى الْعَهْدِ الْوَثِيقِ — They expelled people without any regard for the firm covenant they had entered.

    Ayah: تَظَاهَرُونَ عَلَيْهِم بِالْإِثْمِ وَالْعُدْوَانِ

    Translation: "...cooperating against them in sin and aggression."

    • تَظَاهَرُونَ = تَتَعَاوَنُونَ عَلَيْهِمْ — You cooperate/assist against your own brothers.
    • They helped the enemies of their brothers (the Mushrikeen of Aws or Khazraj) against their own fellow Jews in sin and transgression.

    Ayah: وَإِن يَأْتُوكُمْ أُسَارَىٰ تُفَادُوهُمْ وَهُوَ مُحَرَّمٌ عَلَيْكُمْ إِخْرَاجُهُمْ

    Translation: "And if they come to you as captives, you ransom them — although their expulsion was forbidden to you."

    • After the war, when the same Jews who had been expelled/captured came to their brothers seeking ransom, Bani Isra'il would honor the Torah's command to free Jewish captives — but had completely ignored the Torah's command not to fight against them in the first place.
    • وَهُوَ مُحَرَّمٌ عَلَيْكُمْ إِخْرَاجُهُمْ — Yet their expulsion was already haram upon you. So why did you permit (تُحِلُّونَ) their killing and expulsion, but not permit leaving them in captivity?
    • This is the core accusation: selective, hypocritical application of the Torah.

    Ayah: أَفَتُؤْمِنُونَ بِبَعْضِ الْكِتَابِ وَتَكْفُرُونَ بِبَعْضٍ

    Translation: "So do you believe in part of the Scripture and disbelieve in part?"

    • They fulfilled the Torah's ruling on ransoming captives (فِدَاء) but violated the Torah's prohibition against killing and expelling fellow Jews.
    • Believing in some rulings of the Torah while rejecting others = كُفْر بِالْكِتَابِ as a whole.
    • The implication: partial obedience to divine revelation is not acceptable; selective application of scripture constitutes a form of disbelief.

    Punishment (فَمَا جَزَاءُ مَنْ يَفْعَلُ ذَٰلِكَ مِنكُمْ إِلَّا خِزْيٌ فِي الْحَيَاةِ الدُّنْيَا)

    Translation: "So what is the recompense for those among you who do that except disgrace in worldly life?"

    • خِزْي فِي الْحَيَاةِ الدُّنْيَا — Humiliation, disgrace, and the anger of Allah in this world.
    • وَيَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ يُرَدُّونَ إِلَىٰ أَشَدِّ الْعَذَابِ — On the Day of Resurrection, they will be returned to the most severe punishment (يُرَدُّونَ — Majhul; passive: "they will be returned").
    • لِأَنَّهُ عَذَابٌ خَالِدٌ لَا يَنْقَضِي وَلَا يَنْتَهِي — Because it is an eternal punishment that never ends.
    • وَمَا اللَّهُ بِغَافِلٍ عَمَّا تَعْمَلُونَ — And Allah is not unaware of what you do — a severe warning (وَعِيدٌ شَدِيدٌ) for those who disobey Allah's commands.

    Ayah: أُولَٰئِكَ الَّذِينَ اشْتَرَوُا الْحَيَاةَ الدُّنْيَا بِالْآخِرَةِ

    Translation: "Those are the ones who have purchased the worldly life in exchange for the Hereafter."

    • اشْتَرَوُا الدُّنْيَا بِالْآخِرَةِ = اخْتَارُوهَا وَآثَرُوهَا عَلَى الْآخِرَةِ — They chose this world and preferred it over the Hereafter.
    • This is identified as the root cause (سَبَبُ ذَلِكَ الْعِصْيَانِ وَالْعُدْوَانِ) of all the disobedience and transgression described above.
    • فَلَا يُخَفَّفُ عَنْهُمُ الْعَذَابُ — The punishment in the Hereafter will not be lightened, even for a moment.
    • وَلَا هُمْ يُنصَرُونَ — And they will not be helped — لَيْسَ لَهُمْ نَاصِرٌ يَنْصُرُهُمْ وَلَا مُجِيرٌ يُنْقِذُهُمْ مِنْ عَذَابِ اللَّهِ الْأَلِيمِ — there is no helper or protector to save them from the painful punishment of Allah.

    Grammar and Language Notes

    1. تَظَاهَرُونَ — Omission of One تَاء

    • The base form in الْمُضَارِع is تَتَظَاهَرُونَ (from تَظَاهَرَ يَتَظَاهَرُ تَظَاهُرًا).
    • In Arabic, it is permissible to omit one تَاء from the مُضَارِع of بَابُ تَفَاعَلَ when two تَاءَانِ come together at the start.
    • So تَتَظَاهَرُونَ → تَظَاهَرُونَ (one تَاء omitted: حُذِفَتْ تَاءٌ مِنْ تَاءَيْنِ فِي هَذِهِ الصِّيغَةِ).
    • Original: كَانَتْ أَصْلُهَا بِتَتَظَاهَرُونَ، وَتَاءٌ مِنْ تَاءَيْنِ حُذِفَتْ، فَبَقِيَتْ تَظَاهَرُونَ.

    2. تُفَادُوهُمْ — Derivation and Noon Omission

    • From فَادَى يُفَادِي مُفَادَاةً — بَابُ الْمُفَاعَلَة (not from أَفَادَ يُفِيدُ).
    • Conjugation of الْمُضَارِع: تُفَادِي → تُفَادِيَانِ → تُفَادُونَ → with pronoun: تُفَادُونَهُمْ.
    • The نُون is omitted here because the sentence is شَرْطٌ وَجَزَاءٌ (conditional sentence):
    • إِنْ is شَرْطِيَّة; يَأْتُوكُمْ is فِعْلُ الشَّرْطِ; تُفَادُوهُمْ is جَوَابُ الشَّرْطِ (الْجَزَاء).
    • Rule: إِذَا كَانَتِ الْجُمْلَةُ شَرْطِيَّةً جَزَائِيَّةً، فَنُونُ الْمُضَارِعِ تُحْذَفُ فِي صِيغَةِ الْمُثَنَّى وَالْجَمْعِ فِي فِعْلِ الشَّرْطِ وَفِعْلِ الْجَزَاءِ كِلَيْهِمَا.
    • So نُون is dropped in BOTH: يَأْتُوكُمْ (فِعْل شَرْط) and تُفَادُوهُمْ (جَزَاء).

    3. يَأْتُوكُمْ — Noon Omission in Conditional Sentence

    • Base: يَأْتِي يَأْتِيَانِ يَأْتُونَ → with pronoun: يَأْتُونَكُمْ.
    • Noon dropped because it is فِعْلُ الشَّرْطِ in a شَرْطِيَّة sentence: يَأْتُوكُمْ.

    4. يُرَدُّونَ — Passive (Majhul) Form

    • From رَدَّ يَرُدُّ — الْمُضَارِعُ الْمَجْهُولُ: يُرَدُّ → يُرَدُّونَ.
    • "They will be returned" — passive construction, stressing that there is no escape; they are compelled back to punishment.

    Lessons and Practical Takeaways

    1. Covenants with Allah are serious. The Mithaq is called الْعَهْدُ الْمُؤَكَّد — an extremely firm and confirmed agreement. When someone acknowledges such a covenant, breaking it is a grave sin, not a minor matter.

    2. Brotherhood demands consistency. The case of Bani Isra'il shows the danger of tribal loyalties overriding religious obligations. When worldly alliances conflict with divine commands, the divine command must take precedence.

    3. Selective application of Islam is a form of Kufr. This ayah is a direct warning to all believers: you cannot pick and choose from Allah's commands. Obeying some of Allah's orders while deliberately ignoring others is not partial obedience — it is a form of rejection of the whole. This principle applies to Muslims as much as it did to Bani Isra'il.

    4. Love of dunya is the root cause of sin. Allah identifies اشْتِرَاءُ الدُّنْيَا بِالْآخِرَةِ as the root cause of all this disobedience. When the heart prefers worldly gain (here: tribal alliances, political power, material spoils) over Allah's commands, it leads to the most severe violations.

    5. Double standards are exposed. The hypocrisy of honoring one Torah rule (ransoming captives) while ignoring another (not making them captives or killing them in the first place) is explicitly condemned. This is a reminder to avoid performative religiosity that serves convenience rather than genuine submission.

    6. The punishment for injustice includes worldly humiliation. خِزْيٌ فِي الْحَيَاةِ الدُّنْيَا — history demonstrates that those who persistently oppress and transgress face disgrace even in this world, in addition to the punishment of the Hereafter.

    7. Allah is never unaware. وَمَا اللَّهُ بِغَافِلٍ عَمَّا تَعْمَلُونَ — This should instill مُرَاقَبَة (awareness of being observed by Allah) in the believer at all times.

    Historical Context and Stories Mentioned

    Background: Banu Qurayza, Banu Nadir, Aws, and Khazraj

    The historical context (from the تَنْبِيه/footnote in the tafseer text) is as follows:

    • At the time of the Prophet ﷺ in Madinah, there were two major Jewish tribes:
    • Banu Qurayza — allied (مُحَالِف) with the Aws tribe (Mushrikeen).
    • Banu Nadir — allied with the Khazraj tribe (Mushrikeen).
    • The Aws and Khazraj were the two dominant Arab tribes of Madinah, frequently at war with each other.
    • When war broke out between Aws and Khazraj (فَكَانَتِ الْحَرْبُ إِذَا نَشَبَتْ بَيْنَهُمَا), each Jewish tribe was obligated to fight alongside its Arab ally — meaning Banu Qurayza and Banu Nadir would end up fighting against each other, Jewish brother against Jewish brother.
    • The consequences of these wars included:
    • Jews killing fellow Jews (violating the first clause of the covenant).
    • Jews expelling fellow Jews from their homes (violating the second clause).
    • Looting the properties and goods of fellow Jews (also forbidden in their Torah).
    • After the war: When some Jews were taken as captives, the other Jewish tribe would come together to ransom them, citing the Torah's command to free Jewish captives. They would pay the ransom to free their brothers.
    • The contradiction Allah exposes: They remembered and honored the Torah's ruling on ransoming captives, but completely ignored the Torah's prohibition on killing and expelling those same people in the first place. This is the double standard condemned in أَفَتُؤْمِنُونَ بِبَعْضِ الْكِتَابِ وَتَكْفُرُونَ بِبَعْضٍ.
    • وَذَلِكَ حَرَامٌ عَلَيْهِمْ فِي دِينِهِمْ وَفِي نَصِّ التَّوْرَاةِ — All of these actions (killing, expelling, looting fellow Jews) were explicitly forbidden in the text of the Torah.

    Q&A Highlights

    Q: Why is the covenant still referred to as الْعَهْدُ الْوَثِيق ("the firm covenant") even though they kept breaking it?

    A: The description الْعَهْدُ الْوَثِيق refers to the nature of the covenant itself when it was made — it was a confirmed, explicit agreement that Bani Isra'il had acknowledged and witnessed. The title describes what it was, not what they did with it. Calling it "firm" while noting they broke it heightens the condemnation: the more solemn and explicit the agreement, the greater the betrayal in breaking it.

    Q: Why did the Bani Isra'il expel people from their homes in the first place?

    A: This was answered through the historical background (تَنْبِيه). The expulsions were a consequence of their tribal alliances. Because Banu Qurayza was allied with Aws and Banu Nadir with Khazraj, when Aws and Khazraj went to war, each Jewish tribe had to fight alongside their allied tribe. The expulsion of enemy families was a standard outcome of these inter-tribal wars. The condemnation is that they prioritized these worldly alliances over the clear commands of their own Torah.

    Q: Are the events described (killing, captives) referring to the time of Musa عليه السلام or the time of the Prophet ﷺ?

    A: Both. The covenant was made in the time of Musa عليه السلام (through the Torah and the Prophets). But the breaking of that covenant described in these ayaat refers specifically to the Jews living in Madinah at the time of the Prophet ﷺ — Banu Qurayza and Banu Nadir — and their conduct in the wars between Aws and Khazraj. Allah is addressing them directly, holding them accountable for violating the covenant their ancestors made.

    Q: Does تُفَادُوهُمْ come from أَفَادَ يُفِيدُ?

    A: No. It is from فَادَى يُفَادِي مُفَادَاةً — بَابُ الْمُفَاعَلَة, meaning to ransom someone. It has no connection to أَفَادَ (to benefit). The conjugation is: فَادَى / يُفَادِي / تُفَادِي / تُفَادِيَانِ / تُفَادُونَ → تُفَادُوهُمْ (noon dropped due to the conditional sentence structure).
