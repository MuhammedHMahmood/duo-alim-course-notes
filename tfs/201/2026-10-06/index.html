    Tafseer 201 - Study Notes

    Date: 2026-10-06 Surah Covered: Surah Al-Baqarah, Ayat 71–76 (with focus on 71–74 and opening of 75–82)

    Session Overview

    This session continued the tafseer of Surah Al-Baqarah, picking up from Ayah 71. The first portion (Ayat 71–74) concluded the story of the slaughtered cow (قصة البقرة), explaining the final description of the cow, the slaughter, the murder mystery and its miraculous resolution, and the lesson drawn about Allah's power to resurrect the dead. The session then addressed Ayah 74, which criticizes the hardness of the hearts of Bani Israel by comparing them unfavorably to rocks and stones. The final portion introduced the theme and context of Ayat 75–82, covering the stubbornness of Bani Israel, their distortion of Allah's words, their false claims, and the reason this passage was revealed (نزل) regarding the Ansar's hope for Jewish acceptance of Islam.

    Key Themes and Topics

    1. Completion of the Cow's Description (Ayah 71) — The final attributes: not trained for plowing or irrigation, sound and without defect, uniform yellow color with no markings differing from its body.
    2. The Slaughter and Its Reluctance (Ayah 71) — Bani Israel eventually slaughtered the cow but were on the verge of not doing so, due to the cow's high price and fear of scandal.
    3. The Murder Mystery Revealed (Ayah 72–73) — The reason Allah commanded the slaughter: a murder had occurred among Bani Israel; by striking the dead body with a piece of the slaughtered cow, the victim was briefly revived and named his killer.
    4. Resurrection as a Sign (Ayah 73) — This miracle is presented as a foretaste of the general resurrection on the Day of Judgment.
    5. Hardness of the Hearts of Bani Israel (Ayah 74) — Their hearts became harder than rocks; even stones burst with rivers, split from fear of Allah, and crumble — but their hearts remain unmoved.
    6. Allah's All-Awareness (Ayah 74) — Warning that Allah is not unaware of their deeds and will recompense them on the Day of Resurrection.
    7. Introduction to Ayat 75–82 — Context (سبب النزول) and themes: Muslim despair of Jewish Iman, Jewish claims of being beloved by Allah, and their false belief that hellfire will only touch them for a few days.

    Main Tafseer Points and Explanations

    Ayah 71 — Final Description of the Cow

    Allah (through Musa عليه السلام) gave the final, definitive description: - لَا ذَلُولٌ تُثِيرُ الأَرْضَ — Not trained/broken in to plow the earth. - وَلَا تَسْقِي الحَرْثَ — Nor used to irrigate crops. - مُسَلَّمَةٌ — Sound and whole; no physical defect whatsoever. - لَا شِيَةَ فِيهَا — Completely uniform yellow; no spot or patch of any other color on its body.

    Upon hearing this, Bani Israel said: الآنَ جِئْتَ بِالحَقِّ — "Now you have come with the truth." They acknowledged that Musa had now explained it بَيَانًا شَافِيًا — clearly and completely, with no remaining غُمُوض (ambiguity) or لَبْس (confusion).

    فَذَبَحُوهَا وَمَا كَادُوا يَفْعَلُونَ — They slaughtered her, yet they were on the verge of not doing so. Two reasons are given for their reluctance: 1. The price of such a specific cow was extraordinarily high. 2. Fear of scandal (خَشْيَةُ الفَضِيحَة) — embarrassment at being unable to find such a cow.

    Ayat 72–73 — The Purpose Behind the Command: The Murder Mystery

    Allah reveals the reason (سبب) for the slaughter command:

    • وَإِذْ قَتَلْتُمْ نَفْسًا — A person had been murdered among Bani Israel.
    • فَادَّارَأْتُمْ فِيهَا — Each group pushed the blame (accusation) away from itself onto others; no one would admit guilt.
    • وَاللَّهُ مُخْرِجٌ مَّا كُنتُمْ تَكْتُمُونَ — Allah was going to reveal what they were concealing.

    The miracle: Allah commanded them to strike the dead body with a piece (part of the flesh) of the slaughtered cow. When they did: - The dead man was revived (temporarily). - He named his killer. - وَيُخْبِرُكُمْ عَنْ قَاتِلِيكُمْ — He informed them of who killed him.

    This resolved the murder case in a miraculous, undeniable way — witnessed with their own eyes.

    Ayah 73 (continued) — Lesson on Resurrection

    كَذَلِكَ يُحْيِي اللَّهُ الْمَوْتَى — Just as Allah brought this murdered man back to life before your eyes, He will likewise resurrect all the dead from their graves on the Day of Judgment.

    وَيُرِيكُمْ آيَاتِهِ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَعْقِلُونَ — Allah shows you the signs of His power so that you may: - تَفَكَّرُوا — think and reflect - تَدَبَّرُوا — contemplate deeply - تَعْلَمُوا أَنَّ اللَّهَ عَلَى كُلِّ شَيْءٍ قَدِيرٌ — know that Allah has power over all things

    The teacher noted that لَعَلَّكُمْ تَعْقِلُونَ is not addressed to Bani Israel alone — it is an address to all readers of the Quran until the Day of Judgment. We should always seek lessons and wisdom in Quranic stories.

    Why command the slaughter of a cow for a murder? The apparent disconnect (cow slaughter vs. murder case) contains a hidden wisdom: Bani Israel had a deep cultural attachment to cows — prior to receiving Tawheed through Musa they were cow-worshippers. Allah commanded them to slaughter what they revered, to test their submission. Their delay and endless questioning revealed this attachment.

    Ayah 74 — Hearts Harder Than Stones

    ثُمَّ قَسَتْ قُلُوبُكُمْ مِّن بَعْدِ ذَلِكَ — After witnessing all these dazzling miracles (آيَات بَاهِرَة), your hearts still became hard. This reveals the spiritual failure of Bani Israel: no sign, warning, or miracle softened them.

    فَهِيَ كَالْحِجَارَةِ أَوْ أَشَدُّ قَسْوَةً — Their hearts are like rocks, or even harder. The teacher explained three categories drawn from the ayah:

    Type Arabic Description
    Rivers burst forth تَتَفَجَّرُ مِنْهُ الأَنْهَارُ Some rocks have rivers of water bursting from them
    They split/crack يَشَّقَّقُ Some rocks crack and split, with water springing out, out of fear of Allah
    They crumble/fall يَهْبِطُ مِنْ خَشْيَةِ اللَّهِ Some rocks crumble and fall from mountaintops out of awe of Allah

    The comparison: water emerging from rock = a tear emerging from the human heart out of fear of Allah. Rocks are the paradigm of hardness, yet even they respond to Allah's greatness. Bani Israel's hearts did not.

    Scholarly difference on "the stone" (الحَجَر): There is a difference of opinion among the mufassireen: - Jumhur (majority of scholars): The definite article الحَجَر indicates a specific rock, likely the rock in the miracle of Musa striking it. - Hasan al-Basri and some others: It refers to an indefinite, general category of rocks — and this non-specificity is actually more eloquent (more بَلِيغ), because the lesson is universal.

    وَمَا اللَّهُ بِغَافِلٍ عَمَّا تَعْمَلُونَ — This closing contains both a warning (وَعِيد) and a threat (تَهْدِيد): Allah is fully aware and will recompense on the Day of Resurrection.

    Munasabah (Connection) to Ayat 75–82

    The transition to this new section is introduced:

    After mentioning (in prior ayat) the عِنَاد (stubbornness) of Bani Israel, their failure to act on Allah's commands, their arguments with the prophets, and their refusal to submit — Allah follows by mentioning additional crimes (قَبَائِح وَجَرَائِم):

    1. تَحْرِيف كَلَامِ اللَّه — Distortion of the Torah.
    2. ادِّعَاؤُهُمْ بِأَنَّهُمْ أَحِبَّاءُ اللَّهِ — Their claim to be the beloved friends of Allah.
    3. ادِّعَاؤُهُمْ أَنَّ النَّارَ لَنْ تَمَسَّهُمْ إِلَّا أَيَّامًا مَعْدُودَةً — Their claim that the hellfire will only touch them for a few days.

    These false hopes (أَمَانِيٌّ كَاذِبَة) were inherited from their fathers and grandfathers (وَرِثُوهَا عَنْ آبَائِهِمْ وَأَجْدَادِهِمْ).

    Grammar and Language Notes

    • وَمَا كَادُوا يَفْعَلُونَ — The verb كَادَ (kāda) is a verb of proximity/nearness in Arabic, indicating that something was about to happen but did not. Here: "they were about not to do it" — i.e., they very nearly refused entirely.
    • فَادَّارَأْتُمْ — Root: دَرَأَ (to push/repel). The Form VIII (افْتَعَلَ) with idgham gives اِدَّرَأَ → تَدَارَأَ (Form VI, mutual action) → contracted in Quranic recitation as ادَّارَأْتُمْ. Meaning: mutual pushing — each side pushing the accusation away from itself toward others.
    • قَسَا / يَقْسُو / قَسْوَةً — Verb: to become hard. قَسَتْ قُلُوبُكُمْ — "your hearts hardened." The verbal root is used for the hardening of both physical objects and the human heart spiritually.
    • تَفَجَّرَ / يَتَفَجَّرُ — Form V (تَفَعَّلَ), meaning to burst forth or gush out (of water).
    • يَهْبِطُ — From هَبَطَ: to descend, fall, or crumble down.
    • غَافِلٌ — Active participle from غَفَلَ: one who is heedless or unaware. مَا اللَّهُ بِغَافِلٍ — the بـ here is زَائِدَة (extra/emphatic) after negation, intensifying the denial: Allah is absolutely not unaware.
    • أَفَتَطْمَعُونَ — Hamzah of استفهام (interrogation) on فـ of عطف. The rhetorical question implies a negative answer: Do you really hope? (No, you should not.)

    Lessons and Practical Takeaways

    1. Obey Allah's commands without excessive questioning. Bani Israel's repeated demands for more and more specification before acting was a form of spiritual resistance. A believer submits promptly.

    2. Every Quranic story carries lessons for us today. لَعَلَّكُمْ تَعْقِلُونَ is addressed to every reader. We should contemplate: What is the wisdom here? What does this reveal about Allah's power and our own condition?

    3. Soften your heart before it hardens. The comparison of hearts to rocks is a direct warning to every believer. If even mountains crack and rivers flow from stones out of Allah's awe, our hearts should be softer still — moved to tears, to repentance, to remembrance.

    4. The Prophet's ﷺ du'a against a hard heart:

      اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أَعُوذُ بِكَ مِنْ عِلْمٍ لَا يَنْفَعُ، وَمِنْ قَلْبٍ لَا يَخْشَعُ، وَمِنْ نَفْسٍ لَا تَشْبَعُ، وَمِنْ دَعْوَةٍ لَا يُسْتَجَابُ لَهَا

      O Allah, I seek refuge in You from knowledge that does not benefit, from a heart that does not humble itself, from a soul that is never satisfied, and from a supplication that is not answered.

    The teacher emphasized: the heart is the core. The Quran says فَإِنَّهَا لَا تَعْمَى الأَبْصَارُ وَلَكِن تَعْمَى الْقُلُوبُ الَّتِي فِي الصُّدُورِ — it is not the eyes that go blind, but the hearts in the chests.

    1. Behind every command of Allah is wisdom (حِكْمَة). The apparent disconnect between "a murder" and "slaughter a cow" teaches us not to question Allah's commands but to trust that the wisdom may be revealed later — or may remain with Allah.

    2. Allah is ever-watchful. وَمَا اللَّهُ بِغَافِلٍ عَمَّا تَعْمَلُونَ is not only a warning to Bani Israel. It is a reminder to every person that their deeds are being recorded and will be recompensed.

    3. Do not place false hopes in inherited beliefs. The Jews inherited أَمَانِيٌّ كَاذِبَة (false hopes) from their forefathers — such as that they were Allah's beloved and the fire would barely touch them. Inherited religious ideas must be examined against revelation.

    Historical Context and Stories Mentioned

    The Murder of Bani Israel and the Slaughtered Cow

    A man was murdered within the community of Bani Israel. No one admitted guilt; instead each group accused the others (ادَّرَأْتُمْ). Allah revealed the solution: slaughter a specific cow (which became the subject of Ayat 67–71), then strike the murdered man's dead body with a piece of it. By Allah's power, the dead man was temporarily revived and identified his killer, resolving the case miraculously.

    Historical Significance of the Cow in Bani Israel's Culture

    Before receiving full Tawheed through Musa عليه السلام, Bani Israel had been cow-worshippers. The love of the cow (تَعْظِيم البَقَرَة) was embedded in their nature. This is why they delayed slaughtering it — both due to its high price and their residual veneration of cows (as evidenced later by their worship of the Golden Calf). Allah's command to slaughter a cow was itself a test of their Tawheed.

    Sabab al-Nuzul (Reason for Revelation) of Ayah 75

    • Narration 1: This ayah was revealed regarding the Ansar (رضي الله عنهم), who had prior bonds with the Jews of Madinah — bonds of alliance (حِلْف), neighborliness (جِوَار), and breastfeeding relations (رَضَاعَة). The Ansar sincerely wished and hoped that their Jewish neighbors would accept Islam. Allah revealed this ayah to inform the Muslims: do not hope for their Iman — they will not accept it.

    • Narration 2 (from Ibn Abbas via Mujahid): Jews claimed the total duration of this world is 7,000 years. They reasoned: for every 1,000 years of the world's existence, we will receive only one day of punishment in the Hellfire. Since the world is 7,000 years, our total punishment = 7 days only. Therefore: "the fire will not touch us except a few days." This false calculation gave them false security about the Hereafter — one of the أَمَانِيٌّ كَاذِبَة addressed in Ayat 75–82.

    Q&A Highlights

    Q: Are the three examples of rocks (rivers bursting, cracking, crumbling) metaphorical or literal?

    A: The teacher clarified these are literal examples, not merely metaphors. In mountainous regions (like Kashmir, which the teacher referenced), you can physically observe water gushing from solid rock faces — something scientifically remarkable, since water is expected near sea level, not mountain tops. Researchers have studied these phenomena. The point is: these are real, observable signs of Allah's power.

    However, the examples are also being used comparatively (تَشْبِيه) with the human heart — rocks are the paradigm of hardness, yet they respond to Allah. The hearts of Bani Israel did not.

    Q: If rocks are compared to hearts, what does the water represent?

    A: Water represents softness, tears, and the signs of a heart that fears Allah. When a person's heart is soft (قَلْبٌ رَطِيب / لَيِّن), they weep out of fear of Allah — tears from the eyes are the "water" emerging from the heart. A hard heart produces no tears, no خُشُوع (humility), no effect from reminders. The teacher connected this to the deeper meanings students can draw from contemplating (تَدَبُّر) the ayah.

    Q: Was "the stone" in Ayah 74 (الحِجَارَة) a specific stone or a general reference?

    A: Two scholarly positions: - Jumhur (majority): The definite article الـ in الحِجَارَة indicates a specific, known rock — likely the rock that Musa struck with his staff (from which 12 springs flowed for the 12 tribes), referenced earlier in Surah Al-Baqarah (Ayah 60). - Hasan al-Basri and others: It is non-specific (نَكِرَة in meaning), and the generality is actually more eloquent — the lesson applies to all rocks and all hard things, making the comparison to the heart even stronger.

    The teacher noted the textbook appears to lean toward the view of Hasan al-Basri and some others, though the majority view holds for specificity.
