    Fiqh 201 - Study Notes

    Date: 2026-11-18

    Session Overview

    This session covered two major topics from Imam Quduri's text: (1) the chapter of Shaheed — defining who qualifies as a martyr, the categories of martyrdom, and the specific rulings distinguishing their burial rites from those who die at home; and (2) the chapter on praying inside the Ka'bah, including rulings for different positions within the Ka'bah and praying on top of it. The session also included extended Q&A covering rebellion, ghiba (backbiting), qisas, diyat, and practical salah questions.

    Key Themes

    1. Definition and Categories of Shaheed — Who qualifies legally as a martyr and the two-tier distinction between shaheed for akhirah only versus shaheed for both dunya and akhirah.
    2. Burial Rites of the Shaheed — Why ghusl is omitted, what remains on the body, and what must be removed.
    3. The Murtas — The wounded fighter who exits the battlefield alive and regains consciousness, and why they lose the hukm of shaheed.
    4. Those Not Prayed Upon — The categories of Muslims (rebels and highway robbers) for whom salatul janazah is not performed, and the reasoning.
    5. Salah Inside and On Top of the Ka'bah — The permissibility, conditions, and scholarly disagreements around praying within or upon the Ka'bah.
    6. Closing Announcement — Kitab al-Zakat begins next Saturday; a test on Kitab al-Salah is planned with a study guide.

    Important Vocabulary

    Shahādah (شَهَادَة) Testimony; here referring to the testimony that the martyr will enter Jannah
    Mayyat (مَيِّت) Deceased person who dies at home (non-martyr)
    Dafan (دَفْن) Burial; placing the body in the ground
    Ghusl (غُسْل) Ritual washing of the deceased; omitted for shaheed
    Kafan (كَفَن) Shroud; the cloth wrapping for burial
    Kamees (قَمِيص) Long shirt/garment; first piece of the men's kafan
    Izar (إِزَار) Lower wrap; second piece of the men's kafan
    Lifafa (لِفَافَة) Full outer wrap; third piece of the men's kafan
    Diyat (دِيَة) Blood money; financial compensation for a killing
    Qisas (قِصَاص) Retaliation in kind; the legal right to take the life of a killer
    Hadd / Hudud (حَد / حُدُود) Fixed Qur'anic punishment(s) for major crimes
    Murtas (مُرْتَث) A wounded fighter removed alive from the battlefield who regains consciousness and receives treatment

    Janabah (جَنَابَة) Major ritual impurity requiring ghusl (e.g., after sexual intercourse)
    Junubi (جُنُبِي) A person in the state of janabah
    Wujub / Wajibiyyat (وُجُوب / وَاجِبِيَّة) Obligation; the binding requirement for an act

    Istiqbal (اسْتِقْبَال) Facing the qibla
    Makruh (مَكْرُوه) Disliked/reprehensible act; subcategories: makruh tahrimi (closer to haram) and makruh tanzihi (closer to permissible)
    Nawafil (نَوَافِل) Supererogatory/voluntary prayers
    Fard (فَرْض) Obligatory prayer/act
    Rajam (رَجْم) Stoning; prescribed punishment for a married person convicted of zina
    Zina (زِنَا) Unlawful sexual intercourse
    Fitna (فِتْنَة) Discord, civil strife, or unrest in the Muslim community

    Fiqh Rulings & Evidence

    1. Shaheed Receives No Ghusl but Is Shrouded and Prayed Upon

    Ruling: A shaheed (battlefield martyr) is given a kafan and salatul janazah is prayed over them, but ghusl is not performed.

    Evidence: This follows the established practice of the Shuhadā' of Uhud, who were buried without ghusl, in their blood and wounds, as ordered by the Prophet ﷺ.

    2. Blood and Clothing Are Not Removed from the Shaheed

    Ruling (Ahnaf): The blood on the shaheed's body is not washed off, and their clothing is not removed before burial.

    Evidence: The Prophet ﷺ said: "Wrap the shuhada in their clothes with their blood and wounds."

    Opposing view (Imam Shafi'): Even non-clothing items on the body should not be removed, since just as the body is not washed, nothing else should be altered either.

    Ahnaf rebuttal: The hadith of Ibn Abbas رضي الله عنه states that the Prophet ﷺ ordered the removal of armor and weapons, and burial with their blood and clothes — this hadith is explicit on the distinction.

    3. Armor, Weapons, and Non-Cloth Items Must Be Removed

    Ruling: Any item that is not part of the standard kafan — armor, helmet, sword, leather garments, socks — must be removed before burial, even from a shaheed.

    Principle: The kafan is composed solely of cloth (three pieces for men). Leather and metal are not of the kafan material and must be removed.

    4. If the Shaheed's Clothes Are Insufficient, Kafan Is Added

    Ruling: If the clothing on the shaheed's body does not meet the minimum kafan requirement, additional cloth is added to complete the standard kafan count.

    5. The Junubi Who Dies as Shaheed

    Question: If a person in the state of janabah (major ritual impurity) is killed as a shaheed, is ghusl given?

    Scholar Ruling
    Imam Abu Hanifa رحمه الله + Imam Malik رحمه الله Ghusl is given because the state of janabah was present
    Imam Abu Yusuf + Imam Muhammad (the Sahibain) + Imam Shafi' رحمهم الله No ghusl; they are buried as shaheed without ghusl

    Daleel of the Sahibain: The obligation of ghusl for janabah exists because the living person needs to perform ibadah (e.g., salah). Upon death, that obligation (wajibiyyat) becomes sakit (dropped), since the deceased no longer performs any act of worship. Therefore, ghusle janabah is not required.

    6. A Civilian Killed Unjustly with a Weapon Is Also Treated as Shaheed

    Ruling: If a person (not on a battlefield) is killed unjustly with a sharp weapon, is an adult, is in a state of purity (tahir), and no diyat or qisas was established against the killer, they are treated as shaheed — buried without ghusl but with janazah salah.

    7. Those Killed by Hadd or Qisas Are Given Ghusl and Janazah

    Ruling: A Muslim executed for a hadd crime (e.g., rajam for zina) or by qisas is given full ghusl and salatul janazah is prayed over them. They are still Muslim and deserve respect and dua.

    8. Salatul Janazah Is Not Prayed Upon Bughat or Qutta' al-Tariq

    Ruling: The funeral prayer is not performed for rebels (bughat) who took up arms against the legitimate Muslim state, nor for highway robbers (qutta' al-tariq).

    Reason: This is to publicly express disapproval of their destructive actions. Such people cause immense bloodshed and unrest; withholding the janazah prayer signals the gravity of their crime to the community.

    Important clarification: This ruling applies to their outward, worldly treatment (hukm al-dunya). Their internal state and maqam with Allah is a separate matter — if their intention was truly just, Allah knows and will treat them accordingly.

    9. Salatul Ghayb (Funeral Prayer in Absentia)

    Ruling: Ideally, salatul ghayb should not be performed as a binding practice. The Prophet ﷺ's prayer over Najashi is cited as the basis for its permissibility.

    Note: The Prophet ﷺ also prayed janazah over the Shuhadā' of Uhud eight years after their burial, which demonstrates that revisiting a janazah is permissible. If people perform salatul ghayb to show love and respect for a pious person, it is permissible but does not add to the original janazah. The authoritative janazah is the one performed where the deceased is present.

    10. Salah Inside the Ka'bah

    Ruling (Ahnaf): Permissible for both fard and nawafil, in any direction.

    Ruling (Imam Shafi'): No salah of any kind should be performed inside the Ka'bah.

    Ruling (Imam Malik): Nawafil are permissible inside; fard is not.

    Imam Malik's daleel: While every direction inside the Ka'bah faces the qibla, every direction also has one's back to the qibla simultaneously — this technical contradiction makes it impermissible for obligatory prayer.

    Ahnaf daleel: The Prophet ﷺ prayed nafil salah inside the Ka'bah on the Day of Fath Makkah — narrated by Umar رضي الله عنه who asked Bilal رضي الله عنه directly, and Bilal confirmed it. The Prophet ﷺ prayed between the two Yamani pillars. Since all conditions of salah are present inside the Ka'bah, and nawafil are confirmed by hadith, there is no logical distinction that excludes fard.

    11. Positions of the Muqtadi Relative to the Imam Inside the Ka'bah

    Muqtadi Position Ruling
    Back to back with the imam (facing opposite direction) Permissible
    Facing the imam (face to face) Permissible but makruh
    Standing directly in front of the imam with back to imam's face (ahead of imam) Salah of that muqtadi is invalid (mufsid)
    Closer to the Ka'bah wall than the imam (in a circular jama'ah) Permissible, provided he is not standing immediately beside or just ahead of the imam

    12. Praying On Top of the Ka'bah

    Ruling (Ahnaf): Permissible; no sutra is required, as the Ka'bah is not the name of the physical building but refers to that entire column of space from earth to sky. Even if the building were removed, the qibla remains.

    Ruling (Imam Shafi'): Permissible only if a sutra is placed in front.

    Daleel for it being makruh: A hadith in Tirmidhi narrated by Ibn Umar رضي الله عنه lists seven places where the Prophet ﷺ prohibited salah — one of which is on top of the Ka'bah. However, if someone does pray there, the salah is valid and accepted.

    Conditions & Categories

    Categories of Shaheed

    Type Description Ghusl? Janazah?
    Shaheed of Akhirah only Dies from disease, accident, pandemic, etc. — up to 70 categories mentioned by fuqaha Yes (normal burial rites) Yes
    Shaheed of Dunya and Akhirah Killed on the battlefield — this is the subject of Kitab al-Quduri No Yes

    Three Sub-Types of Shaheed (Dunya & Akhirah)

    1. Killed by mushrikun or kuffar — the highest degree; they are killing him specifically because he is Muslim.
    2. Found dead on the battlefield with wound marks — signs of fighting visible (wounds from sword/spear, bleeding from nose/eyes/ears). If no wounds are found, they are treated as a shaheed of akhirah only (dunya burial rites apply normally).
    3. Killed unjustly by Muslims (without diyat or qisas) — must be confirmed the killing was not by rajam or qisas (those killed in Hadd or Qisas are explicitly excluded).

    Definition of Murtas

    A murtas is a wounded fighter who: - Was found injured on the battlefield, AND - Was removed from the battlefield while conscious (ya'qil), AND - Was given food, drink, or medical treatment — OR — slept — OR — stayed alive for the duration of one full salah time (e.g., found in Zuhr time, remained alive until Asr)

    Hukm of Murtas: He is no longer in the hukm of shaheed (for dunya burial purposes). He will receive full ghusl and janazah. His status with Allah in the akhirah remains what Allah knows.

    Exception: If the wounded fighter was moved but never regained consciousness, never received treatment, and died in that state — he remains a shaheed, because he never left the condition of a dying fighter.

    The Seven Places Where Salah Is Prohibited (Hadith in Tirmidhi, narrated by Ibn Umar)

    1. The dung heap (animal waste area)
    2. The slaughtering area (place of excessive blood and filth)
    3. The graveyard (directly on or immediately among graves)
    4. Commonly used roads/pathways (causes harm to passers-by and distorts image of Islam)
    5. The washing area (bathroom/ritual washing area)
    6. Where camels rest
    7. On top of the Ka'bah

    Note: Salah performed in any of these places is valid but makruh. Praying on a clean cloth laid over such an area may bring it into the makruh tanzihi category — the precise sub-category requires further research in the hashiyah of the hadith.

    Scholarly Opinions

    On Salatul Janazah for the Shaheed

    Scholar Opinion Reasoning
    Imam Shafi' رحمه الله Janazah is NOT prayed on the shaheed The shaheed's sins are wiped by the sword; they need no intercession
    Ahnaf Janazah IS prayed (1) The Prophet ﷺ prayed janazah on the Shuhadā' of Uhud after 8 years; (2) Janazah is also an act of respect/farewell, not only intercession; (3) Even the Prophet ﷺ (sinless) and children (sinless) have janazah prayed — dua and respect are always appropriate

    On Ghusl for a Junubi Shaheed

    Scholar Opinion
    Imam Abu Hanifa + Imam Malik Ghusl is given (janabah obligation remains)
    Sahibain (Abu Yusuf + Muhammad) + Imam Shafi' No ghusl (janabah obligation is sakit upon death)

    On Removal of Items from the Shaheed's Body

    Scholar Opinion
    Ahnaf Blood and clothing kept; non-cloth items (leather, armor, weapons) removed
    Imam Shafi' Nothing is removed — same as the body, nothing on it is altered

    Imam Shafi' Not permissible Not permissible

    On Rebellion (Bughat) — Nuanced Discussion

    • Clear rebel: One who actively incites Muslims to overthrow the government and leads or inspires an armed uprising → Shaheed of dunya hukm does not apply; janazah withheld.
    • Vocal critic / peaceful protester: Trying to raise awareness, reform, or inform the public → Not considered bughat; this is permissible and even necessary in some circumstances.
    • Context of non-Sharia governments: Most contemporary Muslim-majority countries do not implement Sharia properly; "rebellion" against such governments is a more complex fiqhi discussion — the outward hukm still applies publicly, but the internal maqam with Allah depends on sincerity and justice of cause.

    Lessons & Practical Application

    1. The shaheed is honored through burial as-is. The unwashed blood and wounds are a testimony to their sacrifice. When burying a shaheed, do not wash the body; do not remove the clothes; do remove all non-cloth items (weapons, armor, leather).

    2. Know the murtas distinction. If you encounter a severely wounded Muslim fighter who is still alive: if they are moved from the battlefield while conscious and begin to receive treatment, they exit the hukm of shaheed for burial purposes. This is a critical practical ruling for anyone in a situation of conflict.

    3. Salah inside the Ka'bah (for those who get the opportunity): Any direction is valid according to Ahnaf. Do not stand directly ahead of the imam with your back to their face — this invalidates your prayer. Facing the imam is allowed but makruh. Face-to-back (the common circle arrangement in Masjid al-Haram) is fully valid.

    4. Sutra etiquette: Place the sutra slightly to the right or left — not directly centered in front of the musalli. The imam's sutra covers the entire first row. If someone is about to pass in front of you and is within reach, you may extend a hand to stop them — this is protecting your salah, not an action against it. If the person ignores you and passes anyway, your salah remains valid; refocus and continue.

    5. Seven prohibited places for salah: Memorize these. Salah is valid in all of them but makruh. Avoid them unless there is genuine necessity.

    6. On ghiba in unavoidable settings: If trapped in a situation where others are backbiting (e.g., in a car), try to redirect the conversation or state your position. If neither is possible, maintain firm rejection in your heart — this is the lowest level of iman regarding changing a wrong. Plan to avoid such company in the future.

    7. On qisas and diyat: Qisas (like-for-like retaliation, including capital punishment) is only required in one of five categories of killing — the deliberate, premeditated killing. Diyat (blood money) is a settlement alternative. Detailed rulings will come in Kitab al-Hudud wa al-Qisas.
