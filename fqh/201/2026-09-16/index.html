    Fiqh 201 - Study Notes

    Date: 2026-09-16

    Session Overview

    This session was primarily a Q&A and review session covering several topics from Kitab al-Tahara (the Book of Purification). The class addressed student questions on: irregular menstrual bleeding (with gaps), Qur'an recitation during Haid, tanning and permissible use of animal by-products, conditions for Masa on Jorabayn (socks), the Najasat-e-Mughalaza threshold (dirham size), Najasat-e-Mukhafaffa and the one-fourth rule, visibility-based washing requirements, and the rulings on the three male discharges (Mani, Madhi, Wadi). The class concluded by announcing an upcoming review quiz on Kitab al-Tahara and noting that Istinja remains to be covered next session.

    Key Themes

    1. Irregular Haid and the 15-day Tuhar rule — how to classify bleeding when there is a gap of less or more than 15 days between two episodes.
    2. Prohibitions during Haid — Qur'an recitation, entering the Masjid, and how intent (niyyat) distinguishes du'a from recitation.
    3. Tanning (Dabaghat) and animal by-products — what tanning purifies, the eight permissible non-food parts of an animal, and the exclusion of pig and human.
    4. Masa on Jorabayn — three conditions that must be met for wiping over socks to be valid.
    5. Najasaat thresholds for Salah validity — dirham size for Najasat-e-Mughalaza (with weight alternative of 3.5 Masha), one-fourth of garment for Najasat-e-Mukhafaffa.
    6. Purifying visible vs. invisible Najasa — washing until traces are gone vs. washing until the heart is satisfied.
    7. The three male discharges — Mani, Madhi, Wadi: which require Ghusl vs. Wudu only, and their Tahara rulings when wet vs. dry.
    8. Istihada and chronic discharge — persons with continuous or near-continuous impurity are treated as ill (Marid) with relaxed Wudu rules.

    Fiqh Rulings & Evidence

    1. Irregular Menstrual Bleeding (Gap Between Two Bleedings)

    • If a woman bleeds, then has a gap of less than 15 days, then bleeds again → the entire period (both bleedings + gap) is counted as Haid. She does not pray and does not have relations during this entire period.
    • If the gap between two bleedings is 15 days or more → the gap is a valid Tuhar. If bleeding restarts after it, the second episode is a new Haid. The earlier episode is evaluated separately.
    • Minimum Tuhar: 15 days.
    • Minimum Haid: 3 days; maximum: 10 days.

    2. Qur'an Recitation During Haid

    • Reciting the Qur'an is not permissible during Haid — whether from memory, from a written copy, or while teaching.
    • Du'a using Qur'anic verses is permissible if the intention is du'a, not recitation of the Qur'an itself.
    • A woman who is a Qur'an teacher may not teach recitation during her Haid, even remotely (outside the Masjid).
    • Non-verbal correction (gesture, hand-signal) to a student making a recitation mistake is permissible since no Qur'anic words are uttered.
    • The impurity of Haid (and Janabat) is considered to be within the body, including inside the mouth — this is a reason the mouth is washed (madmada/garara) during Ghusl from Janabat.

    3. Entering the Masjid During Haid

    • A woman in Haid may not enter the proper Masjid (the designated prayer area where the Imam leads Salah).
    • A Musalla (general prayer space, not a formal Masjid) is permissible.

    4. Tanning (Dabaghat) Purifies Leather

    • Any animal hide — regardless of how the animal was killed (Zabiha, non-Zabiha, hunted, otherwise slaughtered) — becomes pure after tanning.
    • Dalil: Multiple ahadith establish this.
    • Exceptions: The skin of swine (pig) and humans cannot be purified by tanning and are not permissible.
    • Leather from stores (cow, sheep, etc.) is pure and permissible for use and Salah.
    • Zabiha is not a condition for tanning to be effective.

    5. Eight Permissible Animal By-Products (Without Tanning Required)

    These eight parts of any animal (except pig and human) are inherently pure and permissible for use without tanning:

    # Part
    1 Hair
    2 Bones (when fully dried)
    3 Hooves
    4 Horns
    5 Wool
    6 Nails
    7 Feathers
    8 Beak
    • Teeth and raw skin (hide before tanning) are not included in this list.
    • These are for external usage, not consumption under normal circumstances. In extreme necessity (no other food), consumption may be permissible.
    • Zabiha is not a condition for using these eight parts.

    6. Conditions for Masa on Jorabayn (Socks)

    Three conditions must all be met for Masa on fabric socks to be valid:

    # Condition Detail
    1 Above the ankle and stays up Must cover above the ankle bone and not slide down on its own after wearing
    2 Water-resistant When wet hands are placed on the sock, water does not seep through within ~2–3 seconds (the duration of normal wiping)
    3 Durable for 1 mile Can be walked in for approximately one mile without tearing at the bottom
    • Leather Khuff: Only the "above-ankle and stays up" condition is universally required; leather is inherently waterproof. Coldness felt through leather from stepping in water does not invalidate it.
    • Minority opinion (Qila / Waqala): Only the waterproof condition is required for fabric socks. The Muftabih (ijma) opinion requires all three.
    • Test for socks: Wet your hands thoroughly and place them on the sock — if water seeps through within 2–3 seconds, the sock does not meet the condition.

    7. Najasat-e-Mughalaza — Threshold for Salah Validity

    Classified as Najasat-e-Mughalaza: Blood, urine (human), feces, Khamar (wine). Also: pus (same ruling as flowing blood). Not included: Vomit (food returning from inside is not Najis in the same ruling).

    Amount on body/garment Ruling
    Equal to or less than dirham size (liquid) / 3.5 Masha (~3.2–3.5 g) (solid) Salah is valid (with Makruh Tanzihi — disliked; washing it first is strongly preferred)
    More than dirham size / 3.5 Masha Salah is not permissible without washing
    • Dirham size: Approximately the inner circle of the palm, ~3.5 cm diameter.
    • 3.5 Masha weight: 1 Masha = 0.972 g; 3.5 Masha ≈ 3.2–3.5 g (teacher noted to verify if it is 5 g).
    • Imam Shafi'i and Imam Zufar (rah): Even a small amount of Najasat-e-Mughalaza invalidates Salah — no concession. Shawafi follow this.
    • Ahnaf (Hanafi position): Up to dirham size is pardoned; Salah is valid with Karahat.
    • Any discharge from the body (urine, blood) breaks Wudu the moment it exits, regardless of quantity.
    • If found after Salah (person was unaware), the Salah is valid and does not need to be repeated.

    8. Najasat-e-Mukhafaffa — Threshold for Salah Validity

    Examples: Urine of animals whose meat is Halal (cow, goat, etc.).

    Amount on garment Ruling
    Less than one-fourth of the garment Salah valid (with Karahat)
    One-fourth or more Salah not permissible without washing
    • Hanafi Usul: The rule of one-fourth appearing as equivalent to the whole is a principle applied in several acts of worship (e.g., Masa on one-fourth of the head in Wudu is sufficient).

    9. The Three Male Discharges

    Discharge Description Ruling
    Mani (semen) Ejaculation Requires Ghusl; if wet → must be washed off regardless of quantity; if dry → rubbing/scraping off is sufficient (Hadith of Aisha RA); Imam Shafi'i does not consider it Najis but Ahnaf follow the ahadith directing washing
    Madhi (pre-seminal fluid) Released due to arousal without full ejaculation Breaks Wudu only (no Ghusl); treated as Najasat-e-Mughalaza for threshold purposes; must be washed off
    Wadi (post-urinary discharge) Thick fluid sometimes appearing after urination Breaks Wudu only (no Ghusl); wash and make Wudu
    • Dalil for Madhi: Hadith of Ali (RA) — he had this condition and was embarrassed to ask the Prophet ﷺ directly due to being Fatima's husband; asked through another Sahabi; Prophet ﷺ said Ghusl is not required.
    • If one of these three is found on a garment not being worn and allowed to dry, it may be rubbed off and prayed in. If touching other clothing/body parts while wet, those must be washed.

    10. Purifying Najasa — Two Methods

    Case 1 — Visible Najasa (has a traceable body/color): - Wash repeatedly until all traces (color, smell) are gone. - If after extensive washing a faint color or mark cannot be removed despite best effort → it is pardoned; the cloth is considered pure.

    Case 2 — Invisible Najasa (cannot be seen, but presence is known): - No size or color to measure; wash the area enough times until one has dominant certainty (ghalabat al-dhann) that the area is clean. - No fixed number of washes; it is based on the washer's satisfaction of heart.

    • General guidance on three washes: Three times is the minimum established by Sunnah that reasonably ensures cleanliness.

    Conditions for Jorabayn Masa (Summary)

    1. Above the ankle and does not slide down.
    2. Water does not seep through within normal wiping duration (~2–3 sec).
    3. Does not tear after walking approximately one mile.

    Istihada / Chronic Impurity — Relaxed Rules

    • A person with a medically chronic, uncontrollable condition (continuous urine leakage, non-stop bleeding, continuous discharge) falls under the ruling of Marid (ill person).
    • Such a person makes one fresh Wudu per Salah time and may perform any number of acts of worship within that time period.
    • If the condition is irregular (some days present, some days absent) → teacher to follow up on the exact qualifying period.

    Lessons & Practical Application

    1. Irregular bleeding: If a woman's bleeding stops and restarts within 15 days, she treats the entire period (including the gap) as Haid. She does not pray, does not fast, and does not have marital relations. She resumes only after full Tuhar (15+ days) and Ghusl.

    2. Teachers/students during Haid: A female Qur'an teacher should suspend teaching during Haid — whether in-person, online, or at a Masjid. Non-verbal correction (gesture, tap) is permissible. Du'a using Qur'anic phrases is permissible with the intention of du'a only.

    3. Purchasing leather goods: Muslims may purchase and use leather products (bags, belts, shoes) from regular stores. As long as it is not pig skin, the item is pure and Salah on/with it is valid. Zabiha is irrelevant for leather.

    4. Sock testing for Masa: Before relying on Masa over fabric socks, test them: (a) confirm they reach above the ankle and don't slide, (b) wet your hand and press — water should not seep through in 2–3 seconds, (c) confirm they are sturdy enough to walk a mile in.

    5. Stain on clothing before Salah: If a small bloodstain (less than dirham size) is found, Salah is technically valid. However, if time and ability permit, washing it before Salah is strongly preferred (Makruh Tanzihi to pray with it even if under the threshold). If found after Salah unknowingly, the Salah is valid and need not be repeated.

    6. Chronic leakage/discharge: A person suffering from uncontrollable, continuous impurity (urine leakage, bleeding from a wound) should make Wudu at the start of each Salah time, perform Salah, and continue with ibadah. If the condition worsens during Salah, the Salah is still valid.

    7. Dry Mani on garment: If dried semen is found on clothing and the garment is not one's own (e.g. a borrowed cloth), one should scrape/rub it off until the traces are gone; Salah is then valid. If wet, it must be washed regardless of quantity.

    8. Washing Najasa: For visible stains, wash until color and odor are gone; residual stubborn color after thorough washing is pardoned. For invisible Najasa (you know something fell but can't see it), wash until you are personally certain the area is clean.

    9. Upcoming: Kitab al-Tahara review quiz (multiple choice, ~10–15 questions, based on matn only — not the extended explanations). Istinja to be covered next session (Saturday). Kitab al-Salah to begin after.
