    Fiqh 201 - Study Notes

    Date: 2026-11-04

    Session Overview

    This session continued the chapter on Salat al-Musafir (prayer of the traveler) from Mukhtasar al-Quduri (Bushra edition, p. 116) and began Salat al-Jumu'ah. Topics included: the musafir entering his own city or old city, the three types of watan, the ruling on qada prayers made up during/after travel, praying on ships and airplanes, combining two prayers (jam'), the virtues of Jumu'ah, and the conditions (shurut) that make Jumu'ah obligatory.

    Key Themes

    1. Watan and its effect on Qasr — Entering one's own city ends the traveler's concession immediately; entering an old city (from which one has migrated) does not.
    2. Three types of Watan — Watani Asli, Watani Iqama, Watan al-Safar; a person can only have one Watani Asli at a time.
    3. Qada prayers preserve their original count — Prayers missed while traveling are made up as two rak'at; prayers missed while resident are made up as four rak'at, even if made up during travel.
    4. Jam' (combining prayers) — Ahnaf permit combining fi'lan (by act, through delay/advance) but not waqtan (by merging their times); Maliki and Shafi'i permit full combination in cases of travel, rain, and sickness.
    5. Praying on a ship — Imam Abu Hanifa: permitted seated in all conditions; al-Sahibayn (Abu Yusuf and Muhammad): only permitted with a valid excuse (uzr).
    6. Jumu'ah — Introduction to the fadail (virtues) of the day, historical origins, and the twelve conditions that make Jumu'ah fard, with scholarly differences on the requirement of a "city."

    Fiqh Rulings & Evidence

    Musafir Entering His Own City

    • When a musafir enters his own city (watan), he must pray full salah, even if he has no intention of staying, and even if he is merely passing through.
    • The boundary of the city is the determining line: the moment one crosses back into the city's limits, the Rukhsa of Qasr ends.

    Musafir Entering His Old City (From Which He Migrated)

    • If a person has migrated away and established a new Watani Asli elsewhere, returning to his old city does not require full salah — he prays Qasr.
    • Evidence: The Prophet ﷺ left Makkah and settled in Madinah (Madinah became his new Watani Asli). When he returned to Makkah (for Fath Makkah and Hajj), he prayed Qasr there.

    Niyyah (Intention) of Imam and Muqtadi

    • The niyyah of the imam and muqtadi must match — same prayer, same number of rak'at, same fard.
    • If a musafir leads the prayer, he makes salaam after two rak'at and announces that he is a musafir; resident followers stand and complete the remaining two rak'at independently.

    Niyyah of 15-Day Stay Split Between Two Cities

    • If a traveler intends to stay 15 days between two cities (e.g., Makkah and Mina), this does not make him a Muqim — he still prays Qasr.
    • Reason: His 15-day niyyah is divided, not in one single city.

    Qada Prayers: Original Status Is Preserved

    • Salah missed during travel → made up at home: Pray as Qasr (two rak'at). The reduced number is what became obligatory.
    • Salah missed at home (while resident) → made up during travel: Pray as full (four rak'at). The full number is what became obligatory and must be fulfilled.

    Praying Seated on a Ship

    • Imam Abu Hanifa: Permitted to pray seated in all conditions while on a ship — no uzr required. His reasoning: the nature of a ship is constant instability, so the uzr is inherent.
    • Note: If the ship is docked, the ruling changes — one should stand and pray.
    • Al-Sahibayn (Abu Yusuf & Muhammad): Only permitted with a valid uzr (dizziness, fear of falling, excessive movement).
    • Airplanes: More stable than ships in most conditions; therefore the Rukhsa is more restricted. If the plane is stable, one should stand and pray. If turbulence poses a genuine risk, one may sit.
    • If a passenger cannot face the Qibla and is not permitted to stand, two Hanafi opinions exist:
      1. Pray in whatever direction the plane faces, then repeat the salah later.
      2. Leave the salah entirely and pray upon reaching the destination (this is the dominant/preferred opinion among Ahnaf).

    Qasr for a Traveler with Sinful Intent

    • Ahnaf: The Rukhsa of Qasr applies to all travelers regardless of their intention — good or sinful — because the Nusus (texts) granting Qasr contain no restriction (qaid) limiting it to righteous travelers. The underlying 'illah is the travel itself.
    • Other scholars: Deny the Rukhsa to one traveling for an impermissible purpose.

    Combining Two Prayers (Jam')

    • Ahnaf position: Permitted fi'lan (by act) but not waqtan (by merging actual times).
    • Method: Delay Zuhr to the very end of its time, then immediately pray Asr at the start of its time — the two appear combined but each is prayed in its own time.
    • Imam Malik and Imam Shafi'i: Permit true combination (one prayer brought into the other's time) in cases of travel, rain, and sickness.
    • Permissible combinations only: Zuhr + Asr, and Maghrib + Isha. No other combination is permitted by any madhab.
    • Exception — Hajj: At Arafat, Zuhr and Asr are combined (taqdim of Asr into Zuhr time). At Muzdalifah, Maghrib and Isha are combined (takhir of Maghrib into Isha time). Ahnaf prefer the fi'lan method even here, but following the Amir al-Hajj's lead is permissible and the congregant does not repeat.
    • Following a Shafi'i imam: A Hanafi may pray both prayers behind a Shafi'i imam who combines them while traveling — the salah is valid and need not be repeated.

    Conditions & Categories

    Three Types of Watan

    Type Definition Ruling
    Watani Asli Primary homeland — where one lives permanently with family Full salah required upon entry; a person can only have one Watani Asli
    Watani Iqama A city where a traveler intends to stay 15+ days Full salah required for the duration of the stay
    Watan al-Safar A city where one stays fewer than 15 days or is simply passing through Qasr continues; some scholars hold this is not a true "watan" at all

    Note: Some scholars hold there are only two types of watan (Asli and Iqama), not three.

    Determining Watani Asli: Defined by where one spends most of their nights and has settled with family and belongings. One cannot hold two Watani Aslis simultaneously. The Watani Asli is not invalidated by a stay in a Watani Iqama (the Sahabah retained Madinah as their Watani Asli even during lengthy ghazawat).

    Twelve Conditions for Jumu'ah to be Fard

    Six conditions relating to the Musalli (the individual):

    # Condition
    1 Free person (not a slave — Jumu'ah is not fard on a slave)
    2 Male (not fard on women; they pray Zuhr at home)
    3 Muqim / Resident (not fard on a musafir)
    4 Healthy (not fard on the sick)
    5 Able to see (not fard on the blind)
    6 Adult / Baligh (not fard on children)

    Six external/circumstantial conditions:

    # Condition
    1 A Misr (city) — Jumu'ah is not valid in a small village (per Ahnaf)
    2 Jama'at (congregation) — not valid for an individual praying alone
    3 Sultan or his deputy — in modern context, the appointed imam of the masjid stands in this role
    4 Time of Zuhr — Jumu'ah is only valid within the time of Zuhr
    5 Khutbah — the sermon is a shart (condition) of the Jumu'ah prayer itself
    6 Idhn 'Aam (general permission/open invitation) — the gathering must be publicly open

    Definitions of "Misr" (City) for Jumu'ah Validity

    Scholar Definition
    Imam Abu Hanifa A place where all types of facilities for deen and dunya are available
    Imam Abu Yusuf (1st opinion) A place where a ruler and qadi are present to establish Islamic rulings (accepted by Imam al-Qarkhi)
    Imam Abu Yusuf (2nd opinion) A place so populated that if everyone came to one masjid, they would not all fit (accepted by Muhammad ibn Shuja')
    Sufyan al-Thawri Whatever the people of a given era call a "city" — it is a city; whatever they call a "village" — it is a village
    Imam al-Shafi'i Any place where 40 Muslims reside year-round (not seasonally) — Jumu'ah is valid there
    Imam Ahmad (one opinion) Same as Imam al-Shafi'i: 40 permanent residents

    Scholarly Opinions

    On Praying Seated on a Ship

    • Imam Abu Hanifa: Seated prayer permitted in all circumstances (the ship's inherent instability constitutes a permanent uzr).
    • Al-Sahibayn: Seated prayer permitted only with a specific, present uzr (dizziness, fear of falling).

    On Combining Prayers

    • Ahnaf: Only fi'lan (apparent combination by stretching times), not waqtan (actual time-merging). Exceptions: Arafat (Zuhr+Asr) and Muzdalifah (Maghrib+Isha) during Hajj, following the Amir al-Hajj.
    • Imam Malik and Imam Shafi'i: True combination permitted in travel, rain, and sickness.

    On Jumu'ah in Villages

    • Ahnaf: Only valid in a Misr (city) or its designated open prayer field (musalla); not valid in villages.
    • Dalil: Hadith of Sayyiduna Ali ؓ: "Jumu'ah, Tashriq, and Idayn prayers are only valid and mashroo' in cities (Amsar)."
    • Response to Masjid Abdul Qais argument: The place mentioned (Jawsah, Bahrain) was in fact a city, not a village; in that era, both cities and villages were called "qariya," but Jawsah was a major city.
    • Corroborating evidence: Hadith of Aisha ؓ in Bukhari describes people coming from al-Awali (outskirts of Madinah, ~4 miles away) to attend Jumu'ah — indicating it was a large, central city gathering.
    • Imam al-Shafi'i and Imam Ahmad (one opinion): No restriction on village vs. city; valid wherever 40 permanent Muslim residents exist.
    • Dalil: The Quranic ayah in Surah al-Jumu'ah does not restrict by size of locality.
    • Second dalil: First Jumu'ah outside Masjid al-Nabi was in Masjid Abdul Qais (a "village" in Bahrain).

    On Qasr for a Traveler with Sinful Intent

    • Ahnaf: Rukhsa applies regardless of intent; the texts granting Qasr carry no restriction to righteous travel.
    • Other scholars: Rukhsa is denied to one traveling for an impermissible purpose.

    Lessons & Practical Application

    1. Qasr begins upon leaving the city, not upon reaching 48 miles. The intention must be to travel 48+ miles, but Qasr starts the moment you exit your city's boundaries — not when you hit the distance marker.

    2. Qasr ends upon re-entering your city. Even one foot inside the city limits ends the concession. It is permissible — and encouraged by Ahnaf — to take advantage of the Rukhsa by praying Qasr at a masjid just before entering your city if Salah time is running out.

    3. Rukhsa is a gift — accept it. Ahnaf hold that refusing a concession Allah has given is like refusing a gift. When Qasr is available, one should take it.

    4. Sunnah prayers during travel: The concession covers fard prayers. Sunnah prayers may be left without issue during travel, though if one has ample time and is settled, they are encouraged.

    5. The 15-day rule requires niyyah in one place. If your 15 days are split between two cities (even as close as Makkah and Mina), you do not become Muqim — you continue praying Qasr.

    6. Missed prayers retain their original form. If you missed a prayer while traveling, you will always make it up as two rak'at — no matter when or where you make it up. If you missed a prayer while resident, you will always make it up as four rak'at — even during travel.

    7. Following a non-Hanafi imam in jam': A Hanafi praying behind a Shafi'i imam who combines prayers in a valid scenario (travel, rain) need not repeat the prayer. The salah is valid. However, a Hanafi should not lead such a combination himself.

    8. Jumu'ah is not fard on: a musafir, a slave, a woman, a sick person, a blind person, or a child. If any of these categories attend and pray behind the imam, their prayer is valid and rewarded.

    9. Jumu'ah: the Friday prayer is "Jumu'ah," not "Jum'ah." Pronunciation note from the class.

    10. The virtues of Jumu'ah: The Prophet ﷺ described it as the best day the sun rises upon. Adam ﷺ was created on it, entered Jannah on it, was sent to earth on it, his du'a was accepted on it, and he passed away on it. All living beings (except humans and jinn) live in fear of Qiyamah occurring on this day — from Subh Sadiq until sunrise. It is a day to seek death upon (the Prophet ﷺ's words encourage this).

    11. Practical Jumu'ah planning for residents: Since Jumu'ah is only fard in a Misr, those living in areas that might be categorized as large villages should verify which scholarly opinion their locality falls under — particularly the Abu Yusuf opinion (too many people for one masjid = effectively a city) may apply to densely populated suburbs.
