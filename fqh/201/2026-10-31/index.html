    Fiqh 201 - Study Notes

    Date: 2026-10-31

    Session Overview

    This session covered two major topics from Imam Quduri's Mukhtasar: (1) Sajda Tilawat (Prostration of Recitation) — its obligatory nature, the 14 Quranic positions, rulings for the reciter and listener, procedural details, and edge cases involving salah; and (2) Salat al-Musafir (Prayer of the Traveler) — the definition of safar, the distance threshold (48 miles), when qasr begins and ends, the ruling of two rakat for four-rakat prayers, and conditions for resuming full prayer.

    Key Themes

    1. Sajda Tilawat is wajib on both the reciter (tali) and the listener (sami'), regardless of whether the listener intended to hear Quran.
    2. 14 positions of sajda in the Quran according to the Hanafi madhab; other scholars count 15.
    3. Safar (travel) legally begins the moment a person leaves the boundary of their city with the intention to travel at least 48 miles.
    4. Qasr is wajib for the Hanafi traveler — praying four rakat when two are required is impermissible and causes deficiencies in salah.
    5. 15-day intention rule: a traveler becomes a resident (muqim) only if they intend to stay in a city for 15 days or more.

    Important Vocabulary

    Arabic Term Meaning

    Fiqh Rulings & Evidence

    Sajda Tilawat

    Ruling 1 — Wajib on reciter and listener alike Sajda Tilawat is wajib on both the tali (reciter) and the sami' (listener), even if the listener had no intention to listen to Quran.

    Dalil 1 — Hadith of Ibn Abbas (Bukhari): The Prophet ﷺ said: "I prostrated while reciting Surah al-Najm, and with me prostrated the Muslims, the pagans, the jinn, and all human beings." (Note: The prostration of the pagans and all beings may indicate a different dimension/realm — the instructor indicated further research on this point.)

    Dalil 2 — Hadith of Ibn Umar (Bukhari): When the Prophet ﷺ recited a surah containing a sajda verse and the companions were with him, they would all prostrate together. Sometimes due to the heavy crowd, some could not find a place for their foreheads — prostrating on another person's back is permissible when there is absolutely no space.

    Ruling 2 — Three types of sajda verses Ahnaf categorize sajda verses into three types: 1. Explicit command (amr/hukum) of sajda — directly makes sajda wajib. 2. Action of a Prophet mentioned — following the anbiya in that action is necessary. 3. Condemnation of those who refused sajda — in opposition to their kufr, sajda is performed.

    Ruling 3 — Sajda inside salah upon hearing an outside reciter If a person is praying and someone outside the salah recites a sajda verse: - They cannot perform sajda during the salah (as it would be an action not part of the salah's structure in this context). - However, sajda is still wajib; they must perform it after finishing their salah. - If they do fall into sajda during the salah: the salah is not broken (because sajda is itself a rukn of salah), but the sajda does not count toward fulfilling the wajib — they still owe it after salah.

    Ruling 4 — Sajda in salah covering a pre-salah recitation If a person recited a sajda verse before starting salah but did not yet perform the prostration, then recited it again during salah and performed the sajda: that one sajda covers both recitations (the pre-salah and in-salah occurrences).

    Ruling 5 — New recitation in salah after already having performed sajda If a person recited a verse, performed sajda, then entered salah and recited the same verse again: the previous sajda is not sufficient. This is a new tilawah requiring a new sajda.

    Ruling 6 — Multiple recitations in one majlis If a person recites a sajda verse multiple times in the same sitting (majlis) — e.g., while memorizing — one sajda suffices for all those recitations.

    Ruling 7 — Making up missed sajdas If someone has missed many sajdas throughout their life, they should make their best estimate and make up those sajdas. They may make a general intention (e.g., "I am performing sajda for 10 missed tilawat sajdas") without needing to specify each individual instance.

    Ruling 8 — Muktadi reciting behind imam Ahnaf hold that the muktadi should not recite behind the imam (the imam is the dhamin). If a muktadi recites a sajda verse on their own (e.g., silently in the third rakat), sajda is not wajib on the imam or any of the muktadis.

    Salat al-Musafir

    Ruling 9 — Qasr is wajib, not merely permissible (Hanafi position) As per Ahnaf, a traveler must pray two rakat for all four-rakat prayers. It is not merely a concession to take or leave.

    Dalil — Hadith of Ibn Umar (Bukhari): "I accompanied the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and he never offered more than two rakat during a journey — nor did Abu Bakr, Umar, or Uthman."

    Dalil — Hadith of Aisha: "Originally salah was established as two rakat; for the resident it was increased to four, while for the traveler it remained as two."

    Dalil — Hadith of Ibn Abbas: "Allah, through the tongue of the Prophet ﷺ, made four rakat during residence and two rakat during travel."

    Dalil — Hadith of Umar: "The salah of travel and of Duha are two rakat each."

    Ruling 10 — Praying four rakat while on safar If a traveler prays four rakat: - If they sat for the duration of tashahhud after the second rakat: the first two count as fard, the last two are counted as nafl. - If they did not sit after the second rakat and proceeded directly to the third: the salah is batil (void).

    Ruling 11 — Doing four knowingly introduces four deficiencies Knowingly praying four rakat when qasr is required creates four issues: 1. Delaying salam beyond the wajib point (after 2nd rakat) — making sajda sahu wajib. 2. Omitting the wajib of qasr itself. 3. Omitting the opening takbir for the nafl portion (no fresh intention was made). 4. Combining fard and nafl in a single prayer without separate intention.

    Ruling 12 — Imam Shafi'i's position (for contrast) Imam Shafi'i holds qasr is a concession, not an obligation, citing the Quranic principle of la junaha (no blame) — the same word used for permitting divorce, which does not make divorce wajib. Ahnaf respond with the consistent practice of the Prophet ﷺ and the Khulafa al-Rashidun.

    Ruling 13 — Musafir leading muqimin in salah If a musafir leads as imam for residents (muqimin): he prays only two rakat and makes salam. The muqimin behind him then stand and complete their remaining two rakat on their own. It is recommended (mustahabb) for the musafir imam to turn to them after salam and say: "Atimmu salatakum fa inna qawmun safar" — "Complete your salah, for we are travelers."

    Ruling 14 — Musafir joining a muqim imam If a musafir joins the salah of a resident imam while the prayer time is still in: he prays the full four rakat (itmam), following the imam.

    Ruling 15 — Musafir joining a muqim imam for a qada prayer If a musafir joins a resident imam who is praying a makeup (qada) prayer, the musafir's salah is not valid behind him in this case.

    Ruling 16 — Missing prayers while on safar; making them up in hadar (residence) A prayer missed during safar is made up as two rakat even if performed later while a resident. A prayer missed during hadar is made up as four rakat even if performed later while traveling.

    Ruling 17 — Army in enemy territory (Dar al-Harb) A Muslim army that has entered enemy territory and intends to stay 15 days is still given the concession of qasr — the normal 15-day rule that would convert them to muqimin does not apply in a war zone.

    Conditions & Categories

    Conditions for Sajda Tilawat to be Wajib

    • The specific ayah must be from the 14 (Hanafi) designated positions in the Quran.
    • The verse must have been recited or heard (intention to listen is not required for the listener).
    • For a listener inside salah: the reciter must be the imam of that salah. A reciter outside the salah, or a muktadi reciting behind the imam, does not trigger sajda on the congregation.

    How to Perform Sajda Tilawat

    1. Say Allahu Akbar (takbir) — hands are not raised to the ears.
    2. Go into a single sajda.
    3. Say Allahu Akbar while raising the head.
    4. There is no tashahhud.
    5. There is no salam.
    6. It is a single sajda only (not two).

    Conditions for Becoming a Musafir

    Two conditions must both be met: 1. Niyyah (intention): intention to travel to a destination that is 48 miles or more away. 2. Actual departure: physically leaving one's city (crossing the boundary where the buildings of the city end).

    When Qasr Begins and Ends

    • Begins: the moment one crosses the boundaries/buildings of one's own city.
    • Ends: either (a) upon returning and entering the boundary of one's home city, or (b) upon forming a firm intention (niyyah) to stay in a given city for 15 days or more — at which point the traveler becomes a muqim and must pray full salah.

    The 15-Day Rule

    • 15 days or more (with firm intention): becomes muqim; must pray full salah.
    • Less than 15 days: remains musafir; prays qasr.
    • No fixed intention (keeps saying "just a few more days"): remains musafir indefinitely, even if objectively they stay for months or a year.

    Distance Threshold: 48 Miles

    • Ahnaf: a person walks approximately 16 miles per day on average (with luggage, at caravan pace).
    • Three days × 16 miles = 48 miles (also the opinion of Imam Malik, via his 4-burud measure).
    • Imam Malik's measure: 4 burud = 48 miles (coincides with Hanafi calculation).
    • Other opinions: some say 10, 15, or 21 farsakh; Imam Malik also mentions 4 burud separately.
    • The 48-mile figure is the destination distance from home, not the distance already traveled at any given moment.
    • Ships/water travel: not used as the reference measure due to inconsistent speeds (wind direction, current); the standard is average land travel (on foot or camel caravan).

    Scholarly Opinions

    Number of Sajda Tilawat Positions

    Opinion Scholars Count Detail
    Hanafi (Ibn Mubarak also) Imam Quduri, Ahnaf 14 The 2nd sajda in Surah al-Hajj (v.77) is only for ta'lim (instructional), not an actual hukum of sajda
    15 positions Ibn Mubarak, Laith, Ibn Shurayh, Imam Ahmad, Ishaq b. Rahawayh, Imam Malik, Ahl al-Madinah 15 Both verses of Surah al-Hajj (v.18 and v.77) require sajda

    Some Fuqaha's Distinction (within the 14)

    • Positions 1–6 and position 10 (Surah Alif Lam Mim Tanzeel/al-Sajda, v.15): fard to perform.
    • Positions 7 (al-Hajj, 1st), 9 (al-Naml) and 11 (Sad): wajib.
    • Positions 8, 12, 13, 14: mustahabb (recommended).
    • Hanafi position: all 14 are wajib without distinction.

    Qasr: Wajib vs. Permissible

    Position Scholar(s) Ruling
    Qasr is wajib Ahnaf A traveler must shorten; praying four is an error with deficiencies
    Qasr is permissible (not wajib) Imam Shafi'i A relaxation; the traveler may choose full or shortened prayer

    Lessons & Practical Application

    1. Sajda Tilawat during Quran class or recitation circles: If an ayah of sajda is recited aloud, everyone who hears it — even without intending to listen — owes a sajda. This applies in halaqas, on recordings, and during salah recitation by the imam.

    2. Praying behind an imam who reads a sajda verse: Follow the imam into sajda. If you are a muktadi who hears it from someone outside the salah, wait until your salah is complete, then perform the sajda.

    3. Sajda Tilawat procedure is simple: One takbir (without raising hands), one sajda, one takbir to rise. No tashahhud, no salam. Can be done in under ten seconds.

    4. Making up lifetime missed sajdas: Make a reasonable estimate based on how many times you have read or heard the Quran. Group them under a general intention and perform them. You do not need to pinpoint each individual instance.

    5. Traveling in the U.S. (48-mile rule): If your destination is 48+ miles from your home city, you are a musafir. Qasr starts the moment you cross your city's boundary — which may be only a few miles from home. For example, a Bay Area resident leaving Fremont is a musafir after crossing Fremont's boundary, even though 48 miles is the destination threshold.

    6. Daily commuters to a university or workplace: Scholars have confirmed that if the daily commute is 48+ miles round-trip and you return home the same day (no intention to stay), you pray qasr at that destination.

    7. Traveling to one's home country (e.g., visiting family in India/Pakistan): If your current watan (domicile) is in the U.S. and you travel back to your country of origin for a visit:

    8. Staying 30 days → not musafir; pray full salah.
    9. Staying 10 days → still musafir; pray qasr.

    10. The key is your intention of stay, not the location.

    11. "I'll decide later" travelers: If you travel without fixing a return date and never commit to staying 15+ days anywhere, you remain a musafir the entire time — even for months or a year — and continue to pray qasr throughout.

    12. The musafir imam: If asked to lead prayer while traveling, pray your two rakat and make salam, then inform the congregation to complete their salah. This is not only permissible but is the sunna method — the Prophet ﷺ and companions practiced it.

    13. Caravan speed, not maximum speed: The legal distance of three days was calculated at average caravan pace (with luggage, people, and animals), not the maximum speed of a camel or vehicle. This is why the jurists settled on 48 miles rather than a higher figure.
