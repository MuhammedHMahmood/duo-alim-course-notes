    Fiqh 201 - Study Notes

    Date: 2026-09-09

    Session Overview

    This session covered two major topics: (1) the completion of the 11 rulings (masā'il) that apply during the state of ḥayḍ (menstruation), and (2) an introduction to istiḥāḍah (irregular/pathological bleeding), including its definition, ḥukm, the four opinions within the Ḥanafī school regarding purity periods between two bleeding episodes, and the three categories of women with respect to their menstrual habits.

    Key Themes

    1. Completion of the 11 Masā'il of Ḥayḍ — reviewing each ruling that applies during the menstrual state and the rationale behind them
    2. The Concept of Ḥaraj — hardship as a basis for concession in Sharīʿah, and why concessions differ between ṣalāh and fasting
    3. Istiḥāḍah — definition, conditions, ḥukm, and how it differs from ḥayḍ
    4. Intra-Ḥanafī Differences — four distinct opinions among Ḥanafī imāms on how to treat purity periods between bleeding episodes
    5. Categories of Women — mubtadaʾah, muʿtādah, and mustaribah, and how each determines her ʿādat (habit)
    6. The Maʿdhūr — the person with a chronic condition (bleeding, urinary incontinence) and how they maintain ṭahārah

    Important Vocabulary

    Arabic Term Meaning
    ḥayḍ (حيض) Menstruation; the natural monthly cycle
    istiḥāḍah (استحاضة) Irregular/pathological bleeding (less than 3 days or more than 10 days); not classified as ḥayḍ
    ṭuhūr / ṭahārah (طهور / طهارة) State of purity/cleanliness
    ṭuhr (طهر) The clean period between two menstrual cycles
    nifās (نفاس) Post-natal bleeding (to be covered next session)
    ḥāʾiḍah (حائضة) A woman in a state of menstruation
    junubī (جنبي) A person in a state of major ritual impurity (janābah)
    ʿādat / ʿādah (عادة) A woman's established menstrual habit/pattern
    maʿdhūr (معذور) A person excused due to a chronic condition (e.g., continuous bleeding, urinary incontinence)
    ḥaraj (حرج) Hardship; a recognized basis for concession in Sharīʿah
    iddah (عدة) The waiting period a woman observes after divorce or the death of her husband
    bālighah (بالغة) A girl who has reached puberty
    wuḍūʾ (وضوء) Minor ritual ablution
    ghusl (غسل) Full ritual bath (major purification)
    qadāʾ (قضاء) Making up missed obligations
    ṭalāq sunnī (طلاق سني) Divorce given in the Sunnah manner (one ṭalāq in each period of purity)
    ṭalāq bidʿī (طلاق بدعي) Divorce given in an innovated/impermissible manner (e.g., during ḥayḍ or all at once)
    mubtadaʾah (مبتدأة) A woman experiencing her very first menstrual cycle
    muʿtādah (معتادة) A woman with an established, regular menstrual habit

    masalla (مصلى) A designated prayer space that does not carry the full ḥukm of a masjid

    rukn (ركن) An essential pillar of an act of worship whose omission invalidates the act
    Kitāb al-Ḥudūrī The primary Ḥanafī fiqh text being studied in this course

    Fiqh Rulings & Evidence

    Masāʾil of Ḥayḍ (Rulings 1–11)

    Ruling 1 — Prevention from all types of ṣalāh A ḥāʾiḍah is prevented from every form of ṣalāh, including Ṣalāt al-Janāzah and Sajdat al-Tilāwah, even though Ṣalāt al-Janāzah itself contains no sajdah.

    • Rationale for no qaḍāʾ on ṣalāh: Ṣalāh is a daily obligation. If a woman's habit is 10 days, she misses 50 ṣalawāt per month (~600/year). This constitutes a recognized ḥaraj, so Sharīʿah grants the concession of no qaḍāʾ. Analogies to the sick (praying seated/lying) and traveler (shortening prayers) illustrate how Sharīʿah removes ḥaraj.

    Ruling 2 — Prevention from fasting, but qaḍāʾ is required Unlike ṣalāh, missed fasts must be made up.

    • Evidence: A ḥadīth in Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim — Muʿādh (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu) asked ʿĀʾishah (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhā) about the qaḍāʾ of fasting. She replied: "We were ordered to make qaḍāʾ of fasts but not of ṣalāh."
    • Rationale for no concession on fasting: Missed fasting days (e.g., 10 days) can be spread across the remaining 11 months of the year — even one makeup day a month closes the gap. The cumulative burden does not reach the same level of ḥaraj as 600 ṣalawāt.

    Ruling 3 — Prohibition from entering the masjid A ḥāʾiḍah (and a person in janābah) may not enter the masjid.

    • Evidence: Ḥadīth in Abū Dāwūd and Ibn Mājah: "The masjid is not permitted for a junubī or a ḥāʾiḍah woman."
    • Nuance — masjid vs. muṣallā:
    • Masjid: Land permanently dedicated as waqf for the purpose of a masjid; this ḥukm cannot be reversed. A ḥāʾiḍah may not enter the designated prayer hall.
    • Muṣallā: A rented or temporary space used for prayer (e.g., a garage, a unit in a strip mall). It is not waqf, can serve multiple purposes, and a ḥāʾiḍah may enter it.
    • If a masjid has multiple rooms, a ḥāʾiḍah may enter the premises (courtyard, outer rooms) but not the main prayer hall where the imām leads ṣalāh.
    • The criterion is intention and designation, not the presence of a minaret or dome. (Example: the Tāj Maḥal has both a dome and minarets but is not a masjid; a small adjacent masjid without decoration still carries the full ḥukm.)
    • Minarets/domes: These are symbols with historical, not juridical, significance. The Prophet's masjid initially had no minaret or dome. The first lamp in the masjid was lit by Tamīm al-Dārī (raḥimahullāh), approximately 12 years after the Prophet's era.

    Ruling 4 — Prohibition from ṭawāf A ḥāʾiḍah cannot perform ṭawāf in any form — farḍ (Ḥajj/ʿUmrah) or nāfil — because ṭawāf takes place inside Masjid al-Ḥarām.

    • Practical Ḥajj scenario: Ṭawāf al-Qudūm (arrival ṭawāf) is not a rukn; missing it does not invalidate Ḥajj. Ṭawāf al-Ziyārah is a rukn and must be performed. If a woman cannot perform it before departing, she may give dam for certain obligations, but she would ultimately need to return to complete the ṭawāf al-ziyārah. The Wuqūf at ʿArafāt remains accessible during ḥayḍ (it is not inside the masjid); presence there, even briefly, fulfills that rukn. Full detail deferred to Kitāb al-Ḥajj (expected completion: December).

    Ruling 5 — Prohibition of marital relations The husband may not have conjugal relations with his wife during ḥayḍ.

    • This does not impose social/domestic restrictions — she may cook, eat together with family, touch utensils, etc. Normal domestic life continues; only this one act is prohibited.
    • Important ʿaqīdah point (from Fatḥ al-Qadīr): If a husband or wife considers this prohibition to be ḥalāl — i.e., denies that it is ḥarām — this constitutes kufr, because it is an explicit Qurʾānic ruling. If they know it is ḥarām but commit it anyway, they must make tawbah and istighfār, and it is mustaḥab to give ṣadaqah; this does not constitute kufr.

    Ruling 5 (continued) — Prohibition of reciting or touching the Qurʾān Both the ḥāʾiḍah and the junubī are prohibited from reciting or physically touching the Qurʾān.

    • Question raised: Is there an exception in the Ḥanafī madhhab for a female student memorizing the Qurʾān? The teacher indicated this ḥukm appears muṭlaq (unconditional) and he is unaware of any textual exception, but committed to researching and following up.

    Rulings 6–9 — When ḥayḍ stops before completing her habit If bleeding stops before her habitual number of days: - Husband and wife may resume relations only after either: 1. She performs ghusl, or 2. A full ṣalāh time passes (e.g., she becomes clean in the morning → they must wait until the Ẓuhr time passes before relations are permissible without ghusl) - If bleeding stops on the last day of her habit (e.g., day 9 of a 9-day habit), neither the waiting nor the ghusl restriction applies — relations are immediately permissible after she is clean.

    Ruling 8 — Ḥayḍ as a meter for ʿIddah The ʿiddah after ṭalāq or death of husband is measured in terms of three complete periods of ṭuhr passing. Nothing other than ḥayḍ can be used as the measure.

    Ruling 9 — Confirmation that the womb is free The passage of menstrual cycles confirms the absence of a prior husband's child in the womb, a prerequisite for permissibility of remarriage.

    Ruling 10 — Establishment of bulūgh (puberty) The onset of menstruation marks a girl's entry into puberty (bulūgh). This triggers major Sharīʿah consequences, including the right of khiyār al-bulūgh — if a minor girl was married off by a guardian, she gains the right upon reaching puberty to accept or reject the marriage. (Details deferred to Kitāb al-Nikāḥ, next semester.)

    Ruling 11 — Differentiating ṭalāq sunnī from ṭalāq bidʿī The menstrual cycle defines the framework within which ṭalāq must be given: - Ṭalāq Sunnī (Ḥasan): One ṭalāq is pronounced in each period of ṭuhr (purity). The husband does not give multiple ṭalāqs simultaneously or during ḥayḍ. - Ṭalāq Bidʿī: Giving three ṭalāqs at once, or pronouncing ṭalāq while the wife is in ḥayḍ — both are impermissible. Sharīʿah has given very specific guidelines to ensure that even if separation occurs, it does not permanently rupture family ties (especially important when children are involved). - Note: The Prophet (ṣallallāhu ʿalayhi wa sallam) considered but did not ultimately give ṭalāq to one of his wives (the daughter of ʿUmar raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu).

    Conditions & Categories

    Definition of Istiḥāḍah

    Any vaginal bleeding that is: - Less than 3 days, or - More than 10 days

    Such blood is classified as istiḥāḍah and carries the same ḥukm as epistaxis (nosebleed — ḥukm al-ruʿāf).

    Key ḥukm: Istiḥāḍah does not prevent: - Ṣalāh - Fasting - Conjugal relations (waṭʾ)

    The same ḥukm applies to other chronic conditions: continuous nosebleeds, urinary incontinence, or a wound that bleeds constantly.

    When Bleeding Exceeds 10 Days

    Scenario Ruling
    Woman has an established habit (e.g., 10 days) and bleeds 12 days First 10 days = ḥayḍ; days 11–12 = istiḥāḍah
    A girl's first-ever period lasts 12 days First 10 days = ḥayḍ; days 11–12 = istiḥāḍah
    Subsequent months also exceed 10 days Same rule: 10 days = ḥayḍ, remainder = istiḥāḍah

    During istiḥāḍah days: she performs ghusl on day 10, then makes fresh wuḍūʾ for every farḍ ṣalāh and prays normally.

    Minimum Period of Ṭuhr (Purity Between Cycles)

    • Minimum: 15 days
    • Maximum: No limit (a woman may go months or years without a cycle; those days are all considered ṭuhr)
    • Spotting within the 15-day minimum ṭuhr period is classified as istiḥāḍah, not a new ḥayḍ.

    Four Ḥanafī Opinions on Purity Between Two Bleeding Episodes

    (When a clean period (ṭuhr) occurs between two bouts of bleeding within a 10-day span, how is it classified?)

    Opinion Held By Position
    1st Imām Abū Ḥanīfah & Imām Abū Yūsuf If an incomplete purity is surrounded by blood on both sides within a total 10-day span (even if the gap is only 1 day), the entire span — including the clean days — is counted as ḥayḍ
    2nd Imām Abū Ḥanīfah & Imām Muḥammad If blood persists for 10 days or less, all 10 days count as ḥayḍ regardless of gaps, as long as this falls within her habitual period
    3rd Imām Abū Ḥanīfah & ʿAbdullāh ibn Mubārak Bleeding must occur for 3 consecutive days at the start and 3 consecutive days at the end; any gap in between is subsumed into ḥayḍ
    4th Imām Abū Ḥanīfah & Ḥasan ibn Ziyād If the clean period in between reaches 3 days or more, it breaks the ḥayḍ — only the initial bleeding counts as ḥayḍ; the subsequent bleeding (after the 3-day gap) is istiḥāḍah

    Three Categories of Women (Regarding Menstrual Habit)

    1. Mubtadaʾah (Beginner) - Experiencing her very first menstrual cycle - Establishes her ʿādah based on this first cycle - If her first cycle exceeds 10 days: only the first 10 days = ḥayḍ; the rest = istiḥāḍah

    2. Muʿtādah (Woman with Established Habit) - Has menstruated for years with a consistent pattern - If her bleeding stays within her habit and within the 3–10 day range, she follows her habit - If bleeding stays within 10 days but changes (e.g., 8 → 9 → 10 days): each new number becomes her updated ʿādah - If bleeding exceeds 10 days: only the first [habit-number] of days = ḥayḍ; the excess = istiḥāḍah - If her strictly regular habit is 7 days and she bleeds 11 days: 7 days = ḥayḍ, 4 days = istiḥāḍah - If she bleeds 9 days (under 10-day limit): all 9 days = ḥayḍ; 9 days becomes her new ʿādah

    3. Mustaribah (Woman with Fluctuating Habit) - Habit changes frequently with no consistent pattern - Should track her cycles carefully, look for seasonal patterns, and consider family history - Best course: consult a qualified scholar (muftī/ʿālim) in purdah to explain her specific situation and receive a fatwa tailored to her condition

    The Maʿdhūr — Ṭahārah Rules for Chronic Conditions

    A maʿdhūr is someone with a condition causing continuous discharge (istiḥāḍah, nosebleed, urinary incontinence, open wound):

    Scholar Position on Duration of Wuḍūʾ
    Imām al-Shāfiʿī Wuḍūʾ made for a farḍ is valid only for that farḍ; nawāfil after it require a new wuḍūʾ. (Dalīl: Ḥadīth in Abū Dāwūd)
    Imām Muḥammad (Ḥanafī) Wuḍūʾ remains valid until the time of that ṣalāh exits (e.g., wuḍūʾ made for Ẓuhr is valid until Ẓuhr time ends)
    Imām Abū Ḥanīfah (Ḥanafī) Wuḍūʾ remains valid until the next ṣalāh time begins (e.g., wuḍūʾ for Ẓuhr lasts until the Aṣr adhān)
    Imām Abū Yūsuf (Ḥanafī) Same as Imām Abū Ḥanīfah — wuḍūʾ valid until the next ṣalāh time begins

    Practical implication (Ḥanafī — Abū Ḥanīfah / Abū Yūsuf position): A maʿdhūr who makes wuḍūʾ for Fajr may pray Fajr and then pray Ishrāq, Ḍuḥā, read Qurʾān, etc. — all with the same wuḍūʾ — until the Ẓuhr time begins. Once Ẓuhr begins, the wuḍūʾ expires and she/he must make a new one.

    Scholarly Opinions

    • Minimum ḥayḍ duration — Aḥnāf: 3 days minimum, 10 days maximum
    • Minimum ḥayḍ duration — Imām al-Shāfiʿī / Imām Mālik and others: Differ significantly; some allow as little as 1 day; some extend maximum to 15 days — each madhab has its own detailed framework
    • Ṭawāf during ḥayḍ — all schools: Unanimous that it is not permissible
    • Touching/reciting Qurʾān during ḥayḍ — Ḥanafī: Prohibited (muṭlaq); no confirmed textual exception for memorization students (teacher to research and follow up)
    • Masjid entry — Ḥanafī: Prohibited in the masjid proper; courtyard and outer premises are permissible
    • Ṭalāq — Ḥanafī: Must follow the sunnī method; ṭalāq given during ḥayḍ is bidʿī and sinful
    • Considering ḥarām marital relations during ḥayḍ to be ḥalāl — Fatḥ al-Qadīr: Constitutes kufr

    Lessons & Practical Application

    1. Sharīʿah accounts for hardship (ḥaraj) with precision. The exemption from qaḍāʾ of ṣalāh — but not of fasting — is not arbitrary. The cumulative burden of 600+ missed ṣalawāt per year constitutes a recognized ḥaraj; 10 missed fasting days spread across 11 months do not reach the same threshold. Understanding the ʿillah (reason) sharpens one's grasp of the entire legal framework.

    2. The masjid vs. muṣallā distinction is practically important. Many communities in the West operate in rented spaces. A rented space, even one used exclusively for prayer, is a muṣallā and does not carry the ḥukm of a masjid with respect to ḥāʾiḍah entry, waqf obligations, etc.

    3. A woman should track her cycle carefully. Knowing one's ʿādah with precision — and recording when it changes — is not merely a health practice; it is a religious necessity that determines her ṣalāh, fasting, conjugal relations, and even validity of ṭalāq. Women with irregular cycles (mustaribah) should seek individualized guidance from a scholar.

    4. Denying an explicit Qurʾānic ḥukm is a matter of ʿaqīdah, not just fiqh. The lesson on marital relations during ḥayḍ illustrates that the line between sinful conduct (committed while knowing something is ḥarām) and kufr (denying the prohibition altogether) is legally and theologically significant.

    5. For the maʿdhūr: worship continues, ṭahārah is managed. A person with chronic bleeding or incontinence is not excused from ṣalāh, fasting, or Qurʾān recitation — they simply manage ṭahārah by making fresh wuḍūʾ for each ṣalāh time (per Ḥanafī ruling). This is an important point for those dealing with post-surgical conditions, diabetes-related wounds, etc.

    6. Ḥajj for a woman who gets her ḥayḍ during the trip: She should not panic. Ṭawāf al-Qudūm can be skipped without consequence. The Wuqūf at ʿArafāt (the greatest rukn) is accessible regardless of state. Ṭawāf al-Ziyārah is the critical point — if she absolutely cannot perform it, dam may cover secondary obligations, but this rukn itself must ultimately be fulfilled. Full detail in Kitāb al-Ḥajj (inshāAllāh, by December).

    7. Preserve your knowledge. The teacher emphasized that Kitāb al-Ḥudūrī contains approximately 10,000–12,000 masāʾil. Students should write brief notes after each class, read the relevant section before the next class, and engage with these rulings in daily life to make them permanent.
