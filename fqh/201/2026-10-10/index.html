    Fiqh 201 - Study Notes

    Date: 2026-10-10

    Session Overview

    This session continued the discussion on the rules of congregational prayer (Jama'at), focusing on: the order of preference for selecting an Imam, categories of people who should be avoided as Imam, the proper arrangement of rows (Sufuf), the conditions under which a woman standing in a men's row invalidates the prayer, rulings on women attending congregational prayers, and permissible/impermissible forms of Iqtida (following an Imam). The session also covered the key Fiqh principle that the Imam is answerable (Damin) for the validity of the congregation's prayer.

    Key Themes

    1. Criteria for Imam selection — knowledge, recitation, Wara', Taqwa, age, manners, lineage, appearance
    2. Categories to avoid as Imam — slaves, the illiterate (Arabi/Bedouin), the Fasiq, the blind, and those born of Zina — all Makruh Tanzihi, not prohibitively so
    3. Proper Saf formation — positioning of Muqtadi(s) relative to Imam; order: men → children → Khunsa → women
    4. Fasid of men's prayer when a woman stands in the same row — five conditions required
    5. Women attending Jama'at — generally Makruh; exceptions for older women per Imam Abu Hanifa
    6. The Imam as Damin — the Imam's prayer validity carries the congregation; if the Imam's prayer is invalid, the congregation must repeat
    7. Permissible and impermissible forms of Iqtida — Tayammum behind Wudu, sitting Imam behind standing congregation, Fard behind Nafil (impermissible), Nafil behind Fard (permissible)

    Important Vocabulary

    Arabic Term Meaning
    Wara' (ورع) Avoidance of doubtful matters (Shubuhat), not just Haram
    Taqwa (تقوى) Avoidance of Haram and Makruh Tahrimi
    Fasiq (فاسق) An open sinner; one who commits major sins openly
    Ummi (أمي) One who is illiterate in Quranic recitation; cannot recite properly
    Arabi / Bedouin (أعرابي) One from the desert/village; used here to mean illiterate or rough in manner
    Muqtadi (مقتدي) The follower in congregational prayer
    Imamat (إمامة) Leading the congregational prayer
    Saf / Sufuf (صف / صفوف) Row(s) in congregational prayer
    Khunsa (خنثى) A person who is neither clearly male nor female
    Istihada (استحاضة) Irregular/continuous non-menstrual bleeding in women
    Mu'mi (مومئ) One who prays with gestures/indications (Ishara) due to inability to perform Ruku/Sujud
    Ishara (إشارة) A gesture or indication (used in place of physical Ruku/Sujud)
    Iqtida (اقتداء) Following the Imam; making one's prayer contingent on the Imam's
    Zamin (ضامن) Guarantor; answerable/responsible — the Imam is Zamin for the congregation's prayer
    Fasid (فاسد) Invalid/nullified
    Makruh Tanzih (مكروه تنزيه) Disliked but not sinful; prayer remains valid if done
    Makruh Tahrimi (مكروه تحريمي) Disliked at a level close to Haram
    Mansukh (منسوخ) Abrogated; a ruling that was later cancelled
    Masa 'alal Khuffayn (مسح على الخفين) Wiping over leather socks instead of washing the feet in Wudu
    Tayammum (تيمم) Dry ablution with clean earth/dust when water cannot be used
    Hasab wa Nasab (حسب ونسب) Family status and lineage
    Tartib (ترتيب) Order/arrangement
    Mani'at (موانع) Prohibitions; things that prevent validity
    Tiwali Bufassal (طوال المفصل) The longer Surahs of the Mufassal section of the Quran
    Masnoon (مسنون) Established by the Sunnah of the Prophet ﷺ

    Fiqh Rulings & Evidence

    1. Wara' vs. Taqwa — Distinction

    • Taqwa: Avoiding Haram and Makruh Tahrimi
    • Wara': Going further — avoiding anything that carries even a shade of doubt (Shubha)
    • Hadith: The parable of the king's sanctuary (from Arba'een of Imam Nawawi) — doubtful matters are like the restricted zone around a king's palace; those who linger near it risk crossing into the prohibited

    2. Criteria for Imam Selection (in order of priority)

    1. Most knowledgeable in Deen (Masail of Fiqh)
    2. Best in Quranic recitation
    3. Highest in Wara'
    4. Older in age — Dalil: Hadith of Malik ibn Huwayrith رضي الله عنه (Rasulullah ﷺ told two companions: "whichever of you is older should lead")
    5. Better in manners
    6. Better in Hasab and Nasab (family lineage and status from a Dini perspective)
    7. Better in appearance (though beauty is subjective)

    3. Categories to Avoid as Imam (Makruh Tanzih)

    Category Reason
    Slave Limited time to study with scholars; knowledge likely incomplete
    Illiterate (Arabi/Bedouin) Generally ignorant of Deen; often harsh in manner; congregation may dislike them
    Fasiq Open sinner — morally unfit to lead
    Blind person Risk of stepping in Najasa (cannot see impurity on ground or clothes); prayer may be invalid due to Najasa
    Walad al-Zina (born of Zina) People may generally dislike him; social discomfort (no personal fault)

    Important clarification: If any of these individuals leads the prayer, the Salah of the entire congregation is valid. This is only Makruh Tanzih — avoidance is preferred, not obligatory.

    Exception noted: Many great Mufassirun and Fuqaha historically came from a background of slavery — when masters provided time and resources for Islamic learning, slaves became highly knowledgeable. The ruling is based on the general case, not an absolute.

    Dalil for blind Imam: Abdullah ibn Maktum رضي الله عنه — Rasulullah ﷺ appointed him as his deputy to lead Salah in Madinah during expeditions (Ghazawat).

    4. Imam Should Not Lead Excessively Long Prayers

    • Dalil: Hadith in Bukhari narrated by Abu Mas'ud رضي الله عنه — a man told Rasulullah ﷺ he avoided Fajr prayer because the Imam prolonged it; Rasulullah ﷺ became visibly angry and said: "Some of you make people dislike good deeds... whoever leads people in prayer should shorten it, for among them are the weak, the old, and the needy."
    • Imam should read the minimum Masnoon amount at minimum; adjusting upward is acceptable only if the congregation is able and willing
    • If the congregation loves long recitation and is capable, there is no harm in a longer prayer

    5. Women Leading Their Own Jama'at

    • Ruling: Makruh for women to form their own Jama'at
    • If they do: the female Imam stands in the middle of the first row, not in front of the Saf — unlike a male Imam who stands ahead
    • Analogy: Same as a group of naked men — the Imam stands in the middle of the row, not forward
    • Dalil: Hadith in Daraqutni

    6. Women's Jama'at for Janazah (when no men are present)

    • Valid, because Salat al-Janazah must be performed and no men are available
    • Female Imam again stands in the middle of the row
    • Alternative: Each woman present performs Salat al-Janazah individually — also acceptable

    7. Proper Saf Positioning (one or two Muqtadis)

    • One Muqtadi: Stands to the right side of the Imam, such that the heel of the Imam aligns with the toes of the Muqtadi
    • Two Muqtadis: Imam moves forward; both stand behind him
    • Imam Abu Yusuf رحمه الله: permits the Imam to stand in the middle with one Muqtadi on each side (acceptable for space constraints)
    • Dalil: Hadith in Bukhari — Ibn Abbas رضي الله عنه prayed with Rasulullah ﷺ; the Prophet kept him to his right side

    8. Saf Order for Mixed Congregations

    Order (front to back): 1. Men 2. Children 3. Khunsa 4. Women

    • Dalil: Hadith in Bukhari narrated by Anas ibn Malik رضي الله عنه — Rasulullah ﷺ prayed; Anas and an orphan were behind him; Umm Sulaym stood behind them

    9. Best and Worst Rows

    • For men: Best row = first row (closest to Imam); worst = last row
    • For women: Best row = last row (maximum distance from men); worst = first row
    • Dalil: Hadith in Muslim — Rasulullah ﷺ stated this explicitly
    • Encouragement: men should fill the front rows and not leave gaps

    10. Woman Standing in a Man's Row — Five Conditions for Prayer to Become Fasid

    If all five conditions are met, the Salah of the man (or all men in that row) becomes Fasid:

    1. They are both following the same Imam in the same prayer
    2. There is no barrier (Hajiz) between the woman and the men's row — even a pillar counts as a barrier
    3. The prayer involves Ruku and Sujud (Salat al-Janazah is excluded as it has neither)
    4. The woman is Ahli Shahwa — of an age that naturally attracts attention; very old women and young children are excluded
    5. The Imam made intention to lead both men and women together

    Effect: The Salah of the man/men becomes Fasid. The Salah of the woman remains valid.

    Imam Shafi's opinion: The prayer of both sides becomes only Makruh — neither becomes Fasid.

    11. Women Attending Congregational Prayers at the Masjid

    • General ruling: Makruh for women to attend Jama'at at the Masjid (on their own, without a Mahram)
    • Imam Abu Hanifa: Older women may attend Fajr, Maghrib, and Isha — the three night/dark prayers, when they are less visible
    • Imam Abu Yusuf and Muhammad: Older women may attend all five prayers
    • Young women: Makruh for all prayers

    Evidence: - Hadith in Abu Dawud (Ibn Mas'ud رضي الله عنه): "It is more excellent for a woman to pray in her house than in her courtyard, and more excellent to pray in her private chamber than in her house" - Hadith in Muslim (Amra bint Abd al-Rahman, from Aisha رضي الله عنها): "If the Messenger of Allah ﷺ had seen what new things women have introduced, he would have definitely prevented them from going to the Masjid as the women of Bani Isra'il were prevented"

    12. The Imam is Answerable (Zamin) — Core Principle

    • Hadith in Tirmidhi: "The Imam is Zamin (answerable/guarantor), and the Mu'adhdhin is trusted. O Allah, guide the Imams and pardon the Mu'adhdhins."
    • Ahnaf's Usul: If the Imam's prayer is valid → the congregation's prayer is valid even if a Muqtadi made a minor mistake. If the Imam's prayer is invalid → the congregation must repeat.
    • Imam Shafi's Usul: Each person (Imam and Muqtadi) is Zamin for their own prayer independently; the Imam's invalidity does not affect the Muqtadi.

    Usul for physical condition: The Imam must be in equal or better physical condition than the Muqtadis to lead. If the gap is minor, Iqtida is valid; if major, it is not (per Ahnaf).

    13. Who Cannot Lead (Prohibitions on Iqtida)

    Situation Ruling
    Imam prays with Ishara (cannot do Ruku/Sujud); Muqtadi can do both Not permissible — major physical gap
    Imam praying Nafil; Muqtadi praying Fard Not permissible — Fard has higher status
    Imam praying one Fard (e.g. Asr); Muqtadi intending a different Fard (e.g. Zuhr) Not permissible — different intentions
    Person with urinary incontinence (Ma'dhur) leading those without Not permissible
    Woman with Istihada leading another woman Not permissible
    Qari (memorizer of Quran) praying behind Ummi (illiterate in recitation) Not permissible; makes prayer Fasid
    Fully clothed person behind a naked Imam whose Satr is uncovered Not permissible

    14. What Is Permissible in Iqtida

    Situation Ruling
    Person with Tayammum leading those with Wudu Permissible — Dalil: Hadith of Amr ibn al-As رضي الله عنه in Abu Dawud (led Fajr with Tayammum in battle of Dhat al-Salasil on an extremely cold night; Rasulullah ﷺ did not disapprove)
    Imam with Masa 'alal Khuffayn leading those who washed feet Permissible — Rasulullah ﷺ did this with Sahaba
    Standing Muqtadis behind a sitting Imam (valid excuse) Permissible — Dalil: Hadith in Bukhari (Anas ibn Malik); Rasulullah ﷺ led prayer sitting after falling from a horse; Sahaba stood behind him; later in his illness the Sahaba stood while he sat and he did not stop them → later practice taken as Dalil
    Nafil Muqtadi behind Fard Imam Permissible — Fard is a higher station; lower intention joining higher is acceptable
    Person who already prayed Fard joins Jama'at Permissible — second prayer counts as Nafil; Dalil: Hadith in Tirmidhi (Jabir's father; Rasulullah ﷺ told those who had prayed at their camps to join the Jama'at and it would be Nafil for them)

    15. Discovery That Imam Had No Wudu

    • If discovered after Salah: entire congregation must repeat the prayer
    • If known before Salah: making Iqtida of that Imam is not permissible
    • Ahnaf: Imam is Zamin → his invalid Wudu invalidates the congregation's prayer
    • Imam Shafi: Each person is independently Zamin → Muqtadi's Salah remains valid even if Imam had no Wudu

    16. Group of Illiterate Worshippers (No Qari Present)

    • If none of them can recite properly, none should lead the others
    • If an Ummi leads other Ummis, the entire congregation's Salah becomes Fasid

    Conditions & Categories

    Five Conditions for Woman's Presence in Men's Row to Invalidate Prayer

    (See ruling #10 above — repeated here for reference) 1. Same Imam, same prayer 2. No physical barrier between the woman and the men's row 3. Prayer has Ruku and Sujud 4. Woman is Ahli Shahwa (of mature age) 5. Imam intended to lead both men and women

    Categories Makruh as Imam (Makruh Tanzih)

    1. Slave
    2. Illiterate (Arabi/Bedouin)
    3. Fasiq
    4. Blind person
    5. Walad al-Zina (child born out of wedlock)

    Physical Conditions That Prevent Leading Prayer (Ahnaf)

    1. Cannot hold urine (Ma'dhur)
    2. Constant uncontrollable bleeding (wounds, nosebleeds — like Istihada)
    3. Satr not covered / praying naked
    4. Ummi (illiterate in recitation) leading a Qari

    Lessons & Practical Application

    1. Pursue Wara', not just legal permissibility — the Prophet ﷺ taught that habitually engaging with doubtful matters erodes the boundary to Haram. Practically: when unsure about the permissibility of food, a transaction, or an action, lean toward avoidance.

    2. Choose your Imam carefully — when appointing or selecting an Imam in a gathering or community, follow the established order: knowledge, recitation, piety, age. Do not let social pressure or convenience override the Sunnah criteria.

    3. Do not fight the appointed Imam — these selection criteria apply when there is no designated Imam. In a Masjid with an appointed Imam, do not contest his position.

    4. Fill the front rows — Rasulullah ﷺ explicitly said the best row for men is the first. When entering the Masjid, fill gaps in the front rather than praying at the back.

    5. Imams: shorten the prayer for your congregation — Rasulullah ﷺ became visibly upset at an Imam who prolonged the prayer and caused people to miss Fajr. Know your congregation: the old, sick, and busy are behind you.

    6. The Imam-Damin principle has practical consequences — if you discover after Salah that your Imam had no Wudu, the Ahnaf position requires you to repeat the prayer. Be aware of your Imam's state when possible.

    7. A person who already prayed Fard should still join the Jama'at — if you prayed at home and arrive at the Masjid to find the congregation in prayer, join with the intention of the same Salah. The reward will be that of Nafil, but the Jama'at is not to be abandoned.

    8. Women praying at home is spiritually superior — this is the explicit teaching of Rasulullah ﷺ and was emphasized even more strongly by Aisha رضي الله عنها. For women, the private chamber at home is the most rewarding place of prayer.

    9. The Damin principle cuts both ways (Ahnaf) — if the Imam's Salah is valid, minor errors by individual Muqtadis are covered. This is a mercy, but it also means that choosing a capable, ritually pure Imam is a collective responsibility of the congregation.
