    Fiqh 201 - Study Notes

    Date: 2026-08-08

    Session Overview

    This session covered three main areas: (1) a review of the four sources of Islamic law and the role of the madhabs, (2) biographical introductions to Imam Abu Yusuf and Imam Muhammad al-Shaybani (the two primary students of Imam Abu Hanifa), and (3) a foundational introduction to the Ahkam al-Shar'iyyah — the legal categories of Islamic rulings — covering both the Azimat (positive/obligatory) and Mahi (prohibitory) categories. The session also included a detailed biographical introduction to Imam al-Quduri, the author of the primary text (Mukhtasar al-Quduri), and a brief orientation to the Zahir al-Riwayah (the six foundational books of Hanafi fiqh).

    Key Themes

    1. The necessity of knowing the dalil (evidence) behind every ruling — knowing a mas'ala is incomplete without knowing its source and how it was derived.
    2. The hierarchical categories of Islamic legal rulings (Ahkam) — the distinction between Fard, Wajib, Sunnah, and Nawafil on the positive side, and Haram and Makruh on the prohibitory side.
    3. The distinction between Fard and Wajib — a defining feature of Hanafi fiqh not found as explicitly in other madhabs.
    4. The caliber and contribution of the Hanafi Imams — particularly Abu Yusuf and Muhammad al-Shaybani, and how their roles shaped Hanafi fiqh.
    5. The unique shura (consultative council) methodology of Imam Abu Hanifa — every mas'ala was discussed and agreed upon by 40 senior scholars before being recorded.
    6. The importance of scholarly humility — even Imam Malik said "I don't know" to the majority of questions posed to him; students of fiqh must exercise caution before issuing verdicts.
    7. The greatness of Imam al-Quduri and his Mukhtasar — the miracle of his revision at the Ka'bah and the book's 12,000 masa'il.

    Fiqh Rulings & Evidence

    The Four Sources of Islamic Law

    All rulings in every madhab are ultimately derived from one of four sources: 1. Quran 2. Sunnah (Hadith) of Rasulullah ﷺ 3. Ijma' — scholarly consensus 4. Qiyas — analogical reasoning

    Principle: Dalil Must Match the Ruling's Level

    • For a ruling to be Fard, the dalil must be Qat'i (absolute) — from the Quran or Hadith Mutawatir.
    • For a ruling to be Wajib, the dalil may be Zanni — such as Khabar al-Wahid.
    • For a ruling to be Haram, the dalil must also be Qat'i; one cannot declare something haram based on zanni evidence alone.
    • For Makruh Tahrimi, the dalil is Zanni — hence it does not reach the level of Haram, but the practical obligation to avoid it is treated similarly.

    Example — Witr Prayer

    • There is a hadith supporting Witr, but it comes through Khabar al-Wahid (not Mutawatir).
    • Therefore, in Hanafi fiqh, Witr is Wajib, not Fard.
    • Practical implication: when making up missed prayers (Qadha 'Umri), one must also make up Witr alongside Fard prayers.

    Example — Sadaqah al-Fitr

    • Similarly established through Khabar al-Wahid → classified as Wajib.

    Example — Timing of Salah

    • Jibril led Rasulullah ﷺ in prayer over two days — early time on Day 1, late time on Day 2 — establishing that both times are valid.
    • Sahabah who settled in different regions (e.g., Abdullah ibn Mas'ud in Kufa) passed on the practice they followed, leading to regional differences in timing.
    • This is the origin of Hanafi prayers being performed slightly later — both timings are authentically established.

    Example — Chess

    • Playing chess is Makruh Tahrimi — established by zanni dalil, not categorised as Haram, but must be treated with near-Haram avoidance.

    Example — Shrimp

    • In Hanafi fiqh, the majority position is that eating shrimp is Makruh Tanzihi — it should be avoided, but one who eats it cannot be told it is Haram.

    Example — Haram with Rukhsah

    • Alcohol/wine is Haram in all circumstances. Exception: if a person is extremely ill and the only available medicine contains it, the Shari'ah permits taking the medically prescribed amount.
    • Gelatin from swine in capsules: Haram ordinarily; permitted only when it is the sole available treatment.

    Skins — Absolute Prohibitions (No Tanning Exception)

    Two skins are impermissible under any circumstances (unlike other animal skins which become permissible after tanning): 1. Skin of a pig 2. Skin of a human being (Full discussion will come in the chapter on Tanning.)

    Domain Preferred Opinion
    Ibadat (worship) Imam Abu Hanifa
    Mu'amalat / Kitab al-Buyu' (transactions) Imam Abu Yusuf (due to his 10–12 years as Qadi)
    'Ilm al-Miras (inheritance law) Imam Muhammad al-Shaybani

    Conditions & Categories

    The Full Framework of Ahkam al-Shar'iyyah

    AHKAM
├── AZIMAT (Obligatory/Positive Side)
│   ├── 1. FARD
│   │     ├── Dalil: Qat'i (Quran or Hadith Mutawatir)
│   │     ├── Fard al-'Ayn — obligatory on every individual
│   │     └── Fard al-Kifayah — collective obligation; some fulfilling it exempts others
│   ├── 2. WAJIB
│   │     ├── Dalil: Zanni (Khabar al-Wahid)
│   │     ├── Must be treated practically like Fard
│   │     └── Denying it does NOT make one a kafir (unlike denying a Fard)
│   ├── 3. SUNNAH
│   │     ├── Sunnah al-Huda (related to Ibadat)
│   │     │     ├── Sunnah Mu'akkadah (emphasised — e.g., 2 rak'at after Maghrib)
│   │     │     └── Sunnah Ghayr Mu'akkadah (non-emphasised; level of Mandub/Nafl)
│   │     └── Sunnah al-Zawaid (non-devotional habits of Rasulullah ﷺ)
│   └── 4. NAWAFIL
│         └── Any additional act above Fard and Wajib
│               (Linguistically: "addition on top of something")
│
└── MAHI (Prohibitory Side)
    ├── 1. HARAM
    │     ├── Dalil: Qat'i (Quran or Hadith Mutawatir)
    │     ├── Absolutely impermissible
    │     └── Rukhsah possible in extreme necessity (e.g., medicine)
    └── 2. MAKRUH
          ├── Makruh Tahrimi
          │     ├── Dalil: Zanni
          │     ├── Must be treated practically like Haram in avoidance
          │     └── Denying it does NOT make one a kafir
          └── Makruh Tanzihi
                ├── Mildly disliked
                └── One has the option; avoidance is preferred but not at the level of Tahrimi

    Fard vs. Wajib — The Hanafi Distinction

    Aspect Fard Wajib
    Dalil Qat'i (Quran/Mutawatir) Zanni (Khabar al-Wahid)
    Practical obligation Must be done Must be treated like Fard practically
    Denying its obligation Makes one a Kafir Does NOT make one a Kafir
    Example All five daily prayers Witr, Sadaqah al-Fitr

    Makruh Tahrimi vs. Makruh Tanzihi

    Aspect Makruh Tahrimi Makruh Tanzihi
    Dalil Zanni Zanni (weaker application)
    Avoidance level Treat like Haram in practice Avoid when possible; latitude exists
    Kafir if denied? No No
    Example Chess Eating shrimp (Hanafi position)

    Scholarly Opinions

    On the Shura Council of Imam Abu Hanifa

    Imam Abu Hanifa did not derive rulings alone. He assembled a council of 40 of the finest scholars of his time — muftis, fuqaha, muhaddithun, mufassirun, and linguists. Each mas'ala was discussed, sometimes for weeks, until consensus was reached. He would not record a ruling until all agreed — and he would even wait for the return of Qadi 'Afiyah specifically before finalising a mas'ala.

    On Imam Abu Yusuf's Authority in Mu'amalat

    After the passing of Imam Abu Hanifa, Imam Abu Yusuf served as Chief Qadi (judge) for approximately 10–12 years. His practical judicial experience led him to revise certain opinions of Imam Abu Hanifa in the domain of commercial transactions. Therefore, in Kitab al-Buyu', the mufta bihi (adopted fatwa position) often follows Imam Abu Yusuf over Imam Abu Hanifa.

    On Imam Muhammad in Inheritance Law

    The detailed coverage of inheritance ('ilm al-miras) in Hanafi fiqh follows Imam Muhammad al-Shaybani's opinions. Miras is defined directly in the Quran with no other source — making it one of the most precisely defined areas of fiqh.

    On Ikhtilaf within Hanafi Fiqh

    Unlike the ikhtilaf between the four madhabs (which is extensive), differences of opinion between the Hanafi Imams are comparatively rare — a testament to the shura methodology. Where differences do exist (particularly in Imam Abu Yusuf or Imam Muhammad differing from Imam Abu Hanifa), they are rooted in the same usul but reflect changed circumstances or additional judicial experience.

    On Later Hanafi Scholars Revising Earlier Masa'il

    Imams such as Imam al-Quduri and Sahibi Hidayah (Imam al-Marginani) — writing 3–4 generations after Imam Abu Hanifa — sometimes recorded a different ruling than what the first generation wrote, not by abandoning the usul, but by applying the same principles to changed circumstances. This occurred primarily in Kitab al-Buyu' (transactional law), not in Ibadat. Such revisions required the consensus of the Hanafi muftis and fuqaha of their time.

    On Scholarly Humility — The Example of Imam Malik

    When Imam Malik traveled to Kufa, approximately 70 masa'il were presented to him. He responded "I don't know" to the majority of them. This is cited as the model: a scholar of the highest rank openly acknowledging the limits of his knowledge rather than issuing an unfounded verdict.

    Biographical Notes — Key Imams

    Imam Abu Yusuf (Rahimullah)

    • Real name: Ya'qub bin Ibrahim
    • Born: 113 AH, Kufa/Baghdad
    • Passed away: 182 AH, Baghdad
    • Primary teacher: Imam Abu Hanifa
    • Famous students: Imam Ahmad ibn Hanbal, Imam Muhammad al-Shaybani, Yahya ibn Ma'in
    • Major works:
    • Kitab al-Athar
    • Kitab al-Kharaj (on land tax/state revenues)
    • Distinction: Served as Qadi for ~10–12 years after Imam Abu Hanifa's death; his opinions in Mu'amalat are given priority in Hanafi fatawa.

    Imam Muhammad al-Shaybani (Rahimullah)

    • Real name: Muhammad bin Hasan
    • Born: 132 AH, Shayban (hence "al-Shaybani")
    • Passed away: 189 AH, Ra'id
    • Primary teachers: Imam Abu Hanifa, Imam Abu Yusuf, Sufyan al-Thawri/al-Sari
    • Famous students: Imam al-Shafi'i, Yahya ibn Ma'in, Abu Hafiz
    • Major works (the Zahir al-Riwayah — 6 books):
    • Al-Mabsut (also known as Al-Asl)
    • Al-Ziyadat
    • Al-Jami' al-Saghir
    • Al-Jami' al-Kabir
    • Al-Siyar al-Saghir
    • Al-Siyar al-Kabir
    • Note on Siyar: The last two books (al-Siyar al-Saghir and al-Kabir) deal with international relations and constitutional principles; various national military codes and constitutional frameworks have drawn from them.
    • Also wrote: Muwatta' Imam Muhammad, Nawadir al-Riwayah
    • Distinction: Primary author of the Zahir al-Riwayah. His opinions in 'Ilm al-Miras are given priority in Hanafi fatawa. Included in Imam Abu Hanifa's council of 40 despite his young age (~18 at the time of Imam Abu Hanifa's death).

    Imam al-Quduri (Rahimullah) — Author of the Primary Text

    • Full name: Imam Abul Husain Ahmad bin Muhammad bin Ahmad al-Baghdadi al-Quduri
    • Born: 362 AH, Baghdad
    • Passed away: 428 AH, Baghdad (Sunday, 5 Rajab), age 66
    • Primary teacher in fiqh: Rukn al-Islam Abu 'Abdillah Muhammad bin Yahya bin Mahdi al-Jorjani
    • Chain to Imam Abu Hanifa: Al-Jorjani ← Abu Bakr al-Jassas ← Abu al-Hasan 'Ubaidullah Archi ← Abu Sa'id Badra'i ← 'Allama Musa Razi ← Imam Muhammad al-Shaybani ← Imam Abu Hanifa (approximately 6–7 teachers removed)
    • Teachers in Hadith: Muhammad bin 'Ali bin Su'id; 'Ubaidullah bin Muhammad Joshini
    • Famous students: Abu Bakr Ahmad bin 'Ali bin Thabit Khatib al-Baghdadi (famous hadith scholar and author); Sahib al-Tariq
    • Why "al-Quduri": Either from his village name Qudur, or from his association with the trade of large cooking vessels (qudur = large pots)
    • Miracle of Hajj: After completing the Mukhtasar, he carried the full manuscript to Makkah, raised it at the Mataf after Tawaf, made du'a for Allah to allow him to correct any errors, then reviewed all 12,000 masa'il page by page — finding only 5–6 mistakes.

    Lessons & Practical Application

    1. Know the dalil, not just the ruling. Every mas'ala should be known alongside its source: which ayah, which hadith, and which category of evidence. This is what distinguishes real understanding from surface memorisation.

    2. The Fard/Wajib distinction is uniquely Hanafi — other madhabs largely use "Wajib" to mean what Ahnaf call "Fard." When reading cross-madhab discussions, be aware of this difference in terminology.

    3. Makruh Tahrimi should be treated practically like Haram — the difference is at the level of i'tiqad (belief): denying a Makruh Tahrimi does not make one a kafir, but in practice it must be avoided with the same seriousness.

    4. Never issue a ruling without thorough investigation. Imam Malik said "I don't know" to most of ~70 masa'il put to him. Students of fiqh must be honest about the limits of their knowledge, especially while still studying. If in doubt, say: "Give me time to look into it" and consult a local 'alim.

    5. Never label something Haram without absolute certainty — saying "haram" without a thorough analysis is a gravely serious matter. Avoid either extreme: knowing and hiding, or not knowing and speaking anyway.

    6. Kitab al-Buyu' will have masa'il that differ from the earliest Hanafi books — this is expected and deliberate. Imam al-Quduri and Sahibi Hidayah applied the same usul to changed circumstances; this is not innovation but proper application of the principles.

    7. Studying method for this class: After each session, write out all masa'il from the text (Mukhtasar al-Quduri) from memory, then check against the book. Post questions to the class WhatsApp group immediately — they benefit all students.

    8. The value of this knowledge: Learning one mas'ala of deen is reported to be better than months of nafl worship. Completing Mukhtasar al-Quduri means learning approximately 12,000 masa'il — an immense treasure.

    9. Connection to one's teacher (isnad) matters. Imam al-Quduri's chain back to Imam Abu Hanifa through 6–7 scholars is a sign of the authenticity and continuity of this knowledge. The sanad in fiqh, like in hadith, is a guarantee of transmission.

    10. Next class (Wednesday): Begin Kitab al-Taharah (the book of purification) — read ahead in the Quduri text and prepare to discuss the masa'il.
