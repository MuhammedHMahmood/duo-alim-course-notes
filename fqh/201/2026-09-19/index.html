    Fiqh 201 - Study Notes

    Date: 2026-09-19

    Session Overview

    This session covered two major topics: (1) the completion of Kitab al-Tahara with an in-depth discussion of istinja (purification after relieving oneself), including its ruling, correct method, permissible materials, and the dirham threshold for when water becomes obligatory; and (2) the opening of Kitab al-Salah, beginning with the linguistic and shari'i definitions of salah and the detailed timings of the five daily prayers according to Imam Abu Hanifa and the Sahibayn (Imam Abu Yusuf and Imam Muhammad al-Shaybani), along with the timing of Witr.

    Key Themes

    • Istinja: Its ruling (sunnah per Ahnaf, wajib per Imam Shafi'i), correct method (three-stroke technique), permissible materials, and what is impermissible to use
    • The Dirham Threshold: When filth exceeds the makharaj beyond the size of a dirham, water becomes obligatory rather than optional
    • Transition to Kitab al-Salah: Linguistic vs. shari'i definitions of salah; dalil for its obligatory status
    • Prayer Times: The beginning and end times of all five daily prayers, including differences of opinion between Imam Abu Hanifa and the Sahibayn
    • Shafaq Disagreement: Whether the end of Maghrib time is determined by the red twilight (Sahibayn) or the white twilight (Imam Abu Hanifa; muftabi opinion)

    Important Vocabulary

    Arabic Term Meaning
    الاستنجاء (Istinja) Removal of najasa from the place of exit after relieving oneself; purification of the front or back passage
    النجاسة (Najasa) Ritual impurity / filth
    المخرج (Makharaj) The place of exit; the opening from which bodily waste emerges
    الحجر (Hajar) Stone (used for istinja)
    المدر (Madar) Clod; a dried lump of clay or soil (used for istinja)
    الطهارة (Tahara) Purification; cleanliness
    الدرهم (Dirham) A silver coin; used as a unit of measurement for the allowable spread of najasa
    مسنون (Masnoon) That which follows the Sunnah
    الشيخان (Shaykhayn) Imam Abu Hanifa and Imam Abu Yusuf رحمهما الله
    الصاحبان (Sahibayn) Imam Abu Yusuf and Imam Muhammad al-Shaybani رحمهما الله
    مكروه (Makruh) Disliked/reprehensible; valid but blameworthy
    الصلاة (Salah) Prayer; linguistically: supplication, mercy, praise, or recitation
    الظل (Zill) Shadow/shade; used to determine Zuhr and Asr times
    الفجر الكاذب (Fajr al-Kadhib) False dawn; a vertical streak of light appearing ~70–90 minutes before true dawn
    الفجر الصادق / الفجر الثاني (Fajr al-Sadiq / Fajr al-Thani) True dawn; the horizontal spreading of white light across the horizon — marks the beginning of Fajr time
    الزوال (Zawal) The sun's declination from its zenith; marks the beginning of Zuhr time
    الشفق (Shafaq) Twilight after sunset; its disappearance marks the end of Maghrib and beginning of Isha
    الشفق الأحمر (Shafaq al-Ahmar) Red twilight; the redness remaining on the horizon after sunset
    الشفق الأبيض (Shafaq al-Abyad) White twilight; the white light remaining after the red has faded; muftabi opinion for end of Maghrib
    الوتر (Witr) The odd-unit prayer prayed after Isha; its time begins after Isha and ends before Fajr al-Thani
    مفتى به (Muftabi) The relied-upon fatwa position (the opinion on which legal verdicts are issued)
    أفضلية (Afdaliyya) The more virtuous/preferred option

    Fiqh Rulings & Evidence

    Ruling of Istinja

    • Ahnaf (Imam Abu Hanifa): Istinja is sunnah — the Prophet ﷺ consistently performed it, and this is why Imam Quduri رحمه الله classified it as such.
    • Imam Shafi'i رحمه الله: Istinja is wajib (obligatory), based on the same consistent prophetic practice.

    Permissible Materials for Istinja

    Anything that: 1. Is pure in itself 2. Has the ability to absorb/clean 3. Is not excessively expensive

    Examples: stones, clods of clay/soil, tissue paper, cloth. Water is the most superior and preferred method.

    Dalil for using water: Hadith in Bukhari and Muslim narrated by Anas رضي الله عنه, who served the Prophet ﷺ; he reported carrying water for the Prophet ﷺ to use for istinja and wudu.

    Dalil for the praise of cleaning with water: Allah سبحانه وتعالى praised the people of Masjid Quba in the Quran for their practice of cleaning themselves thoroughly (combining stone and water).

    The Dirham Threshold (Istinja and Najasa Spread)

    • If najasa stays within the makharaj or spreads no more than the size of a dirham, using dry materials (stone, tissue) is sufficient; water is not obligatory.
    • If najasa exceeds the size of a dirham beyond the makharaj, water (or another flowing liquid capable of cleansing) becomes obligatory.
    • Shaykhayn (Abu Hanifa & Abu Yusuf): The dirham measurement excludes the makharaj itself.
    • Imam Muhammad al-Shaybani: The dirham measurement includes the makharaj, making the permissible spread area slightly smaller.
    • This applies to the body, clothing, and place of worship equally.
    • Praying with less than a dirham of najasa is valid but with karaha (dislike).

    Number of Strokes in Istinja

    • Ahnaf: No fixed number is stipulated. The goal is sufficient cleanliness. One wipe is enough if cleanliness is achieved; more may be used as needed.
    • Imam Shafi'i: A minimum of three, five, or seven strokes is required (odd numbers), based on a hadith in Sahih Muslim and Abu Dawud.
    • Dalil of Ahnaf: Hadith of Aisha رضي الله عنها in Abu Dawud — three stones give sufficient cleaning; the goal is the cleaning itself, not the number.
    • Practical note: Three is generally considered a sound and sufficient number.

    Ruling on Witr Time

    • Witr time begins after Isha is prayed.
    • Witr time ends before Fajr al-Thani (true dawn).

    Salah is Fard — Dalil

    • Quranic evidence: Surah al-Nisa, Ayah 103 — "Indeed, prayer has been decreed upon the believers a decree of specified times."
    • The consistent practice of the Prophet ﷺ and all the Sahaba serves as further definitive evidence.

    Conditions & Categories

    Correct Method of Istinja (Step-by-Step)

    1. When sitting to relieve oneself, lean/put slight pressure on the left side to ease the process.
    2. Cover one's satr (private area) even in an enclosed bathroom if there is any fear of exposure.
    3. When using dry materials for cleaning from defecation, use the three-stroke method:
    4. Stroke 1: Front to back
    5. Stroke 2: Back to front
    6. Stroke 3: Front to back
    7. Do not use the right hand to clean, unless there is a genuine necessity (injury, disability, etc.).

    What Is Impermissible to Use for Istinja

    Prohibited Material Reason
    Animal bones (عظم) No absorptive capacity; unable to achieve cleanliness
    Dried dung / animal waste (روث) Is itself najasa; najasa cannot purify another najasa
    Food items (طعام) — e.g. bread, dough Grave disrespect (ihana) toward food; blameworthy
    Right hand (يمين) Prophetic guidance to reserve the right hand from impurity

    Important note: If someone does use a prohibited material (e.g., bone or food), their istinja is legally considered valid (the najasa is removed to the extent possible), but it is makruh (blameworthy). A bone, lacking absorptive capacity, would generally not achieve istinja regardless.

    Prayer Times — Conditions and Parameters

    Prayer Begins Ends
    Fajr Fajr al-Thani (true dawn — horizontal white light spreading across horizon) Sunrise (first ray of sun appearing)
    Zuhr Zawal (sun begins to decline from zenith; ~5–10 minutes after noon) Shadow of object equals twice its length (Abu Hanifa) or its own length (Sahibayn), plus the shadow at zawal
    Asr Immediately when Zuhr time ends Sunset
    Maghrib Complete sunset Disappearance of Shafaq al-Abyad / white twilight (Abu Hanifa — muftabi) or Shafaq al-Ahmar / red twilight (Sahibayn)
    Isha After Shafaq disappears (white per muftabi; red per Sahibayn) Fajr al-Thani (true dawn)
    Witr After praying Isha Fajr al-Thani (true dawn)

    Note on Fajr al-Kadhib (False Dawn): A vertical streak of light appearing ~70–90 minutes before true dawn; it disappears before Fajr al-Sadiq arrives. It does not mark the beginning of Fajr time.

    Scholarly Opinions

    On the Number of Strokes in Istinja

    Scholar Opinion
    Ahnaf No fixed number; cleanliness is the criterion
    Imam Shafi'i Minimum three strokes (odd number); dalil: hadith in Muslim and Abu Dawud

    On the Dirham Measurement (Makharaj)

    Scholar Opinion
    Shaykhayn (Abu Hanifa + Abu Yusuf) Dirham size measured excluding the makharaj itself
    Imam Muhammad al-Shaybani Dirham size measured including the makharaj

    On End of Maghrib Time (Shafaq)

    Scholar Opinion
    Imam Abu Hanifa رحمه الله Shafaq = white twilight (Shafaq al-Abyad); Maghrib extends until it disappears — muftabi opinion
    Imam Abu Yusuf & Imam Muhammad (Sahibayn) Shafaq = red twilight (Shafaq al-Ahmar); Maghrib ends earlier

    On the Ruling of Istinja

    Scholar Opinion
    Imam Abu Hanifa (Ahnaf) Sunnah
    Imam Shafi'i Wajib

    On Alcohol as Najasa (briefly raised in Q&A)

    • A student asked about the Hanafi position distinguishing types of alcohol.
    • The teacher indicated a difference of opinion exists within Ahnaf on the matter, with the muftabi opinion being that all intoxicants fall under the same ruling (haram).
    • The specific positions of Imam Abu Hanifa / Abu Yusuf vs. Imam Muhammad on the impurity (najasa) of non-grape/date alcohol will be researched and addressed in a future session.

    On End of Zuhr / Beginning of Asr — Shadow Length

    Scholar Shadow Length Signaling End of Zuhr
    Imam Abu Hanifa Shadow equals twice the object's height (plus shadow at zawal)
    Sahibayn (Abu Yusuf & Muhammad) Shadow equals once the object's height (plus shadow at zawal)

    Lessons & Practical Application

    1. Modern bathroom context: Even when using modern toilet paper, the sunnah method (front-to-back, back-to-front, front-to-back) should be followed where possible. Three wipes is the practical standard; more is permissible if needed.

    2. Water access: If water is available, always use it — this is the afzal (superior) method of istinja even when dry materials suffice legally. Do not treat the permissibility of dry materials as license to avoid water when it is accessible.

    3. Dirham threshold in daily life: Workers and students who cannot always access water in public restrooms should understand this ruling: if the spread of najasa is within the dirham size, salah is still valid (though with karaha). When water becomes available, one should clean properly.

    4. Bone and food: Never use bones or food to clean oneself, even in emergencies. If no suitable material is available, it is better to leave the area with the najasa and clean properly later at home before praying.

    5. Prayer times and modern life: The shadow-based indicators for Zuhr/Asr times are harder to observe in urban settings. Following a reliable Islamic prayer time application that applies the Hanafi method (shadow = twice the object for Asr) is the practical equivalent.

    6. Shafaq and Isha timing: Because the muftabi Hanafi opinion extends Maghrib until Shafaq al-Abyad disappears, Isha time begins slightly later than some other calculations — be aware of this when using prayer time apps, which may use different standards.

    7. Witr is tied to Isha: Never pray Witr before praying Isha. Its window closes at Fajr al-Thani — if a person sleeps and fears missing Witr, they should pray it before sleeping; if confident they will wake for Tahajjud, they may delay it.

    8. The rectifying nature of salah: The teacher noted that the linguistic root of salah — the straightening of bent wood — is instructive: salah rectifies and straightens a person's life, which is why the word was chosen.
