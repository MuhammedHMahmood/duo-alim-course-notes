    Fiqh 201 - Study Notes

    Date: 2026-09-26

    Session Overview

    This session focused on the Shurut al-Salah (conditions of prayer) as presented in Imam al-Quduri's Mukhtasar. The teacher covered the seven pre-prayer conditions, with detailed discussion on tahara (purification), satr al-'awra (covering of the nakedness), facing the qibla, and niyyah (intention). The session concluded with an introductory reading of Bab Sifat al-Salah (the chapter on the description of prayer), listing the six internal fara'id of salah.

    Key Themes

    1. Shurut vs. Arkan — Conditions (shurut) are prerequisites external to the salah itself; they must be met before Takbir al-Tahrimah is said.
    2. Tahara — Purity of body, clothing, and place as obligatory prerequisites for prayer.
    3. Satr al-'Awra — Detailed rulings on what must be covered for men, free women, and slave women.
    4. The One-Fourth Rule (Hukm al-Rub') — A recurring Hanafi legal principle applied in multiple contexts.
    5. Praying in the absence of clothing or clean clothing — What a person does when conditions cannot be fully met.
    6. Niyyah — The internal intention and its correct placement relative to Takbir al-Tahrimah.
    7. Facing the Qibla — Requirements, ijtihad when direction is unknown, and the ruling if one discovers an error.
    8. Introduction to the Fara'id inside Salah — The six internal obligatory pillars.

    Fiqh Rulings & Evidence

    1. Tahara (Purification) — Obligation and Evidence

    • Ruling: It is fard (wajib in Imam Quduri's usage = compulsory) to be pure from both Hadath (ritual states) and Najas (physical filth) before prayer.
    • Three things that must be pure: (1) the person's body, (2) the person's clothing, (3) the place of prayer.
    • Dalil for bodily purification: Qur'an, Surah al-Ma'idah (5:6).
    • Dalil for purity of clothing: Qur'an, Surah al-Muddaththir (74:4) — "And your clothing purify."

    2. Satr al-'Awra — Obligation and Evidence

    • Ruling: Covering the 'awra is a fard condition of salah.
    • Dalil: Qur'an, Surah al-A'raf (7:31) — "O children of Adam, dress properly at every place of worship."
    • Dalil (supplementary): Hadith in Tirmidhi regarding covering the satr.

    3. Facing the Qibla — Obligation and Evidence

    • Ruling: Facing the Qibla is a fard condition of prayer.
    • Dalil: Qur'an, Surah al-Baqarah (2:144) — "Turn your face toward the Sacred Mosque."
    • Exception (fear): Qur'an, Surah al-Baqarah (2:115) — "To Allah belongs the east and the west; wherever you turn, there is the Face of Allah." Used as dalil that one may face any direction when fearing for one's life.

    4. Praying with Najis Clothing (when no alternative exists)

    Scenario Ruling
    Less than ¼ of clothing is najis Pray normally; salah is valid
    More than ¼ is najis, but person has other clean clothing Must change; salah in najis clothing invalid
    More than ¾ is najis, no other clothing, no means to clean Abu Hanifa & Abu Yusuf: pray naked OR in najis cloth; preferred = pray in najis cloth
    — Imam Muhammad & Imam Malik: pray in najis cloth
    — Imam Shafi'i (opinion 1): pray in najis cloth; (opinion 2): pray naked

    Key principle: Praying naked exposes two violations (exposing 'awra + absence of clothing), so praying in najis clothing is preferred when forced to choose.

    5. Praying Completely Naked (No Clothing at All)

    • Ruling: Pray sitting, making isharah (gesturing) for ruku' and sujud, with legs extended toward the Qibla and private parts covered with hands as best as possible.
    • If one prays standing: Salah is valid (ajza'ahu) but sitting is preferred (al-awwal afdal).
    • Dalil: Athar of 'Abdullah ibn 'Umar and Anas ibn Malik (radiAllahu 'anhuma) — Sahaba whose boat capsized prayed in this manner sitting naked, and this was given permission.

    6. Niyyah (Intention)

    • Ruling: Intention must be made before or at Takbir al-Tahrimah. No gap (fasl) of action should occur between niyyah and Takbir al-Tahrimah.
    • Niyyah is an act of the heart — verbalization with the tongue is recommended but not obligatory.
    • If Takbir precedes niyyah: Salah is invalid — per Zahir al-Riwayah, the niyyah cannot come after the Takbir al-Tahrimah.
    • Practical exception: If one rushes to join the imam mid-prayer (e.g., imam is in ruku'), the general intention one carried upon entering the masjid suffices.

    7. Error in Qibla Direction

    Situation Ruling
    Did ijtihad, prayed, then discovered error after salah No repetition required
    Discovers error during salah Must perform istidarah (turn toward correct direction) and continue
    Did not do ijtihad at all Must repeat salah
    • Dalil: Hadith in Bayhaqi and Tirmidhi confirming that one who makes tahharri (best effort) and is wrong is excused.
    • Imam Shafi'i's additional condition: If one prayed exactly 180° opposite the Qibla, he must repeat even after ijtihad. Ahnaf do not make this distinction.
    • Dalil for turning mid-prayer: Hadith in Bukhari — while people were praying Fajr at Masjid Quba, someone called from outside announcing the change of Qibla; they turned immediately mid-prayer.

    Conditions & Categories

    The Seven Conditions (Shurut) of Salah

    All seven are external to the prayer — none require Takbir al-Tahrimah to have been made:

    # Condition Notes
    1 Time (Waqt) Already covered earlier in Kitab al-Salah
    2 Purity of body from Hadath & Najas Wudu/ghusl as appropriate
    3 Purity of clothing Free of Najasa Ghaleeza/Mukhaffafa
    4 Purity of place Where one prays must be clean
    5 Satr al-'Awra Covering required body parts
    6 Niyyah Intention for the specific salah being prayed
    7 Facing the Qibla Or any direction in genuine fear

    'Awra Categories

    Free Man ('Awra): - From below the navel to below the kneecap (inclusive) - The navel itself is NOT 'awra (slight exposure permitted) - The knee IS 'awra (per Ahnaf) — must be fully covered

    Free Woman ('Awra): - Entire body is 'awra except the face and the hands (up to the wrist) - Dalil: Qur'an, Surah al-Nur (24:31) — "...except what normally appears"; Ibn 'Abbas (raḍiAllahu 'anhu) interpreted this as the face and hands - Note on feet: Some fuqaha hold that the feet are 'awra in salah (based on hadith in Abu Dawud), though not outside of it

    Slave Woman (Ama) 'Awra: - Same as a man's 'awra, PLUS her stomach and back - Arms, hair, and lower legs are NOT 'awra - This ruling applies to all four categories: regular slave (ama), Umm Walad, Mukatabah, and Mudabbarah

    The One-Fourth Rule (Hukm al-Rub') — Hanafi Principle

    Appears in multiple rulings: - Najasa on clothing: Less than ¼ najis → treated as fully clean; ¼ or more → salah invalid - 'Awra exposure: Less than ¼ of kneecap exposed → salah valid; ¼ or more exposed → salah invalid - Masah of the head: Wiping ¼ of the head is treated as wiping the whole head - Usul principle (from Usul al-Shashi): "One-fourth equals the whole" (الرُّبْع كَالْكُلّ) - Note: For Najas on a person's body (not animal), the threshold is the size of a dirham, not the ¼ rule

    The Six Internal Fara'id of Salah (Fara'id al-Salah Sittah)

    Introduced at the end of the session (full discussion deferred to next class):

    1. Takbirat al-Ihram (opening Allahu Akbar)
    2. al-Qiyam (standing)
    3. al-Qira'ah (recitation)
    4. al-Ruku' (bowing)
    5. al-Sujud (prostration)
    6. al-Qa'dah al-Akhirah — the final sitting for at least the duration of tashahhud

    Anything beyond these is Sunnah.

    Scholarly Opinions

    On the 'Awra of the Knee

    Scholar/School Position
    Ahnaf Knee is part of 'awra; must be covered; if ¼ or more is exposed, salah is invalid. Dalil: Hadith in Dar Qutni
    Imam Shafi'i & Imam Ahmad Knee is not 'awra — exposure does not invalidate salah, similar to slight navel exposure. Dalil: Hadith in Abu Dawud
    Practical note Both schools agree the area immediately above and below the knee is 'awra; covering the knee entirely avoids the dispute

    On Praying Naked (No Clothing Available) — When > ¾ of Clothing is Najis

    Scholar Position
    Abu Hanifa & Abu Yusuf Two valid options: pray naked OR in najis cloth; preferred = naked (removes the filth) — though some understanding holds praying in najis cloth is preferred to preserve satr
    Imam Muhammad Pray in the najis cloth (exposes 'awra if removed — two violations vs. one)
    Imam Malik Pray in the najis cloth
    Imam Shafi'i Two opinions: (1) pray in najis cloth; (2) pray naked

    On Error in Qibla Direction after Ijtihad

    Scholar Position
    Ahnaf No repeat required regardless of how far off the direction was, as long as ijtihad was performed
    Imam Shafi'i No repeat required — except if one faced exactly 180° opposite the Qibla

    On the Takbir al-Tahrimah Wording

    Scholar Position
    Abu Hanifa & Imam Muhammad Any formula of ta'zim (glorification) that conveys greatness of Allah (e.g., Allahu A'zam, Allahu Ajall) is sufficient
    Abu Yusuf Only the specific wording "Allahu Akbar" is valid

    Lessons & Practical Application

    1. Understand the chapter titles of fiqh and hadith books. The 'Abwab' (chapter headings) carry the author's intent and fiqhi direction — especially critical in Bukhari, where the chapter title is the only text Imam Bukhari himself wrote.

    2. The distinction between Shurut and Arkan is practically significant: conditions must be met before entering salah; pillars are performed within it. Mixing them up leads to errors about what invalidates salah.

    3. Shorts and prayer: If wearing shorts in salah, ensure the knee remains fully covered even during sujud and qa'dah, as the short fabric tends to ride up. Safer to wear clothing that covers well below the knee.

    4. Exposure of less than ¼ of 'awra does not invalidate salah per Ahnaf — but one should not deliberately rely on this; covering fully is always best.

    5. When phone direction apps are unavailable, perform tahharri — use road direction, sun/star position, landmarks — and make your best judgment. If wrong, salah is still valid as long as you tried. Praying without any attempt at ijtihad means the salah must be repeated.

    6. Niyyah is in the heart — in a rush to catch the jama'ah, the general intention you carried to the masjid is sufficient; full verbal formulation is sunnah, not fard.

    7. If corrected about Qibla mid-prayer, follow the correction and turn immediately — this is supported by the incident at Masjid Quba when the change of Qibla was announced and the congregation turned mid-Fajr prayer.

    8. On Islamic slavery: Islam did not abolish slavery instantly but engineered a sustainable path toward its elimination — through strong targhib (encouragement) to free slaves, making it an expiation for many sins, and cultivating the understanding that freeing a slave is one of the best actions a person can do. The Sahaba who were slaves were often given full support by their masters to devote themselves to deen.

    9. Knowledge of deen is transmitted heart to heart — do not rely solely on external sources including AI; seek to understand the why behind each ruling, preserve it, and pass it on through proper transmission (the teacher-student chain).
