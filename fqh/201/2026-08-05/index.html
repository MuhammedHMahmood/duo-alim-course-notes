    Fiqh 201 - Study Notes

    Date: 2026-08-05

    Session Overview

    This was the first session of Fiqh 201, serving as an orientation and introduction to the course. The teacher began by congratulating students on completing their first year, acknowledging the difficulty of balancing learning with work and other responsibilities. The bulk of the session covered: (1) the definition and scope of Ilm al-Fiqh, (2) the four sources of Islamic legal rulings with their evidences, (3) an overview of the four Imams and their madhabs, and (4) a brief introduction to the key Hanafi scholars relevant to the main text (Quduri). The class text, Mukhtasar al-Quduri, will begin on Saturday. No chapter of the book was covered today.

    Key Themes

    1. The purpose and structure of Ilm al-Fiqh — Fiqh is the knowledge of subsidiary legal rulings derived from detailed evidences; it does not deal with matters of belief (Aqidah).
    2. The four sources (Masadir) of Fiqh — Quran, Hadith, Ijma, and Qiyas, each with its own dalil.
    3. Mukallaf — not every ruling applies to every person; the concept of legal responsibility defines on whom a ruling falls.
    4. The progression of Fiqh books in the curriculum — Quduri (year 2) → Hidayah (year 3–4) → Daura-e-Hadith (year 6), each building on the last.
    5. The four Imams and their schools — their lives, teachers, students, and key contributions, presented chronologically.
    6. Adab of learning — respect for books, maintaining wudu, punctuality, active participation, and sincerity of intention.

    Faqih (فَقِيه) One who clarifies and opens the door of understanding for others in matters of Deen
    Ahkam (أَحْكَام) Rulings/laws

    Ahkam Shar'iyyah Fari'iyyah (أَحْكَام شَرْعِيَّة فَرْعِيَّة) Subsidiary legal rulings; the domain of Fiqh

    Usul (أُصُول) Foundations/principles

    Mu'amalat (مُعَامَلَات) Social/financial transactions and interpersonal dealings
    Mukallaf (مُكَلَّف) A legally responsible person upon whom Shari'ah rulings apply

    Ijma (إِجْمَاع) Consensus of qualified scholars

    Af'al (أَفْعَال) The physical actions of the Prophet ﷺ

    Tadwin al-Fiqh (تَدْوِين الفِقْه) The formal codification/compilation of Fiqh — a key contribution of Imam Muhammad Rahimullah
    Usul al-Fiqh (أُصُول الفِقْه) The principles and methodology of Islamic jurisprudence
    Usul al-Hadith (أُصُول الحَدِيث) The science of Hadith methodology and terminology

    Rijal (رِجَال) The science of narrators; the biographical study of hadith transmitters
    Takhwij (تَخْرِيج) The process of tracing a hadith back to its original source
    Masadir (مَصَادِر) Sources (here: the four sources of Fiqh)
    Istilahi (اصْطِلَاحِي) Technical/terminological meaning (as opposed to linguistic)

    Wajib (وَاجِب) Near-obligatory act established by less definitive evidence

    Fiqh Rulings & Evidence

    Definition of Fiqh (Istilahi)

    الْعِلْمُ بِالْأَحْكَامِ الشَّرْعِيَّةِ الْفَرْعِيَّةِ عَنْ أَدِلَّتِهَا التَّفْصِيلِيَّة

    "The knowledge of subsidiary Shari'ah rulings derived from their detailed evidences."

    Breakdown of the definition: - "Knowledge" — Ilm; this is a formal, structured discipline. - "Subsidiary" (Fari'iyyah) — pertains to the Furu', i.e., practical acts, not foundational beliefs. - "Shari'ah rulings" — restricted to what Shari'ah has addressed. - "Detailed evidences" — each ruling traced to a specific Quranic verse, hadith, Ijma, or Qiyas.

    The Four Sources (Masadir) of Fiqh

    # Source Description
    1 Quran Primary and foremost source
    2 Hadith/Sunnah Words, actions, approvals, and character of Rasulullah ﷺ
    3 Ijma Consensus of qualified scholars of Ahl al-Sunnah wal-Jama'ah
    4 Qiyas Analogical reasoning applied by qualified scholars

    Dalil for Qiyas and the first three sources — Hadith of Mu'adh ibn Jabal:

    When the Prophet ﷺ dispatched Mu'adh ibn Jabal رضي الله عنه to Yemen, he asked him: "How will you give rulings?" - Mu'adh: "By the Book of Allah." - Prophet ﷺ: "What if you do not find it there?" - Mu'adh: "By the Sunnah of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ." - Prophet ﷺ: "What if you do not find it there either?" - Mu'adh: "I shall practice my own reasoning (Qiyas)." - The Prophet ﷺ patted his chest and said: "All praise to Allah who gave success to the messenger of the Messenger of Allah in achieving what pleases the Messenger of Allah."

    This hadith establishes Quran, Sunnah, and Qiyas as sources. (Referenced as a well-known hadith of Mu'adh.)

    Dalil for all four sources — Hadith of Abdullah ibn Mas'ud (from Nasa'i):

    "Whoever is faced with an issue, he should decide by the Book of Allah. If an affair comes to him not in the Book of Allah, then by what the Prophet ﷺ decided. If an affair comes to him that is not in the Book of Allah nor decided by the Prophet ﷺ, then by what the righteous (Sahaba) decided [Ijma]. And if an affair comes to him that none of the above covers, then he should decide using his own reasoning [Qiyas]."

    This hadith from Sunan al-Nasa'i explicitly names all four sources.

    Scope of Ilm al-Fiqh

    • Fiqh does not apply to Ahkam I'tiqadi (matters of belief): Wahdaniyyah of Allah, Prophethood, Day of Judgment. Issues in these areas relate to Iman itself, not Fiqh.
    • Fiqh applies to Ahkam Shar'iyyah Fari'iyyah — all practical actions a Muslim performs after accepting Islam: Salah, Zakat, Hajj, Sawm, trade, Nikah, and all of Mu'amalat.

    On Salah Being Fard

    • Dalil: Surah al-Baqarah, Ayah 43 — "And establish Salah."
    • This establishes the obligation of Salah, but the ruling applies specifically to the Mukallaf — not to every person without qualification.

    Conditions & Categories

    The Mukallaf — Conditions for Legal Responsibility

    A person is Mukallaf (legally responsible for a ruling) when applicable conditions are met. Examples of those excluded from certain rulings:

    Category Ruling
    Non-Muslim Salah is not applied as a Fiqhi obligation upon them (though theoretically addressed in Quran)
    Insane person Salah does not apply — lacks legal capacity
    Child (before maturity) Salah not obligatory; parents should habituate them at a prescribed age to build the habit
    Severely intoxicated person Not Mukallaf for the duration of intoxication — Quran (referring to Sukara) says do not come to Salah in that state

    Example — Hajj: Being wealthy alone does not immediately make one Mukallaf for Hajj; other specific conditions must also be met.

    Categories of Rulings Applied to the Mukallaf

    The goal of Fiqh is to correctly categorize every human action into one of the following:

    Category Description
    Fard (فَرْض) Obligatory — established by definitive Quranic or Mutawatir evidence
    Wajib (وَاجِب) Near-obligatory — established by strong but slightly less definitive evidence
    Sunnah Mu'akkadah Emphasized Sunnah — regularly practiced by Rasulullah ﷺ
    Sunnah Ghayr Mu'akkadah Non-emphasized Sunnah
    Nafl / Mustahab Voluntary/recommended
    Haram (حَرَام) Absolutely prohibited
    Makruh Tahrimi Prohibitively disliked (close to Haram)
    Makruh Tanzahi Mildly disliked

    On Ijma — What Qualifies

    • Not the consensus of scholars of a single city, country, or locality.
    • Must be agreed upon by the majority of Ulama of Ahl al-Sunnah wal-Jama'ah across the Muslim world.
    • Strongest form: consensus of the Sahabah. After them: Tabi'een. After them: the majority of qualified Ulama globally.

    On Qiyas — Who May Perform It

    • Qiyas is not for the general Muslim to perform independently.
    • It requires extremely deep knowledge of Deen — it is the domain of qualified Mujtahids.
    • Once established by qualified scholars and accepted by the Ummah, it becomes a binding source.
    • Examples given:
    • Cryptocurrency: no direct ruling exists; a scholar looks at the closest analogous ruling (e.g., currency, gold/silver trading) and applies the underlying principle.
    • Marijuana: no explicit text; the ruling on intoxicants is applied via Qiyas because it produces the same effect (intoxication/loss of discernment) as khamr.

    Scholarly Opinions

    The Four Imams — Overview

    1. Imam Abu Hanifa Rahimullah (Hanafi Madhab)

    Detail Information
    Full name Nu'man ibn Thabit
    Born 80 AH, Kufa
    Died 150 AH, Baghdad
    Lifespan ~70 years
    Key teachers Ibrahim al-Nakha'i Rahimullah, Hammad ibn Sulayman Rahimullah
    Famous students Imam Abu Yusuf, Imam Muhammad, Abdullah ibn Mubarak Rahimullah
    • Never wrote books himself.
    • The six books of Zahir al-Riwayah (the most authoritative books of Hanafi Fiqh) were all written by Imam Muhammad Rahimullah.
    • Imam Muhammad Rahimullah performed two foundational services: (1) established/codified Hanafi Fiqh, and (2) was the first to formally define and lay down Usul al-Fiqh.
    • The Hanafi school emerged from Kufa (Iraq), which became the center of Islamic learning after Ali رضي الله عنه moved the Khilafah there. Major Sahabah including Abdullah ibn Mas'ud settled there.
    • Key principle of Ahnaf: Hanafi rulings are not whimsical — they follow defined Usul. Differences between Madhabs arise from different but internally consistent principles, not personal preference.

    2. Imam Malik Rahimullah (Maliki Madhab)

    Detail Information
    Full name Malik ibn Anas
    Born 95 AH, Medina
    Died In Medina (spent his entire life there)
    Key teachers Nafi' Rahimullah (among many — reportedly over 900 teachers)
    Famous students Qatan Rahimullah, Abdullah ibn Mubarak Rahimullah
    Key work Muwatta Imam Malik
    • Core Usul of Imam Malik: The practice ('Amal) of the people of Medina takes precedence — even over a Sahih hadith — because Medina is where Rasulullah ﷺ lived and died, and its people preserved his Sunnah through lived practice better than any other locality.
    • Muwatta is considered by many Muhaddithin to deserve inclusion among the Sihah Sittah (six canonical hadith books).

    3. Imam Shafi'i Rahimullah (Shafi'i Madhab)

    Detail Information
    Full name Muhammad ibn Idris al-Shafi'i
    Born 150 AH (the year Imam Abu Hanifa passed away), Asqalan
    Died 204 AH, Misr (Egypt)
    Key teachers Imam Malik Rahimullah, Imam Muhammad Rahimullah (Hanafi), Sufyan ibn 'Uyayna Rahimullah
    Famous students Imam Ahmad ibn Hanbal, Ali ibn Madini, Ishaq ibn Rahoya
    Key works Al-Risala, Kitab al-Umm
    • Studied under Imam Malik in Medina first; Imam Malik then directed him to study under Imam Abu Yusuf. By the time he arrived, Imam Abu Yusuf had also passed away, so he studied under Imam Muhammad.
    • Thus, Imam Shafi'i uniquely mastered both the Hanafi and Maliki schools before establishing his own.
    • His opinions in certain matters (e.g., recitation behind the Imam) shifted after he moved to Egypt, because of his exposure to the Sahabah's legacy there.
    • Was the first to formally write on Usul al-Fiqh — Al-Risala is considered the first work in this field.

    4. Imam Ahmad ibn Hanbal Rahimullah (Hanbali Madhab)

    Detail Information
    Born 164 AH, Baghdad
    Died 241 AH
    Key teachers Imam Abu Yusuf Rahimullah, Imam Shafi'i Rahimullah, Yahya ibn Qattan
    Famous students Imam Bukhari, Imam Muslim, Imam Abu Dawud, Abdullah ibn Ahmad
    Key work Musnad Imam Ahmad (127,000 hadith)
    • Studied directly under Imam Abu Yusuf (could not study under Imam Abu Hanifa, who passed away 14 years before his birth).
    • The Madhabs were codified before the major Hadith collections — Imam Bukhari, Imam Muslim, and Abu Dawud were students of Imam Ahmad; the canonical Hadith books came after the Madhabs were established.
    • Faced severe persecution over the Khalq al-Quran (createdness of the Quran) controversy — was imprisoned and beaten for firmly holding that the Quran is the uncreated speech of Allah.
    • Musnad Imam Ahmad contains 127,000 ahadith (compare: Sahih Bukhari ~6,700–6,800 hadith).
    • Imam Bukhari himself memorized over 600,000 ahadith with complete Sanad — a level of memorization far beyond the Matan alone.

    The Two Key Hanafi Imams (Sahibayn)

    (Full detail deferred to Saturday; mentioned as essential to Quduri study)

    • Imam Abu Yusuf Rahimullah — student of Imam Abu Hanifa; his opinions are cited throughout Quduri alongside Imam Abu Hanifa's.
    • Imam Muhammad Rahimullah — author of the six Zahir al-Riwayah books; studied under both Imam Abu Hanifa and was a teacher to Imam Shafi'i.

    In Quduri, students will regularly see: - Imam Abu Hanifa's opinion — the primary Hanafi position - Imam Abu Yusuf's opinion — sometimes differs - Imam Muhammad's opinion — sometimes differs

    In Hidayah (year 3–4), all four Madhabs are compared with their underlying evidences.

    Lessons & Practical Application

    Adab (Etiquettes) of Seeking Knowledge

    1. Maintain wudu during class — many senior scholars would not even write the name of Rasulullah ﷺ without being in a state of wudu.
    2. Make du'a before class — the time immediately after a class of Deen is a time of acceptance of du'a.
    3. Wear perfume (where appropriate) — with the note that women should avoid heavy fragrances as it falls under their hijab in public settings.
    4. Respect the books of Deen — keep a designated place for them; do not place anything (pens, phones, other books) on top of them.
    5. Anecdote of Anwar Shah Kashmiri Rahimullah: He would physically reposition himself to read the marginalia of a book rather than move the book itself — his reverence for the written Deen is credited as a source of his vast knowledge.
    6. Read actively in class — builds the habit needed for Daura year, where students read hadith aloud and the teacher explains.
    7. Punctuality — attend on time as a basic adab.

    Mindset for This Course

    • Do not measure yourself by exam marks — measure by how much Deen has entered your life, how much you apply, and how much you convey to others.
    • Imam Shafi'i Rahimullah said: "If you do not give your 100% to the Deen of Allah, this Deen will not come to you — and even when you give 100%, only a fraction of it may return."
    • Encouragement to persevere even when not understanding — the teacher shared his own experience of nearly giving up in first year upon Mufti Yaseer's advice: "Even if you do not understand, continue. There will come a time — perhaps not this month, perhaps not this year — but stay and you will get it."
    • Hadith principle: "Whomever Allah wants good for, He gives them the understanding of Deen." (Agreed upon — Bukhari and Muslim) — our being here is solely from the mercy of Allah.
