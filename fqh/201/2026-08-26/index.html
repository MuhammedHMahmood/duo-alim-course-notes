    Fiqh 201 - Study Notes

    Date: 2026-08-26

    Session Overview

    This session covered two major topics in Kitab al-Tahara: (1) the purification of animal skins through tanning (dabaghat) and the permissibility of using parts of dead animals, and (2) the rulings on wells (bi'r) that have been contaminated by najasa — specifically what quantity of water must be removed depending on what fell in, and what actions must be repeated by those who unknowingly used the contaminated water.

    Key Themes

    • Dabaghat (tanning) purifies animal hides, making them usable for prayer mats, vessels, and containers — with two exceptions: pig skin and human skin.
    • Eight parts of a dead animal are inherently pure and do not require tanning — a position unique to the Hanafi school.
    • Well contamination (bi'r) rulings are categorized by the size/type of animal that fell in and whether the animal died, decomposed, or swelled.
    • Quantity of water to remove varies by animal category, with six distinct opinions among Hanafi jurists for wells with a constant water source.
    • Repeating salah and ghusl is required for those who unknowingly used contaminated well water, with differing opinions on how many days' worth of salah must be repeated.
    • Shari'ah concession (rukhsa) applies when strict enforcement would cause undue hardship — e.g., small quantities of dried droppings blown into a well.

    Fiqh Rulings & Evidence

    Ruling 1: Tanning Purifies Animal Hides

    Every animal skin — whether from a zabihah or non-zabihah animal — becomes pure once it undergoes dabaghat (tanning), except: - The skin of swine (khinzir) — najis in its essence, no process purifies it - The skin of a human being — impermissible to use out of respect for human dignity (karamat/azmat al-adam)

    Once tanned, the leather may be used for: - Praying upon (salah on it is valid — detailed ruling deferred to Kitab al-Salah) - Making wudu with water carried in it - Constructing vessels/sacks for water transport

    Evidences: - Hadith in al-Nasa'i: "Every skin that is tanned becomes pure." - Hadith of A'isha (r.a.) in al-Nasa'i on the purification of dead skin through tanning. - Surah al-An'am (6:145) — cited as dalil for the impermissibility of swine skin.

    Ruling 2: Eight Parts of a Dead Animal Are Inherently Pure (Hanafi Position)

    The following eight parts of a dead animal (mayta) do not require tanning and are usable:

    1. Hair (sha'r)
    2. Bone (azm)
    3. Hoof (hafir)
    4. Horn (qarn)
    5. Wool (suf)
    6. Nail/claw (zufr)
    7. Feather (rish)
    8. Beak (minqar)

    Hanafi Principle: Any part of an animal that does not contain life (i.e., has no blood, no sensation) remains pure even after the animal's death.

    Exception: None of these eight are permissible if they come from swine.

    Evidence: The Prophet ﷺ gave Fatima (r.a.) a bracelet made from ivory (elephant tusk) — used as dalil that bones/tusks of non-slaughtered animals are permissible.

    Opposing Opinion (Imam al-Shafi'i): All eight parts are najis. None may be used from a dead animal.

    Ruling 3: Contamination of Wells — Water Removal by Animal Category

    Animal/Scenario Water to Remove
    Small mouse, small bird (usfura), sa'wa, sudaniyya 20–30 buckets (20 = minimum/sufficient; 30 = mustahab)
    Pigeon, chicken, cat, or similarly-sized animal 40–50 buckets (40 = sufficient; 50 = mustahab)
    Dog, goat, human being Entire well must be emptied
    Any animal whose body has swollen and begun to crack (regardless of size) Entire well must be emptied

    Note: The condition in Imam Quduri's text for the third category (dog/goat/human) is that the animal died in the well. However, some fuqaha hold that for inherently najis animals (e.g., dog), the entire water must be removed even if the animal came out alive.

    Dalil for removing entire well: 'Abdullah ibn al-Zubayr (r.a.) was asked about a person who died in the well of Zamzam and ruled that the entire well must be drained. This athar is recorded in al-Tahawi and is taken as a dalil.

    Ruling 4: Wells with a Constant Water Source (Ma'in Well)

    If a well has a constantly replenishing underground source (ma'in), draining it entirely is impractical. Six opinions exist among Hanafi jurists:

    # Opinion Method Source
    1 Consult well-digging experts in the locality who can estimate when the well is effectively drained Expert testimony —
    2 Lower two knowledgeable people into the well; they ascertain sufficiency Two expert witnesses in the well Imam Abu Hanifa; Sahib al-Hidaya
    3 Dig a second identical-sized well nearby; transfer water until new well is full Parallel well method —
    4 Insert a measuring stick; remove buckets incrementally and measure water-level drop per bucket; extrapolate total Stick/measurement method Imam Abu Yusuf
    5 Remove 200–300 buckets Fixed quantity Imam Muhammad (preferred; agreed upon by Hanafi fuqaha — mu'tamad)
    6 Remove 250–300 buckets Fixed quantity (variant) Imam Muhammad (second narration)

    Mu'tamad (relied-upon) position: Opinion #5 — 200 to 300 buckets (average local size).

    Ruling 5: Repeating Salah After Unknowing Use of Contaminated Well Water

    Scenario A — Animal found dead, body NOT swollen (time of fall unknown): - Remove water as per the animal's category. - All who used the water must repeat salah of one full day and night = 5 prayers. - Clothes/utensils washed with it should be re-washed.

    Scenario B — Animal found dead AND body has swollen and cracked (time of fall unknown):

    Position Ruling
    Imam Abu Hanifa (mu'tamad) Repeat salah of 3 days and 3 nights = 15 prayers
    Sahiban (Abu Yusuf & Muhammad) No repetition is required until investigation confirms how long ago the animal fell; only repeat for the confirmed period

    Ruling 6: Concession for Small Quantities of Dried Droppings

    If dried animal droppings (e.g., from goats grazing nearby) are blown by wind into a well in small quantities — not enough to visibly dirty or alter the water — the Hanafi school grants a rukhsa: the water remains usable for wudu and tahara. This is based on the principle that hardship lifts restriction (al-mashaqqah tajlib al-taysir).

    Exception: Chicken droppings — even a small quantity is considered najis by consensus of all four madhabs.

    Note on pigeon droppings: Not considered najis. Dalil: Pigeons were present in Masjid al-Nabawi at the time of the Prophet ﷺ and no instruction was given to clean the masjid on their account.

    Ruling 7: Using Contaminated Water for Izalat al-Najasa

    Musta'mal water (used water) that has lost its capacity to remove hadath (ritual impurity) may still be used for izalat al-najasa (removing physical najasa from a surface or garment).

    Conditions & Categories

    Conditions for Tanning to Purify Hide

    • Must undergo either hakiki or hukmi dabagha
    • Must not be from swine or human

    Conditions for Well Contamination Ruling to Apply

    1. Animal must have died in the well (per Imam Quduri's text) — for dog/goat/human category; some fuqaha differ
    2. If animal swam out alive: rulings on full drainage may still apply for inherently najis animals
    3. If two animals die simultaneously: remove water quantity corresponding to the larger animal — one cleaning suffices for both

    Conditions for Salah Repetition

    • Person must have had no valid prior wudu before using the contaminated water
    • If person had performed wudu at another source and only used this well for another wudu on top of it, their salah does not need to be repeated
    • Ghusl performed with contaminated water: must be repeated
    • Ghusl performed elsewhere; contaminated water used only for other purposes: nothing to repeat

    Bucket Size Determination

    • Determined by local custom ('urf); whichever size is the average in that locality
    • Approximate average bucket: ~3.5 liters
    • Approximate large bucket: ~60–70 liters (≈ 20× average)
    • Large buckets may be used and converted proportionally to the equivalent number of average-sized buckets

    Lessons & Practical Application

    1. Rukhsa and hardship: Shari'ah consistently provides concessions where strict enforcement creates undue difficulty. The well-contamination rulings are calibrated to real-world practicality — small droppings blown by wind, the ability to use locally-sized buckets, and the use of expert judgment are all accommodations for daily life in rural/agrarian contexts. The same principle underpins tayammum (dry ablution when water would cause harm).

    2. 'Urf (custom) as a legal tool: The bucket size ruling is a clear example of how Hanafi fiqh integrates local custom into its rulings. There is no fixed universal measurement; what counts is the standard of the community. This is a foundational Hanafi usul principle applicable across many chapters of fiqh.

    3. Studying historical masail builds legal reasoning: These well-water rulings may rarely arise today due to indoor plumbing, but studying them teaches how fuqaha derive rulings, apply qiyas, weigh conflicting evidences, and accommodate necessity — skills transferable to contemporary masail.

    4. Zamzam as a living example: The discussion of ma'in wells becomes vivid through the example of Zamzam, whose level never diminishes despite continuous heavy pumping — a contemporary miracle that connects directly to the historical fiqh scenario of a well with a constant source.

    5. Comparative madhab awareness: The Shafi'i position on the eight animal parts being najis versus the Hanafi position of them being pure illustrates how the same hadith evidence can be weighted differently, and why knowing the dalil behind a ruling matters for proper application and scholarly dialogue.
