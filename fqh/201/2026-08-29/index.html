    Fiqh 201 - Study Notes

    Date: 2026-08-29

    Session Overview

    This session covered two major topics: (1) the final categories of leftover water (su'r) from animals and their rulings for purification, and (2) an introduction to Tayammum — its linguistic and legal definition, historical origin, conditions, obligatory elements (arkan), and sunnahs. The session was primarily based on Imam al-Quduri's Mukhtasar (p. 29 onwards), with supporting hadith from Bukhari, Abu Dawud, Bayhaqi, and Dar al-Qutni, and comparative opinions from the four madhabs.

    Key Themes

    • Completing the chapter on water: which animals' leftover water (su'r) is pure, impure, makruh, or doubtful
    • The five categories of su'r and where the four madhabs agree or differ
    • Introduction to Tayammum: its linguistic origin, shar'i definition, historical context (Ghazwah Zat al-Riqa'), and the role of Aisha (RA) in its revelation
    • The arkan (obligatory elements) and sunnah acts of Tayammum
    • What breaks Tayammum, including the special case of sighting water
    • Comparative opinion: Imam Abu Hanifa/Imam Muhammad vs. Imam Abu Yusuf on valid substances for Tayammum

    Fiqh Rulings & Evidence

    Su'r of Halal Animals and Humans

    • Ruling: Pure (tahir); permissible to use for wudu and ghusl.
    • Dalil: Hadith of the Prophet ﷺ sharing his leftover water with the Sahabah.
    • Scope: No restriction on whether the human is Muslim or non-Muslim, male or female, in a state of janabah or hayd.
    • Aisha (RA) narrated she gave her leftover water to the Prophet ﷺ while she was in a state of janabah and he drank from the same vessel.
    • Thumamah ibn Uthal (RA) was kept in Masjid al-Nabawi before accepting Islam — proof that a kafir is not physically najis in his person.
    • Abu Hurayrah (RA) hadith: he was in janabah and walked with the Prophet ﷺ — no prohibition issued.
    • Hadith in Bayhaqi confirms no restriction on the person's state.

    Su'r of Dogs and Swine

    • Ruling: Najis (impure); forbidden for use in purification. Agreed upon by all four madhabs.
    • Dalil: Hadith in Bukhari — a vessel from which a dog has drunk must be washed.
    • Ahnaf: 3 washings suffice.
    • Imam al-Shafi'i: 7 washings required.
    • Swine is najis in all circumstances.

    Su'r of Wild Hunting Animals (other than dogs/swine)

    • Ahnaf ruling: Najis — the saliva of wild hunting animals contaminates the water.
    • Imam al-Shafi'i's ruling: Pure — he uses as dalil a hadith where the Prophet ﷺ was asked about a pond where wild animals drank and he replied: "What they drank is in their stomachs; what remains is for us."
    • Key distinction: Ahnaf hold that these animals' mouths carry impurity from hunting/eating carrion, making their saliva najis.

    Su'r of Domestic/Free-Roaming Animals (Cat, Chicken, House Mouse, Snake)

    • Ruling (Ahnaf): Makruh — disliked but not forbidden; wudu performed with it is valid.
    • Dalil: Hadith in Dar al-Qutni — the Prophet ﷺ placed water before a cat to drink, then performed wudu from the leftover water.
    • Reason cats are not classified with wild animals: They are so thoroughly domesticated that excluding them from households is practically impossible.
    • Imam al-Shafi'i and Imam Abu Yusuf (Ahnaf): Consider cat's su'r pure — no issue with using it.

    Su'r of Donkeys and Mules

    • Ruling (Ahnaf): Mashkuk (doubtful).
    • Reason for doubt: Donkey meat was initially permissible, then prohibited (around the time of Fatih Makkah or Khaybar). Ikhtilaf existed among Sahabah: Ibn Umar (RA) held it impermissible; Ibn Abbas (RA) held it pure.
    • Practical ruling: If no other water is available, perform both wudu and tayammum (in any order), then proceed with salah.
    • Imam al-Shafi'i: Su'r of donkey is fully pure.
    • Note: Horse su'r falls into the halal-meat category (pure) and is not in this doubtful group.

    Hunting Dogs Used in Halal Hunting

    • The general ruling on dogs' su'r being najis does not automatically render the hunted animal haram — this topic has its own chapter (Kitab al-Sayd) with specific conditions.

    Conditions & Categories

    Five Categories of Su'r (Summary)

    Category Animals Ruling Agreement
    1 Humans + all halal-meat animals Pure (tahir) Muttafaq 'alayh (all 4 madhabs)
    2 Dogs + swine Najis (impure) Muttafaq 'alayh (all 4 madhabs)
    3 Wild hunting animals (other than dog/swine) Najis (Ahnaf) / Pure (Shafi'i) Ikhtilafi masala
    4 Domestic free-roaming animals (cat, chicken, house animals) Makruh Ahnaf position; Shafi'i & Abu Yusuf say pure
    5 Donkeys and mules Mashkuk (doubtful) Ikhtilafi masala

    Conditions Permitting Tayammum

    1. Traveling and water is unavailable (no water within approximately one mile).
    2. Water is present but one fears sickness will worsen if used.
    3. Healthy person fears cold water will cause fatal illness.
    4. Time of salah is imminent and reaching water would cause the prayer time to lapse.

    Valid Substances for Tayammum

    • Imam Abu Hanifa and Imam Muhammad: Anything from the genus of earth — soil, sand, stone (hajar), limestone (nurah), antimony (kuhl), zarnikh, and similar minerals.
    • Imam Abu Yusuf: Only soil or sand; solid rock with no dust does not suffice.
    • Muftaa bihi (operative fatwa): Imam Abu Hanifa's opinion — rock and stone are valid.
    • Practical note: Even dust collected on shelves may suffice if enough is present to wipe the face and hands.
    • The earth must be clean (free of najasah).

    Arkan (Obligatory Elements) of Tayammum — 2

    1. Striking the hands on the earth.
    2. Wiping the face and both hands (up to and including the elbows).

    Six Conditions (Shurut) of Tayammum

    1. Making niyyah (intention) — fard in tayammum (unlike wudu where it is only mustahab).
    2. Wiping the face and hands completely with dust.
    3. Minimum three fingers must be used.
    4. The substance must be from the genus of earth.
    5. The earth must be free of najasah.
    6. Water must be either unavailable or harmful to use.

    Eight Sunnahs of Tayammum

    1. Saying Bismillah before starting.
    2. Striking with the inner (soft) portion of the palms.
    3. Striking with fingers spread apart and palms laid firmly on the surface.
    4. Moving the hands slightly forward after striking.
    5. Drawing the hands back to their original position.
    6. Shaking off excess dust by clapping the hands lightly.
    7. Observing Tarteeb: face first, then right hand, then left hand.
    8. Continuity — not allowing too long a pause between wiping the face and wiping the hands.

    What Breaks Tayammum

    Everything that breaks wudu also breaks tayammum: - Anything exiting from the sabilaan (front/back passages). - Blood, pus, or any discharge exiting through the skin. - Vomiting (when mouthful). - Laughing aloud during salah. - Loss of consciousness or sleep. - Insanity.

    Additional breaker specific to tayammum: - Sighting water one is able to use — tayammum is immediately invalidated. If water is seen but is inaccessible (e.g., at the bottom of a dangerous ravine, surrounded by crocodiles, or a full day's journey away), the tayammum remains intact.

    Tayammum: Salah Already Performed vs. Mid-Salah

    Scenario Ruling
    Sees water during salah Both tayammum and salah are invalidated; must redo both.
    Sees water after completing salah (after salam) Tayammum invalidated; wudu must be renewed — but the completed salah stands and need not be repeated.

    Tayammum for Janabah vs. Hadath

    • Ahnaf ruling: The method of tayammum is identical regardless of whether one is purifying from major (janabah) or minor (hadath) impurity — two strikes, wipe face then hands. The body is not wiped entirely.

    Lessons & Practical Application

    Tayammum as a Divine Gift to This Ummah

    • Tayammum is exclusive to the Ummah of the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ — no previous nation was given this concession.
    • Hadith (Bukhari): "The entire earth has been made a masjid for me and a means of purification." — The earth serves a dual function: a place of prayer and an instrument of purification.

    Historical Origin of Tayammum

    • The ayah of tayammum (Surah al-Ma'idah) was revealed during Ghazwah Zat al-Riqa' (dominant opinion; Imam al-Kasani, author of Bada'i' al-Sana'i, supports this).
    • Imam al-Bukhari placed it after Ghazwah Khaybar.
    • The incident: Aisha (RA)'s necklace (belonging to Asma) went missing; Sahabah stayed searching; water ran out by Fajr time — prompting the revelation.
    • The Sahabi Usaid ibn Hudayr (RA) said to Aisha (RA) after the revelation: "May Allah have mercy on you — never were you beset with a difficulty but Allah made a way out for you and for the Muslims."

    The Role of Aisha (RA) in Fiqh

    • Numerous masail in Islamic law were clarified through Aisha (RA). A dedicated book exists compiling the fiqhi masail she derived and resolved.
    • Even senior Sahabah such as Abu Bakr and Uthman (RA) would refer masail to her when they could not reach a conclusion.
    • She is considered a muftia among the Sahabah, and her verdicts carried the highest authority.

    Practical Scenarios for Modern Travelers

    • If the end of salah time is near and the next rest stop is too far to make wudu and still pray on time — tayammum is valid.
    • Dust on shelves or surfaces can suffice for tayammum if there is enough to visibly cover the face and hands; no exact quantity is stipulated.
    • Using rock or stone (as per Imam Abu Hanifa — the operative fatwa) is valid even if no loose soil is present.
    • For mashkuk (doubtful) water like donkey/mule su'r: perform both wudu and tayammum, then pray — order does not matter.

    Key Reference Works Mentioned

    • Bada'i' al-Sana'i' (Imam al-Kasani) — primary Hanafi reference; cited in modern fatawa.
    • Dur al-Mukhtar / Radd al-Muhtar — later Hanafi fatawa compilation.
    • Al-Mabsut, Jami' al-Saghir, Jami' al-Kabir (Imam Muhammad al-Shaybani) — Zahir al-Riwayah: the six core books of Hanafi fiqh.
    • Modern fatawa always trace back through these works to the Zahir al-Riwayah as ultimate authority.
