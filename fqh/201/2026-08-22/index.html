    Fiqh 201 - Study Notes

    Date: 2026-08-22

    Session Overview

    This session covered two major topics: (1) a brief completion of the previous session's discussion on types of ghusl and bodily fluids, and (2) an extended treatment of water (maa') — its categories, what renders it permissible or impermissible for tahara, the rules of stagnant versus flowing water, animals that die in water, and the concept of maa' musta'mal (used water). Primary text used is Imam Quduri's Mukhtasar, with supporting evidence from Qur'an, hadith, and the opinions of the four Imams.

    Key Themes

    1. Completing the categories of ghusl (fard, sunnah, wajib, mustahab) from the previous session
    2. The three bodily fluids (mani, madhi, wadi) and their effect on wudu and ghusl
    3. Sources of water permissible for tahara vs. impermissible sources
    4. The three qualities of water (color, taste, smell) and how their alteration affects permissibility
    5. Stagnant water (maa' raakin): small body vs. large body and the effect of najasa
    6. Flowing water (maa' jaar) and its ruling when najasa enters it
    7. Animals without blood dying in water
    8. Maa' musta'mal — its definition, categories, and what may or may not be done with it

    Fiqh Rulings & Evidence

    1. Completing Previous Session — Categories of Ghusl

    Category Ruling Examples
    Fard Obligatory Janaba, menstruation, post-natal bleeding
    Sunnah/Masnoon Sunnah Jumu'ah, Eid al-Fitr, Eid al-Adha
    Wajib Necessary Person who gave ghusl to the deceased (mayyat) must make ghusl for themselves — because droplets fall on the body and one does not know the condition of the deceased
    Mustahab Recommended (1) When someone accepts Islam; (2) When a child reaches adulthood; (3) When an insane person regains sanity

    Note: The Wajib and Mustahab categories are not explicitly in Imam Quduri's text; they were added supplementally in class.

    2. The Three Bodily Fluids

    Fluid Description Effect on Wudu Effect on Ghusl
    Mani Seminal discharge with shahwa (desire) Breaks wudu Ghusl becomes obligatory
    Madhi Pre-seminal fluid during foreplay Breaks wudu Ghusl NOT required — wash and make wudu
    Wadi Slightly thicker fluid sometimes seen after urination Breaks wudu Ghusl NOT required

    Ruling on wadi: If wudu was already performed and wadi appears afterward, wudu must be repeated. Also related: if urine drops appear on clothing, check the size — if larger than roughly a quarter of a coin, wudu is required; if less, it is excused. (Detailed rulings on this topic are in a forthcoming chapter.)

    3. Water Permissible for Tahara

    Imam Quduri states: "Tahara from hadath is permissible with water from the sky, valleys (awdiya), springs (uyun), wells (abar), and seas (bihar)."

    Dalil: Qur'an, Surah al-Furqan (25:48) — Allah sent down purifying water (maa'an tahura) from the heavens. The origin of all these sources ultimately traces back to rainwater.

    4. Water IMPERMISSIBLE for Tahara

    Source Reason
    Juice/extract squeezed from fruits or trees (e.g., maple syrup, carrot juice) No longer called "water"; qualities of water (color, taste, smell) have changed
    Water in which another substance has dominated (ghalaba) — e.g., vinegar, rose water, soup, Gatorade, Rooh Afza, saffron water The name and qualities have changed — it is no longer maa'

    Key Principle: Water has three qualities — color, taste, and smell. If all three (or two, per Imam Quduri's text) are altered by a foreign substance, the water cannot be used for tahara.

    Hadith Dalil (Ibn Majah): "Water is not made impure by anything except that which changes its smell, taste, and color."

    Exception (Sahib al-Hidaya's view): If juice has not been forcibly extracted but has naturally seeped from a fruit, and the consistency still resembles water (not pulp), it may be permissible to perform wudu with it.

    5. Water Permissible Despite Additives (One Quality Changed)

    If only one of the three qualities is altered, tahara remains permissible. Imam Quduri mentions: - Ishnan (cleansing herb/grass) - Sabun (soap — as used in that era) - Zafran (saffron)

    Imam Shafi'i's objection: If saffron is mixed, the water is now called "saffron water," not water — so wudu should not be performed.

    Ahnaf's response: Just as water stored in a well is called "well water" but retains its tahur status, calling it "saffron water" does not strip it of that status.

    Dalil (Hadith in Sahih Muslim): When washing a deceased person, ishnan and other cleansing substances should be added to the water. If this invalidated the water, the ghusl of the deceased would be invalid — yet the hadith commands this practice, confirming these additives do not negate the water's purity.

    Practical modern analogy: Flood water — even though color, taste, and smell may all be affected by soil and debris, the fuqaha permit tahara from it because it is a large, natural body of water. Similarly, autumn lake water with fallen leaves remains permissible.

    6. Stagnant Water (Maa' Raakin) — Small Body

    Ruling: If any amount of najasa falls into a small, stagnant body of water, the water becomes impure and may not be used for wudu or ghusl.

    Dalil: The Prophet ﷺ commanded that stagnant water be protected. He forbade: - Urinating into or near stagnant water (such that splashes could enter) - Performing ghusl al-janaba in or near it

    Also established: A person waking from sleep should not put their hands directly into a larger vessel of water before washing them three times, because they do not know what their hands did during the night. (This ruling is specific to waking from sleep; during the day, one washes hands as part of wudu normally.)

    Question (addressed in class): Does washing hands three times at the start of wudu satisfy this sunnah? — Yes, if a person wakes up and then uses the restroom before wudu, the three-time hand wash at the start of wudu suffices. However, to specifically fulfill the sunnah of the Prophet ﷺ, one should wash hands three times before placing them into any water vessel.

    7. Stagnant Water — Imam Malik's Opinion

    Imam Malik (Rahimullah): If najasa falls into a small stagnant body and is so small that none of the three qualities (color, taste, smell) of the water change, the water remains clean.

    His dalil (Tirmidhi): Someone asked the Prophet ﷺ about Bi'r Budha'a — a well in Medina into which menstrual rags, dog flesh, and refuse were thrown. The Prophet ﷺ replied: "Indeed, water is pure; nothing makes it impure."

    Ahnaf's response to this hadith: Bi'r Budha'a had an underground stream replenishing it — it was effectively flowing water, not truly stagnant. Flowing replenishment changes the ruling. This does not override the general principle for genuinely stagnant small bodies.

    8. Defining "Small" vs. "Large" Body of Stagnant Water

    School Definition of Small Body Definition of Large Body
    Ahnaf (opinion of Imam Muhammad, adopted by the madhhab) Less than 10 hand-spans × 10 hand-spans (roughly 10 ft × 10 ft) 10 × 10 hand-spans or more, with enough depth that the bottom is not exposed when water is scooped
    Imam Shafi'i Less than two large jars/jugs of water Two jars or more

    Ahnaf's large body test (ripple test): If movement (e.g., someone taking a ghusl) on one side does not immediately cause perceptible movement on the other side, it is a large body. - Imam Abu Hanifa + Imam Abu Yusuf (Shaykhain): the benchmark action is a full ghusl on one side - Imam Muhammad: the benchmark is scooping water with the hands on one side

    Ruling for a large stagnant body: Najasa falling in does not automatically render it impure — it only becomes impure if one of the three qualities (color, taste, smell) is visibly changed. This is why the large hawd (water tanks) in South Asian masajid are ruled permissible even when many people perform wudu from them.

    9. Flowing Water (Maa' Jaar)

    Ruling: Najasa falling into flowing water does not render it impure, as long as no athar (trace/effect) of the najasa remains.

    Reason (Imam Quduri): "The najasa cannot settle or remain because of the flow of the water" — it disperses and is carried away.

    Three definitions of "flowing water" in Shariah: 1. Orf (local custom) — if the people of that locality consider it flowing water, it is treated as such 2. If dry leaves or grass stems placed on the surface flow away with the current 3. If you scoop water and immediately a new quantity fills your hand from upstream — it is flowing

    10. Animals Dying in Water

    Ruling (Ahnaf and one opinion of Imam Shafi'i): If an animal that has no flowing blood dies in water, the water does NOT become impure.

    Examples: Fish, frogs, crabs, mosquitoes, flies, scorpions, ticks (zanaabir, aqarib)

    Dalil: Qur'an, Surah al-An'am — flowing blood is najis. Therefore, if an animal has no flowing blood to begin with, its death in water does not introduce najasa.

    Extension of this principle: If a fly or mosquito falls into food or drink, it can be removed and the food/drink remains permissible. (Dalil: the famous hadith about dipping the fly fully and then drinking.)

    Imam Shafi'i's nuance: Fish — agrees with Ahnaf. Other water-dwelling animals — does not fully agree.

    If an animal WITH blood dies in stagnant water: Rulings differ based on the size of the body of water and whether the carcass decomposes (if it decomposes in a small body, the entire body of water is rendered impure). Details forthcoming in a dedicated chapter.

    11. Maa' Musta'mal (Used Water)

    Definition: Water that has been used to remove a hadath (ritual impurity), whether: - To perform a new wudu (after hadath occurred), OR - To perform wudu over wudu (i.e., renewing wudu when existing wudu was intact)

    When does water become musta'mal? — All four madhhabs agree: the moment the water leaves the body (i.e., drips off), it becomes maa' musta'mal. While still on the body, it retains its tahur status.

    Opinions on the Status of Maa' Musta'mal:

    Scholar Status
    Imam Abu Yusuf (Rahimullah) Najasat-e-Khafifa (minor impurity)
    One opinion of Imam Abu Hanifa (Rahimullah) Najasat-e-Ghaliza (major impurity)
    Imam Muhammad (Rahimullah) Still Tahir (pure), but not Mutahhar
    Muftabi (operative fatwa) — Ahnaf Tahir but not Mutahhar (in line with Imam Abu Hanifa's other opinion and Imam Muhammad)

    This is also one of the opinions of Imam Malik, Imam Shafi'i, and Imam Ahmad (Rahimahumullah).

    What can Maa' Musta'mal be used for?

    Use Permissibility
    Cleaning after using the restroom (istinja) Permitted
    Irrigation, cooking, general use Permitted
    Making wudu NOT permitted
    Making ghusl NOT permitted

    Dalil (two proofs): 1. The Prophet ﷺ used to give leftover wudu water to the sick for baraka and cure. If it were najis, he would not have given it to others. 2. Leftover wudu water splashed onto the bodies and clothes of Sahaba, yet no narration exists of them changing their clothes before prayer because of this — confirming the water is tahir in itself.

    Conditions & Categories

    Summary: Types of Water and Their Rulings

    Water Type Tahara Permissible?
    Rainwater, valley water, springs, wells, oceans Yes — tahur
    Squeezed fruit juice / tree extracts No
    Water in which one quality has changed due to additives (soap, ishnan, saffron) Yes
    Water in which two or three qualities changed (vinegar, rose water, soup, Gatorade) No
    Small stagnant body (< 10×10 spans) with najasa in it No
    Large stagnant body (≥ 10×10 spans) with najasa in it, qualities unchanged Yes
    Flowing water with najasa, no visible trace/effect remaining Yes
    Maa' musta'mal No (wudu/ghusl); Yes (other cleaning)
    Water in which a bloodless animal dies Yes
    Water in which a blooded animal dies Depends on body size and decomposition

    Lessons & Practical Application

    1. Know your water source: Islam places enormous emphasis on the purity of water used for tahara. Understanding the categories of water equips a Muslim to make correct decisions in any environment.

    2. The three qualities test: When in doubt about water's suitability for wudu, check color, taste, and smell. If one is altered by a permissible additive (soap, ishnan), you may proceed. If two or more are altered, do not use it for tahara.

    3. Flood and outdoor situations: Even flood water or autumn lake water with leaves and discoloration is generally permissible for tahara due to the large volume and natural origin — a relevant ruling for disaster situations.

    4. Stagnant water near najasa: If someone urinates near a small pond or visible najasa has entered it, do not make wudu from it, regardless of how small the najasa appeared. The ruling for ma-e-kalil is strict.

    5. Washing hands before submerging in a shared vessel (after sleep): When waking, do not plunge hands into a shared water container before washing them three times. This sunnah is fulfilled if you wash hands three times at the start of wudu after using the restroom.

    6. Conservation of water: Even in wudu, water should not be wasted. The Prophet ﷺ was described as making wudu conservatively even when standing in a river. Excessive water use is israf (extravagance) and is impermissible.

    7. Donating leftover wudu water: The Prophet ﷺ gave his leftover wudu water for healing — a beautiful sunnah that also demonstrates the tahir status of maa' musta'mal.

    8. Large hawd (water tank) in masajid: The collective ruling of South Asian scholars is that large hawd tanks used for communal wudu are permissible, as they meet the criteria of ma-e-kasir. This is a real-world application of the 10×10 rule.

    9. Coming next session (Wednesday): The topic of tanning (dabagha) — the ruling on the skin of a dead animal after it has been tanned, and whether it may be used.
