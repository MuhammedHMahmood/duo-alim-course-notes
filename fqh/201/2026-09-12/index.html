    Fiqh 201 - Study Notes

    Date: 2026-09-12

    Session Overview

    This session completed the discussion of nifas (post-natal bleeding), concluding the three major conditions of women's bleeding (haiz, istihada, nifas). The teacher explained the rules of nifas duration, the edge cases of no bleeding and twin births, and provided an extended discussion on why the opinions of the three Hanafi imams carry different weight in different legal domains. The session then opened the new chapter — Babu'l-Anjas (Chapter on Impurities) — covering the two categories of najasa and the rules for purifying physical impurities from the body, clothing, prayer space, and various surfaces.

    Key Themes

    1. Nifas defined and distinguished from istihada — Post-natal bleeding has no minimum but a maximum of 40 days; anything exceeding this reverts to the rulings of istihada.
    2. The adat (habit) principle in nifas — A woman's previous bleeding pattern governs when istihada begins; without a consistent habit, the full 40-day maximum applies.
    3. Twins and the onset of nifas — A legal disagreement between Hanafi imams on whether nifas is counted from the birth of the first or second child.
    4. Scholarly weight by legal domain — Imam Abu Hanifa's opinion governs ibadah; Imam Abu Yusuf's governs judicial matters; Imam Muhammad's governs inheritance.
    5. Two types of najasa — Najasat-e-hukmi (ritual impurity requiring wudu/ghusl) vs. najasat-e-hakiki (physical filth on body, clothing, or place).
    6. Methods of purification — What liquids can purify najasa, and how various surfaces (leather, hard, ground) are treated differently.

    Important Vocabulary

    Arabic Term Meaning
    نِفَاس (Nifas) Post-natal bleeding; the blood a woman sees after childbirth
    إِسْتِحَاضَة (Istihada) Irregular/dysfunctional uterine bleeding not tied to menstruation or childbirth
    حَيْض (Haiz) Menstruation; the regular monthly cycle of bleeding
    عَادَة (Adat) Habit/custom; a woman's established pattern of bleeding duration from previous cycles
    الشَّيْخَان (Al-Shaykhain) "The two shaykhs" — refers to Imam Abu Hanifa and Imam Abu Yusuf
    نَجَاسَةٌ حُكْمِيَّة (Najasat-e-Hukmi) Ritual/legal impurity requiring wudu or ghusl (e.g., passing gas, discharge)
    نَجَاسَةٌ حَقِيقِيَّة (Najasat-e-Hakiki) Physical/actual impurity — blood, urine, feces (also called khabath/خبث)
    الأَنْجَاس (Al-Anjas) Plural of najis; impurities — title of the new chapter
    طَهَارَة (Tahara) Purity; the state of ritual and physical cleanliness
    مَاء مُطْلَق (Ma' Mutlaq) Absolute/pure water; unrestricted in use for purification
    خُفّ (Khuff) Leather sock/slipper worn over the foot
    مَنِيّ (Mani) Sperm/seminal fluid
    جِرْم (Jirm) Physical body/substance — najasa with jirm has a tangible residue
    مَرْفُوع (Marfu') A hadith traced directly back to the Prophet ﷺ
    مَوْقُوف (Mawquf) A hadith whose chain stops at a Companion, not the Prophet ﷺ
    تَيَمُّم (Tayammum) Dry ablution using clean earth as a substitute for wudu or ghusl
    ظَاهِر الرِّوَايَة (Zahir al-Riwaya) The six foundational books of Hanafi fiqh compiled by Imam Muhammad
    إِجْمَاع (Ijma') Scholarly consensus
    قِيَاس (Qiyas) Analogical reasoning
    سَتْر (Satr) Covering the `awrah (private parts) — a condition of salah
    مُفْتَى بِهِ (Muftabih) The opinion on which a fatwa is issued; the operative legal ruling

    Fiqh Rulings & Evidence

    Nifas

    Definition: Nifas is the blood that exits after a child is born. - Bleeding before childbirth (even if very close to delivery) = istihada, not nifas. - Ruling of istihada: no ghusl required; fresh wudu required for each obligatory prayer.

    Duration: - Minimum: No minimum — even zero days is possible if no bleeding occurs. - Maximum: 40 days. - Any bleeding beyond 40 days is treated as istihada.

    Evidence (maximum of 40 days): Based on the transmitted practice (athar) of the Companions and scholarly consensus recorded in the Hanafi texts; Imam Sahib states: وَمَا زَادَ عَلَى ذَلِكَ فَهُوَ إِسْتِحَاضَةٌ.

    When bleeding exceeds 40 days — applying the adat: - If the woman has given birth before and has an established adat (e.g., 35 days last time): revert to that adat. Days within the adat = nifas; any days beyond 40 = istihada. - Example: Adat is 35 days; this time she bleeds 43 days → 35 days nifas, then 8 days istihada. - If she has no consistent adat (e.g., first birth, or every delivery had a different duration), and bleeding exceeds 40 days: the full 40 days = nifas; everything beyond = istihada. - Example: First birth 35 days, second birth 39 days, third birth 43 days → 40 days nifas, 3 days istihada (no consistent adat to rely on).

    If there is no bleeding at all: - Nifas does not occur; ghusl is technically not obligatory (since ghusl is triggered by haiz or nifas). - Precautionary position of some fuqaha: she should take a ghusl out of caution.

    Babu'l-Anjas — Chapter on Impurities

    The three conditions of purity for salah (from this chapter): 1. The body of the musalli must be free of najasa. 2. The clothing must be free of najasa. 3. The place of prayer must be free of najasa. (A fourth condition — covering the satr — will be covered under Kitab al-Salah.)

    Removing najasa — what liquids can be used:

    Opinion Ruling Dalil
    Imam Abu Hanifa & Imam Abu Yusuf Water or any flowing liquid (vinegar, rose water) that removes the najasa is permissible Hadith of Aisha (RA) in Bukhari: she used saliva and her nails to remove menstrual blood from cloth
    Imam Muhammad, Imam Malik, Imam Shafi Only water (ma' mutlaq) is permissible for purification Hadith of Asma bint Abi Bakr (RA) in Bukhari

    Hanafi reasoning: the objective is removal of the najasa. Any liquid capable of achieving this fulfills the requirement, since tahara is achieved when the filth is gone.

    Leather sock (khuff) soiled with najasa having jirm (physical body): - If the najasa dried on the khuff: rubbing it firmly on the ground until all residue is removed is sufficient; salah is then permissible. - Dalil (Abu Hanifa & Abu Yusuf): Hadith in Abu Dawud permitting rubbing the khuff on the ground. - Imam Muhammad's opinion: Washing is still required, because leather may absorb a small quantity of the najasa.

    Mani (sperm) — Hanafi ruling: - Mani is najis according to Ahnaf (regardless of what other madhabs say). - If wet: must be washed from cloth or body. - If dry: rubbing/scratching to fully remove all residue is sufficient; washing is not required. - Dalil: Hadith in both Bukhari and Muslim.

    Najasa on a hard, non-absorbent surface (mirror, sword, glass screen): - Wiping with a wet cloth is sufficient; no flowing water required. - Condition: the surface must be genuinely non-absorbent (liquid cannot be drawn into it).

    Najasa on the ground: - If two conditions are met: (1) the earth fully absorbs the najasa, AND (2) the sun completely dries it with no trace remaining — then: - Salah is permissible on that spot. - Tayammum is also permissible from that soil. - Imam Shafi's opinion (one position): Salah is not permissible on that ground (at least in the near term). Note: Imam Shafi had two phases of opinions (pre- and post-Egypt); some of his later opinions align more closely with the Hanafi position.

    Conditions & Categories

    Conditions for Nifas to Apply

    1. A child must actually be born (delivery completed).
    2. Bleeding must occur after the birth.
    3. Bleeding must not exceed 40 days (if it does, the excess is istihada).

    Twin Births — When Does Nifas Begin?

    Opinion Starting Point of Nifas
    Imam Abu Hanifa & Imam Abu Yusuf (Shaykhain) After the birth of the first child
    Imam Muhammad & Imam Zufar (also Imam Malik, Imam Shafi, Imam Ghazali) After the birth of the second child

    Practical implication when births are months apart: - Per Shaykhain: Nifas begins with the first birth (up to 40 days); bleeding between the two births after day 40 is istihada; a new nifas period does NOT restart for the second birth. - Per Imam Muhammad et al.: The woman is not in nifas until the second child is born; all bleeding during the gap and for 40 days after the second birth counts as nifas.

    Muftabih fatwa: On the opinion of the Shaykhain (Imam Abu Hanifa and Imam Abu Yusuf) — to be confirmed.

    Two Types of Najasa

    Type Definition Remedy
    Najasat-e-Hukmi Ritual impurity (hadas) — requires wudu or ghusl; no visible filth involved Wudu or ghusl depending on the type
    Najasat-e-Hakiki (Khabath) Physical filth: blood, urine, feces, mani Washing, wiping, rubbing, or absorption by earth depending on context

    Scholarly Opinions

    Why Different Hanafi Imams' Opinions Are Weighted Differently by Domain

    The teacher gave an extended explanation of the historical basis for this principle:

    Imam Abu Hanifa (d. 150 AH): - Formed a shura of 40 top scholars — experts in hadith and Arabic language — to debate and agree on every ruling before it was issued. - He explicitly stated that if his opinion contradicts the Quran or Sunnah, it should be rejected. - His mastery of the core ibadat and usula gives his opinions primary authority in acts of worship. - At his time, there were over 300 potential madhabs; only four endured.

    Imam Abu Yusuf (d. 182 AH): - The closest and most senior student of Imam Abu Hanifa. - Served as Qadi al-Qudat (Chief Judge) for approximately 12 years over the vast Abbasid caliphate — directly presiding over countless judicial cases. - Had the "best of both worlds": deep fiqhi knowledge and extensive real-world judicial experience. - His opinion carries the most weight in judicial/court matters.

    Imam Muhammad al-Shaybani (d. 189 AH): - Devoted extensive study to ilm al-miras (Islamic inheritance law), regarded as half the knowledge of the entire deen. - He compiled the Zahir al-Riwaya (the six foundational Hanafi texts), preserving the opinions of Imam Abu Hanifa and Imam Abu Yusuf together with his own reasoning. - His opinion carries the most weight in miras (inheritance) matters.

    Legal Domain Preferred Opinion
    Ibadah (acts of worship) Imam Abu Hanifa
    Judicial / court matters Imam Abu Yusuf
    Miras (inheritance law) Imam Muhammad

    Note on issuing fatwa: A mufti must consult the books of Zahir al-Riwaya and Nadir al-Riwaya. The opinion of the three Hanafi mashayikh is given preference unless a subsequent ijma' of muftis of a later era has modified it — in which case the newer position is adopted, still anchored to the framework of the original Zahir al-Riwaya.

    On Mani — Scholarly Disagreement

    Scholar Position Dalil
    Ahnaf (all three Hanafi imams) Mani is najis; must be washed if wet, rubbed off if dry Hadith in Bukhari and Muslim
    Imam Malik Mani is najis; rubbing off when dry is sufficient Same hadith tradition
    Imam Shafi Mani is pure (tahir); no washing required at all Hadith attributed to Ibn Abbas in Dar al-Qutni

    Imam Bayhaqi's rebuttal of Imam Shafi's dalil: the hadith of Ibn Abbas cited is mawquf (stops at the Companion), not marfu' (traced to the Prophet ﷺ), and thus cannot serve as a basis for a ruling of this nature.

    Historical Context: Origins of Ikhtilaf

    The teacher traced the root causes of scholarly disagreement back to: - The Prophet ﷺ performing the same action at different times (e.g., fajr at the earliest and latest permissible times), observed by different Companions. - Sahaba dispersing to Misr, Sham, Kufa, and other regions after the expansion under Umar (RA), carrying the rulings they had personally witnessed. - The transfer of the caliphate from Madina to Kufa under Ali (RA) concentrated many senior Companions (kibar al-sahaba) in Iraq, which is the environment from which Hanafi fiqh primarily descends — through Abdullah ibn Mas'ud (RA).

    Lessons & Practical Application

    1. Tracking bleeding duration matters. Women (and their families or guides) should keep note of their bleeding durations across pregnancies to know their adat for future reference, which determines when istihada rulings apply.

    2. No bleeding does not mean skipping ghusl altogether. If no nifas occurs, ghusl is technically not obligatory, but it is recommended precautionarily. Women should not assume they can simply resume worship without any purification after delivery.

    3. Liquid vs. water for cleaning najasa — a practical distinction. In the Hanafi school, vinegar or rose water on a cloth or surface removes the obligation if the najasa is gone. However, water remains the safer and preferred choice given the disagreement among imams.

    4. Non-absorbent surfaces (phone screens, mirrors, glass): A simple wipe with a wet cloth is legally sufficient to remove najasa. No running water or full washing is needed.

    5. Walking on najasa with leather socks: If the najasa dries and is fully removed by rubbing on the ground (leaving no residue), salah is valid — a relevant ruling for those who wear leather socks.

    6. Ground purified by sun and absorption: An outdoor area that was soiled (e.g., an animal urinated there) and has since been fully absorbed and dried by the sun is legally pure — salah and even tayammum are permissible there.

    7. When seeking a fatwa, domain matters. A student of Islamic sciences should know which imam's opinion is operative for the area of fiqh in question — do not automatically apply the same imam's opinion across all domains.

    8. The Sahaba's zuhd (asceticism) as a lesson: The teacher reflected on the hadith of Aisha (RA) — having only one cloth and using saliva and nails to clean it — as a reminder of how little the early Muslims had and how much they focused on the akhira rather than accumulating dunya. This stands in contrast to us today, who have abundance yet remain anxious about provision.

    9. Two pipelines of knowledge: The session closed the chapter of Kitab al-Tahara. Kitab al-Salah begins next session (Wednesday). Students should review the full nifas and anjas discussion as these topics connect directly to the conditions of salah.
