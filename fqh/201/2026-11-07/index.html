    Fiqh 201 - Study Notes

    Date: 2026-11-07

    Session Overview

    This session continued the chapter on Salatul Jumu'ah, covering who is authorized to lead it, the conditions of the khutbah, the jamaat requirement, categories of people exempt from Jumu'ah, rulings for latecomers, and etiquette during the khutbah. The session then transitioned into a new chapter — Babu Salatul Eidain — covering the conditions, method, and mustahab actions for both Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha prayers, including the Takbirat al-Tashriq.

    Key Themes

    1. Authority and Leadership of Jumu'ah — Jumu'ah must be led by or authorized by the governing authority (sultan/hakim) to prevent conflict and disorder.
    2. Conditions (Shara'it) of Jumu'ah — Time (zuhr window), khutbah before salah, and a minimum jamaat.
    3. Who is Exempt from Jumu'ah — Six categories are identified; their attendance is valid but not obligatory.
    4. Jumu'ah vs. Zuhr — Key structural differences that make them two distinct prayers, not substitutes for one another.
    5. Latecomers and Missing Jumu'ah — Rulings on what to do when one arrives late or prays Zuhr before Jumu'ah.
    6. Etiquette During Khutbah — Obligation to remain silent and attentive; no salah or unnecessary speech.
    7. Two Adhans of Jumu'ah — Historical context and their separate purposes.
    8. Salatul Eidain — Method of Eid prayer (extra takbirat), time window, mustahab actions, and differences between Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha.
    9. Takbirat al-Tashriq — Start and end dates according to different positions within the Hanafi school.

    Fiqh Rulings & Evidence

    Who Leads Jumu'ah

    • Jumu'ah must be led by the sultan (ruler), or someone the sultan/authority has delegated.
    • Reason: In large congregations with diverse madhabs, without centralized appointment there would be conflict over who leads. The authority prevents disputes.
    • In our times: The masjid's managing/khutbah committee serves this function — their appointment carries the same weight.

    Time of Jumu'ah

    • Jumu'ah is only valid within the time of Zuhr (after zawal until before Asr begins).
    • Dalil: Hadith — when the Prophet ﷺ sent Mus'ab ibn 'Umayr رضي الله عنه to Madinah, he instructed him to perform Jumu'ah only after the sun begins to decline from the meridian.
    • Shafi'i position (for context): They permit Jumu'ah before Zuhr time enters. The Hanafi position rejects this based on the above hadith.
    • If the Zuhr time expires while the imam is in the Jumu'ah salah, the Jumu'ah becomes batil (void). The congregation must stop and begin Zuhr salah from the beginning — not continue with Jumu'ah.
    • Why not continue? Jumu'ah and Zuhr are structurally different (raka'at count, jehri vs. sirri qira'ah, khutbah requirement). They cannot be converted into each other mid-salah.
    • Why do Zuhr and not Qada of Jumu'ah? There is no qada for Jumu'ah (like Salatul Eidain). The only "make-up" for a missed Jumu'ah is Zuhr Salah.

    The Khutbah

    • The khutbah is a shart (condition) for Jumu'ah — not an arkan (integral). As a shart, it must be completed before the salah.
    • Dalil: The Prophet ﷺ never prayed Jumu'ah without a khutbah — his consistent practice is evidence of its necessity.
    • Why before the salah? Sharaa'it (conditions) precede the act, just as tahara precedes salah itself.
    • The khutbah cannot begin before zawal — since Jumu'ah's time begins at zawal, its conditions (including khutbah) also cannot precede it.
    • Dalil for khutbah being before salah: Hadith of Sahib ibn Yazid رضي الله عنه — at the time of the Prophet ﷺ, Abu Bakr رضي الله عنه, and Umar رضي الله عنه, the second adhan was given only when the khatib sat on the minbar, establishing the sequence: khutbah first, then salah.

    Content Requirements of the Khutbah

    The khatib should include: 1. Praise of Allah (Hamd) 2. Salutations upon the Prophet ﷺ (Salawat) 3. Admonition and advice for the Muslims

    Minimum Length of Khutbah

    • Imam Abu Hanifa رحمه الله: Even if the imam merely says SubhanAllah, Alhamdulillah (simple dhikr) while standing, it is permissible (ja'iz) — though not ideal.
    • Sahibain رحمهما الله: It must be long enough to be called a "khutbah" (an address) — no specific word count, but must qualify as an address.

    Standing and Wudu During Khutbah

    • The imam should give the khutbah standing and in a state of wudu.
    • If he gives it without wudu or seated — it is makruh, but the khutbah is not invalidated.

    Sitting Between the Two Khutbahs

    • The imam must give two khutbahs with a sitting (qa'dah) between them.
    • Duration of the sitting: Equivalent to reciting three short ayat (e.g., Surah Al-Kawthar or Surah Al-'Asr).
    • Hanafi position: The sitting is sunnah and recommended (not a condition) — if omitted, the khutbah is not void.
    • Shafi'i position: The sitting is a condition (shart) of the khutbah.
    • Dalil for Hanafi position: Hadith of Jabir ibn Samurah رضي الله عنه — the Prophet ﷺ used to give a single khutbah in his earlier years; in old age he gave two and sat between them. This shows the sitting was due to his old age, not a formal condition. It became the ijma' of the Sahaba and Tabi'in, so the Ahnaf follow it as a strong sunnah.

    Jamaat as a Condition

    • Jama'at is a condition (shart) for Jumu'ah.
    • Imam Abu Hanifa and Imam Muhammad (Tarafain): Minimum three people besides the imam (total four).
    • Imam Abu Yusuf: Two people besides the imam (total three) is sufficient. His reasoning: the word "Jumu'ah" is from ijtima' (gathering), and two people constitute ijtima' of one with another.
    • Muftabi opinion: With Imam Abu Hanifa and Imam Muhammad — minimum three besides the imam (four total).
    • The three (or more) must be male — women do not fulfill this requirement for Jumu'ah.
    • These men do not have to be from those upon whom Jumu'ah is wajib — any male can count.

    Qira'ah in Jumu'ah Salah

    • The imam recites aloud (jehri) in both raka'at.
    • There is no specific designated surah — the imam may read from any surah.

    Conditions & Categories

    Six Categories Exempt from Jumu'ah (Jumu'ah is Not Wajib Upon Them)

    1. المُسَافِر (Al-Musafir) — Traveler
    2. الْمَرْأَة (Al-Mar'ah) — Woman
    3. الْمَرِيض (Al-Marid) — Sick person
    4. الصَّبِيّ (As-Sabi) — Child (not yet baligh)
    5. الْعَبْد (Al-'Abd) — Slave
    6. الأَعْمَى (Al-A'ma) — Blind person

    Key rulings for these categories: - If any of them attend and pray Jumu'ah, their salah is accepted and valid — they do not need to repeat Zuhr. - A slave, musafir, or sick person may even lead (do imamat of) Jumu'ah — the salah of the imam and all those behind him is valid. - Exception: Women and children cannot lead Jumu'ah (women cannot lead men; children are not baligh).

    Makruh Action: Exempt Persons Praying Zuhr in Jama'at on Friday

    • It is makruh for ma'zhurun (exempt persons) to gather together on the day of Jumu'ah and form a jama'at to pray Zuhr.
    • Same ruling applies to prisoners — they should not gather on Friday specifically to perform Zuhr with jama'at.
    • Reason: Doing so defeats the purpose of Jumu'ah and resembles performing an action one is not permitted to do.

    Ruling for Someone Who Prayed Zuhr at Home Then Came to the Masjid

    A person prayed Zuhr at home, then came to the masjid to find Jumu'ah in progress:

    • Imam Abu Hanifa رحمه الله: The moment he begins walking toward the masjid with the intention of catching Jumu'ah, his prior Zuhr salah becomes batil.
    • Imam Abu Yusuf رحمه الله: His Zuhr only becomes batil when he actually joins the imam in the masjid. Whatever he prays with the imam is counted as his salah.

    Rulings for Latecomers to Jumu'ah

    The general principle: whatever portion of Jumu'ah a latecomer finds with the imam, he joins and builds (bina') his salah upon it as Jumu'ah.

    Disagreement on extreme lateness:

    Scenario Shaykhain (Abu Hanifa + Abu Yusuf) Imam Muhammad
    Finds Imam in Tashahud Join and do bina' of Jumu'ah Do bina' of Zuhr
    Finds Imam in Sajdat as-Sahw Join and do bina' of Jumu'ah Do bina' of Zuhr
    Finds most of the 2nd raka' Join and do bina' of Jumu'ah Join and do bina' of Jumu'ah
    Finds less than most of 2nd raka' Join and do bina' of Jumu'ah Do bina' of Zuhr
    • Muftabi opinion: With the Shaykhain — the latecomer joins regardless of how late, and builds Jumu'ah. This avoids confusion over niyyah differences between Zuhr and Jumu'ah.

    Two Adhans of Jumu'ah — History and Rulings

    Adhan When Purpose
    Adhan al-Awwal (1st) At Zawal (start of Zuhr time) Signal for people to stop trading and begin walking to the masjid
    Adhan ath-Thani (2nd) When imam sits on the minbar Signal that khutbah is about to begin
    • Historical context: At the time of the Prophet ﷺ, Abu Bakr رضي الله عنه, and Umar رضي الله عنه — only one adhan was called (what is now the 2nd). People would already be in the masjid.
    • Usman رضي الله عنه added the first adhan as the city of Madinah grew and people became occupied in marketplaces — to give them advance warning.
    • When must trading stop?
    • Allama Sarakhsi and Imam Tahawi: Only at the second adhan (when the imam is at the minbar).
    • Correct Hanafi position: Trading must stop at the first adhan (at zawal). Waiting for the second adhan is too late — the imam is already about to begin the khutbah.
    • Dalil: Quranic ayah in Surah Al-Jumu'ah — "when the adhan is called, hasten to the remembrance of Allah and leave off trading."

    Silence and Salah During the Khutbah

    Action Imam Abu Hanifa Sahibain
    Talking as imam walks to minbar Prohibited Permitted (small/brief talk is okay before khutbah begins)
    Praying salah as imam walks Prohibited Prohibited (salah takes too long and will overlap with khutbah)
    Talking during khutbah Prohibited Prohibited
    Quiet dhikr during khutbah Permitted (barely) —
    • Why no salah? Listening to the khutbah is wajib. Praying nawafil — even though virtuous — is outweighed by the wajib obligation of listening.
    • Dalil for Imam Abu Hanifa: Hadith — "When the imam comes out for the khutbah, there is no talk or salah" — this hadith has no restriction to only the khutbah itself, so it covers the entire time from when the imam appears.
    • Unusual observation: Many people who normally skip Tahiyyatul Masjid will make a point to pray it at Jumu'ah time (especially in other madhabs). The Hanafi position based on this hadith is that one should not.

    Lessons & Practical Application

    Jumu'ah — Practical Notes

    1. The authority principle: Modern masjid committees serve the same function as the sultan in classical fiqh — their appointment of the imam/khatib is the valid authorization. This prevents disputes and disorder in the congregation.
    2. Time is critical: Jumu'ah cannot be "made up" (no qada). If the time expires during the salah, everyone must break and start Zuhr. For this reason, imams should not delay Jumu'ah to near-Asr time.
    3. Arriving late: Even arriving at the last moment of Jumu'ah (Imam in Tashahud or Sajdat as-Sahw) — per the Hanafi muftabi opinion — one should still join and build Jumu'ah. Do not make intention of Zuhr in this case.
    4. Praying Zuhr before Jumu'ah at home: Avoid this. If you then walk to the masjid with intention of catching Jumu'ah, your earlier Zuhr is void (Abu Hanifa) or becomes void when you join (Abu Yusuf). The Jumu'ah salah replaces it.
    5. COVID / small gatherings: A family of three or fewer males cannot perform Jumu'ah among themselves — a fourth male must be present. During lockdowns this was a genuine issue.
    6. Khutbah language: Speaking in English briefly before the Arabic khutbah begins is tolerated, but once the formal khutbah starts, silence is wajib.
    7. Khutbah at home / seated: Makruh, not batil — but one should always strive to follow the sunnah properly.
    8. The two adhans: Stop trading at the first adhan (zawal), not the second. Waiting for the second adhan is too late per the relied-upon Hanafi opinion.

    Salatul Eidain — 12 Mustahab Actions

    (Imam Khudur mentions four; full list from fiqh sources:)

    # Mustahab Action
    1 Eat something before leaving (Eid al-Fitr only — to show you are not fasting)
    2 Perform ghusl
    3 Wear your best clothes (new is not required, just the best you have)
    4 Apply perfume
    5 Use miswak
    6 Pay Sadaqatul Fitr before leaving (or even the night before / days before is better)
    7 Wear a turban (imamah) if possible
    8 Rise early
    9 Go to the musalla early
    10 Pray Fajr in your local masjid on that day
    11 Walk to the Eid musalla (rather than drive if possible)
    12 Go by one route and return by another

    Eid Salah — Method (Hanafi)

    • Total takbirat: 9 (Hanafi position based on the narration of Ibn Mas'ud رضي الله عنه)
    • 1st raka': Takbirat al-Ihram + 3 additional takbirat → then Qira'ah → then ruku takbir → complete raka'
    • 2nd raka': Qira'ah first → then 3 additional takbirat → then 4th takbir (going into ruku)
    • Hands are raised with every takbir in Eid salah (one of the seven positions where raf' al-yadain is prescribed in the Hanafi school)
    • Ibn Abbas's narration: 13 total takbirat (7 in first raka', 6 in second). Imam Shafi'i follows this narration — different arrangement.
    • After salah: two khutbahs (note: in Eid, khutbah is after salah — unlike Jumu'ah where it is before).
    • Eid al-Fitr khutbah topic: Sadaqatul Fitr and its rulings
    • Eid al-Adha khutbah topic: Udhiyyah (Qurbani) and Takbirat al-Tashriq

    Eid al-Fitr vs. Eid al-Adha — Key Differences

    Point Eid al-Fitr Eid al-Adha
    Eating before Eid salah Required (sunnah — to show you are not fasting) Delayed until after the salah
    Takbir while walking to musalla Quietly (Imam Abu Hanifa) / Aloud (Sahibain) Aloud (all positions agree)
    Make-up days if 1st day missed Day 1 and Day 2 only Day 1, Day 2, and Day 3
    Khutbah topic Sadaqatul Fitr Udhiyyah + Takbirat al-Tashriq

    Takbir al-Tashriq

    • Recited after every fard salah during the designated days.
    • Imam Abu Hanifa: From Fajr of 9th (Arafah) → Asr of 10th (Yawm an-Nahr) = effectively 2 days + a portion.
    • Sahibain (Muftabi opinion): From Fajr of 9th (Arafah) → Asr of 13th Dhul Hijjah = 5 days (the five days commonly observed).

    No Qada for Eid Salah

    • Just like Jumu'ah — there is no qada for Eid salah.
    • If the first and second days pass without valid prayer (and third day for Eid al-Adha), the opportunity is gone — no makeup is possible.
    • Moon sighting scenario: If the moon was not seen due to clouds, and witnesses came to the imam at or after zawal the next day, the imam should:
    • Accept their testimony
    • Announce that people should break their fast immediately (fasting on Eid is haram)
    • Eid salah will be prayed the following day
    • Dalil: Hadith — in Madinah, the Eid moon was sighted on the 29th but the sky was cloudy. At zawal the next day, some companions came to the Prophet ﷺ and testified they saw the moon. He accepted it, announced breaking the fast, and instructed salah the next day.

    Why Eid Khutbah is After Salah (Unlike Jumu'ah)

    • In Jumu'ah, the khutbah is a shart — it must precede the salah.
    • In Eid, the khutbah is after — the main event is the salah (celebration), and the khutbah is for education/instruction.
    • Eid is described as a gathering and celebration where Allah is the host — the ahkam are broader and more inclusive (everyone is encouraged to attend, unlike Jumu'ah which has specific exemptions).
    • No nawafil should be prayed before or after Eid salah.
    • Dalil: Hadith — the Prophet ﷺ, who loved salah most, never prayed any nawafil before or after Eid salah. Ibn Abbas رضي الله عنه narrated he led the people, then prayed nothing before or after.
