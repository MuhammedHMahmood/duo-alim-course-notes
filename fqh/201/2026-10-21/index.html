    Fiqh 201 - Study Notes

    Date: 2026-10-21

    Session Overview

    This session covered two main topics from Kitāb al-Salāh: (1) the ruling on praying nawāfil before the Maghrib farḍ salāh, and (2) the beginning of Bāb al-Nawāfil (Chapter on Voluntary Prayers), including the distinction between Sunnah and Nawāfil prayers, the prescribed Sunnah salāhs of the day, and the rules governing night and daytime voluntary prayers. The session included significant discussion on the differences of opinion between Imām Abū Ḥanīfah, his two companions (Ṣāḥibayn), and Imām al-Shāfiʿī, as well as practical guidance on duʿāʾ within ṣalāh.

    Key Themes

    1. Prohibition of nawāfil immediately before Maghrib — Aḥnāf hold that the Maghrib farḍ must be prayed immediately after the adhān without any preceding nawāfil, in contrast to Imām al-Shāfiʿī's permissibility.
    2. Sunnah vs. Nawāfil — definitional hierarchy — The luġhawī (linguistic) and sharʿī (legal) meanings of nawāfil are distinguished; sunnah is a subcategory carrying greater weight based on the Prophet's ﷺ punctuality.
    3. Prescribed Sunnah ṣalāhs and their units — Imām al-Qudūrī's enumeration of the daily Sunnah ṣalāhs, their quantities, and their classification as muʾakkadah or mustaḥab.
    4. Night vs. daytime nawāfil — maximum rakʿāt per salām — Aḥnāf allow up to four in the day (two or four); Imām Abū Ḥanīfah allows up to eight at night; Ṣāḥibayn restrict to two; Imām al-Shāfiʿī restricts to two at all times.
    5. Duʿāʾ within ṣalāh — When, where, and in what language duʿāʾ may be made; restrictions specific to farḍ vs. sunnah/nāfil ṣalāh.

    Fiqh Rulings & Evidence

    1. Praying Nawāfil Before the Maghrib Farḍ

    Ḥanafī ruling: It is not permitted to pray nawāfil between the Maghrib adhān and the Maghrib farḍ. The farḍ should be prayed immediately.

    Reason: Any ṣalāh before the Maghrib farḍ causes unnecessary delay of the farḍ, and the Maghrib farḍ should be offered as quickly as possible after its time enters.

    Dalīls: - Ḥadīth in Abū Dāwūd — narrated by Ibn ʿUmar رضي الله عنه: He was asked about praying two rakʿāt before Maghrib. He replied that he had not seen anyone praying them during the time of Rasūlullāh ﷺ (indicating the kibār al-Ṣaḥābah did not do so). - Ḥadīth in Dār al-Quṭnī — "Between every adhān and iqāmah two rakʿāt should be prayed, except for Maghrib." This explicitly excludes Maghrib from the pre-iqāmah sunnah pattern.

    Imām al-Shāfiʿī's ruling: It is permissible to pray two rakʿāt before the Maghrib farḍ.

    Shāfiʿī dalīl: Ḥadīth in Bukhārī — The Prophet ﷺ said three times: "Pray before the Maghrib (farḍ)," and on the third time added: "Whoever wants to may do so" — indicating permissibility without compulsion.

    Ḥanafī response to the Bukhārī ḥadīth: Even though the ḥadīth is authentic, Aḥnāf hold it is not makrūh for someone who does pray (because of this ḥadīth), but it is not sunnah either. The Prophet ﷺ himself added the qualifier "whoever wants to" precisely so people would not take it as a fixed tradition or sunnah.

    2. The Prescribed Daily Sunnah Ṣalāhs (as per Imām al-Qudūrī)

    As listed in Bāb al-Nawāfil (Imām Qudūrī begins with the Sunnah prayers under the broader nawāfil chapter because nawāfil is the larger umbrella term):

    Prayer Before/After Rakʿāt Classification
    Fajr (Ṣubḥ Ṣādiq) Before farḍ 2 Sunnah Muʾakkadah
    Ẓuhr Before farḍ 4 Sunnah Muʾakkadah
    Ẓuhr After farḍ 2 Sunnah Muʾakkadah
    ʿAṣr Before farḍ 4 (or 2) Mustaḥab
    Maghrib After farḍ 2 Sunnah Muʾakkadah
    ʿIshāʾ Before farḍ 4 Mustaḥab
    ʿIshāʾ After farḍ 4 (or 2) Option between both

    Important clarification: Even though Imām Qudūrī titles this chapter "Bāb al-Nawāfil," the prayers listed above are actually Sunnah ṣalāhs, not mere nawāfil. He uses "nawāfil" in its broader linguistic sense (additions beyond farḍ).

    Dalīl: Ḥadīth in Muslim (narrated by ʿĀʾishah رضي الله عنها): "Before the noon prayer he used to pray four rakʿāt in my house, then go out and lead the people in prayer, then return home and pray two rakʿāt. He would then lead the people in the Maghrib prayer, come in, and pray two rakʿāt. Then he would lead the ʿIshāʾ prayer and enter my house and pray two rakʿāt. He would pray nine rakʿāt during the night, including Witr."

    Dalīl for the 12 Sunnah Rakʿāt: Ḥadīth in Abū Dāwūd and Musnad Aḥmad: "Whoever prays these twelve rakʿāt during the day or night besides the farāʾiḍ, Allāh will build for him a house in Jannah." These are: 4 before Ẓuhr + 2 after + 2 after Maghrib + 2 after ʿIshāʾ + 2 before Fajr.

    3. The Missed Fajr Sunnah

    Imām Abū Ḥanīfah and Imām Abū Yūsuf: If the Fajr sunnah is missed (i.e., the Fajr farḍ has already been prayed), it cannot be made up at all — it is gone.

    Imām Muḥammad al-Shaybānī: It may be prayed after sunrise (when Ishrāq time begins) up until the Ẓuhr time — for that day only, not for previous days.

    Key principle: There is no formal qaḍāʾ for Sunnah ṣalāhs. If Imām Muḥammad's position is followed and one prays it, it is still done voluntarily out of devotion — not as a binding obligation.

    Context: The Prophet ﷺ himself, when he missed the Fajr ṣalāh entirely (due to the entire expedition party, including Bilāl, falling asleep), still prayed the Fajr sunnah before the farḍ — demonstrating its great importance.

    Practical note: A person who has not yet prayed the Fajr farḍ and hears the iqāmah may, according to some, begin the sunnah if they are confident of catching the jamāʿah — but cannot start a new ṣalāh once the farḍ has begun.

    4. Daytime Nawāfil — Maximum Rakʿāt

    All Ḥanafī imāms agree: During the daytime (nahar), nawāfil may be prayed in sets of two or four rakʿāt. Going beyond four rakʿāt in a single ṣalāh (with one niyyah and one salām) is makrūh.

    Nahar extends from sunrise to the beginning of ʿAṣr time. (Between ʿAṣr and Maghrib, no nawāfil are prayed; they resume after Maghrib.)

    5. Night Nawāfil — Maximum Rakʿāt (Difference of Opinion)

    Imām Abū Ḥanīfah: During the night, one may pray up to eight rakʿāt with a single salām and single niyyah; going beyond eight is makrūh.

    Dalīl: Ḥadīth in Muslim (narrated by ʿĀʾishah رضي الله عنها): "Rasūlullāh ﷺ used to observe thirteen rakʿāt for the night prayers. Five of them consisted of Witr and he did not sit (for tashahhud) except at the end." — Imām Abū Ḥanīfah understood the eight non-Witr rakʿāt as the maximum unit prayed together.

    Ṣāḥibayn (Imām Abū Yūsuf and Imām Muḥammad): Night nawāfil should be in pairs of two only (i.e., two rakʿāt with each salām, regardless of how many total one prays).

    Dalīl for Ṣāḥibayn: Ḥadīth in Tirmidhī (narrated by Ibn ʿUmar رضي الله عنه): "Prayer at night is two by two (mathnā mathnā). When you fear the approach of dawn, make it odd with one." This establishes the pattern of two-by-two for night prayers.

    Imām al-Shāfiʿī: Both night and day nawāfil should be in pairs of two only.

    Dalīl for Imām al-Shāfiʿī: Ḥadīth in Abū Dāwūd (narrated by Ibn ʿUmar رضي الله عنه): "Prayer by night and day should consist of pairs of rakʿāt (mathnā mathnā)."

    6. The Virtue of Night Prayers (Qiyām al-Layl)

    • The Prophet ﷺ said: "The one who prolongs the night qiyām, Allāh makes things easy for him on the Day of Judgment."
    • Ḥadīth in Tirmidhī: "Make qiyām at night, for it is the way of the righteous (ṣāliḥīn), a means of drawing closer to Allāh, and it prevents sins."
    • The Prophet ﷺ said regarding tahajjud: "If it were not difficult for my ummah, I would have commanded them to do so" — showing its high virtue while explaining why it was not made obligatory.

    7. Duʿāʾ Within Ṣalāh

    Where duʿāʾ may be made: - In sajdah (the most virtuous — one is closest to Allāh) - In jalasah (sitting between the two sajdāt) - After rising from rukūʿ - In the qaʿdah (tashahhud sitting), after the durūd — from the transmitted duʿāʾ maʾthūrah - Before the closing salām (end of ṣalāh is a particularly virtuous time due to having completed a great act of worship) - Not during qirāʾah — this is a rukn and must not be interrupted

    Restrictions: 1. Duʿāʾ must be in Arabic — speaking in any other language verbally invalidates the ṣalāh. 2. Duʿāʾ must be from transmitted supplications (from ḥadīth or Qurʾān) or from recognized Qurʾānic phrases. 3. One may hold a personal need in the heart (without speaking) during a pause in duʿāʾ — this does not break ṣalāh. 4. Unnecessary taʾkhīr (delay) of a rukn of ṣalāh — e.g., pausing too long between qirāʾah and rukūʿ — may require sajdah sahw.

    Farḍ vs. Sunnah/Nāfil: - In farḍ: keep duʿāʾ to what is established from the Prophet ﷺ; be mindful of the congregation. - In Sunnah and nawāfil: one has more latitude to add maʾthūr duʿāʾs. - In a dedicated nāfil (e.g., Ṣalāt al-Ḥājah): one may specify an intention and, at the appropriate points, direct authentic Arabic duʿāʾ toward that specific need.

    Tasbīḥ count: - The minimum in farḍ behind an imām is three per posture; the imām should generally not exceed five out of consideration for the congregation. - When praying alone, one may increase to odd numbers (e.g., five, seven, nine, up to thirteen as mentioned in some fiqh texts). - Numbers may vary between postures within the same ṣalāh. - In nawāfil, one may pray for an extended time as the Prophet ﷺ himself prolonged his sujūd and rukūʿ in voluntary prayers.

    Conditions & Categories

    Categories of Non-Farḍ Ṣalāh (Ḥanafī Framework)

    Category Description If Missed
    Sunnah Muʾakkadah Prophet ﷺ was extremely punctual; rarely abandoned No formal qaḍāʾ, but leaving it is blameworthy
    Mustaḥab Prophet ﷺ did it but also left it at times; recommended No sin for omission
    Nawāfil (general) Voluntary prayers beyond the above Rewarding to do; no consequence for omission

    Key principle: Sunnah is always more virtuous than Mustaḥab, which is more emphasized than general nawāfil. The categorization reflects how punctually the Prophet ﷺ performed the action and how strongly he encouraged it.

    Note on qaḍāʾ for Sunnah: There is no obligatory qaḍāʾ for Sunnah ṣalāhs. However, Imām Muḥammad allows the Fajr sunnah to be made up on the same day after sunrise up until Ẓuhr time.

    Conditions for Changing Niyyah Mid-Ṣalāh

    • According to Aḥnāf, one cannot change the niyyah mid-ṣalāh.
    • Example: If one began tahajjud and the Fajr adhān was called, one should complete the tahajjud, then perform Witr as qaḍāʾ, then pray the Fajr sunnah.
    • One may not convert a two-rakʿāt intention into three to compensate for missed Witr.

    Conditions for Daytime vs. Night Nawāfil

    Imām Abū Ḥanīfah Ṣāḥibayn Imām al-Shāfiʿī
    Daytime max per salām 4 rakʿāt 4 rakʿāt 2 rakʿāt
    Night max per salām 8 rakʿāt 2 rakʿāt 2 rakʿāt

    In all cases: qaʿdah (sitting for tashahhud) is required after every two rakʿāt — even in a four or eight rakʿāt nāfil ṣalāh.

    On Using a Single Hadīth as Sole Evidence (Methodological Discussion)

    The class discussed the danger of relying on a single ḥadīth without cross-referencing with: 1. The amal (practice) of the Ṣaḥābah — did they implement this ḥadīth? 2. Other ḥadīths on the same topic — is there a stronger or broader corpus? 3. The legal principles (qawāʿid) of the madhhab — do they support or restrict this action?

    Example discussed: The Ṣalafī practice of pausing briefly after qirāʾah before going into rukūʿ. The Ḥanafī position holds that any unnecessary delay (taʾkhīr) of a rukn requires sajdah sahw — so a deliberate pause between qirāʾah and rukūʿ would be problematic. A single ḥadīth permitting it must be weighed against the broader established practice of the Ṣaḥābah and the principles of the four madhhabs.

    The teacher emphasized: Usūl al-Shāshī (studied in Year 3) and Tirmidhī (studied in Year 5) will provide the tools to understand why the Ḥanafī madhhab arrives at its positions — particularly how Qurʾānic āyāt, ḥadīths, and ijmāʿ al-Ṣaḥābah are combined and weighted.

    Lessons & Practical Application

    1. Pray Maghrib immediately. When the adhān for Maghrib is called, proceed directly to the farḍ without pausing for nawāfil. This is the Ḥanafī standard practice, which is why Ḥanafī masājid begin Maghrib quickly after the adhān.

    2. Know your Sunnah Muʾakkadah ṣalāhs. The 12 daily Sunnah rakʿāt (2 Fajr + 4+2 Ẓuhr + 2 Maghrib + 2 ʿIshāʾ) carry a specific promise in ḥadīth — a house in Jannah. Prioritize these above general nawāfil.

    3. The ʿAṣr sunnah is mustaḥab, not muʾakkadah. Praying 2 or 4 before ʿAṣr is good, but missing it carries less consequence than missing the Sunnah Muʾakkadah prayers.

    4. Night prayers are best done in sets of two. Even if Imām Abū Ḥanīfah permits up to eight with one salām, praying in sets of two is more virtuous — each salām is an opportunity for additional durūd and duʿāʾ.

    5. Do not change your niyyah mid-ṣalāh. If tahajjud is interrupted by the Fajr adhān, complete it and handle Witr and Fajr sunnah separately afterward (per Imām Muḥammad's position).

    6. Make duʿāʾ in sajdah and at the end of ṣalāh. These are the best moments. Keep it in Arabic from transmitted supplications. For personal needs, hold them in the heart during those moments without verbalizing in a non-Arabic language.

    7. For nawāfil with a specific purpose (e.g., Ṣalāt al-Ḥājah), make the intention explicit: "I am praying this nāfil because of [need]." Then at the appropriate points in the ṣalāh, make the relevant maʾthūr duʿāʾ in Arabic.

    8. Understand the madhhab deeply before critiquing others. When questioned by those of other methodologies, know the dalīl behind the Ḥanafī position. It is not enough to say "we do it this way" — know the ḥadīth, the amal of the Ṣaḥābah, and the usūl behind every ruling.

    9. Intention outweighs quantity. A ṣalāh performed with deep sincerity and correct intention — even if it is a missed sunnah being made up — is potentially more rewarding in the sight of Allāh than many ṣalāhs performed mechanically. Allāh weighs deeds, not merely counts them.

    10. Improve Arabic reading. The teacher encouraged students to practice reading Arabic texts in class, from newspapers, and with ḥarakah-vowelled and unvowelled versions of the same text, to prepare for reading ḥadīth books (al-Mishkāt in Year 5, Bukhārī/Tirmidhī in the final year) and for the sanad graduation ceremony.
