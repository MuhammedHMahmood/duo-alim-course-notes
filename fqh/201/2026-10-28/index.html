    Fiqh 201 - Study Notes

    Date: 2026-10-28

    Session Overview

    This session covered two major topics. The first was a continuation of the chapter on Sahw (forgetfulness in prayer), specifically the rulings when a person mistakenly stands for an extra rakah after what should have been their final sitting (Qadatul Akhira), and the rulings when a person is in doubt about how many rakahs they have prayed. The second major topic introduced was Salatul Mareed (prayer of the sick), detailing the progressive concessions permitted according to one's level of incapacity.

    Key Themes

    1. Sahw in the Final Sitting (Qadatul Akhira): What to do when one mistakenly stands for a fifth rakah during a four-rakah salah — whether before or after completing the Sajdah of the fifth rakah.
    2. Doubt in Number of Rakahs: Distinction between a first-time occurrence of doubt (break and repeat) vs. a habitual occurrence (act on dominant thought).
    3. Conversion of Fard to Nafil: When an extra rakah is completed without recall, the Fard becomes Nafil and a sixth rakah must be added.
    4. Salatul Mareed: A hierarchy of prayer positions for the sick, from standing → sitting → laying down (feet toward Qibla) → laying on one's side → Ishara (indication) — and the point at which prayer must be delayed.
    5. Transition Rules Between Prayer Positions: Going from a higher state of ability to a lower is always permitted; coming from the weakest state (Ishara) back up generally requires breaking and restarting the salah.

    Important Vocabulary

    Arabic Term Meaning

    Fiqh Rulings & Evidence

    1. Mistakenly Standing for the Fifth Rakah (instead of remaining in Qadatul Akhira)

    Scenario A — Recalled before Sajdah of the fifth rakah: - He must return to the sitting position (Qada) immediately. - He completes the Tashahud and performs Sajda Sahu. - The Sajda Sahu here is for delaying an obligatory element (Fard) of the salah — specifically, the delay of Qadatul Akhira. - His four-rakah Fard salah is valid.

    Scenario B — Recalled after completing the Sajdah of the fifth rakah: - His Fard prayer is nullified (Batala Farduhu). - His salah converts to Nafil. - Since there is no odd-numbered Nafil in the Hanafi school, he must add a sixth rakah. - The first four rakahs count as his original intention (if it was Fard, they count as Fard). - The last two rakahs count as Nafil. - He then performs Sajda Sahu.

    Scenario C — He completed the fifth rakah and made Tasleem: - If he has not spoken to anyone and has not turned away from the Qibla, he may still stand and perform a sixth rakah. - If he has spoken to someone, he must repeat the entire four-rakah Fard salah from the beginning. There is no I'ada (repeat) required for the Nafil.

    Key Hanafi principle: There is no valid salah with an odd number of rakahs in Nafil — all Nawafil must be in even numbers.

    Multiple mistakes: Any number of forgetful mistakes in a single salah are covered by one Sajda Sahu — there is no need to perform multiple.

    2. Doubt About Number of Rakahs Prayed

    Case A — First time this has ever happened: - He must break his salah and repeat it entirely. - Daleel: Musannaf Ibn Abi Shaybah, narrated by Ibn Umar (RA): "If I forget whether I prayed three or four, I repeat my salah."

    Case B — Happens frequently/habitually: - He acts on his Ghalib az-Zann (dominant thought/strong presumption). - If he has Yaqeen (certainty) about three rakahs but doubts the fourth: he goes with three and prays the fourth. - If he has no certainty but his dominant thought leans toward four: he makes Tasleem after completing the current rakah and finishes. - Whatever number he is most confident about — that is the baseline he builds from. - He performs Sajda Sahu at the end.

    Daleel: Sahih Muslim, narrated by Abu Sa'eed al-Khudri (RA): "When any one of you is in doubt about his prayer and he does not know how many he has prayed — three or four — he should cast aside his doubt and base his prayer on what he is sure of, then perform two prostrations before giving Salutation. If he has prayed five, they will make his prayer an even number; if he has prayed exactly four, it will be a humiliation for the devil."

    The Shaytan of Forgetfulness: There is a specific Shaytan (Khannas) whose sole role is to make a person lose count of their rakahs by introducing worldly thoughts during salah. When a person acts on Yaqeen or Ghalib az-Zann and successfully completes his salah, this is described in the hadith as a "humiliation for the devil."

    3. Salatul Mareed — Prayer of the Sick Person

    Hierarchy of positions (from highest to lowest capability):

    Level Condition Ruling
    1 Able to stand Must stand; standard salah
    2 Cannot stand due to illness/injury May pray sitting
    3 Cannot perform Ruku and Sujood while sitting Performs Ruku/Sujood by indication (Ishara) while sitting
    4 Cannot sit Lays down; performs Ishara with head movement
    5 Cannot move head for indication Must delay salah; cannot pray with eyes, eyebrows, or heart

    Two positions for laying down: 1. Feet toward Qibla: He raises his neck slightly for Ruku and more for Sujood. This is permissible but considered Makruh (disliked) by Hanafi scholars. - Daleel: Hadith in Bayhaqi narrated by Husain bin Ali ibn Abi Talib (RA) — the hadith indicates that facing feet toward the Qibla while sick is permitted, but Hanafi Fuqaha note Karahiyya in this position. 2. Laying on one's side facing Qibla (right or left): This is the preferred position according to the Hanafi school. The face must be toward the Qibla.

    Regarding the pillow: - When praying lying down, one should remove the pillow from under the head. - Daleel: Hadith in Bayhaqi narrated by Jabir (RA): "The Prophet (ﷺ) threw the pillow placed under the head of a sick man when he visited him and wanted to pray." The Prophet (ﷺ) also took away the stick the sick person intended to lean on. - Exception: A person may perform Sajdah on a pillow if it is on the ground (not elevated on a table or raised surface). Stacking pillows on a surface to bring the sajdah to the person's level is not permissible — he should use the Ishara concession instead.

    What is NOT permissible for Ishara: - Ishara with the eyes only. - Ishara with the eyebrows only. - Salah "in the heart" (imagining Ruku and Sujood internally). - If a person reaches this state of weakness, he must delay his salah.

    4. Transition Rules Between Prayer Positions

    Transition Ruling
    Higher position → Lower (e.g., standing → sitting) Always permissible — sickness can strike mid-salah
    Sitting (with full Ruku/Sujood) → Standing (felt better) Permissible — he may continue and finish standing
    Ishara only → Standing/Sitting (felt better) Not permissible — must break salah and restart (Isti'naf)
    Sitting with Ishara → full Ruku/Sujood (felt better) Must break and restart (confirm ruling; instructor noted this requires validation for Fard specifically)

    Key principle: The Ishara state is the weakest valid form of salah. A salah cannot be "built on" from that state. If health returns, the salah must be restarted from the beginning.

    5. Imam Leading While Sick

    Imam's state Followers' state Ruling
    Imam sitting (able to do Ruku/Sujood) Followers standing Permissible — followers pray normally
    Imam using Ishara only Followers in regular salah Not permissible — cannot follow such an imam; Ishara is the weakest state and those in regular salah cannot follow it

    6. Unconsciousness and Qada Prayer

    Duration of unconsciousness Ruling
    Five or fewer salah times missed Must make Qada (makeup) for all missed salah
    More than five salah times (i.e., sixth salah time also passes) All missed salah are forgiven; no Qada required

    Daleel: Musannaf Abdur-Razzaq — it is narrated that when Umar ibn al-Khattab (RA) became unconscious for more than a day and a night, he did not offer Qada for the missed salah.

    Distinction: This is specifically for unconsciousness, not paralysis or illness where the person retains consciousness. The ruling for a paralyzed but conscious person requires a separate investigation (instructor noted this is pending).

    Conditions & Categories

    Conditions for Returning from the Fifth Rakah to Qada (Scenario A):

    1. He has not yet performed the Sajdah of the fifth rakah.
    2. As long as this condition holds, he must sit down and complete the salah.

    Conditions for Sajda Sahu Being Required:

    • Forgetting Qadatul Akhira and standing up (even if corrected).
    • Performing any delay in a Fard element of salah.
    • Multiple mistakes in the same salah — only one Sajda Sahu is needed regardless of number of errors.

    Conditions for Salatul Mareed Concessions:

    • Genuine inability due to illness or injury — not tiredness or laziness.
    • Concessions apply equally to Fard and Nafil salah.
    • Concessions apply equally to an individual and an Imam leading salah.

    Permitted Sitting Positions for the Sick:

    Any comfortable position is acceptable due to illness, including: - Cross-legged (crisscross) - Kneeling on the knees - Tashahud position - Any other comfortable seated posture

    Scholarly Opinions

    • Imam al-Quduri (Hanafi): The primary source text for this session. He codifies the rulings on Sahw and Salatul Mareed as outlined above.
    • Hanafi school (general): No valid Nafil salah with an odd number of rakahs — this is the basis for requiring the sixth rakah in the five-rakah error scenario.
    • Hanafi school on laying down: The position of laying on one's side facing the Qibla is preferred over feet toward Qibla, despite both being Islamically valid. The scholars noted Karahiyya (dislikedness) in facing feet toward the Qibla even for the sick.
    • On the pillow: Hanafi Fuqaha derive from the Bayhaqi narration that elevating a pillow (off the ground) to facilitate a prostration is not acceptable; the concession of Ishara should be used instead. Prostration on a ground-level pillow is valid.

    Lessons & Practical Application

    1. When you mistakenly stand for an extra rakah: The moment you realize it before completing the Sajdah of that extra rakah, immediately sit down, complete Tashahud, and do Sajda Sahu. Do not delay — the further you proceed, the more complex the remedy becomes.

    2. When you lose count of rakahs: If this is your first time, break the salah and repeat it. If it happens to you regularly, train yourself to identify what you know for certain and build from that. You do not need a higher number — only what you are sure about.

    3. Salah during illness: Islam provides a full ladder of concessions — there is almost always a way to pray. The obligation of salah is not removed except in the case of unconsciousness exceeding five prayer times. Even while bedridden, Ishara with the head is sufficient. Only when even that is impossible must salah be delayed.

    4. Do not improvise new methods: Using eyes, eyebrows, or imagining Ruku/Sujood in the heart are explicitly not valid forms of prayer. The permitted concessions are defined — stay within them.

    5. Gradual deterioration during salah: If you begin salah standing and become ill mid-salah, you may progressively move to lower positions and complete the salah. You do not need to restart. However, recovery mid-salah (going from the weakest state, Ishara, back to standing) requires restarting from the beginning — the salah is not invalidated, but it cannot be continued from that point.

    6. For sick Imams: An Imam who can only pray by Ishara should not lead congregation. An Imam praying seated (but performing physical Ruku and Sujood) may lead, and the congregation stands behind him and prays normally.

    7. The Shaytan of Forgetfulness: Be mindful during salah of intruding thoughts. Recognizing that losing count is a known tool of Shaytan can motivate greater focus and presence during prayer. Acting on certainty — as the hadith instructs — is itself an act of defiance against this.
