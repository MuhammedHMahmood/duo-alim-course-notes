## Answer Key

### Nahw 201

1. Ghayr Munsarif rejects two things: **Kasra** (in the jarr position, Fatha substitutes for it) and **Tanwin** (dropped entirely in all positions).

2. **Adl Tahqiqi** is verified deviation — there is evidence (dalil) pointing to the original form. The meaning itself serves as proof. Example: ثُلَاث means "three and three" — the repetition in meaning proves the original form was سَلَاسَةٌ سَلَاسَةٌ, and the word deviated to its current pattern. **Adl Taqdiri** is assumed deviation — grammarians could find no external evidence, so they assumed/inferred (qaddara) the original form to explain why Arabs read the word as Ghayr Munsarif. Example: عُمَر — only Alamiyya was visible; grammarians assumed the original was عَامِر, giving a second sabab.

3. ثُلَاث / مَثْلَث is Ghayr Munsarif because it carries **Wasf** (it was originally designed as a descriptor — "three and three") and **Adl Tahqiqi** (it deviated from the original form سَلَاسَةٌ سَلَاسَةٌ, proven by the repetition in its meaning). Those are the two asbab.

4. **Wasf Asli** is a quality the word was born with — it was designed from the outset to describe, and it retains that quality permanently regardless of how it is later used. **Wasf Ardi** is a descriptor function that a word only has within a specific syntactic structure. أَرْبَع in مَرَرْتُ بِنِسْوَةٍ أَرْبَعٍ is a Sifa for Niswah in that sentence — but standalone, أَرْبَع is simply a number with no Wasf nature. Because it is Wasf Ardi (not Asli), it does not trigger the Wasf sabab, and the word remains Munsarif (takes Tanwin: أَرْبَعٍ).

5. Adl cannot pair with **Wazn Fi'l** as its second sabab. Adl commonly pairs with **Alamiyya** (e.g., عُمَر، زُفَر) and with **Wasf** (e.g., ثُلَاث، مَثْلَث).

6. For هِنْد: Ta'nīth Ma'nawī ✓ + 'Alam ✓ — so two asbab are present in principle. But هِنْد is Thulāthī (three letters: ه-ن-د), so the sub-conditions apply. First check: is the middle letter (نون) mutaḥarrik? No — it carries sukoon, so it is Sākin al-Awsaṭ. Second check: is the word 'Ajamī? No — هِنْد is Arabic. Both required conditions have failed, which means the second sabab is weak/contested. Therefore grammarians held both rulings valid: treating it as Munsarif or Ghayr Munsarif are both grammatically acceptable.

**Vocabulary:**

- **مُنْصَرِف** — It accepts all three harakaat (Damma, Fatha, Kasra) along with Tanwin; it "turns" to all states of declension.
- **غَيْر مُنْصَرِف** — Fatha; it takes Fatha in the jarr position instead of the expected Kasra.
- **عَدْل** — Deviation from the word's original pattern (Sigha Asliyya) to a different pattern.
- **تَقْدِير** — Grammatical assumption; inferring something not explicitly stated. For عُمَر, grammarians inferred/assumed the original form was عَامِر to supply the missing second sabab.
- **وَصْف أَصْلِي** — "Once Wasf, forever Wasf" — the Wasf Asliyya is never removed, even if the word shifts in daily use to function as a noun (e.g., أَسْوَد becoming a name for a type of snake).
- **تَأْنِيث لَفْظِي** — The three markers are: Tā' marbūṭah (ة), Alif maqṣūrah (ى), and Alif mamdūdah (اء).
- **سَاكِن الأَوْسَط** — With Arabic origin, this results in both Munsarif and Ghayr Munsarif being valid (the word sits on the fence).
- **عَلَمِيَّة** — True; Alamiyya and Wasf are mutually exclusive as the two asbab — they can never co-occur as the pair that triggers Ghayr Munsarif.

### Tafseer 201

1. The two foundational acts are: (1) **bringing creation out of non-existence** (إِخْرَاجِهِمْ مِنَ الْعَدَمِ) and (2) **abundantly bestowing blessings** — specifically rain at the right time, producing crops and fruits as sustenance for humans and their livestock. The logical conclusion: because Allah alone creates and sustains everything, He alone deserves worship. The mushrikeen acknowledged Allah as Creator — their error was adding partners alongside Him.

2. The challenge is directed at all of humanity (الإِنس), jinn (الجِنّ), and even the idols and false gods (الآلِهَة). They are challenged to produce a single surah (سُورَة وَاحِدَة) comparable to the Quran in its rhetoric (balagha), clarity (bayan), composition (nazm), words, meanings, and legislation.

3. **Grammatically**, لَن negates a future action permanently and forever — not merely "you did not" but "you never will, in all time to come." It is stronger and more definitive than لَا, which is a general negation. **Theologically**, وَلَن تَفْعَلُوا is a decisive divine declaration (qati'an jaziman) that the Quran will never be successfully challenged — and history has confirmed this from the Prophet's ﷺ time until today.

4. According to Mujahid, the stones are **stones of sulfur (كِبْرِيت)**. Sulfur stones are described as more foul-smelling and more pungent than carrion (جِيفَة — rotting flesh of a dead animal). The people of Hellfire will be tormented alongside this unbearable stench, adding an olfactory dimension to their punishment beyond heat and flame.

5. The four supreme desires: (1) **Best place of residence** — fulfilled by Jannat with its palaces and flowing rivers; (2) **Best food and fruits** — identical in appearance to familiar things but entirely different and ever-new in taste; (3) **Best spouse/companion** — purified wives (Azwaajun Mutahharatun), free of all physical and moral impurity; (4) **Eternal life** — never losing any of the above blessings. Eternity is تَمَامُ السَّعَادَة because even perfect blessings are incomplete if they can be lost — the greatest anxiety of this life is the fear of death and loss. Jannah removes that anxiety entirely, making happiness complete.

6. جَنَّاتٍ is a **Jam' Mu'annath Saalim** (sound feminine plural). For this category of nouns, the sign of nasb (accusative) is **kasra** — not fatha. This is an exception to the general rule. Although the ism of Anna is always mansoob, how that nasb is marked on the surface depends on the word's grammatical category; jam' mu'annath saalim always uses kasra as its nasb marker.

**Vocabulary:**

- **أَنداد / نِدّ** — A nidd is an equal, rival, or partner. The mushrikeen set up idols, human beings, and created things as equals to Allah in power and worship — which is the essence of Shirk.
- **إِعْجَاز القُرآن** — Three dimensions cited: eloquence/rhetoric (بَلَاغَة), clarity of expression (بَيَان), and composition/arrangement (نَظْم); also words, meanings, and legislation (شَرِيعَة).
- **الرَّيب** — Doubt, suspicion, or confusion. It appears in وَإِن كُنتُمْ فِي رَيْبٍ مِّمَّا نَزَّلْنَا — the opening of the challenge ayah.
- **لَن تَفْعَلُوا** — A جُمْلَة اعْتِرَاضِيَّة is a parenthetical clause — a sentence inserted within the main flow of speech to reinforce or emphasize a point, without being the primary statement of the ayah.
- **وَقُود** — The fuel or substance that kindles and sustains a fire. In Jahannam, the two things that serve as waqud are **people** (the disbelievers) and **stones** (specifically sulfur stones per Mujahid).
- **أُعِدَّتْ** — Passive past tense (Madhi Majhul). Root: أَعَدَّ (to prepare). Active: أَعَدَّ اللَّهُ (Allah prepared). Passive: أُعِدَّتْ (it was prepared). The ta' marbuta (ت) is added because the subject — the Fire (النَّار) — is feminine.
- **مُتَشَابِهًا** — According to the majority of mufassiroon: the fruits are similar in shape, color, and appearance, but entirely different in taste and quality each time they are served.
- **أَزْوَاجٌ مُطَهَّرَة** — Per Imam Mujahid: purified from Haydh (menstruation), Nifas (postpartum bleeding), Ghaa'it (defecation), Bawl (urine), and Nukhaam (sputum/phlegm) — all physical impurities of the human bodily condition.
- **تَرْغِيب وَتَرْهِيب** — The purpose is to move the heart in both directions simultaneously: the description of Jannah motivates through hope (Targheeb) and the description of Jahannam deters through fear (Tarheeb). Placing them side by side maximizes the effect on the reader's heart.

### Fiqh 201

1. A woman **lacking wudu only (hadas asghar)**: may recite the Quran from memory; may not touch the written text of the Quran. A woman **in Haydh** (or a person in Janabah): may not recite the Quran at all — neither from memory nor from the text — and may not touch the written text. The reason for the stricter restriction: Haydh and Janabah are a major impurity (hadas akbar) that encompasses the entire body including the interior of the mouth, whereas a broken wudu only restricts touching the physical text.

2. The three conditions for Masa on regular Jorabayn: (1) **Water impermeability** — the sock must be thick enough that wet hands passing over it do not cause water to seep through to the foot; (2) **Self-supporting on the shin** — the sock must stay up on its own without being tied, laced, or held; (3) **Durability for one mile of walking** — the sock must withstand a full mile of walking without tearing. Thin professional/office socks fail because: water seeps through them immediately (condition 1 fails), and they typically cannot stay up without elastic bands or lacing (condition 2 may fail), and they are not constructed to withstand rough walking without tearing (condition 3 fails).

3. The four differences between Masa on Jorabayn and Masa on Jabair:

   | # | Jorabayn | Jabair |
   |---|---|---|
   | 1 | Must be worn while in complete wudu — prior wudu is a prerequisite | Can be applied at any time, with or without wudu — no prior wudu required |
   | 2 | Has a time limit: 24 hours for a resident, 72 hours for a traveler | No time limit — valid for as long as the bandage/cast is medically necessary |
   | 3 | If one sock is removed, both must come off and both feet washed | If the bandage falls and the wound is still unhealed, simply retie it — wudu remains valid |
   | 4 | Duration expires when the time limit is reached | The Masa ruling expires only when the wound is fully healed and the bandage removed |

4. **Imam Abu Hanifa (Ahnaf):** Minimum 3 days and 3 nights; maximum 10 days and 10 nights. **Imam Shafi'i:** Minimum 1 day and 1 night; maximum 15 days and 15 nights. **Ahnaf evidence:** A hadith in Dar al-Qutni and Tabrani in which the Prophet ﷺ explicitly stated the minimum is 3 days and 3 nights and the maximum is 10 days and 10 nights.

5. **Salah**: completely **saqit** (dropped/lifted) — missed prayers during Haydh are permanently forgiven and do not need to be made up. **Sawm**: **haram** to fast during Haydh, but **qada is required** — whatever Ramadan days are missed must be made up after the period ends. The difference: the sharia permanently exempts women from the burden of making up prayers (which would be enormous over a lifetime) but requires makeup of fasting days since they are far fewer and manageable.

6. **Ahnaf**: Performing multiple prayers with one Tayammum is permissible — Tayammum is a complete Tahara in itself, not limited to a specific prayer or time. **Imam Shafi'i**: Tayammum is only valid for the specific prayer it was made for (plus its related Sunnah prayers); a new Tayammum is required for the next prayer time. **Ahnaf evidence**: (1) The hadith that earth is a means of Tahara for Muslims even for 10 years — indicating it is a full, time-independent Tahara; (2) The hadith of the Bedouin woman who was told to continue with Tayammum for months, proving it is not prayer-specific.

**Vocabulary:**

- **الجَوْرَبَيْن** — Regular socks made of cotton, wool, or synthetic material, as opposed to Khuffayn (thick leather socks). They qualify for Masa without the three conditions if they are **Mujallad** (entire outer surface wrapped in leather) or **Muna'al** (leather applied to the sole only).
- **الجَبَائِر** — Bandages, casts, or splints applied to an injured limb. The key difference from socks: prior wudu is **not** required — the bandage may have been applied at any time, in any state of purity.
- **سَاقِط** — Dropped/lifted — an obligation that is completely removed and requires no makeup whatsoever. Its practical implication for Haydh: missed salah prayers are permanently forgiven; a woman must not attempt to make them up.
- **اسْتِحَاضَة** — Irregular or abnormal vaginal bleeding due to illness or a medical condition, outside the valid Haydh window (less than 3 days or more than 10 days per Ahnaf). Unlike Haydh, Istihada does not suspend ibadah — the woman continues to pray, fast, and fulfill all obligations.
- **الحَيْض** — Linguistically: **سَيَلَان** — a flow. Shari' definition: blood that flows from the womb of a healthy, mature woman (who has reached puberty and is not pregnant) within the valid duration parameters.
- **مُجَلَّد / مُنَعَّل** — Mujallad: the entire outer surface of the Jorab is covered/wrapped in leather. Muna'al: leather is applied only to the sole/bottom of the Jorab. Both qualify for Masa; Muna'al has a hadith in Sunan al-Bayhaqi as its specific evidence.
- **رُخْصَة** — A concession or dispensation granted by Sharia to ease hardship. The correct attitude toward a Rukhsa is to use it — taking the concession is an act of gratitude (Shukr) to Allah. Refusing it out of false piety is not more virtuous and may amount to failing to appreciate what Allah has given.
- **غَالِبَة الظَّنّ** — Dominant/strong opinion or near-certainty. In the context of Tayammum: if a person has a strong dominant conviction — based on clear facts about the environment — that no water exists, they may perform Tayammum immediately without searching. If they later find water, their Tayammum and salah remain valid (per Tarfain — Imam Abu Hanifa and Imam Muhammad). If they only had a vague doubt and did not search, then found water, they must repeat the salah.
