## Nahw 201

**In a nutshell:** This week completed Tamiz and introduced Mustasna, the last two major topics in the Mansubat chapter.

### Key Points

- **حَال** is always نَكِرَة and must agree with its **ذُو الْحَال** in gender and number; when ذو الحال is نَكِرَة, Hal comes first to prevent confusion with a Mawsuf-Sifah construction.
- **تَمْيِيز** is a نَكِرَة مَنْصُوب that removes **إِبْهَام** (ambiguity) in quantity, weight, volume, or area; it is Mansub after مِقْدَار words and more commonly Majrur when specifying material.
- **الْمُسْتَثْنَى** is the noun after **إِلَّا** excluded from the ruling on the مُسْتَثْنَى مِنْهُ; if the exception is from the same category it is **مُتَّصِل**, otherwise **مُنْقَطِع**.
- In an affirmative sentence (**كَلَام مُوجَب**), Mustasna is obligatorily مَنْصُوب; in a negative sentence with مُسْتَثْنَى مِنْهُ present, it may instead be parsed as **بَدَل**, taking the case of its مُبْدَل مِنْهُ.

### Vocabulary

| Arabic Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| تَمْيِيز (Tamiz) | Nakirah mansub that removes ambiguity in quantity or measure |
| مُمَيَّز (Mumayyaz) | The word or sentence whose ambiguity Tamiz resolves |
| مِقْدَار (Miqdar) | Quantity/measure: weight, volume, area, or number |
| مُسْتَثْنَى (Mustasna) | The excepted noun — the word after Illa |
| مُتَّصِل / مُنْقَطِع | Connected / disconnected exception |
| كَلَام مُوجَب (Kalam Mujab) | Affirmative sentence — no negation, prohibition, or question |

### Easy to Mix Up

- **Tamiz vs. Khabar:** Khabar gives general information; Tamiz specifically resolves quantitative or material ambiguity — in زَيْدٌ كَثِيرٌ إِيمَانًا, إِيمَانًا is Tamiz (specifying *in what way*), not Khabar.

## Tafseer 201

**In a nutshell:** Al-Baqarah 84–86 condemns Bani Isra'il for breaking their covenant by killing and expelling fellow Jews, then hypocritically ransoming those same captives while ignoring what caused their captivity.

### Key Points

- Allah's **مِيثَاق** with Bani Isra'il contained two prohibitions: do not kill one another, and do not expel one another from your homes; they knowingly broke both.
- They fought their own Jewish brothers to honor **tribal alliances** with the Aws and Khazraj, then ransomed captured brothers citing the Torah — honoring one ruling while ignoring the other.
- **أَفَتُؤْمِنُونَ بِبَعْضِ الْكِتَابِ وَتَكْفُرُونَ بِبَعْضٍ** — Believing in part of scripture while rejecting other parts constitutes disbelief in the whole Book.
- Allah identifies **اشْتِرَاءُ الدُّنْيَا بِالْآخِرَة** — preferring worldly gain over divine commands — as the root cause of all this disobedience.

### Vocabulary

| Arabic Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| مِيثَاق (Mithaq) | A firm, confirmed covenant; stronger than a simple promise |
| خِزْي (Khizy) | Humiliation and disgrace in this world |
| أُسَارَى (Usara) | Captives of war |
| تُفَادُوهُمْ (Tufaduhum) | You ransom them — from فَادَى يُفَادِي, not أَفَادَ |
| إِجْلَاء (Ijla') | Expulsion from one's homeland |

## Fiqh 201

**In a nutshell:** This week finished the traveler's prayer chapter and opened Jumu'ah, covering the three types of watan, qada rules, combining prayers, and the twelve conditions that make Jumu'ah obligatory.

### Key Points

- Entering your **وَطَن أَصْلِي** ends Qasr immediately; returning to a former city you have migrated away from does not — the Prophet ﷺ prayed Qasr in Makkah after settling in Madinah.
- Missed prayers keep their original form: missed **while traveling** → always make up as two rak'at; missed **while resident** → always make up as four, even during travel.
- **Ahnaf** permit combining prayers only **فِعْلاً** (stretching times so they appear adjacent) but not **وَقْتًا** (merging actual windows); Maliki and Shafi'i permit true combination in travel, rain, and sickness.
- Jumu'ah is only obligatory in a **مِصْر** (city) per Ahnaf; it is not fard on a musafir, slave, woman, sick person, blind person, or child.
- The **خُطْبَة** is a **شَرْط** (condition) of the Jumu'ah prayer itself, not merely a recommended sunnah.

### Vocabulary

| Arabic Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| وَطَن أَصْلِي (Watani Asli) | Primary homeland — permanent residence with family |
| وَطَن إِقَامَة (Watani Iqama) | City where a traveler intends to stay 15+ days |
| قَضَاء (Qada) | Making up a missed prayer |
| جَمْع فِعْلاً (Jam' Fi'lan) | Apparent combination by stretching prayer times to meet |
| مِصْر (Misr) | A city; the Hanafi condition for Jumu'ah validity |
