## Answer Key

### Nahw 201

1. The noun must be: (1) **mufrad** (singular); (2) **munsarif** (accepts tanwīn and kasra); (3) **ṣaḥīḥ** in the naḥw sense — its final letter is not a ḥarf 'illah (ا، و، ي).

2. In **naḥw**, ṣaḥīḥ means only the **final letter** is free of ḥarf 'illah. In **ṣarf**, a noun is ma'tall if a ḥarf 'illah appears *anywhere* — beginning, middle, or end. A noun ending in wāw or yā' with **sukūn on the preceding letter** is called **jārī majrā l-ṣaḥīḥ** ("acting like a ṣaḥīḥ noun") — e.g., دَلْوٌ, ظَبْيٌ. It follows **Category 1** i'rāb.

3. عُمَرَ is **ghayr munsarif** — it rejects both tanwīn and kasra. When majrūr, it cannot take kasra, so **fatḥa substitutes for kasra**. Full i'rāb statement: *عُمَرَ: مَجْرُورٌ، وَعَلَامَةُ جَرِّهِ الْفَتْحَةُ نِيَابَةً عَنِ الْكَسْرَةِ لِأَنَّهُ غَيْرُ مُنْصَرِفٍ* — this is i'rāb far'ī (secondary i'rāb by substitution of ḥarakāt).

4. The four conditions: (1) the noun must be one of the **six nouns** (أَبٌ، أَخٌ، حَمٌ، فَمٌ، هَنٌ، ذُو); (2) **mukabbara** — in its base form, not the diminutive (muṣagghar); (3) **muwāḥada** — singular, not dual or plural; (4) **muḍāf to other than yā' al-mutakallim**. If muḍāf to yā' al-mutakallim (أَخِي), all three cases become identical — wāw/alif/yā' cannot be distinguished — so the special i'rāb does not apply and a separate rule governs.

5. The difference is the **harakah before the yā'**: رَأَيْتُ مُسْلِمَـيْن has **fatḥa** before yā' → tasniyah (dual) → "I saw two Muslims." رَأَيْتُ مُسْلِمِـيْن has **kasra** before yā' → jam' muzakkar sālim → "I saw [many] Muslims." Confusing these two entirely changes the meaning.

6. Four steps: (1) **Noon drops** when made muḍāf → مُسْلِمُو + يَ = مُسْلِمُوي; (2) **Wāw converts to yā'** (rule of ta'līl — wāw before yā' with sukūn weakens to yā') → مُسْلِمُيي; (3) **Two yā's merge** into one with shaddah → مُسْلِمُيَّ; (4) **Ḍamma changes to kasra** — since yā' demands kasra on the letter before it → **مُسْلِمِيَّ**.

**Vocabulary:**

- **غَيْرُ مُنْصَرِفٌ** — Fatḥa substitutes for kasra in the majrūr state (since this noun rejects kasra entirely).
- **جَارٍ مَجْرَى الصَّحِيحِ** — "Acting like a ṣaḥīḥ noun." A noun ending in wāw or yā' with sukūn on the preceding letter qualifies. It follows **Category 1** i'rāb.
- **أَسْمَاءُ السِّتَّةِ** — أَبٌ (father), أَخٌ (brother), حَمٌ (father-in-law), فَمٌ (mouth), هَنٌ (a vague/private noun), ذُو (possessor of).
- **يَاءُ الْمُتَكَلِّمِ** — The word's form does not change across raf', naṣb, and jarr — all three cases look identical (e.g., جَاءَ أَخِي / رَأَيْتُ أَخِي / مَرَرْتُ بِأَخِي). The i'rāb is understood from context, not visible on the word.
- **اسْمٌ مَنْقُوصٌ** — A noun ending in original yā' (ي) with kasra before it (e.g., الْقَاضِي). Its i'rāb is visible only in **naṣb**, which takes a visible **fatḥa** (رَأَيْتُ الْقَاضِيَ); raf' and jarr are hidden (taqdiree).
- **نُونُ التَّثْنِيَةِ** — Always carries **kasra (maksūr)**. The noon of jam' muzakkar sālim always carries **fatḥa (maftūḥ)**. This is how you distinguish the two in naṣb/jarr.
- **أُولُو / أُولِي** — Follows **Category 6** i'rāb — same as jam' muzakkar sālim (wāw in raf', yā' in naṣb/jarr). It is inherently plural and has no singular form in this pattern.
- **تَقْدِيرِيٌّ vs. لَفْظِيٌّ** — Taqdiree: the i'rāb is hidden/estimated — the word's written and spoken form does not change regardless of case (e.g., ism maqsur in all three cases). Lafẓī: the i'rāb is visible and explicitly pronounced (e.g., the ḍamma on a regular noun in raf').

### Tafseer 201

1. خَتَمَ means **to seal** — described by the mufassirūn as التَّغْطِيَة (covering) and الطَّبْع (imprinting/stamping). The root cause is **kufr** — persistent, willful rejection of the truth; accumulated sins obscure the نُور الْبَصِيرَة (light of inner insight) until no pathway (مَسْلَك) remains for imān to enter. The sealing is **not physical** — these people are fully healthy and functional. It is a Quranic metaphor (majāz) describing their spiritual and moral condition. The three sealed faculties are: (1) the **hearts** (القُلُوب), (2) the **hearing** (السَّمْع), and (3) the **eyesight** (الأَبْصَار).

2. Allah devoted 13 verses to the Munafiqeen because of the **great danger (خَطَر عَظِيم)** they pose to the Muslim community from within and the **great harm (ضَرَر كَبِير)** they can inflict. After the 13 verses, Allah gives **two parables (مَثَلَان)** to further expose the inner darkness of their souls and the ultimate destination (مَآل) of their hypocrisy — destruction and ruin (هَلَاك وَدَمَار).

3. Ibn Kathir's definition: **النِّفَاقُ هُوَ إِظْهَارُ الْخَيْرِ وَإِسْرَارُ الشَّرِّ** — "Nifaq is displaying goodness outwardly and concealing evil inwardly." **Nifaq I'tiqadi** (creedal): the person inwardly disbelieves while outwardly claiming Islam — consequence: **eternal Hellfire (يُخَلِّدُ صَاحِبَهُ فِي النَّار)**; this is the most severe form. **Nifaq 'Amali** (practical): the person's aqeedah is sound but their actions resemble those of hypocrites (e.g., lying, breaking promises, betraying trust) — consequence: a **major sin** requiring repentance, but not eternal damnation.

4. In reality the Munafiqeen cannot deceive Allah, since nothing is hidden from Him. The evil consequence (وَبَال) of their deception **returns to them alone** — they are the ones harmed most by their own nifaq. They cannot perceive this because of **excessive heedlessness (تَمَادِي غَفْلَتِهِمْ)** and **the completeness of their foolishness (تَكَامُل حَمَاقَتِهِمْ)**.

5. Four devices in أَلَا إِنَّهُمْ هُمُ الْمُفْسِدُونَ: (1) **أَلَا** — particle of alerting (المُنَبِّه), draws the listener's full attention; (2) **إِنَّ** — particle of emphasis (حَرْف مُقَرِّرَة), confirms and strengthens the meaning; (3) **هُمْ** as ḍamīr al-faṣl (separating pronoun) between mubtadaʾ and khabar — restricts the predicate exclusively to this subject ("it is *they* and none other"); (4) **الْمُفْسِدُونَ** with the definite article — making the predicate definite intensifies the emphasis further. Together these constitute the most emphatic possible refutation in Arabic rhetoric.

6. In Makkah, opposition to Islam was open — the Kuffar and Mushrikeen made no pretense of being Muslim. Nifaq as a social phenomenon arose only in **Madinah** after the Hijrah, when Islam became the dominant political and social force. Some individuals joined the Muslim community outwardly for worldly or political benefit while internally retaining kufr. Therefore all major Quranic descriptions of the Munafiqeen are found in **Madani surahs** (e.g., Al-Baqarah, Al-Munafiqun).

**Vocabulary:**

- **نُورُ الْبَصِيرَة** — The light of inner insight and imān. Sins gradually obscure it; once lost, there is no pathway for imān to enter or for kufr to exit the heart.
- **النِّفَاقُ الِاعْتِقَادِيُّ** — Eternal Hellfire (يُخَلِّدُ صَاحِبَهُ فِي النَّار) — the most severe consequence of all forms of nifaq.
- **الْمُزَبْزَبُ** — "Confused/wavering" — the Munafiqeen waver between the Mu'mineen and the Kafireen, fully belonging to neither group.
- **وَبَال** — Evil/bad consequence or result; the harmful outcome of one's own action returning upon oneself.
- **أَخْبَثُ** — Superlative of **خَبِيث** (vile/wicked): "the most vile." The Munafiqeen are described as أَخْبَثُ الْكَفَرَة — the most vile of all disbelievers.
- **رِجْس** — Moral/spiritual filth or abomination (used for Nifaq, Kufr, and idols — فَاجْتَنِبُوا الرِّجْسَ مِنَ الْأَوْثَان). **نَجَاسَة** is physical ritual impurity (substances affecting wuḍū/ṣalāh). Rijs is moral; najāsa is physical.
- **أَلَا** — Called **المُنَبِّه** — the alerting/warning particle that draws the listener's attention before an emphatic statement.
- **ضَمِيرُ الْفَصْل** — Placed between the mubtadaʾ and khabar; its grammatical effect is restriction — confirming that the predicate belongs *exclusively* to that subject and to no one else.
- **سَبَبُ النُّزُول** — Reported by **Ibn Abbas (رضي الله عنه)**. The named leaders are **Abdullah ibn Ubayy ibn Salul** (the foremost leader of the Munafiqeen in Madinah) and **Mu'tab ibn Qushayr** — hypocrites from Ahl al-Kitab who outwardly professed Islam while concealing kufr.

### Fiqh 201

1. The three qualities are **color, taste, and smell**. If **two or more** are altered by a foreign substance, the water is no longer permissible for ṭahāra. If **only one** quality is changed by a permissible additive (soap, ishnan, saffron), ṭahāra remains valid — the Ḥanafī position is that altering one quality does not strip the water of its essential nature as mā'.

2. **Manī** (seminal fluid discharged with desire/shahwa) — requires **ghusl**. **Madhī** (pre-seminal fluid released during foreplay) — breaks **wuḍū only**; wash the area and make wuḍū, no ghusl required. **Wādī** (slightly thick fluid sometimes seen after urination) — breaks **wuḍū only**, no ghusl required.

3. **Ma-e-kalīl** (small): less than **10 hand-spans × 10 hand-spans** in area with shallow depth — if *any* amount of najāsa enters, the entire body of water becomes impure regardless of whether the three qualities change (strict ruling). **Ma-e-kathīr** (large): **10 × 10 hand-spans or more** with sufficient depth that the bottom is not exposed when scooping — najāsa does not render it impure unless one of the three qualities is visibly altered. The **ripple test**: if movement on one side does not cause perceptible movement on the other, it qualifies as a large body; the madhhab adopted Imam Muhammad's version (benchmarked by scooping water with the hand on one side).

4. Mā' musta'mal is water used to remove a ḥadath — whether for a new wuḍū after ḥadath or for renewing an existing wuḍū. Water acquires this status the moment **it drips off/leaves the body** — while still on the skin it remains ṭahūr. The operative Ḥanafī fatwa (in line with Imam Muhammad): it is **ṭāhir but not muṭahhar** — pure in itself but cannot purify others. It **may not** be used for wuḍū or ghusl. It **may** be used for istiñjā', irrigation, cooking, and general purposes.

5. Imam Malik's dalīl: the hadith of **Bi'r Budha'a** — a well in Madinah into which menstrual rags, dog carcasses, and refuse were thrown; the Prophet ﷺ stated: *"Indeed, water is pure — nothing makes it impure."* The Ḥanafī response: Bi'r Budha'a had an underground stream continuously replenishing it — it was effectively **flowing water**, not truly stagnant. The hadith therefore cannot override the rule for genuinely still, stagnant small bodies.

6. According to the Ḥanafī position (and one opinion of Imam Shafi'i), an animal **with no flowing blood** (fly, mosquito, frog, crab, fish) dying in water does **not** render it impure. The Quranic dalīl is **Surah Al-An'am**: flowing blood (الدَّمَ مَسْفُوحًا) is declared najis — if an animal has no flowing blood to begin with, its death introduces no najāsa into the water.

**Vocabulary:**

- **طَهُور** — (1) Pure in itself AND (2) capable of purifying others. Examples: rainwater, river/valley water, well water, seawater, spring water.
- **طَاهِر (not muṭahhar)** — **Mā' musta'mal** — the operative Ḥanafī fatwa classifies used wuḍū water as ṭāhir (pure) but incapable of being used for ṭahāra itself.
- **مَاءٌ مُسْتَعْمَل** — Water becomes musta'mal the moment **it drips off the body** after being used for wuḍū or ghusl — not while it is still in contact with the skin.
- **نَجَاسَةٌ خَفِيفَة** — Minor/light impurity, less severe than najasat-e-ghalizah (major impurity). Imam Abu Yusuf's opinion classifies mā' musta'mal as this lighter form, whereas one opinion of Imam Abu Hanifa placed it in the heavier category.
- **الشَّيْخَانِ** — **Imam Abu Hanifa + Imam Abu Yusuf**.
- **الصَّاحِبَانِ** — **Imam Abu Yusuf + Imam Muhammad** (Imam Abu Yusuf is the shared scholar between both terms).
- **مَاءٌ جَار** — Najāsa entering flowing water does not make it impure as long as **no trace (athar) of the najāsa** — no change in color, taste, or smell — remains visible in the water.
- **إِسْرَاف** — Extravagance/wastefulness. Using excessive water even in wuḍū is isrāf and is **impermissible (ḥarām)**; the Prophet ﷺ was described as making wuḍū conservatively even when standing beside a river.
