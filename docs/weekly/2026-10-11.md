## Answer Key

### Nahw 201

1. (a) The Ism of Kana and its sisters is always **Marfu'** (nominative). (b) The Khabar of Inna and its sisters is always **Marfu'** (nominative). The two groups work symmetrically but in opposite directions: Kana makes the Mubtada Ism (Marfu') and the Khabar Mansoob; Inna makes the Mubtada Ism (Mansoob) and keeps the Khabar Marfu'.

2. Khabar Muqaddam is **permissible** for the first nine أفعال ناقصة (e.g., كَانَ قَائِمًا زَيْدٌ). It is **prohibited** for those beginning with ما (e.g., مَا زَالَ، مَا دَامَ). For **Laisa**, there is scholarly disagreement (Ikhtilaf) — some permit it, others do not.

3. **ما** Mushabbeha may come with both Ma'rifa (definite) and Nakira (indefinite) nouns. **لا** Mushabbeha comes **only with Nakira** (indefinite) nouns.

4. The three conditions: (1) The word is a **Siga Sifat** (اسم فاعل، اسم مفعول، صفة مشبهة، or اسم تفضيل). (2) It appears **after ما نافية or Istifham** (e.g., هل). (3) It gives **Rafa to an Ism Zahir** (an explicit noun following it).

5. The diagnostic test is **agreement (مطابقة)**. If the Sifat **matches** the following noun in number/gender → it is not doing Amal → it is **Khabar Muqaddam**, and the Ism Zahir is Mubtada Mu'akhkhar. If the Sifat is **Mufrad** and does **not match** (e.g., the noun is Tasniya) → it is doing Amal → it is the **special Mubtada**, and the Ism Zahir is Khabar. Example of special Mubtada: مَا صَائِمٌ الزَّيْدَانِ (Mufrad Sifat, Tasniya noun → Sifat is Mubtada). Example of Khabar Muqaddam: مَا صَائِمَانِ الزَّيْدَانِ (both Tasniya → Sifat is Khabar Muqaddam).

6. **Bayan al-Naw'** (type/manner): جَلَسْتُ جِلْسَةَ الْمَلِكِ — "I sat like a king." **Bayan al-'Adad** (number): جَلَسْتُ جَلْسَتَيْنِ — "I sat twice."

7. The inversion is only permissible when both nouns carry **clear i'rab markings**, so there is no ambiguity about which is the fa'il and which is the maf'ul.

8. **Jawāzan** (optional drop): when context (qareena) makes the meaning clear — e.g., أَكْلًا كَثِيرًا said while watching someone eat excessively. **Wujūban** (obligatory drop): in specific sam'i constructions — e.g., **شُكْرًا** (the verb شَكَرْتُكَ is never stated; Arabs always drop it); **سَقْيًا** (may you be given to drink).

**Vocabulary:**

- **أَفْعَالٌ نَاقِصَةٌ** — "Incomplete verbs": they are incomplete because they cannot form a full sentence with a subject alone; they require both an Ism and a Khabar (a complement structure) to complete their meaning.
- **نَفْيُ الْجِنْسِ** — Negation of the entire genus/category. Because the negation is total (no member of the genus exists), بَلْ cannot follow — you cannot say "No, but..." when the entire category has been denied.
- **مُطَابَقَةٌ** — Agreement. If the Sifat matches the following noun (in number/gender), this tells you the Sifat is **not** doing Amal (not governing the noun as Amil); it is therefore Khabar Muqaddam, not the special Mubtada.
- **مَفْعُولٌ مُطْلَقٌ** — Yes, it can come from a different root, provided the **core meaning** is the same as the governing verb. Example: أَنْبَتَهُ نَبَاتًا — أَنْبَتَ (fi'l mazid) and نَبَات (from fi'l mujarrad) share the same core meaning of growing.
- **قَرِينَةٌ** — A contextual indicator. The verb may be dropped جَوَازًا only when there is a qareena — some circumstantial or contextual clue — that makes the full meaning retrievable without the verb being stated.
- **سَمَاعِي** — No; you cannot re-insert the dropped verb. These constructions were transmitted from Arab usage as fixed forms. The verb's absence is itself part of the established convention.
- **بَيَانُ الْعَدَد** — جَلَسْتُ جَلْسَتَيْنِ illustrates Bayan al-'Adad — specifying the number of occurrences (twice).
- **فِعْلٌ مَزِيدٌ** — In أَنْبَتَهُ اللَّهُ نَبَاتًا: **أَنْبَتَ** is the fi'l mazid (augmented with the أ of Bab If'al); **نَبَات** is derived from the fi'l mujarrad نَبَتَ.

### Tafseer 201

1. The four attributes: (1) **لَا ذَلُولٌ** — not broken in/trained, excluding work animals used for plowing. (2) **لَا تُثِيرُ الأَرْضَ** — does not plow the earth, excluding farm cows. (3) **وَلَا تَسْقِي الحَرْثَ** — not used for irrigation, excluding cows used for water-work. (4) **لَا شِيَةَ فِيهَا** — no markings of a different color, requiring a completely uniform yellow cow with no patches.

2. Two reasons for their reluctance: (1) The price of such a precisely specified cow was **extraordinarily high**. (2) **Fear of scandal (خَشْيَةُ الفَضِيحَة)** — embarrassment at their inability to find it and at having delayed so long. Grammatically, **كَادَ** is a verb of proximity/nearness indicating something was *about* to happen but did not. Here: they were on the verge of not acting — but they did slaughter it in the end.

3. Allah commanded them to strike the **dead body of the murdered man** with a **piece of the slaughtered cow**. When they did, the dead man was **temporarily revived** and **named his killer**, resolving the murder case miraculously. The broader lesson drawn (كَذَلِكَ يُحْيِي اللَّهُ الْمَوْتَى): just as Allah revived this man before their eyes, He will **resurrect all the dead** on the Day of Judgment — this miracle is a foretaste and proof of the general resurrection.

4. Three behaviors of rocks: (1) **تَتَفَجَّرُ مِنْهُ الأَنْهَارُ** (rivers burst forth) — some rocks have rivers gushing from them. (2) **يَشَّقَّقُ** (they split/crack) — some rocks crack open with water springing out from fear of Allah. (3) **يَهْبِطُ مِنْ خَشْيَةِ اللَّهِ** (they crumble and fall) — some rocks crumble and fall from mountaintops out of awe of Allah. In the comparison: water emerging from rock = tears from a believing heart. Rocks are the paradigm of hardness, yet even they respond to Allah's greatness; Bani Israel's hearts did not.

5. The **بـ** is زَائِدَة (extra/emphatic), added after negation to intensify the denial: Allah is absolutely, completely not unaware. The rhetorical tone is both a **warning (وَعِيد)** and a **threat (تَهْدِيد)** — a reminder to Bani Israel (and all readers) that every deed is recorded and will be recompensed on the Day of Resurrection.

6. Two crimes: (1) **تَبْدِيل/تَأْوِيل of the Torah** — outright substitution of words or false interpretation of meaning. This was particularly grave because it was done **عَلَى بَصِيرَة** — with full understanding, deliberately, neither from mistake nor forgetfulness. Knowing the truth and then distorting it is a far greater crime than distorting out of ignorance. (2) **Outward hypocrisy** — claiming belief to Muslims while privately conducting themselves otherwise (addressed in the second crime of Ayah 76).

7. Their fear: if they acknowledged to Muslims that the Torah confirms Muhammad ﷺ, then on the Day of Judgment Muslims would use that acknowledgment as **evidence against them** — "You knew he was the true Prophet, yet you did not follow him." Sharing this information would only **strengthen the case against themselves** in the Akhirah.

8. The tip: look at the **first sighah** (مَاضِي or مُضَارِع). If that form ends in **alif maqsurah (ى)**, the plural takes **فتحة**; if not, it takes **ضمة**. For **لَقِيَ**: the مَاضِي is لَقِيَ, which ends in a regular ya (ي), not alif maqsurah — therefore the plural مَاضِي takes **ضمة → لَقُوا**. The مُضَارِع يَلْقَى ends in alif maqsurah, so its plural takes فتحة → يَلْقَوْنَ.

**Vocabulary:**

- **لَا شِيَةَ فِيهَا** — "No markings differing from its color; without spots." It means the cow must be completely uniform in color — not a single patch, spot, or marking of any other color anywhere on its body.
- **وَمَا كَادُوا يَفْعَلُونَ** — كَادَ indicates something was *about* to happen but did not. The sentence means they **did** slaughter it — but they came very close to not doing so at all.
- **فَادَّارَأْتُمْ فِيهَا** — Root: دَرَأَ (to push/repel). The Form VI (mutual action) meaning is each group pushing the blame/accusation away from itself toward others — **mutual deflection of accusation** among Bani Israel over who committed the murder.
- **قَسَا / قَسْوَةٌ** — To become hard; hardness. Used for both physical hardness and the spiritual hardening of the human heart. Used in this session in: **ثُمَّ قَسَتْ قُلُوبُكُمْ مِّن بَعْدِ ذَلِكَ** (Ayah 74).
- **تَحْرِيفٌ** — Distortion. Two forms: (1) **تَبْدِيل** — literal substitution/changing of the actual words of the Torah. (2) **تَأْوِيل** — false interpretation, reading a meaning into the text that was not intended, without altering the words themselves.
- **عَلَى بَصِيرَةٍ** — "With full insight/awareness." It intensifies guilt by establishing that the distortion was **deliberate** — the scholars heard, understood, and then distorted it knowingly. There is no plea of ignorance or error.
- **يُسِرُّونَ / يُعْلِنُونَ** — To conceal / to reveal openly. Allah's statement that He knows both means their private scheming is entirely futile — nothing is outside His knowledge, making their behavior a profound self-contradiction for people who claim to believe in Allah.
- **أَمَانِيٌّ كَاذِبَةٌ** — False hopes. Two specific ones mentioned: (1) **Their claim to be the beloved friends of Allah** (أَحِبَّاءُ اللَّهِ). (2) **Their claim that Hellfire will only touch them for a few days** — based on a false calculation that the world lasts 7,000 years, so they owe only 7 days of punishment.

### Fiqh 201

1. Witr cannot be Fard because there is **no Dalil Qat'i** — no Quranic ayah. The Miraj established exactly five obligatory prayers; to call Witr Fard would effectively create a sixth fard, which is textually unsupported. The established ahadith carry strong emphasis (taqeed) but are Dalil Zanni. Hence it is **Wajib**. The practical consequence of Wajib status: if Witr is missed, **Qadha is required** — unlike a Sunnah prayer, which has no Qadha.

2. Three differences from Maghrib: (1) In the third rakah of Witr, the Imam **recites aloud** (in Maghrib, the third rakah is sirri). (2) A surah must be added after Surah al-Fatihah in **every rakah** of Witr (in fard salah, Fatihah alone is recited in the third and fourth rakahs). (3) Before Ruku in the third rakah, a **takbir is made and Dua al-Qunut is recited** (no such addition in Maghrib).

3. Hanafi position: Qunut is recited in the **third rakah of Witr**, **before Ruku**, **throughout the entire year** (not restricted to Ramadan). Procedure: after completing the surah recitation in the third rakah, raise the hands and say **Allahu Akbar** (takbir), then recite Dua al-Qunut, then proceed to Ruku.

4. **Qunut al-Witr**: a fixed supplication recited in the third rakah of Witr before Ruku, year-round — it is a permanent, ongoing practice. **Qunut al-Nazila**: a special supplication recited during times of hardship or calamity for the Ummah; it is not permanent and is recited across all five daily prayers for the duration of the hardship. Hanafi dalil against permanent Qunut in Fajr: (1) The Prophet ﷺ performed it for **only one month** for a specific necessity then stopped — the time-limited nature is itself a dalil. (2) Hadith in Ibn Majah from Umm Salamah رضي الله عنها: the Prophet ﷺ explicitly **prohibited** Qunut in Fajr. (3) Tabarani: Ghalib stayed two months with Anas رضي الله عنه and **never saw him perform Qunut in Fajr**.

5. Two intentions: (1) **Niyyat al-Salah** — intention for the specific salah being performed. (2) **Niyyat al-Mutaba'ah** — intention to follow this particular Imam. Without both, the prayer does not count as Jama'ah. If someone left home with both intentions and joins during Ruku: the Rakah **is counted**, provided his hands reach his knees **before the Imam says سَمِعَ اللهُ لِمَنْ حَمِدَهُ**.

6. Five conditions: (1) Both are following **the same Imam** in the same prayer. (2) There is **no barrier (Hajiz)** between the woman and the men's row. (3) The prayer includes **Ruku and Sujud** (Salat al-Janazah is excluded). (4) The woman is **Ahli Shahwa** — of an age that naturally attracts attention (very old women and children are excluded). (5) The **Imam made intention to lead both men and women**. If all five are met, the men's prayer becomes **Fasid**. The **woman's own prayer remains valid**.

7. The Imam being Zamin means his prayer validity **carries the congregation** — if his prayer is valid, minor errors by individual Muqtadis are covered; if his prayer is invalid, the congregation's prayer is also invalid. Practically: if discovered after Salah that the Imam had no Wudu, the **entire congregation must repeat the prayer** (Ahnaf). Imam Shafi'i's position: each person (Imam and Muqtadi) is independently Zamin for their own prayer; the Imam's invalid Wudu does **not** affect the Muqtadi's prayer.

8. Yes, it is permissible. The person joins with the intention of the **same Salah**; the second prayer counts as **Nafil**. Dalil: the Prophet ﷺ told companions who had already prayed at their camps to join the Jama'ah and told them it would be Nafil for them (Tirmidhi).

**Vocabulary:**

- **وَاجِب vs. فَرْض** — Both are obligatory, but Fard is established by **Dalil Qat'i** (definitive proof — Quran or mutawatir hadith), while Wajib is established by **Dalil Zanni** (probable proof — an ahad hadith with taqeed/emphasis). Denying a Fard is kufr; denying a Wajib is not.
- **وَرَع (Wara')** — Avoidance of anything carrying even a shade of doubt (Shubuhat), beyond just avoiding Haram. Taqwa is avoiding Haram and Makruh Tahrimi; Wara' goes further. The parable: a king has a restricted sanctuary around his palace — whoever lingers at its edges risks stepping inside the prohibited zone; doubtful matters are like that boundary.
- **فَاسِق (Fasiq)** — An open sinner. Makruh Tanzih means the prayer is **disliked but remains valid**; no sin is incurred by the congregation, and no repeat is required. Avoidance is simply preferred.
- **ضَامِن (Zamin)** — Guarantor. Yes, it cuts both ways: (1) If the Imam's prayer is valid, minor errors of individual Muqtadis are covered by his validity — a mercy for the congregation. (2) If the Imam's prayer is invalid, the congregation's prayer is also invalid — a collective risk, making the choice of a qualified Imam a communal responsibility.
- **اقْتِدَاء (Iqtida)** — The asymmetry follows the principle that a **lower intention may join a higher**, but a **higher intention cannot follow a lower**. Fard is a higher station than Nafil. A Nafil Muqtadi joining a Fard Imam is a lower joining a higher — permissible. A Fard Muqtadi joining a Nafil Imam would mean a higher obligation being led by a lower — not permissible.
- **طِوَالُ الْمُفَصَّل** — Masnoon for **Fajr and Zuhr**. Range: Surah al-Hujurat (Surah 49) to Surah al-Buruj (Surah 85).
- **مُتَعَيَّن (Muta'ayyan)** — Fixing a surah as the *only* permissible option for a given Salah, such that no other surah is considered acceptable in that slot. This is **Makruh**, because it implies the other surahs are lesser (which is incorrect — all parts of the Quran carry equal sanctity) and can lead to neglect of other portions of the Quran.
- **مَعْذُور (Ma'dhur)** — A person with a valid excuse preventing full physical prayer, such as continuous urinary incontinence or uncontrollable bleeding. Example: a Ma'dhur with urinary incontinence cannot lead those without this condition because his state of Wudu is weaker (re-made for each prayer only). The Ahnaf principle requires the Imam to be in **equal or better physical condition** than the Muqtadis — a major gap in physical state prevents valid Iqtida.
