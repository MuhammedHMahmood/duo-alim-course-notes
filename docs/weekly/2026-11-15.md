## Nahw 201

**In a nutshell:** The class completed the Mansubaat chapter, covering the final categories of Mustasna i'rab, Laa Nafi al-Jins, and the Khabar of Maa.

### Key Points

- **Mustasna Mufarragh** (المستثنى المفرَّغ) occurs when the Mustasna Minhu is absent; إلا has no i'rab effect, and the noun takes its i'rab from the **Amil** — drop إلا and read the grammatical role.
- After **غير / سِوى / حَاشَ**, the Mustasna is Majrur; غير itself takes the same i'rab the Mustasna would have with إلا (the key Usul).
- **لا نافية للجنس** negates an entire category; its Ism is Mabnī ʿalā al-fatḥ (mufrad), Mansūb (muḍāf), or Mansūb with tanwīn (shibh muḍāf) — "mufrad" here means not muḍāf, regardless of number.
- **لا نافية للجنس** loses its amal when a maʿrifa follows it (repetition of لا becomes obligatory) or when a **fāṣil** intervenes between لا and its ism.
- The amal of Hijazi **مَا** is void in three cases: khabar comes after إلا, khabar is muqaddam, or إن زائدة follows مَا — in all three, both ism and khabar are Marfūʿ.

### Vocabulary

| Arabic Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| مُفَرَّغ | "Empty" exception — Mustasna Minhu is absent |
| لا نافية للجنس | Laa that negates the entire genus/category |
| شبه المضاف | Resembles a muḍāf; depends on what follows for meaning |
| فاصل | A separator between لا and its ism |
| إن زائدة | Pleonastic إن — adds no meaning |

## Tafseer 201

**In a nutshell:** Ayat 87–103 of Surah Al-Baqarah catalogued Bani Isra'eel's persistent rejection of prophets, their false claims about paradise and Sulaiman ﷺ, and the story of Harut and Marut.

### Key Points

- Bani Isra'eel's rejection of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ was driven by **ḥasad** (حَسَد) — they had recognized him from the Torah's descriptions but refused to accept prophethood going to another nation.
- They claimed their hearts were **غُلْف** (covered/sealed) to make the Prophet ﷺ despair; Allah responded that they were cursed (لَعْنَة) — distanced from His mercy — because of their disbelief.
- The love of calf-worship had been **وَشُرِبُوا فِي قُلُوبِهِمُ** (drunk into their hearts) — mixed into their very being like dye into fabric — a consequence of pre-existing kufr.
- **Sulaiman ﷺ was entirely innocent** of magic (مَا كَفَرَ سُلَيْمَانُ); his kingdom rested on divine miracles; it was the shayateen who taught sihr.
- **Harut and Marut** were sent to Babylon as a divine **فِتْنَة** (test), always warning "do not disbelieve" before teaching; magic can only harm by Allah's permission.

### Vocabulary

| Arabic Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| حَسَد | Envy — resenting a blessing given to others |
| غُلْف | Coverings/sheaths — "our hearts are covered" |
| لَعْنَة | Curse — expelled from the mercy of Allah |
| مُعْجِزَة | Divine miracle — exclusively from Allah |
| فِتْنَة | Trial/test — the purpose of Harut and Marut's sending |

### Easy to Mix Up

- **مُعْجِزَة vs. سِحْر**: a miracle is exclusively from Allah and cannot be learned; magic is a human craft, real but bounded by Allah's permission.

## Fiqh 201

**In a nutshell:** The class covered Salat al-Khawf and completed Bab al-Janazah, from preparing the dying through burial.

### Key Points

- **Salat al-Khawf** divides soldiers into two groups alternating behind one imam; each group prays their missed raka'at independently after the imam's salam — actual fighting **invalidates** the prayer.
- **Kafan Masnoon** for a man is 3 pieces (Qamees, Izar, Lifafa); for a woman it is 5 — the **Khimar** (head covering) is never omitted even at the bare minimum (Kafan Kifaya).
- **Salatul Janazah** has four takbirat; hands are raised only for the first; it is led by the Sultan, then the local imam, then the Wali — for a deceased woman, her **father** takes precedence over her son.
- Salatul Janazah may be performed at the grave for up to **three days** if burial occurred without it, as long as the body has not begun to disintegrate (Imam Abu Yusuf's relied-upon position).
- A **stillbirth** (no signs of life) requires only kafan wrapping — no ghusl and no Salatul Janazah; a child born alive receives the full rites even if death follows moments later.

### Vocabulary

| Arabic Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| صَلَاةُ الخَوْف | Prayer of Fear — modified congregational prayer under military threat |
| كَفَن مَسْنُون | Recommended (Sunnah) number of kafan layers |
| لَحْد | Preferred grave type: a niche cut into the side wall toward the qibla |
| حَنُوط | Perfumed compound applied to the head and places of sajda after ghusl |
| لا بِن | Unbaked mud bricks used to seal the lahd (preferred over baked bricks) |
