## Answer Key

### Nahw 201

1. The two asbāb are **'Alamiyyah** (proper noun status) and **Wazan al-Fi'l** (verbal pattern). For فُعِلَ to qualify as a wazan al-fi'l sabab, the wazan must be **exclusive (khāṣṣ) to the fi'l** — this passive pattern appears only in verbs, never in nouns; because it is exclusively verbal, placing a proper noun on it creates a Ghayr Munṣarif word.

2. The flaw: beginning with a ḥarf mudāra'ah is only the **first** of two simultaneous conditions required when a wazan is shared between fi'l and ism. The second condition — **the word must not accept tā' marbūṭah** — also must be met. يَعْمَلَةٌ already carries tā' marbūṭah, so the second condition fails. The correct ruling: it is **munṣarif**.

3. مَصَابِيح follows the pattern **مَفَاعِيل** — this is **Jam' Muntahal Jumū'** (the ultimate broken plural). It is Ghayr Munṣarif because this single sabab counts as the equivalent of **two asbāb**, making a second sabab unnecessary. Qur'anic evidence: بِمَصَابِيحَ in Sūrat al-Mulk (fatḥah, no tanwīn).

4. **Correct.** When alif-lam (ال) is prefixed to a Ghayr Munṣarif noun, it **removes the Ghayr Munṣarif status**, restoring full declension. مَدَارِس is normally Ghayr Munṣarif, but with ال it accepts kasrah in majrūr position → بِالْمَدَارِسِ is correct.

5. The eight: (1) الفاعل — the doer; (2) مفعول ما لم يُسَمَّ فاعله — the passive subject; (3) المبتدأ — subject of a nominal sentence; (4) الخبر — predicate; (5) خبر إنَّ وأخواتها; (6) اسم كان وأخواتها; (7) اسم ما ولا المشبهتين بـ"ليس"; (8) خبر أفعال التي رُفِعَ فيها الجنس.

6. Swapping is **forbidden** when both Fā'il and Maf'ūl are **Ism Maqsūr** (indeclinable, ending in alif maqsūrah) and **neither i'rāb nor meaning** can distinguish which is which — e.g., ضَرَبَ مُوسَى عِيسَى. The exception: swapping is permitted even with two Ism Maqsūr words when **meaning resolves the ambiguity** — e.g., أَكَلَ الكُمَّثْرَى يَحْيَى (a pear cannot eat a person, so Yahyā must be the Fā'il).

**Vocabulary:**

- **غير منصرف** — It rejects **tanwīn** and **kasrah** in the majrūr (genitive) position, showing fatḥah instead.
- **جمع منتهى الجموع** — Its structural pattern (مَفَاعِل, مَفَاعِيل, etc.) was held by classical grammarians to inherently carry the weight of two separate causes; one sabab equals two.
- **تأنيث لفظي** — Yes, it still counts as a sabab for Ghayr Munṣarif — the **form** of the word governs this rule. Verb agreement, however, follows the **meaning**: if the referent is male, use the mudhakkar verb (e.g., جَاءَ طَلْحَةُ).
- **ضمير مستتر** — From the root **سَتَرَ** (to conceal, to hide). Example: زَيْدٌ ذَهَبَ — the verb ذَهَبَ contains a hidden هُوَ as its Fā'il.
- **فعل متعدٍّ** — From **تَعَدَّى** (to exceed, to transgress — cf. وَمَنْ يَتَعَدَّ حُدُودَ اللَّهِ). The action exceeds/goes beyond the Fā'il to an object; a sentence with this verb requires a **Maf'ūl (object)** in addition to the Fā'il.
- **فصل** — With no fasl, tā' al-ta'nīth is **obligatory** on the verb when Fā'il is Mu'annath Haqīqī. With fasl present, the tā' becomes **optional** — both mudhakkar and mu'annath verb forms are valid.
- **اسم مقصور** — Since both words carry no visible i'rāb markers, it becomes impossible to tell from case-endings alone which word is the doer and which is the object — producing *labs* (ambiguity).
- **ألف نون زائدتان** — The root of سَلْمَان is **سلم** (sīn-lam-mīm); the alif-nūn at the end are not part of the original root and are therefore *zā'idah* (extra/additional).

### Tafseer 201

1. The two concerns: (1) **Spreading corruption (ifsād)** through sin and disobedience, and (2) **Shedding blood (safk al-dimā')** through transgression and violation of Shari'ah's boundaries. The angels contrasted these with themselves: *wa naḥnu nusabbiḥu biḥamdika wa nuqaddisu lak* — "We glorify You with praise and sanctify You," implying they were sufficient for worship of Allah.

2. Ibn Abbas (RA) explained that *al-asmā' kullahā* means Allah taught Adam the name of **every single thing that exists** — every creature, animal, object, and utensil — down to ordinary items like a bowl (*qas'ah*) and ladle (*miqrafah*). This explains the **ongoing expansion of human knowledge** across science and technology: it is the fulfillment of what was encoded in human nature (*fitrah*) from the time of Adam.

3. *Tabkit* means **rebuking and scolding**. The test was framed as a rebuke because the angels had raised objections to humanity's fitness for khilafah. By challenging them to name what they could not, Allah implicitly replied: if you claim to be more deserving than this new creation, demonstrate it.

4. *Subḥānaka*: the word *Subḥān* is **manṣūb** (accusative) and functions as a **Maf'ūl Mutlaq** (cognate accusative/absolute object). The *ka* is the possessive pronoun (muḍāf ilayh). The verb and fā'il are **hidden/implied** before it. Since the angels are speaking in the plural, the full implied sentence is: **نُسَبِّحُ سُبْحَانَكَ** — "We glorify You with glorification." *Nusabbiḥu* is the implied fi'l and fā'il; *Subḥānaka* is the maf'ūl mutlaq.

5. The dual form establishes that **both Adam AND Hawa were equally responsible** for eating from the forbidden tree — the sin cannot be attributed to Hawa alone. Surah Ṭā-Hā (20:121) reinforces this most explicitly: *wa 'aṣā Ādamu rabbahu fa-ghawā* — "Adam disobeyed his Lord and went astray." Attributing sole blame to Hawa has no Quranic or hadith basis and is textually false.

6. **Taba ilā** is the servant's act of turning back and repenting to Allah. **Taba 'alā** is Allah's act of accepting repentance — attributed **only to Allah**. Ayat 37 uses *fataba 'alayhi* (the *'alā* form), indicating that **Allah accepted Adam's repentance**. Before this acceptance, Allah had inspired Adam specific words — identified by the mufassirun as the du'ā in Surah Al-A'raf 7:23: *Rabbanā ẓalamnā anfusanā wa in lam taghfir lanā wa tarḥamnā la-nakūnanna minal-khāsirīn*.

**Vocabulary:**

- **خَلِيفَة** — Two interpretations: (1) Adam (AS) specifically as the first vicegerent on earth; (2) all of humanity as a continuous succession of generations, each succeeding the last, implementing Allah's rulings century after century.
- **سُجُودُ تَحِيَّةٍ** — Prostration of greeting and respect, not of worship. Sujudu 'ibadah (worship prostration) is exclusively for **Allah alone**; no other created being may receive it.
- **تَبْكِيت** — Rebuking/scolding. The purpose was to challenge the angels who had questioned humanity's fitness for khilafah — if they claimed superiority, they had to prove it.
- **تَلَقَّى** — To receive. Adam received specific words of supplication from his Lord — the du'ā of Surah Al-A'raf 7:23: *Rabbanā ẓalamnā anfusanā...*.
- **تَوَّاب** — Built on the **Ism al-Mubālaghah** pattern (*fa''āl*), denoting an intensive/superlative degree: Allah is *extremely and abundantly* accepting of repentance — *kathīru al-qabūl lit-tawbah*.
- **اسْتَكْبَرَ** — To become arrogant / to be haughty. The direct consequence: Iblis became *minal kāfirīn* — among the disbelievers.
- **مَتَاع** — Temporary enjoyment/benefit. In *mata'un ilā ḥīn*, it indicates that the pleasures of worldly life last only until one's appointed term — the world is not a permanent home.
- **أَلَا** — It is a **harf tanbi** (particle of alert/notice), drawing attention to what follows. It has **no position in *tarkīb*** — no i'rāb role in the sentence structure.

### Fiqh 201

1. The entire period — 4 days of bleeding, 10-day gap, and 3 days of bleeding — is classified as **one continuous Haid**. The reason: the gap between the two bleedings is **less than 15 days**. The governing rule: if the clean gap between two episodes of bleeding is **less than 15 days**, the entire period (both bleedings plus the gap) counts as a single Haid. The minimum valid Tuhar (purity interval) separating two distinct Haid episodes is **15 days**.

2. **Not permitted:** (1) Reciting the Qur'an — whether from memory, from a written copy, or by teaching recitation. (2) Entering the proper Masjid (the designated prayer area where the Imam leads). **Permitted:** (1) Making du'ā using Qur'anic phrases, as long as the **intention is du'ā** — not recitation of the Qur'an itself. (2) **Non-verbal correction** of a student's recitation mistake (gesture, hand signal) — no Qur'anic words are uttered, so this is permissible.

3. Correct ruling: the belt is **pure and permissible** for use and Salah. **Tanning (dabāghat) purifies any animal hide regardless of how the animal was killed** — Zabiha is not a condition. Multiple ahadith establish this. The only two exceptions where tanning does not purify: the hides of **swine (pigs)** and **humans**.

4. The three conditions (Muftabih/consensus opinion): (1) The sock must reach **above the ankle bone** and stay up on its own without sliding down. (2) The sock must be **water-resistant** — wet hands pressed against it should not cause water to seep through within approximately 2–3 seconds (the duration of normal wiping). (3) The sock must be **durable enough to walk approximately one mile** without tearing at the bottom.

5. **Stain below dirham size, discovered after prayer:** The prayer is **valid** and does not need to be repeated — the person was unaware during prayer. **Stain larger than dirham size, discovered only after prayer:** The prayer is still **valid** and need not be repeated, because the person was completely unaware while praying. Had they known about the stain exceeding the threshold before praying, the prayer would not have been permissible and would need to be repeated.

6. Madhi requires **Wudu only** — no Ghusl. This differs from **Mani (semen)**, which requires full Ghusl. The dalil for the ruling on Madhi is the hadith of **Ali ibn Abi Talib (RA)**: he frequently experienced this condition but was embarrassed to ask the Prophet ﷺ directly due to his relationship as Fatima's husband. He asked through another Sahabi, and the Prophet ﷺ replied that Ghusl is not required for Madhi — only washing the area and making Wudu.

**Vocabulary:**

- **طُهر** — Minimum duration: **15 days**. This is critical because any gap shorter than 15 days between two bleedings means the entire period (bleedings + gap) is counted as one Haid — it does not qualify as a valid purity interval separating two distinct episodes.
- **دَبَاغَة** — The process of tanning, which **purifies animal hides** and makes them ritually pure and permissible for use regardless of how the animal was killed. It does **not** purify the hides of **pigs** or **humans**.
- **مَسْح** — Wiping. In Wudu, it refers to wiping over footwear — **leather khuffain** or fabric **Jorabayn** — as a valid alternative to washing the feet, subject to its own conditions.
- **نَجَاسَت مُغَلَّظَة** — Two examples: human urine, blood (also: feces, Khamar, pus). Threshold below which Salah remains valid (with Makruh Tanzihi dislike): **dirham size** for liquid / **3.5 Masha** (~3.2–3.5 g) for solid.
- **نَجَاسَت مُخَفَّفَة** — Example: urine of Halal animals (cow, goat, etc.). Salah becomes impermissible at **one-fourth (¼) or more** of the garment; below that threshold, Salah is valid (with dislike).
- **وَدِي** — A thick discharge that sometimes appears after urination. It requires **Wudu only** — no Ghusl; wash the area and make a fresh Wudu.
- **مَرِيض** — In Tahara rulings, a chronically ill person with uncontrollable, continuous impurity makes **one fresh Wudu per Salah time** and may perform Salah and any acts of worship within that time period, regardless of ongoing discharge during it.
- **قَضَاء** — No Qadā is required. If a person prayed while **completely unaware** of bearing Najasat-e-Mughalaza — even above the pardoned threshold — the prayer is valid and need not be repeated. Qadā would be required only if they knowingly prayed with Najasat above the pardoned amount.
