## Nahw 201

*Sessions: Oct 12 & Oct 16*

**This week in a nutshell:** Both sessions focused on the mansubat (accusative nouns), covering obligatory verb-dropping constructions (tahdhir and ishtighaal), the five categories of the vocative (munada) and their i'rab rules, and three new maf'ul types: maf'ul fih (zarf), maf'ul lahu, plus tarkhim and mandub as extensions of the munada. The sessions build on each other — Monday established the munada framework and Thursday completed it while introducing the zarf and reason-adverb categories.

### Key Points

- **At-Tahdhir (warning construction):** The verb اِتَّقِ is dropped obligatorily; the warned person appears as إِيَّاكَ (maf'ul bihi of the dropped verb) and the danger follows with واو. The attached pronoun كَ becomes detached إِيَّاكَ precisely because attached pronouns cannot float without a host verb.
- **Al-Ishtighaal (occupied-verb construction):** When a fronted noun is followed by a verb already occupied with a back-referring pronoun (e.g., زَيْدًا ضَرَبْتُهُ), a second verb is mentally supplied as amil for the noun and then dropped obligatorily. A pronoun (ضمير) can never precede its referent (مرجع) — this is why reordering is impossible.
- **Munada categories — marfu' (mabni on damma):** (1) Mufrad ma'rifa — a non-muzaf definite noun, including a nakirah maqsuda (specific person addressed, even if the word is grammatically indefinite). (2) Note: "mufrad" here means *not-muzaf*, so duals and plurals that are not in construct state still count.
- **Munada categories — mansub:** Muzaf (يَا عَبْدَ اللهِ), shibhu al-muzaf (an ism fa'il needing a complement, e.g., يَا طَالِعًا جَبَلًا — the following word is mansoob as maf'ul bihi of the ism fa'il, not due to the nida itself), nakirah ghayr mu'ayyana (unspecified person, e.g., يَا رَجُلًا), and mu'arraf bil-lam (requires أَيُّهَا/أَيَّتُهَا as separator since harf nida cannot directly precede الـ).
- **Tarkhim:** Dropping the last letter(s) of a munada for ease (e.g., يَا مَالُ from مالك). The trimmed form takes either damma or its original underlying vowel (harakata asliyya).
- **Maf'ul fih / Zarf:** Always mansoob with في implied — except zarf makan mahdud (a defined, bounded place like مسجد), where في appears explicitly and the noun is majrur. Both zaman mubham and zaman mahdud keep في hidden and take nasb.
- **Maf'ul lahu:** Answers "why?" — the reason for the action; mansoob with لام التعليل implied (e.g., قَعَدْتُ عَنِ الْحَرْبِ جُبُنًا — "I stayed away from war out of cowardice").

### Easy to Mix Up

- **Mufrad in munada ≠ singular.** In the munada context, "mufrad" means *not-muzaf*. A dual (يَا زَيْدَانِ) or plural (يَا زَيْدُونَ) that is not in construct state is still called mufrad and takes the marfu'/mabni form, not nasb.
- **Shibhu al-muzaf vs. muzaf:** In يَا صَاحِبَ الْكِتَابِ (muzaf), the second word is majrur. In يَا طَالِعًا جَبَلًا (shibhu mudaf), the second word is mansoob as maf'ul bihi of the ism fa'il — not due to the nida.
- **Zarf makan mubham vs. mahdud:** Mubham places (خلف، أمام، فوق) keep في hidden and the noun is mansoob. Mahdud places (دار، مسجد) require في to appear explicitly and the noun becomes majrur.

## Tafseer 201

*Sessions: Oct 13 & Oct 15*

**This week in a nutshell:** Tuesday covered Ayaat 78–79 — the illiterate Jewish masses who blindly followed fabricated wishful thinking, and the corrupt scholars who deliberately distorted the Torah for worldly gain while attributing their fabrications to Allah. Thursday continued with Ayaat 80–86, refuting the Jews' claim that the Fire would only touch them briefly, then enumerating the specific crimes of Banu Isra'eel and the covenant they broke. Both sessions are connected: the first establishes who the distorters are and what they did; the second delivers the verdict and the evidence against them.

### Key Points

- **Al-ummiyyun (the illiterate masses):** They had no knowledge of the actual Torah — only amaniyy (wishful thinkings) instilled by their rabbis, such as: the hellfire will only touch them for a few days, their prophets will intercede for them, and they are the sons and beloved of Allah. Their certainty was merely ظَنّ (conjecture), not yaqin (certainty).
- **The double crime of the corrupt scholars (Ayah 79):** They combined two grave sins simultaneously — (1) tahrif al-kitab: distorting the Torah with their own hands, and (2) kidhb 'ala Allah: attributing their fabrications to Allah. Their motive was worldly gain (thamanan qalilan — a paltry price).
- **Refutation with balā (Ayah 80–81):** Allah negates the Jews' claim with the word بَلَى, which negates the previous false statement and affirms the true reality. The condition for eternal punishment: whoever earns evil AND whose sin encircles him (aḥāṭat bihi khaṭī'atuh) from all sides, blocking every pathway of salvation.
- **The covenant of Banu Isra'eel (mithaq):** A solemn covenant was taken from them in the Torah covering: tawhid, kindness to parents, care for relatives/orphans/poor, kind speech, humility, salah, and zakah.
- **Crimes enumerated (Ayaat 83–86):** Breaking the covenant, unlawful killing, seizing wealth unjustly, attacking and expelling their own brethren — earning la'nah (damnation), khizy (shame), and damar (destruction) as a consequence.
- **Morphology in the Quran:** The session included sarf analysis of مَسَّ يَمَسُّ (Bab Fa'ala-Yaf'alu, fa'il al-naru is mu'annas so the sighah is tamassu → tamassa after lan) and اِتَّخَذَ يَتَّخِذُ (Bab Ifti'al from أَخَذَ; the alif in أَتَّخَذْتُمْ is hamzah al-istifham, not part of the verb; hamzah al-wasl of ittakhaztum drops after it).

### Easy to Mix Up

- **بَلَى vs. نَعَم:** Both are translated as "yes," but they work differently. نَعَم confirms what was said. بَلَى negates a prior negative claim and asserts the opposite — it is used specifically to refute a false denial.
- **ummiyyun here ≠ non-Jews:** In this context, al-ummiyyun refers specifically to illiterate members of the Jewish community (juhhala al-'awam), not to Gentiles/non-Israelites — the more common use of the term elsewhere.

## Fiqh 201

*Sessions: Oct 14 & Oct 17*

**This week in a nutshell:** Wednesday completed the chapter on makruhat in salah and introduced the Hanafi ruling of bana (continuing salah after wudu breaks), covering its 13 conditions and the pivotal qa'dah akhirah principle. Saturday completed the Masa'il of 12, opened the chapter on making up missed prayers (qada al-fawait) including the rules of tartib and sahib-i-tarteeb, and covered the five makruh times for salah. Both sessions work as a pair: Wednesday builds the mechanics of salah interruption; Saturday builds the obligations that come after.

### Key Points

- **Makruhat in salah — general principle:** If an outside observer would think the musalli is not praying based on their movement, the movement is excessive. Specific prohibitions: playing with clothes/body, cracking fingers, takhassur (hands on waist), sadl (loose cloth draped freely), tucking garments, iqa' (sitting like a dog), verbal or gestured response to salam.
- **Bana (continuing salah after wudu breaks):** The Hanafi position permits leaving, making wudu, and returning to complete salah from the point of interruption — provided all 13 conditions are met. Key conditions: the hadath was involuntary, does not require ghusl, no speech was made, no unnecessary delay, no unnecessarily distant route taken.
- **Qa'dah akhirah principle:** Once a worshipper has sat for the minimum duration of the tashahud in the final sitting, the fundamental arkan of salah are complete. Any subsequent act — even deliberate breaking of wudu or speech — does not nullify the salah. (Sahibain's position is mufta bihi; Abu Hanifa held such acts make salah batil.)
- **Tartib (sequential order of qada):** A Sahib-i-Tarteeb (person with five or fewer missed prayers) must make up qada prayers in the order they were missed, before the current salah — unless (a) praying qada first would cause the current salah to also be missed, (b) he genuinely forgot, or (c) he has more than five qada (kasrat al-qada drops tartib entirely). Breaking tartib knowingly requires I'ada (repetition) of the current salah after the qada.
- **Sahib-i-Tarteeb threshold:** Tartib drops when the sixth salah becomes fully qada (time expires) — not when its time merely enters. When the qada count falls back to five, tartib immediately returns.
- **Three absolute prohibition times:** No salah of any kind (fard, nafl, qada, janazah, sajda-e-tilawat) during: sunrise (tulu' al-shams), sunset (ghurub al-shams), and the sun's zenith (istiwa'/zawal). Backed by explicit hadith in Muslim.
- **Two nawafil-restriction times:** After Fajr salah until sunrise, and after Asr salah until sunset — nawafil are prohibited but qada, janazah, and sajda-e-tilawat remain permitted.

### Easy to Mix Up

- **Bana conditions: involuntary hadath only.** If wudu is broken deliberately mid-salah, bana is not valid — the salah must be restarted entirely. Bana only applies when the hadath was beyond the person's control.
- **After Fajr/Asr: nawafil prohibited, but qada is fine.** These two are nawafil-restriction times, not absolute prohibition times. Qada prayers, janazah, and sajda-e-tilawat may still be performed in these windows — unlike the three absolute prohibition times where nothing at all is permitted.
- **Sahib-i-Tarteeb threshold — six qada, not five.** Tartib drops when you have *more than five* missed prayers — meaning after the sixth prayer's time fully expires (six are now qada). Having exactly five means tartib still applies.
