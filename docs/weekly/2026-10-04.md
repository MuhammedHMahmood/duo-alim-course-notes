## Tafseer 201

*Sessions: Sep 29, Oct 01 & Oct 03*

**This week in a nutshell:** The class moved through Surah Al-Baqarah, ayat 55–74, covering a dense sequence of Bani Israel's history: divine punishments and miracles in the field of Tih, the command to enter Bayt al-Maqdis and their mockery of it, and the opening of the famous cow story. The sessions are unified by a single theme — Allah's continuous bestowal of extraordinary favors met with continuous ingratitude, disobedience, and impudence from Bani Israel.

### Key Points

- **As-Sa'iqah (thunderbolt):** When the 70 chosen representatives of Bani Israel demanded to see Allah openly (*jahratan*) at Mount Tur, a thunderbolt struck and killed them. Musa (AS) wept and supplicated at length; Allah revived them after a full day and night, commanding gratitude (*la'allakum tashkurun*).
- **The field of Tih:** As punishment for refusing to fight the tyrants of Palestine, Bani Israel wandered a barren field for 40 years, unable to exit. Despite the punishment, Allah provided shade from clouds, *al-mann* (a honey-like liquid from tree leaves), and *as-salwa* (quail) — all without effort or labor.
- **Command to enter Bayt al-Maqdis:** They were told to enter prostrating (*sujjadan*), saying *Hittah* (seeking forgiveness), and eat abundantly (*raghadan*). The wrongdoers changed the word to a mocking phrase and entered crawling on their backsides; a plague (*ta'un*) killed 70,000 in one hour.
- **The rock and 12 springs:** In the Tih, Musa (AS) struck a rock with his staff at Allah's command; 12 springs gushed forth, one dedicated to each of the 12 tribes, preventing conflict over water.
- **Consequences imposed on Bani Israel (Ayah 61):** Humiliation (*dhull*) and wretchedness (*maskanah*) were imposed permanently, and they incurred Allah's wrath — because of their disbelief in Allah's signs, their killing of prophets, and their willful transgression.
- **Universal conditions for salvation (Ayah 62):** Regardless of religious label — Muslim, Jewish, Christian, or Sabian — salvation requires: (1) sincere belief in Allah, (2) belief in the Last Day, and (3) righteous deeds. Those who fulfill these conditions will have no fear and no grief in the Hereafter.
- **The Sabt incident (Ayat 65–66):** Bani Israel were prohibited from fishing on Saturday; they violated this and were transformed into monkeys (*qiradah khasi'in* — disgraced apes). This was a *nakal* — a deterrent for those who witnessed it and a warning for all who came after.
- **The cow story (Ayat 67–74):** A nephew murdered his wealthy uncle for the inheritance, then framed others; the community brought the case to Musa (AS). Allah's command was simple: slaughter any cow. But Bani Israel questioned repeatedly (type? color? further qualities?), and each round of questioning tightened the specifications upon themselves — *shaddadu fa-shaddada Allahu 'alayhim*. Had they simply obeyed, any ordinary cow would have sufficed. After finally slaughtering the correct cow, they struck the corpse with a part of it; the dead man revived, identified his killer, then fell dead again. The nephew was executed and inherited nothing.
- **The ruling of *al-qatil la yarith*:** A murderer does not inherit from the person he killed — an established principle of Islamic inheritance law derived from this story.

| السَّلْوَى (*as-salwā*) | Quail bird sent as food; delicious in taste |

### Easy to Mix Up

- **Dhull vs. maskanah:** Both are punishments imposed on Bani Israel, but *dhull* is outward humiliation and disgrace, while *maskanah* is wretchedness and abject poverty — two distinct consequences imposed together.
- **Sujjadan (command to enter) vs. Maskh (punishment for Sabt):** Both involve a form of physical lowering, but they are separate incidents — entering Bayt al-Maqdis prostrating was a command of gratitude that they mocked; the transformation into monkeys was a separate punishment for fishing on Saturday in a different era.

## Fiqh 201

*Sessions: Sep 30 & Oct 03*

**This week in a nutshell:** Both sessions were devoted to *Bab Sifat al-Salah* — the detailed mechanics of how Salah is performed. The first session covered the six obligatory pillars (*Fara'id*), Takbir al-Tahreemah through the second Sajdah. The second session picked up with the second Rak'ah onward, covering sitting positions, Tashahud, Tasleem, Sajda Sahu, the 25 differences between men's and women's Salah, and the distinction between Jahri and Sirri Salah.

### Key Points

- **The six Fara'id of Salah:** Takbir al-Tahreemah, Qiyam (standing), Qira'at (recitation), Ruku (bowing), Sujood (prostration), and Al-Qad'ah al-Akhirah (final sitting for the duration of Tashahud). These are the pillars without which Salah is invalid.
- **Classification of acts:** A *Fard* omitted — even accidentally — cannot be recovered by Sajda Sahu; the Salah must be repeated. A *Wajib* omitted accidentally is covered by Sajda Sahu; a *Sunnah* requires neither. One set of Sajda Sahu covers any number of missed Wajibat in a single Salah.
- **Ta'deel al-Arkan — the most overlooked Wajib:** After rising from Ruku, the musalli must stand fully upright and still before going into Sajdah. Between the two Sajdahs, a proper sit with stillness (*itmi'nan*) is required. Per Ahnaf this is *Wajib*; per Imam Shafi'i and Imam Abu Yusuf, it is *Fard*. An Imam who skips these pauses may affect the validity of the entire congregation's Salah.
- **Seven bones in Sajdah:** Forehead and nose tip, both hands, both knees, and toes of both feet must touch the ground. Ahnaf (Imam Abu Hanifah): either forehead or nose suffices. Sahibayn (Imam Abu Yusuf and Imam Muhammad) — the operative *mufta bihi* position: both must touch without excuse. All scholars require at minimum the soft tip of the nose.
- **Tashahud and sitting positions:** The *recitation* of Tashahud is Wajib; the *sitting* itself is Fard. In Qada al-Ula (the first sitting in a three- or four-Rak'ah Salah), one rises immediately after completing Tashahud — adding Darud here is an error requiring Sajda Sahu. In Qada al-Akhira, Darud is Sunnah and Du'a (from Quran or Masnoon wordings only) follows. The correct sitting position throughout is *Iftirash* per Ahnaf.
- **Tasleem is Wajib, not Fard:** Salah is technically complete at the end of Tashahud; Salam is required but its omission leaves a "deficient Salah" (*Salatul Nakas*) rather than voiding it entirely. The Prophet ﷺ turned so completely that those seated behind him could see the whiteness of his cheek.
- **Du'a within Salah:** Only Quranic and Masnoon wordings are permitted. Using one's own language or composing personal requests in one's own words renders the Salah *Fasid* (void). After Salam, any Du'a in any language is freely permitted.
- **Jahri vs. Sirri Salah:** Fajr, and the first two Rak'ahs of Maghrib and Isha are recited loudly (*Jahri*); Zuhr, Asr, and the remaining Rak'ahs of Maghrib and Isha are recited quietly (*Sirri*). The distinction was established by Surah Al-Isra: *"Wa la tajhar bi salatika wa la tukhafid biha"* — during early Makkah, the Prophet ﷺ recited loudly and the Mushrikeen would insult and disrupt; this ayah guided toward a middle path.
- **25 differences in women's Salah:** Key ones include — raising hands only to shoulder/chest level (not earlobes); placing hands on the chest in Qiyam; bowing only enough for hands to reach the knees with back not fully horizontal; contracting the body fully in Sajdah with arms on the ground; sitting on the left buttock with feet extended to the right rather than Iftirash; alerting the Imam by clapping rather than saying Subhanallah; Jumu'ah and Eid prayers are not obligatory; congregational prayer for women alone is Makrooh; women cannot lead men.

### Vocabulary to Know

| Arabic Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| فَرَائِض (*Farā'id*) | Obligatory pillars of Salah; their omission invalidates it |
| وَاجِب (*Wājib*) | Necessary act; unintentional omission covered by Sajda Sahu |
| تَعْدِيلُ الْأَرْكَان (*Ta'dīl al-Arkān*) | Giving each posture its due stillness and measured pause |

| تَكْبِيرَةُ التَّحْرِيمَة (*Takbīr al-Taḥrīmah*) | The opening "Allahu Akbar" that begins Salah |

| تَوَرُّك (*Tawarruk*) | Sitting with left foot extended out to the right, sitting on the ground; preferred by Shafi'i and Maliki |

| سِرِّي (*Sirrī*) | Quiet recitation (lips and tongue move; audible only to oneself) |

### Easy to Mix Up

- **Fard vs. Wajib in Salah:** Both are obligatory, but a missed *Fard* cannot be compensated — the Salah must be repeated. A missed *Wajib* (if unintentional) is covered by Sajda Sahu. Surah Fatihah is Wajib per Ahnaf (Fard per Shafi'i); Tasleem is Wajib per Ahnaf (not Fard). This distinction has major practical consequences.
- **Qada al-Ula vs. Qada al-Akhira:** Both use Iftirash and both require completing Tashahud. But in Qada al-Ula only Tashahud is recited — one must rise immediately after; Darud and Du'a are reserved for Qada al-Akhira alone.

## Nahw 201

*Sessions: Oct 02*

**This week in a nutshell:** The session covered two connected topics: the four forms of Khabar (predicate) and the rules governing each, including the important exception allowing a *Sifat* to become Mubtada after a particle of negation. The session then introduced the *Nawasikh* — specifically Inna and her sisters — and how they alter the I'rab of the Mubtada-Khabar structure, including the restriction on fronting the Khabar of Inna.

### Key Points

- **Khabar has four forms:** Mufrad (single word), Jumlah Ismiyya (nominal sentence), Jumlah Fi'liyya (verbal sentence), Jumlah Shartiyya (conditional sentence), and Zarfiyya/Jar-Majrur (adverbial). All are valid as Khabar.
- **The Damir rule:** Whenever Khabar is a Jumlah (any of the four sentence types), there must be a *Damir* (pronoun) inside the Khabar referring back to the Mubtada. This pronoun is what logically links the two parts of the sentence.
- **Zarfiyya Khabar and the hidden verb:** When the Khabar is a Zarf or Jar-Majrur, it is *muta'alliq* (linked) to an implied verb — *istaqarra* (to be present/stay) — which is *mahzoov* (omitted). So زَيْدٌ فِي الدَّارِ means زَيْدٌ اسْتَقَرَّ فِي الدَّارِ.
- **Sifat as Mubtada — the exception:** Normally a Musnad (predicate element) cannot become Mubtada (subject), but three conditions enable this exception: (1) the word must be a *Sighat Sifat* (Ism Fa'il, Ism Maf'ul, Sifat Mushabbaha, or Ism Tafdhil); (2) it must come after a *Harf Nafi* (مَا) or *Harf Istifham* (هَلْ); (3) it must give *Rafa'* to the Ism Zahir that follows it. When all three are met, the Sifat is Mubtada and the Ism Zahir is Khabar.
- **Key diagnostic test — matching vs. not matching:** If the Sifat stays *Wahid* (singular) regardless of what follows it (e.g., مَا قَائِمٌ الزَّيْدَانِ — Sifat stays singular even though Khabar is dual), then the Sifat is Mubtada acting as Amil. If the Sifat **matches** the following noun in number and gender (e.g., مَا قَائِمَتَانِ الْبِنْتَانِ — both are dual), then the Sifat is Khabar and the noun is Mubtada. This mirrors how a Fi'l stays Wahid regardless of its Fa'il's number (ذَهَبَ الزَّيْدَانِ).
- **Inna and her sisters — effect:** These Huruf enter upon Mubtada-Khabar and change the Mubtada to *Mansoob* (called Ism Inna), while the Khabar remains *Marfoo'* (called Khabar Inna). All four forms of Khabar (Mufrad, Jumlah, Shartiyya, Zarfiyya) still apply to Khabar Inna — nothing changes about the nature of the Khabar itself.
- **Restriction on Taqdim of Khabar Inna:** Unlike regular Mubtada-Khabar (where Khabar can be freely fronted), the Khabar of Inna **cannot** be placed before the Ism Inna — unless the Khabar is a Zarf or Jar-Majrur. إِنَّ فِي الدَّارِ زَيْدًا is valid; إِنَّ قَائِمًا زَيْدًا is not.

### Vocabulary to Know

| Arabic Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| الْخَبَر (*Khabar*) | Predicate / comment — what is said about the Mubtada |
| الْمُبْتَدَأ (*Mubtada'*) | Subject / topic of the sentence |
| ظَرْفِيَّة (*Ẓarfiyya*) | Adverbial Khabar — a Zarf or Jar-Majrur serving as predicate |
| اسْتَقَرَّ (*istaqarra*) | To be present / to stay — the implied verb underlying a Zarfiyya Khabar |
| مَحْذُوف (*maḥdhūf*) | Omitted, implied — not explicitly stated in the sentence |
| صِفَة (*Ṣifah*) | Descriptive word — includes Ism Fa'il, Ism Maf'ul, Sifat Mushabbaha, Ism Tafdhil |
| النَّوَاسِخ (*Nawāsikh*) | Abrogators — words that alter the I'rab of Mubtada-Khabar |
| إِنَّ وَأَخَوَاتُهَا (*Inna wa Akhwātuhā*) | "Inna and her sisters" — Huruf that give Nasb to the Mubtada |
| اسم إِنَّ (*Ism Inna*) | The Mubtada after Inna — now Mansoob |
| مُقَدَّم (*Muqaddam*) | Fronted — placed before its normal position |
| عَامِل (*'Āmil*) | The governing word that causes a grammatical effect |
| حَرْف نَفِي (*Ḥarf Nafī*) | Particle of negation — e.g., مَا |

### Easy to Mix Up

- **Sifat as Mubtada vs. Sifat as Khabar:** The single most reliable test is number-gender matching. If the Sifat matches the following noun (dual with dual, feminine with feminine, etc.), the Sifat is Khabar and the noun is Mubtada. If the Sifat stays singular (*Wahid*) while the following noun is dual or plural, the Sifat is Mubtada acting as Amil. Never rely on word order alone.
- **Taqdim of Khabar Mubtada vs. Khabar Inna:** Regular Khabar can be freely fronted before its Mubtada. Khabar of Inna cannot be fronted in front of Ism Inna — the only exception is when the Khabar is a Zarf or Jar-Majrur, since Zuruf are grammatically flexible.
