# Tafseer 201 - Study Notes
**Date:** 2026-10-03
**Surah Covered:** Surah Al-Baqarah, Ayat 63–74

## Session Overview

This session covered two sets of ayat from Surah Al-Baqarah: ayat 63–66 and the beginning of ayat 67–74. The first set deals with the covenant Allah took from Bani Israel at Mount Tur and the consequences of their transgression on the day of Sabt (Saturday), including their transformation into monkeys. The second set introduces the famous story of the cow (Al-Baqarah), which gives the surah its name — a murder mystery among Bani Israel resolved through a divine miracle involving a slaughtered cow restoring a dead man to life long enough to identify his killer.

---

## Key Themes and Topics

1. **The Covenant at Mount Tur** — Allah lifted the mountain above Bani Israel as a warning and took a firm covenant that they would act upon the Torah.
2. **Breaking the Covenant** — Bani Israel turned away from the agreement after accepting it; only Allah's grace saved them from total destruction.
3. **Transgression on the Day of Sabt** — Bani Israel disobeyed Allah's prohibition against fishing on Saturday; as punishment, Allah transformed them into monkeys.
4. **Transformation as a Deterrent** — This punishment served as a *nakal* (deterrent) for those who witnessed it and a lesson for all who came after.
5. **Evil Characteristics of Bani Israel** — The session transitions into enumerating their ongoing crimes: breaking covenants, transgressing on Sabt, disobeying prophets, stubbornness, and harshness toward their own prophet Musa (AS).
6. **The Story of the Cow** — A murder was committed by a nephew who killed his uncle for inheritance; Bani Israel's excessive questioning when commanded to simply slaughter a cow made a straightforward command increasingly difficult upon themselves.
7. **The Ruling on Murderer and Inheritance** — A killer does not inherit from the one he killed.

## Main Tafseer Points and Explanations

### Ayat 63–66: The Covenant at Mount Tur and the Punishment of Sabt

**Ayah 63 — The Covenant:**
- Allah reminds Bani Israel: *"Remember when We took a firm covenant from you."*
- The covenant was to act upon the Torah (*bima fi at-Tawrat*) — to follow all of Allah's rulings mentioned in it.
- Allah raised Mount Tur above their heads until it became like a canopy (*ka-al-dhulla*) over them.
- Under this threat, they were commanded: *"Take what We have given you with firmness (bi-quwwah)"* — meaning act upon it with diligence and determination.
- They were told to remember and preserve the contents of the Torah and not neglect it.
- Purpose: *"La'allakum tattaqun"* — so that they may be protected from destruction in this world and in the Hereafter, and be among the righteous.

**Ayah 64 — Turning Away:**
- Despite taking the covenant, Bani Israel turned away (*tawalla yatawalla tawalli*) and broke it.
- Allah states that if not for His grace (*fadl*) and mercy in accepting their repentance (*tawbah*), they would have been among the losers (*minal khasirin*) — destroyed in this world and punished in the Hereafter.

**Ayah 65–66 — Transgression on the Day of Sabt:**
- *"You know those among you who transgressed in the matter of the Sabt."*
- Bani Israel were explicitly prohibited from fishing on Saturday. They violated this command by hunting/fishing on that day.
- Allah's punishment: He transformed (*maskh*) them from human beings into monkeys (*qiradah*) — not a dignified transformation, but one accompanied by humiliation and disgrace (*khasi'in*).
- This transformation was a *nakal* — a deterrent punishment:
  - *Lima bayna yadayha* — a lesson for those who were present and witnessed it.
  - *Wa ma khalfaha* — a lesson for all those who came after them, including us today.
  - *Maw'izatun lil-muttaqin* — a special admonition for the righteous; a reminder (*dhikr*) for every righteous servant of Allah.
- The teacher noted: when hearing this, one should feel moved to seek forgiveness from Allah and ask to be protected from such transformation — a reminder that we are the best of creation, and disobedience can cause loss of this honor.

### Ayat 67–74: The Story of the Cow (*Qissat Al-Baqarah*)

**Munasabah (connection):**
After mentioning the crimes of Bani Israel — breaking covenants, transgression on Sabt, and rebellion against Allah's rulings — Allah now adds another dimension of their evil: their disobedience toward their prophets, their *lajaj* (obstinacy), and their *jafa'* (harshness) in speaking to Prophet Musa (AS).

**Background of the Story (narrated from Ibn Abi Hatim on the authority of 'Ubaydah al-Salmani):**
- A man from Bani Israel was sterile (had no children) but possessed great wealth.
- His nephew was his heir/inheritor (*warith*).
- The nephew, wanting the inheritance, murdered his uncle.
- He then secretly carried the body at night and placed it at the door of others, then accused them of the murder.
- The community fell into conflict, nearly going to war (*rakiba ba'duhum 'ala ba'd*).
- Wise elders stopped them: *"Why kill each other? We have a prophet among us — let us go to Musa."*
- They came to Musa (AS) and explained the situation, asking him to identify the killer.

**Musa's Response and the Divine Command:**
- Musa (AS) prayed to Allah, who revealed the solution: *"Inna Allaha ya'murukum an tadbahu baqarah"* — Allah commands you to slaughter a cow.
- This was a simple, straightforward command. Any ordinary cow would have sufficed.
- Key principle stated by the mufassirun: *"Wa law lam ya'taridhu la-ajza'at 'anhum adna baqarah"* — Had they not objected, the most ordinary cow would have been sufficient.
- But Bani Israel made it difficult upon themselves (*shaddadu fa-shaddada Allahu 'alayhim*) — they were strict in their questioning, so Allah became strict with them in His requirements.

**The Three Rounds of Questioning:**

| Round | Their Question | Allah's Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | *"Are you mocking us?"* then *"What kind of cow?"* | Middle-aged — neither old (*farid*) nor virgin-young (*bikr*); *'awan bayna dhalik* |
| 2nd | *"What color?"* | Yellow (*safra' faqi'un lawnuha*) — bright, vivid yellow that pleases all who look at it |
| 3rd | *"Further clarify it for us — many cows match this description"* | Additional qualities making it unique (to be continued next class) |

**Their Initial Response — Impudence Toward the Prophet:**
- When Musa (AS) conveyed the command, they said: *"Atattakhidhuna huzuwan?"* — *"Do you mock us?"*
- This was an extremely rude and impudent response to their prophet.
- Musa (AS) replied: *"A'udhu billahi an akuna minal jahilin"* — *"I seek refuge in Allah from being among the ignorant."* Meaning: only an ignorant fool mocks people; I am a prophet of Allah.

**The Role of "Insha'Allah":**
- After the third round of questioning, they finally said: *"Wa inna insha'Allah la-muhtadun"* — *"Insha'Allah, we will be guided."*
- A hadith states: Had they not said "Insha'Allah," they would never have been guided to the right cow and the questioning would have continued endlessly.
- With the help of Allah (through saying Insha'Allah), they eventually found the cow — owned by a man who had no other cow.

**The Cow's Owner and the Price:**
- The owner refused to sell for less than the skin of the cow filled with gold.
- They purchased it at that enormous price.
- They slaughtered it, struck the dead body with a part of the cow, and the dead man came back to life.
- He pointed to his nephew as the killer, then fell dead again.
- The nephew was taken and executed; he received no share of the inheritance.

**The Ruling Established:**
- A murderer does not inherit from the person he killed (*al-qatil la yarith*).
- This is an established principle of Islamic inheritance law (*fiqh al-mawarith*).

## Grammar and Language Notes

**1. Tamarud vs. Isyan:**
- *Isyan* = disobedience (general)
- *Tamarud* = active rebellion; going beyond disobedience to openly defy commands

**2. Lajja yalujju — verb conjugation:**
- Root: ل-ج-ج
- *Lajja yalujju lajjan / lajajan* — to be obstinate
- On the pattern of *Muf'alah*: *Lajja yulajju mulajjatan wal-lijajan*
- Analogous to: *Hajja yuhajj muhajjatan / hijajan*
- Meaning of *lajaj*: verbal obstinacy, fighting with words

**3. Jafa'un fi mukhataba:**
- *Jafa'* = harshness; *mukhataba* = speech/address
- Describing how Bani Israel spoke harshly to Prophet Musa (AS)

**4. "Udu'u lana rabbaka yubayyin lana" — Why is *yubayyin* majzum (not *yubayyinu*)?**
- *Udu'u* is a command (*sighat al-amr*)
- *Yubayyin* is the response/result of that command (*jawab al-amr*)
- The rule: a *mudari'* verb following a command as its *jawab* is *majzum* (has sukun/jazm)
- Pattern: امر + جواب الأمر (مجزوم)
- Similar to: *"Go there and you will find it"* — the result clause is majzum

**5. Safra' / Asfar:**
- *Asfar* is the masculine form (mudhakar)
- *Safra'* is the feminine form (mu'annath) — used here because *baqarah* (cow) is feminine

**6. Faqi'un lawnuha:**
- *Faqi'* = vivid, intense, bright
- Modifies the yellow color to mean a striking, bright yellow

**7. La'allakum tattaqun — Two interpretations:**
- *La'alla* here means: so that you may be protected (*tattaqun al-halaka fi dunya wal-akhira*) — from destruction in this world and punishment in the Hereafter
- OR: so that you may be among the righteous, the group of good people

## Lessons and Practical Takeaways

1. **Covenants with Allah are serious.** Bani Israel's example shows that turning away from agreements with Allah after making them leads to severe consequences. We must be people of our word with Allah.

2. **Obedience should be immediate and wholehearted.** When Allah commands something simple, we must act — not question, delay, or demand more details to avoid compliance.

3. **Making religion difficult upon yourself backfires.** *"Shaddadu fa-shaddada Allahu 'alayhim"* — they were strict in their questioning, so Allah was strict in His requirements. Had they simply obeyed, any cow would have done.

4. **The power of "Insha'Allah."** Saying Insha'Allah sincerely invites Allah's help and guidance. It is not a mere phrase but an acknowledgment of dependence on Allah.

5. **The lesson of maskh (transformation).** We are the best of creation (*ashraf al-makhluqat*). Disobedience to Allah's commands risks dishonoring that status. When hearing the story of transformation into monkeys, one should seek Allah's refuge and be moved toward greater obedience.

6. **Deterrent punishments serve the community.** *Nakal* — a public, severe punishment — protects society by warning others. The transformation of Bani Israel was meant to deter all future generations.

7. **Greed leads to destruction.** The nephew killed his uncle for inheritance and gained nothing — he was executed and stripped of all inheritance rights.

8. **Harshness toward scholars and religious leaders is a sign of spiritual corruption.** Bani Israel's rude response to Musa (AS) was itself a symptom of their diseased hearts.

9. **The killer inherits nothing from his victim** — an important principle of Islamic law derived from this story.

## Historical Context and Stories Mentioned

**The Sabt (Sabbath) Incident:**
Bani Israel were commanded not to fish or hunt on Saturday. Fish would reportedly appear abundantly on the surface on Saturdays and disappear on other days — a test from Allah. Those who violated this by fishing on Saturday were transformed by Allah into monkeys (*qiradah khasi'in* — disgraced apes). This punishment serves as a warning across all of human history.

**The Murder Case (background of Surah Al-Baqarah's central story):**
- A sterile, wealthy man of Bani Israel was murdered by his nephew and heir.
- The nephew framed others by placing the body at their door.
- The community nearly went to civil war.
- They brought the matter to Musa (AS), who received divine guidance: slaughter a specific cow, touch the corpse with it, and the dead man will reveal his killer.
- The story demonstrates both a divine miracle (revival of the dead) and the obstinacy of Bani Israel in following even the simplest command.
- The cow was ultimately found with a man who owned only that one cow and priced it at its skin's weight in gold — which they paid.
- The dead man revived, identified his nephew, then died again. The nephew was executed and disinherited.

**Note:** The teacher mentioned that the surah is named *Al-Baqarah* (The Cow) after this very story — the most detailed single story in the surah and one of the most elaborate in the Quran.

## Q&A Highlights

**Q: When it says "kufr," does that mean disbelief?**
A: Yes, *kufr* literally means disbelief/ingratitude. In the context of *kafaru al-ni'mah*, it means they were ungrateful to Allah's blessings — rejecting them through ingratitude and breaking covenants.

**Q: Does a murderer lose all inheritance rights, or only from the person he killed?**
A: The ruling in Sharia is specific: if a person kills someone from whom they would have inherited, they are disqualified from that inheritance. So the nephew who killed his uncle lost his right to inherit from that uncle specifically. The principle (*al-qatil la yarith*) applies to the murdered person's estate.

**Q: Why is *yubayyin* majzum in the phrase *"Udu'u lana rabbaka yubayyin lana"*?**
A: Because it is the *jawab al-amr* (the result clause following a command). When a *mudari'* verb comes as the response/consequence of a command (*amr*), it takes *jazm* (sukun). The *amr* here is *udu'u* (call upon), and *yubayyin* is what will happen as a result — hence it is *majzum*.
