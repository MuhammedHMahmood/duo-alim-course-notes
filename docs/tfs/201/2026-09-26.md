# Tafseer 201 - Study Notes
**Date:** 2026-09-26
**Surah Covered:** Surah Al-Baqarah, Verses 49–54 (with introduction to 55–59)

---

## Session Overview

This session covered the tafseer of Surah Al-Baqarah, verses 49–54, focusing on Allah's recounting of His specific blessings upon Bani Israel. The munasabah (contextual connection) was established: whereas previous ayat mentioned Allah's blessings in general, these ayat detail each blessing individually — a rhetorical escalation meant to intensify gratitude. The blessings covered include: salvation from Fir'aun's tyranny, the parting of the sea, the promise to Musa (AS) of the Tawrat over 40 nights, the pardoning after the worship of the calf (Ijl), and the giving of the Kitab and Furqan. The session closed with an introduction to verses 55–59, describing Bani Israel's continued ingratitude and how Allah nonetheless treated them with kindness.

---

## Key Themes and Topics

1. **Munasabah (Contextual Link):** The transition from general mention of blessings to specific enumeration — each blessing introduced with وَإِذْ ("and when/remember when") — makes the reminder more eloquent and more conducive to gratitude.
2. **Salvation from Fir'aun:** The address is to the Bani Israel contemporaneous with Prophet Muhammad (ﷺ), yet the blessing is attributed to their forefathers — establishing the principle that a blessing upon the father is a blessing upon the son.
3. **Parting of the Sea:** A miraculous divine intervention — the sea was split so that dry land appeared, Bani Israel crossed safely, and Fir'aun's army was drowned before their eyes.
4. **The 40 Nights and the Tawrat:** Musa (AS) was promised the Tawrat after 40 nights on Mount Tur. The Tawrat was given as a Furqan — a criterion distinguishing haqq from batil.
5. **Worship of the Calf (Ijl):** During Musa's (AS) absence, Bani Israel committed the grave sin of worshipping the calf. Allah called this an extremely ugly (mutanahi fi al-qubh) act.
6. **Tawbah and Pardon:** Musa (AS) commanded his people to repent; the prescribed tawbah involved the innocent killing the guilty among them. Upon their submission to Allah's command, Allah accepted their repentance.
7. **The 70 Men at Mount Tur:** After the tawbah from worshipping the calf, Musa (AS) selected 70 of the best men to go to Tur Sina' to present their apology before Allah. There, they heard Allah speak to Musa (AS), and then made the audacious request to see Allah directly.
8. **Ingratitude as a Pattern:** Despite repeated blessings and pardons, Bani Israel continued in kufr and disobedience, yet Allah continued treating them with lutf (kindness) and ihsan (goodness).

## Main Tafseer Points and Explanations

### Verse 49 — وَإِذْ نَجَّيْنَاكُم مِّنْ آلِ فِرْعَوْنَ

- The repeated use of وَإِذْ throughout these ayat functions as أُذْكُرُوا نِعْمَتِي — "Remember my blessing at the time when..."
- The address is to Bani Israel at the time of the Prophet (ﷺ), yet the event (salvation from Fir'aun) happened to their ancestors. This is valid because a blessing upon the father is considered a blessing upon the son — the generations share in the honor and debt of gratitude.
- آل فِرْعَون: The word آل derives from أَهْل (family/people). It encompasses Fir'aun's family, his close associates, his followers, and his army. Here it specifically refers to Fir'aun's forces and his brutal regime.
- يَسُومُونَكُمْ سُوءَ الْعَذَابِ — Fir'aun's regime inflicted the most severe torment: they slaughtered the male children of Bani Israel and kept the females alive — the females were spared to be used in service (اسْتَبْقَى).
- وَفِي ذَلِكُمْ بَلَاءٌ مِنْ رَبِّكُمْ عَظِيمٌ — "And in that was a great trial from your Lord." The oppression itself was Allah's trial over them. The purpose of the trial: to distinguish the righteous (صَالِح) from the wicked (طَالِح/فَاجِر).

### Verse 50 — وَإِذْ فَرَقْنَا بِكُمُ الْبَحْرَ

- Allah parted the sea for Bani Israel when they reached the shore fleeing Fir'aun, until the dry earth appeared beneath the water (حَتَّى أَظْهَرَتْ لَكُمُ الْأَرْضُ يَابِسًا).
- They crossed on dry land. Fir'aun and his army were drowned — and Bani Israel witnessed this drowning with their own eyes (وَأَنْتُمْ تَنْظُرُونَ).
- وَكَانَ ذَلِكَ آيَةً بَاهِرَةً — This event was a dazzling, overwhelming sign of Allah's power: saving His awliya' (friends) and destroying His enemies.

### Verse 51 — وَإِذْ وَاعَدْنَا مُوسَى أَرْبَعِينَ لَيْلَةً

- After the salvation from Fir'aun and the crossing of the sea, Allah promised Musa (AS) that He would give him the Tawrat after 40 nights on Mount Tur.
- During Musa's (AS) absence, Bani Israel took the calf (عِجْل/كَاف) as an object of worship — an act described as ظُلْم (wrongdoing/oppression) against themselves.

### Verse 52 — ثُمَّ عَفَوْنَا عَنكُم مِّن بَعْدِ ذَلِكَ

- Despite this heinous (جَرِيمَة شَنِيعَة) and extremely ugly (مُتَنَاهٍ فِي الْقُبْح) act of calf worship, Allah pardoned them (تَجَاوَزَ عَنْ تِلْكَ الْجَرِيمَة).
- Purpose of the pardon: لَعَلَّكُمْ تَشْكُرُونَ — so that they might be grateful and continue in obedience to Allah, worship of Allah alone, seeking His forgiveness, and leaving idol worship.

### Verse 53 — وَإِذْ آتَيْنَا مُوسَى الْكِتَابَ وَالْفُرْقَانَ

- The Kitab is the Tawrat. The Furqan is also the Tawrat, described by its function: the criterion that distinguishes haqq from batil, truth from falsehood.
- أَيَّدَهُ بِالْمُعْجِزَات — Allah also supported Musa (AS) with miracles to certify his prophethood.
- Purpose: لَعَلَّكُمْ تَهْتَدُونَ — so they may be guided. Guidance was to be achieved by: (1) deep contemplation (تَدَبُّر) of the Tawrat, and (2) acting upon its rulings (أَحْكَام).

### Verse 54 — كَيْفِيَّة الْعَفْو (The Manner of the Pardon)

- Imam al-Tabari (رحمه الله) narrates: After Bani Israel repented from worshipping the calf, Allah commanded Musa (AS) to select 70 of the best men from among his people to go and present their apology (اعْتِذَار) to Allah for the sin of calf worship.
- Musa (AS) selected 70 of the best men (مِنْ خِيَارِ قَوْمِهِ). He instructed them to fast and purify their garments. He then led them out to Tur Sina'.
- At the mountain: clouds (غَمَام) fell upon Musa and covered the entire mountain. The 70 men entered the clouds, prostrated, and heard Allah speaking to Musa (AS) — commanding and forbidding.
- After the clouds cleared, the 70 men made an additional and audacious request: لَنْ نُؤْمِنَ لَكَ حَتَّى نَرَى اللَّهَ جَهْرَةً — "We will not believe you until we see Allah openly with our own eyes." (Tafseer of this will be covered in the next session.)
- The prescribed tawbah for calf worship: فَتُوبُوا إِلَى بَارِئِكُمْ فَاقْتُلُوا أَنفُسَكُمْ — "Repent to your Creator and kill yourselves (i.e., kill each other)." The innocent (بَرِيء) among them were to kill the guilty (مُجْرِم). This was the divinely ordained form of expiation for that particular sin. Submitting willingly to this command was itself an act of rida' bi-hukm Allah (acceptance of Allah's ruling), which is خَيْرٌ لَكُمْ عِنْدَ اللَّهِ — better for them in Allah's sight. When they carried it out, Allah accepted their tawbah: فَتَابَ عَلَيْكُمْ — for إِنَّهُ هُوَ التَّوَّابُ الرَّحِيمُ.

### Introduction to Verses 55–59

- After enumerating His blessings and their pardon, Allah now transitions to detailing Bani Israel's pattern of oppression (ظُلْم), ingratitude (كُفْر النِّعْمَة), and changing (تَبْدِيل) the commands of Allah.
- The remarkable observation: despite their kufr and 'isyan (disobedience), Allah continued to treat them with لُطْف (kindness) and إِحْسَان (goodness) — prompting the rhetorical exclamation: فَمَا أَقْبَحَهُمْ مِنْ أُمَّةٍ وَمَا أَخْزَاهُمْ — "How ugly a nation they are and how disgraceful!"

## Grammar and Language Notes

- **نَجَّ يُنَجِّ تَنْجِيَةً** — Bab Taf'eel (فَعَّلَ). The shaddah on the 'ayn indicates intensification or transitivity (making someone reach safety).
- **وَإِذْ** — Repeated throughout these ayat. It functions as an adverb of time, with the understood verb أُذْكُرُوا preceding it. Pattern: وَإِذْ = أُذْكُرُوا نِعْمَتِي إِذْ.
- **يَسْتَحْيُونَ** — Bab Istif'al (اسْتَفْعَلَ) from the root ح-ي-ي (hayya). The root means life; Bab Istif'al gives the meaning "to seek to keep alive / to spare (someone's life)."
- **اسْتَبْقَى** — Also Bab Istif'al, from the root ب-ق-ي (baqiya). Means to keep someone alive/spare them (for use/service).
- **عَفَا يَعْفُو عَفْوًا** — Follows the same conjugation pattern as دَعَا يَدْعُو دَعْوَةً (Bab Afa'al of hollow verbs with waw as the 'ayn).
- **أَيَّدَ يُؤَيِّدُ تَأْيِيدًا** — Bab Taf'eel. Root: أ-ي-د or ي-و-د. The hamza appears upon the waw in the present tense form.
- **سَلَّطَ يُسَلِّطُ تَسْلِيطًا** — Bab Taf'eel. Means to give power/authority over someone (transitive).
- **بَارِئ** — Active participle (اسم الفاعل) from بَرَأَ يَبْرَأُ — one who creates/originates. Also carries the meaning of freedom from defect or fault. One of the Divine Names (الأَسْمَاء الحُسْنَى).
- **تَوَّاب** — Intensive (مُبَالَغَة) form on the pattern فَعَّال, from تَابَ. Indicates Allah's attribute of accepting repentance abundantly and repeatedly.
- **لَعَلَّكُمْ** — Particle of hope/purpose. In Quranic usage when Allah is the speaker, it expresses the intended result: "so that you may..." Here it appears twice: لَعَلَّكُمْ تَشْكُرُونَ (so you may be grateful) and لَعَلَّكُمْ تَهْتَدُونَ (so you may be guided).
- **آل vs. أَهْل** — The word آل is originally أَهْل. It refers to family, close associates, followers, and the army of a ruler or leader. Distinct from the broader أَهْل in formal usage.

## Lessons and Practical Takeaways

1. **Specificity increases gratitude.** Allah enumerates blessings one by one rather than speaking of them in general — a pedagogical model for reflecting on blessings. The more specific we are in recounting a ni'mah, the more powerful the recognition of gratitude becomes.

2. **Blessing upon the father is blessing upon the son.** We inherit spiritual debts of gratitude. Recognizing the blessings Allah bestowed upon our predecessors should inspire our own shukr.

3. **Trials are purposeful.** The severe oppression of Fir'aun was Allah's test (بَلَاء) over Bani Israel — intended to distinguish the righteous from the wicked. Every hardship has a divine wisdom behind it.

4. **Tawbah has conditions.** Repentance is not merely verbal. For Bani Israel, the prescribed tawbah after calf worship involved a difficult, physical act of submission to Allah's command. Willingness to submit to Allah's ruling is itself part of sincere tawbah.

5. **Allah's mercy vastly exceeds human sin.** Even after the grave sin of calf worship — described as extremely ugly and heinous — Allah pardoned Bani Israel. إِنَّهُ هُوَ التَّوَّابُ الرَّحِيمُ. No sin is too great for Allah's forgiveness if repentance is sincere.

6. **The Quran as Furqan.** The Tawrat was given as a criterion between haqq and batil. Similarly, the Quran serves this function for us — but it must be approached with deep contemplation (تَدَبُّر) and practice (عَمَل), not merely recitation.

7. **Ingratitude is ugliness.** The contrast between Allah's repeated blessings and pardons, and Bani Israel's repeated ingratitude and disobedience, is described as فَمَا أَقْبَحَهُمْ — extreme ugliness. It serves as a mirror: we must examine our own relationship with Allah's continuous blessings.

## Historical Context and Stories Mentioned

### Oppression Under Fir'aun
Fir'aun's regime systematically slaughtered the male children of Bani Israel and kept the females alive for service and exploitation. This was the سُوء الْعَذَاب — the worst punishment — that Bani Israel endured before their salvation. It was simultaneously a divine test (بَلَاء) to distinguish the righteous.

### The Parting of the Sea
When Musa (AS) led Bani Israel out of Egypt and they reached the sea's shore, Fir'aun and his army pursued them. Allah commanded the sea to part, exposing dry land. Bani Israel crossed on dry land. Fir'aun and his entire army drowned — and Bani Israel witnessed it firsthand. This event is described as an آية بَاهِرَة — a dazzling, overwhelming sign of divine power.

### The 40 Nights and the Calf
After crossing the sea, Musa (AS) was appointed by Allah to come to Mount Tur for 40 nights, after which he would receive the Tawrat. During this absence, Bani Israel fell into the sin of calf worship — an act of shirk that Musa (AS) confronted upon his return, calling them wrongdoers against themselves.

### The 70 Men at Tur Sina'
After Bani Israel's tawbah from calf worship, Allah commanded Musa (AS) to select 70 of the best men from his people. These men fasted, purified themselves and their garments, and traveled with Musa (AS) to Tur Sina'. There, clouds descended and covered the mountain. The 70 men fell into prostration and heard Allah speaking to Musa (AS). After the clouds cleared, they made the additional demand: "We will not believe you until we see Allah openly." The tafseer of this demand and its consequences will be covered in the next session.

- **What is the meaning of آل in آل فِرْعَون?**
  The word آل originally derives from أَهْل and covers: family members, close companions, followers, and the army of a ruler. In this ayah, it refers specifically to Fir'aun's army and tyrannical followers (عُتَاة).

- **Was the killing in verse 54 (فَاقْتُلُوا أَنفُسَكُمْ) metaphorical or literal?**
  The teacher explained it was a literal divine command specific to this situation — the innocent were to kill the guilty among them (لِيَقْتُلِ الْبَرِيءُ مِنْكُمُ الْمُجْرِمَ). This was the form of tawbah prescribed by Allah for that particular sin. It is not a general ruling in our Shari'ah (قِصَاص as we know it does not apply in this way), but was a specific divine ordinance for Bani Israel at that time.
