# Tafseer 201 - Study Notes
**Date:** 2026-08-20
**Surah Covered:** Surah Al-Baqarah (continuation) — Ayat on the Munafiqeen (Hypocrites)

---

## Session Overview

This session continued the tafseer of Surah Al-Baqarah, focusing on the ayat describing the characteristics and condition of the Munafiqeen (hypocrites). The class examined the definition and types of Nifaq as explained by Imam Ibn Kathir, the interpretation of the key phrase يُخَادِعُونَ اللَّهَ, the concept that the hypocrites ultimately only deceive themselves, the disease in their hearts (فِي قُلُوبِهِمْ مَرَضٌ), and their audacious claim to be reformers (مُصْلِحُون) when confronted about their corruption.

---

## Key Themes and Topics

1. **Definition of Nifaq** — Ibn Kathir's definition: إظهار الخير وإسرار الشر (showing goodness outwardly while concealing evil inwardly)
2. **Two Types of Nifaq** — I'tiqadi (creedal) and 'Amali (practical/behavioral)
3. **يُخَادِعُونَ اللَّهَ** — The hypocrites' attempt to deceive Allah and the believers, and why it fails
4. **وَمَا يَخْدَعُونَ إِلَّا أَنفُسَهُمْ** — The hypocrites deceive none but themselves; the evil consequence returns to them
5. **فِي قُلُوبِهِمْ مَرَضٌ فَزَادَهُمُ اللَّهُ مَرَضًا** — The spiritual disease of doubt and hypocrisy, increased by Allah as a consequence of their persistence
6. **Difference between Rijs (رِجْس) and Najas (نَجَاس)** — Moral versus physical impurity
7. **لَا تُفْسِدُوا فِي الْأَرْضِ** — The charge against the Munafiqeen of spreading corruption, and their denial
8. **إِنَّمَا نَحْنُ مُصْلِحُونَ** — Their false claim of being reformers
9. **أَلَا إِنَّهُمْ هُمُ الْمُفْسِدُونَ** — Allah's emphatic refutation using multiple rhetorical devices
10. **Why Munafiqeen are described in Madani Surahs** — Nifaq as a Madinan phenomenon

## Main Tafseer Points and Explanations

### 1. Definition of Nifaq (Ibn Kathir)

Imam Ibn Kathir defines Nifaq as:

> **النِّفَاقُ هُوَ إِظْهَارُ الْخَيْرِ وَإِسْرَارُ الشَّرِّ**
> "Nifaq is displaying goodness outwardly and concealing evil inwardly."

The Munafiq shows Islam, Iman, and all virtuous qualities externally, while concealing Kufr in the heart.

---

### 2. Two Types of Nifaq

**النِّفَاق الِاعْتِقَادِي (Nifaq I'tiqadi — Creedal Hypocrisy)**
- Related to the 'Aqeedah (belief) itself.
- The person inwardly disbelieves while outwardly claiming Islam.
- Consequence: **يُخَلِّدُ صَاحِبَهُ فِي النَّار** — it causes its holder to remain in the Hellfire *forever*.
- This is the most severe form of Nifaq.

**النِّفَاق الْعَمَلِي (Nifaq 'Amali — Practical/Behavioral Hypocrisy)**
- Related to actions, not the core belief.
- The person's Aqeedah is sound but their actions resemble those of hypocrites.
- Consequence: **مِنْ أَكْبَرِ الذُّنُوبِ وَالْأَوْزَار** — it is among the greatest of major sins.
- Example signs from Hadith: breaking promises, lying, betraying trust, acting treacherously in disputes.

**Why Nifaq is a major sin:**
> لِأَنَّ الْمُنَافِقَ يُخَالِفُ قَوْلُهُ فِعْلَهُ وَسِرُّهُ عَلَى نِيَّتِهِ
> "Because the hypocrite's words contradict his actions, and his inner state contradicts his outer intention."

---

### 3. Why Descriptions of Munafiqeen Appear Only in Madani Surahs

> وَإِنَّمَا نَزَلَتْ صِفَاتُ الْمُنَافِقِينَ فِي السُّوَرِ الْمَدَنِيَّةِ

- In Makkah, there was no Nifaq — the Kuffar were openly disbelievers (Kuffar and Mushrikeen).
- No group in Makkah claimed to be Muslim while secretly hiding Kufr.
- Nifaq as a social phenomenon arose only in Madinah, where Islam became a dominant force and some people joined outwardly for worldly benefit.
- Therefore, all major descriptions of the Munafiqeen are found in Madani Surahs (e.g., Surah Al-Baqarah, Surah Al-Munafiqun, etc.).

---

### 4. يُخَادِعُونَ اللَّهَ وَالَّذِينَ آمَنُوا (Al-Baqarah 2:9)

**Meaning:** They deceive Allah and the believers.

- **يُخَادِعُونَ** — from خَادَعَ يُخَادِعُ (مُفَاعَلَة form): to engage in mutual deception; here the hypocrites are the ones attempting to deceive.
- **يَعْمَلُونَ عَمَلَ الْمُخَادِع** — they act as a deceiver acts.
- **بِإِظْهَارِ مَا أَظْهَرُوهُ مِنَ الْإِيمَانِ** — by displaying what they showed of Iman while insisting on Kufr in their hearts.

**Their false belief:** They thought (out of ignorance — بِجَهْلِهِمْ) that they could actually deceive Allah, just as they might deceive some believers.

**Reality:** وَمَا عَلِمُوا أَنَّ اللَّهَ لَا يُخْدَع — "They did not know that Allah cannot be deceived," because nothing is hidden from Him.

**Tarkib (Grammatical Analysis):**
- يُخَادِعُونَ = فِعْل
- اللَّهَ = مَفْعُول بِهِ
- وَالَّذِينَ آمَنُوا = مَعْطُوف (conjoined to مَفْعُول بِهِ); الَّذِينَ = اسم موصول; آمَنُوا = فِعْل; الضمير (هُم) = فَاعِل

---

### 5. وَمَا يَخْدَعُونَ إِلَّا أَنفُسَهُمْ وَمَا يَشْعُرُونَ (Al-Baqarah 2:9)

**Meaning:** "They deceive none but themselves, and they do not perceive it."

- **لَا يَخْدَعُونَ فِي الْحَقِيقَة** — in reality they are not deceiving anyone.
- **إِلَّا أَنفُسَهُم** — but only themselves.
- **How?** لِأَنَّ وَبَالَ فِعْلِهِمْ رَاجِعٌ عَلَيْهِمْ — because the evil consequence of their actions returns back upon them.
- **وَمَا يَشْعُرُونَ** — they do not perceive this reality, because of:
  - **لِتَمَادِي غَفْلَتِهِمْ** — their excessive heedlessness
  - **وَتَكَامُلِ حَمَاقَتِهِمْ** — and the completeness of their foolishness

---

### 6. فِي قُلُوبِهِمْ مَرَضٌ فَزَادَهُمُ اللَّهُ مَرَضًا (Al-Baqarah 2:10)

**Meaning:** "In their hearts is a disease, and Allah has increased their disease."

- The **مَرَض (disease)** is: شَكٌّ وَنِفَاق — doubt about Islam and hypocrisy.
- This is a **spiritual disease**, not a physical one (Ibn Aslam: هَذَا الْمَرَضُ فِي الدِّين، لَا فِي الْجَسَد).
- Allah increases their disease — **فَزَادَهُمُ اللَّهُ رِجْسًا فَوْقَ رِجْسٍ وَضَلَالًا فَوْقَ ضَلَالِهِمْ** — filth upon filth, misguidance upon misguidance.
- This increase is a consequence of their *insistence* on Nifaq and Kufr.
- **One interpretation (Ibn Aslam):** فَزَادَهُمُ اللَّهُ مَرَضًا may be a **du'a (prayer against them)** — like the Arabic formula حَفِظَكَ اللَّه (May Allah protect you), meaning "May Allah increase their disease."

**وَلَهُمْ عَذَابٌ أَلِيمٌ بِمَا كَانُوا يَكْذِبُون:**
- A painful punishment awaits them because of:
  1. **كَذِبُهُمْ فِي دَعْوَى الْإِيمَان** — their lying in claiming Iman
  2. **اسْتِهْزَاؤُهُمْ بِآيَاتِ الرَّحْمَن** — their mockery of the verses of Allah

---

### 7. Rijs (رِجْس) vs. Najas (نَجَاسَة)

| Term | Type | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| رِجْس (Rijs) | Moral/spiritual filth | Used for idols (فَاجْتَنِبُوا الرِّجْسَ مِنَ الْأَوْثَان), Nifaq, Kufr — morally reprehensible things |
| نَجَاسَة (Najasa) | Physical impurity | Ritually impure substances that affect worship |

---

### 8. وَإِذَا قِيلَ لَهُمْ لَا تُفْسِدُوا فِي الْأَرْضِ قَالُوا إِنَّمَا نَحْنُ مُصْلِحُونَ (Al-Baqarah 2:11)

**Scenario:** When the believers advise the Munafiqeen: "Do not spread corruption on earth."

**What is الْفَسَاد فِي الْأَرْض here?**

The mufassir explains: it refers to:
1. **الْكُفْر** — disbelief
2. **وَالْعَمَل بِالْمَعْصِيَة** — all acts of disobedience to Allah
3. **إِثَارَة الْفِتَن** — stirring up strife and troubles
4. **الصَّدّ عَن سَبِيل اللَّه** — preventing people from accepting Islam

> فَمَنْ عَصَى اللَّهَ فَقَدْ أَفْسَدَ فِي الْأَرْض
> "Whoever disobeys Allah has spread corruption on earth."

**The Munafiqeen's response:**
> لَيْسَ شَأْنُنَا الْإِفْسَادُ فِي الْأَرْضِ أَبَدًا — إِنَّمَا نَحْنُ مُصْلِحُون
> "Spreading corruption is not our business at all — we are only reformers!"

**Imam al-Baydawi's commentary:**
> تَصَوَّرُوا الْفَسَادَ بِصُورَةِ الصَّلَاح لِمَا فِي قُلُوبِهِمْ مِنَ الْمَرَض
> "They imagined corruption in the form of righteousness, because of the disease in their hearts."

This condition — calling evil "reform" — is a recurring phenomenon throughout history, visible in every age.

---

### 9. أَلَا إِنَّهُمْ هُمُ الْمُفْسِدُونَ وَلَكِن لَّا يَشْعُرُونَ (Al-Baqarah 2:12)

**Allah's emphatic refutation:** "Verily, indeed, it is they who are the corruptors — but they do not perceive it."

**Rhetorical devices used to emphasize:**

| Device | Function |
|---|---|
| أَلَا | Particle of alerting/warning (المُنَبِّه) — draws attention |
| إِنَّ | Particle of emphasis (حَرْف مُقَرِّرَة) — confirms the meaning |
| هُمْ (pronoun Fasl) | Separating pronoun between مُبْتَدَأ and خَبَر — adds additional emphasis |
| الْمُفْسِدُونَ with أَل | Making the predicate definite — further emphasis |

- Together, these four devices make this the most emphatic possible refutation in Arabic rhetorical style (أَبْلَغُ رَدٍّ).
- **وَلَكِن لَّا يَشْعُرُون** — but they do not perceive it, pointing to their heedlessness and unconsciousness.

---

### 10. الْفِتْنَة أَشَدُّ مِنَ الْقَتْل — Brief Reference

The teacher referenced this concept from within Surah Al-Baqarah itself:
> الْفِتْنَةُ أَشَدُّ مِنَ الْقَتْل — "Fitna is worse than killing."

Context: In the context of fighting between Muslims and Mushrikeen, killing is serious, but allowing the Mushrik to remain alive to spread Kufr, prevent Islam, and corrupt society is *more* dangerous than killing. Hence fitna (Kufr, Nifaq, strife) is graver than physical killing.

## Grammar and Language Notes

### مُفَاعَلَة Verb Form
- **خَادَعَ يُخَادِعُ مُخَادَعَةً وَخِدَاعًا** — the مُفَاعَلَة (Form III) pattern in Arabic typically implies mutual action between two parties. Here the Munafiqeen are the active deceivers.

### قِيلَ — Majhool (Passive Voice)
- قَالَ يَقُولُ → passive: **قِيلَ** (it was said)
- Used because the identity of those giving the advice is unspecified; the focus is on the advice itself.

### فَزَادَهُمُ اللَّهُ — Two Grammatical Interpretations
1. **Khabar (statement):** Allah increased their disease as a direct consequence of their Nifaq.
2. **Du'a (supplication/imprecation):** "May Allah increase their disease" — like حَفِظَكَ اللَّه.

### Key Verb Conjugations
| Root | Verb | Masdar | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| خ-د-ع | خَدَعَ يَخْدَعُ | خِدَاع / خَدْع | To deceive |
| أ-ف-س-د | أَفْسَدَ يُفْسِدُ | إِفْسَاد | To spread corruption |
| أ-ص-ل-ح | أَصْلَحَ يُصْلِحُ | إِصْلَاح | To reform |
| خ-ل-د | خَلَّدَ يُخَلِّدُ | تَخْلِيد | To cause to remain forever |
| س-ع-ي | سَعَى يَسْعَى | سَعْي | To strive, endeavor |
| م-د-د | تَمَادَى يَتَمَادَى | تَمَادٍ | To go too far in something |

### فَصْل (Separating Pronoun)
When a pronoun (هُمْ) is placed between the مُبْتَدَأ and the خَبَر, it is called **ضَمِير الْفَصْل**. Its grammatical purpose is to emphasize restriction — confirming that the predicate belongs *exclusively* to that subject.

Example: **أَلَا إِنَّهُمْ هُمُ الْمُفْسِدُونَ** — "it is *they* (and none other) who are the corruptors."

## Lessons and Practical Takeaways

1. **Nifaq destroys from within.** The hypocrite's deception harms themselves most — وَبَالُ فِعْلِهِمْ رَاجِعٌ عَلَيْهِمْ. Every sin and deception ultimately returns to its doer.

2. **Nifaq 'Amali is a real danger for believers.** When we outwardly present ourselves as practicing Muslims but privately neglect obligations (salah, truthfulness, trustworthiness), we fall into practical Nifaq — a major sin, even if not the creedal kind.

3. **Persistence in sin increases spiritual disease.** The more one insists on Nifaq or Kufr, the more Allah allows their heart to harden — فَزَادَهُمُ اللَّهُ مَرَضًا. This is istidraj — being left to go further in error.

4. **Corruption can disguise itself as reform.** The Munafiqeen called their corruption "Islah." This pattern repeats throughout history. True Muslih must align internal belief and external action; any person whose words and deeds contradict each other is exhibiting Nifaq.

5. **Heedlessness (Ghafla) is spiritually dangerous.** The Munafiqeen's inability to see their own corruption was due to غَفْلَة and حَمَاقَة. Constant self-examination (muhasabah) is necessary to guard against this.

6. **Words are consequential.** Lying about Iman brings عَذَاب أَلِيم. Every false claim made before Allah has weight.

7. **The Quran addresses social phenomena.** Nifaq was a Madinan reality because it arose when Islam became politically significant. This teaches us that hypocrisy tends to emerge in contexts of power and social pressure — relevant to Muslim communities today.

## Historical Context and Stories Mentioned

- **Nifaq as a Madinan phenomenon:** In Makkah, opposition to Islam was open — the Kuffar and Mushrikeen made no pretense of being Muslim. The phenomenon of Nifaq emerged in Madinah after the Hijrah, when Islam became a dominant social force. Some individuals joined outwardly for political/social benefit while harboring disbelief internally. This is why all suwar describing the Munafiqeen in detail are Madani.

- **The recurring pattern of "reformers" claiming to do good while spreading corruption** is noted as a timeless pattern in Muslim history and in contemporary societies — individuals and groups who call corruption "Islah" and label sincere Muslims as extremists or troublemakers.

- **الْفِتْنَةُ أَشَدُّ مِنَ الْقَتْل** — referenced briefly in the context of the severity of Kufr and corruption over physical harm, from elsewhere in Surah Al-Baqarah.

## Q&A Highlights

**Q: What is the difference between إِصْرَار (insisting) and إِسْرَار (concealing)?**
> A: These are two different words. **إِصْرَار** (with Sad) comes from أَصَرَّ — to insist on or persist in something, refusing to leave it. **إِسْرَار** (with Sin) comes from سِرٌّ (secret) — to conceal or hide something secretly. In the context of Nifaq: إِصْرَار عَلَى الْكُفْر means *persisting* in disbelief, while إِسْرَار الشَّرّ means *concealing* evil.

**Q: If a Muslim habitually commits sins (e.g., missing Salah, listening to music, stealing) out of habit rather than conviction, are they a Munafiq?**
> A: This falls under **Nifaq 'Amali** (practical hypocrisy). The signs of Nifaq listed in Hadith (e.g., breaking promises, lying, betraying trust, being treacherous in disputes) relate to actions. If the person's *Aqeedah* is sound — they believe Islam is the truth and do not reject it — then they are not a Nifaq I'tiqadi, but their actions may carry elements of Nifaq 'Amali, which is still a major sin requiring repentance.

**Q: What is the difference between رِجْس (Rijs) and نَجَاسَة (Najasa)?**
> A: **Rijs** refers to moral or spiritual filth — used in the Quran for idols (فَاجْتَنِبُوا الرِّجْسَ مِنَ الْأَوْثَان), Nifaq, and Kufr. **Najasa** refers to physical ritual impurity (substances that invalidate Wudu or Salah). The distinction is moral vs. physical.

**Q: Is فَزَادَهُمُ اللَّهُ مَرَضًا a statement or a prayer?**
> A: Ibn Aslam mentions it can be read as a **du'a (imprecation against them)** — like saying حَفِظَكَ اللَّهُ for du'a of protection, here it would mean "May Allah increase their disease" as a consequence of their persistence. The dominant reading is as a **khabar (statement of fact)**: Allah *does* increase their disease because of their insistence on Nifaq.

**Q: What does اثَارَةُ الْفِتَن mean?**
> A: Isara means to stir up or instigate something. Fitan is the plural of Fitna — troubles, strife, trials. In the Quranic context, Fitna includes Kufr, Nifaq, and anything that causes societal chaos or turns people away from Allah. So إِثَارَةُ الْفِتَن means to actively instigate and spread Kufr, Nifaq, and social disorder.
