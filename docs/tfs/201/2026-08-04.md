## Main Tafseer Points and Explanations

### 1. Tafsir of Al-Isti'adhah (أعوذ بالله من الشيطان الرجيم)

**Linguistic root:** أعوذ is from the pattern استفعال; root letters are ع و ذ.

**Meaning:** "I seek refuge and protection in Allah (أستجير بجناب الله وأعتصم به) and I fortify myself with Him from the evil of Shaytan — the arrogant, the rebellious."

**Two descriptions of Shaytan in this phrase:**
- **العاتي** — the arrogant one (from عتا يعتو, اسم فاعل)
- **المتمرد** — the rebellious one (from تمرد يتمرد)

**Why we seek this protection — three threats from Shaytan:**
1. That he may harm us (يضرني) in our deen or dunya
2. That he may prevent us (يصدني) from doing what Allah commanded
3. That he may reach us through his Hamz, Lamz, and Waswasah

**Three modes of Shaytan's attack (from the hadith of Tahajjud):**
- **الهمز (Al-Hamz):** Physical catching/seizing by Shaytan
- **النفخ (Al-Nafkh):** Shaytan's puffing — leading to arrogance
- **النفث (Al-Nafth):** Shaytan's spitting — a form of whispering, leading people astray

**Key principle:** Nothing protects a person from Shaytan's temptations except Allah, Lord of all worlds.

**Supporting Hadith:** The Prophet ﷺ, when he rose at night for Salat al-Tahajjud, would begin his prayer with Takbir (Allahu Akbar), then say:
> أعوذ بالله السميع العليم من الشيطان الرجيم من همزه ونفخه ونفثه
> *"I seek refuge in Allah, the All-Hearing, the All-Knowing, from the accursed Shaytan — from his seizure, his puffing up, and his whispering."*

### 2. Tafsir of Al-Basmalah (بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم)

**Meaning:** "I begin with the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful" — i.e., أبدأ بتسمية الله وذكره قبل كل شيء مستعيناً به في جميع أموري.

**Significance of starting with Allah's name:**
- I seek Allah's help in all my affairs
- Allah is الرب — the Lord; المعبود — the one worshipped; كثير التفضل والإحسان — abundant in grace and benevolence
- Allah's mercy encompasses all creation: إنسان, حيوان, and all مخلوقات

**The hidden verb (الفعل المحذوف) — see Grammar section below.**

**Why Surah Al-Tawbah has no Basmalah:**
- All Surahs of the Quran begin with بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم except Surah Al-Tawbah
- Reason: This Surah was revealed when Allah's wrath was directed at the Mushrikeen of Arabia who had broken their treaties with the Muslims. The Surah opens with Allah's anger and displeasure — and the mercy (رحمة) expressed in Bismillah cannot be combined with that wrath in the same opening. Therefore, Bismillah was omitted.

**Why Allah begins every Surah with Bismillah — pedagogical wisdom:**
> لِيُرْشِدَ الْمُسْلِمِينَ إِلَى أَنْ يَبْدَأُوا أَعْمَالَهُمْ وَأَقْوَالَهُمْ بِسْمِ اللَّهِ الرَّحْمَنِ الرَّحِيمِ
> *"To guide Muslims to begin their actions and their words with Bismillah."*

This applies to every action of life: eating, drinking, sleeping, going out, sitting, standing — التماساً لمعونته وتوفيقه (seeking His help and enablement).

**Contrast with idol worshippers:** The Mushrikeen began their activities with the names of their idols (اللات، العزى، هبل). A Muslim is commanded to begin everything with the name of Allah alone.

**Imam Al-Tabari's statement:** Allah (whose names are sanctified — تقدست أسماؤه) taught His Prophet ﷺ to mention His Most Beautiful Names before all actions, and made this a Sunnah (سنة) and a path (سبيل) for all creation to follow.

### 3. Introduction to Surah Al-Fatihah (بين يدي السورة)

**Revelation:** Makki (revealed in Makkah)

**Number of verses:** 7 — agreed upon unanimously by all scholars (إجماع)

**Why named Al-Fatihah:** Because it is the first Surah in the Quran في الترتيب (in sequence/arrangement), not في النزول (in order of revelation). The first Surah revealed was Surah Al-'Alaq (اقرأ باسم ربك الذي خلق). The current sequence was determined by Prophet Muhammad ﷺ himself, who instructed the Sahabah who were appointed as scribes to place each Surah in its designated position.

**Comprehensive scope of Surah Al-Fatihah:**
Despite its brevity (وعلى قصرها ووجازتها), it contains all the meanings of the Quran (واشتملت على مقاصده الأساسية بالإجمال). It covers:
- العقيدة — Islamic doctrine/creed
- العبادة — Worship
- التشريع — Legislation
- الاعتقاد باليوم الآخر — Belief in the Last Day
- الإيمان بأسماء الله الحسنى — Belief in the Beautiful Names of Allah
- إفراده بالعبادة — Singling out Allah alone for worship
- الاستعانة والدعاء — Seeking help and supplication
- التوجه إليه بطلب الهداية — Turning to Allah for guidance to the right path
- الصراط المستقيم — The straight path
- Warning against the path of those upon whom Allah's wrath fell and those who went astray
- أخبار الأمم السابقة — Information about previous nations
- معارج السعداء ومنازل الأشقياء — The stations of the blessed and the wretched

**Status:** This Surah is كالأم بالنسبة لبقية السور الكريمة — the mother/foundation in relation to all other Surahs.

**Names of Surah Al-Fatihah:**
The Surah has many names. Imam Al-Qurtubi counted 12 names, including:
1. الفاتحة — The Opening
2. أم الكتاب — Mother of the Book
3. أم القرآن — Mother of the Quran
4. السبع المثاني — The Seven Oft-Repeated Verses
5. الشافية — The Healing One
6. الوافية — The Complete One
7. الكافية — The Sufficient One
8. الأساس — The Foundation
9. الحمد — The Praise

**Connection to Surah Al-Hijr (15:87):**
> وَلَقَدْ آتَيْنَاكَ سَبْعًا مِّنَ الْمَثَانِي وَالْقُرْآنَ الْعَظِيمَ
> *"And We have given you seven of the oft-repeated verses and the great Quran."*
The "seven oft-repeated" refers to Surah Al-Fatihah's seven verses, repeated in every rak'ah of prayer.

**Merits — Two Ahadith mentioned:**

*Hadith 1 (Ubayy ibn Ka'b رضي الله عنه):*
Ubayy recited Surah Al-Fatihah before the Prophet ﷺ. The Prophet ﷺ said:
> وَالَّذِي نَفْسِي بِيَدِهِ مَا أُنزِلَ فِي التَّوْرَاةِ وَلَا فِي الْإِنجِيلِ وَلَا فِي الزَّبُورِ وَلَا فِي الْفُرْقَانِ مِثْلُهَا
> *"By the One in whose hand is my soul, nothing like this Surah has been revealed in the Tawrah, the Injil, the Zabur, or the Furqan (Quran)."*

*Hadith 2 (Sa'id ibn al-Mu'alla رضي الله عنه):*
The Prophet ﷺ said he would teach him the greatest Surah in the Quran — and it is Surah Al-Fatihah, the seven oft-repeated verses.

## Grammar and Language Notes

### The Hidden Verb (الفعل المحذوف) in Bismillah

**Question raised in class:** Bismillah begins with a حرف جر (preposition: بـ). In Arabic, a sentence cannot grammatically begin with a preposition alone — a verb or subject is required. The phrase as stated (بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم) appears to be an incomplete sentence. Where is the verb (فعل)?

**Analysis:**
- If it were a nominal sentence (جملة اسمية), it would need a مبتدأ and خبر
- The بـ is a حرف جر, so what follows it is مجرور — this creates a جار ومجرور which must be attached (متعلق) to something

**Answer:** There is a hidden (محذوف) verb — a فعل مقدر. The sentence is actually a verbal sentence (جملة فعلية). Examples of the understood verb:
- أبدأ بسم الله — I begin with the name of Allah
- أقرأ بسم الله — I recite with the name of Allah
- أتلو بسم الله — I recite/follow with the name of Allah
- أخرج بسم الله — I go out with the name of Allah

**Why is the verb hidden?** This is a matter of Arabic rhetoric (البلاغة العربية). The verb is deliberately omitted because:
- Bismillah is not restricted to one action
- It applies to all activities of a person's life (eating, sleeping, going out, etc.)
- If one specific verb were mentioned (e.g., أقرأ), it would limit the phrase to recitation only
- By leaving the verb unspecified (محذوف), the phrase becomes universal — the listener supplies the appropriate verb based on their context
- This is a hallmark of the high rhetorical level (البلاغة العالية) of Arabic, where omission carries expansive meaning

## Lessons and Practical Takeaways

1. **Always begin with Isti'adhah then Basmalah** — before reciting Quran and before every action of life. This is a Sunnah of the Prophet ﷺ and a Sunnah Allah established for all creation.

2. **Shaytan attacks through three channels** — physical seizure (Hamz), temptation to arrogance (Nafkh), and subtle whispering (Nafth/Waswasah). Being aware of these channels helps the believer guard against them.

3. **Only Allah protects from Shaytan** — no human effort or willpower alone can repel Shaytan's whisperings. Genuine protection requires turning to Allah.

4. **Bismillah is not limited to Quran recitation** — it should precede all permissible actions: eating, drinking, sleeping, leaving the house, working. The hidden verb in the phrase signals this universality.

5. **Surah Al-Fatihah is the most comprehensive Surah** — despite being only 7 short verses, it encapsulates the entire purpose of the Quran: creed, worship, legislation, guidance, and supplication.

6. **The Quran's arrangement is prophetically determined** — the order of Surahs is not arbitrary. It was set by the Prophet ﷺ himself under divine guidance, which is why Surah Al-Fatihah — though not the first revealed — is placed first.

7. **Say Bismillah contrary to the idol worshippers** — the Mushrikeen invoked their idols before actions. A Muslim's Bismillah is an act of Tawhid, distinguishing monotheism from polytheism in everyday life.

## Historical Context and Stories Mentioned

- **Surah Al-Tawbah and the broken treaties:** The Mushrikeen of Arabia violated their peace agreements with the Muslims. Allah revealed Surah Al-Tawbah expressing His wrath toward them. Because of the severity of this divine anger, the Surah opens without Bismillah — since Allah's mercy (expressed in الرحمن الرحيم) cannot be joined with His wrath in the same opening.

- **Ubayy ibn Ka'b رضي الله عنه** recited Surah Al-Fatihah before the Prophet ﷺ, who testified it is unmatched by anything revealed in the Tawrah, Injil, Zabur, or Quran.

- **Sa'id ibn al-Mu'alla رضي الله عنه** was promised by the Prophet ﷺ that he would be taught the greatest Surah in the Quran — which is Surah Al-Fatihah.

- **Prophet ﷺ's Tahajjud practice:** He would wake at night, begin prayer with Takbir, then recite the extended Isti'adhah: أعوذ بالله السميع العليم من الشيطان الرجيم من همزه ونفخه ونفثه.

- **Imam Al-Tabari رحمه الله** is cited: Allah taught the Prophet ﷺ to mention His Names before all actions and made this a universal Sunnah and path (سبيلاً يتبعونه) for all of creation.

- **Imam Al-Qurtubi رحمه الله** counted the names of Surah Al-Fatihah and reached 12 distinct names.

## Q&A Highlights

**Q: Should we buy a large Quran with margins to write commentary alongside the text?**
A: The teacher (Dr. Asghar Mahmood Nadawi) clarified this is not necessary. The book *Safwat al-Tafasir* is the primary text. Students should take personal notes in a notebook during the lesson. What the teacher explains orally should be recorded in one's own notebook — that is sufficient.

**Q: Why is the verb hidden in Bismillah, and why does the phrase start with a Haar Majroor (جار ومجرور)?**
A: In Arabic rhetoric (البلاغة), verbs are sometimes omitted deliberately. In Bismillah, the verb is hidden because the phrase applies to all activities of life — not just one act. If a specific verb were stated (e.g., أقرأ — I recite), it would restrict Bismillah to recitation only. By leaving the verb unmentioned, it encompasses eating, sleeping, going out, and every other action. This is the rhetorical wisdom (حكمة بلاغية) behind the omission. The sentence is a verbal sentence (جملة فعلية) with a hidden verb.
