## Session Overview

This session completed the tafseer of Surah Al-Fatiha, covering all seven ayat in sequence. The teacher explained the Arabic vocabulary, grammatical structure (i'rab), and deeper meanings of each phrase, drawing on classical tafseer texts and hadith. The session also included a Q&A on the distinction between Al-Maghdubi Alayhim and Al-Dallin, practical guidance on reciting Al-Fatiha with presence of mind (khushu'), and administrative notes on classroom expectations for future lessons (Surah Al-Baqarah beginning next class).

---

## Key Themes and Topics

1. **Hamd and Thana (Praise and Thanks)** — Allah SWT taught humanity the correct formula for praising and thanking Him: *Alhamdulillah*.
2. **Allah's Sifat (Attributes)** — The ayat of Al-Fatiha are a sequence of Allah's attributes: Rabb, Al-Rahman, Al-Rahim, Malik — each in the genitive (majrur) case as sifat linked to *Lillahi*.
3. **The Mercy of Allah (Rahmah)** — Al-Rahman and Al-Rahim as Ism al-Mubalagha and Sifat al-Mushabbaha indicating the highest degree of mercy; the famous hadith that 1% of Allah's mercy is manifest in this world, 99% reserved for the Day of Judgment.
4. **Tawhid in Worship and Reliance** — *Iyyaka Na'budu wa Iyyaka Nasta'in*: singling out Allah alone for worship (*ibadah*) and seeking help (*isti'anah*).
5. **The Straight Path and Its People** — *Sirat al-Mustaqeem* identified as the way of the Anbiya, Siddiqeen, Shuhada, and Salihin (referenced from Surah An-Nisa).
6. **Two Groups of Deviation** — Al-Maghdubi Alayhim (those upon whom is Allah's wrath, primarily the Jews) and Al-Dallin (those who went astray, primarily the Christians), and why each designation applies to each group.
7. **Practical Recitation with Meaning** — The teacher's emphasis on repeating ayat during salah with their meanings actively in mind, especially in voluntary (nafl) prayers.

## Main Tafseer Points and Explanations

### Ayah 1 — اَلْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ

- Allah SWT Himself taught us the correct and worthy manner of praising Him by revealing this ayah. The command understood is: *Qulu ya 'ibadi idha aradtum shukri wa thana'i: Alhamdulillah* — "Say, O my servants, when you want to thank Me and praise Me: Alhamdulillah."
- **Alhamdulillah**: All praise and thanks belong exclusively to Allah — not to any false deity or anything worshipped other than Him.
- **Rabb**: Sustainer. This is a sifat (attribute) of Allah. He sustains all of creation.
- **Al-Alameen**: Plural of *'Alam* (world/category of creation). It is an *ism al-jins* (common noun) covering: the world of humans (*'Alam al-Insan*), world of Jinn (*'Alam al-Jinn*), world of Angels (*'Alam al-Mala'ikah*), world of animals (*'Alam al-Haywanat*), this world (*dunya*), and the next (*akhirah*).
- *Rabbul Alameen* is one of the grammatical reasons the full phrase *Lillahi Rabbil Alameen* has kasra — all sifat here are majrur because they are linked to *Lillahi*.

### Ayah 2 — اَلرَّحْمَنِ الرَّحِيمِ

- Both *Al-Rahman* and *Al-Rahim* are sifat of Allah, both in the genitive (majrur) continuing the grammatical chain from *Lillahi*.
- *Al-Rahman*: Ism al-Mubalagha — indicates the highest, most intense level of mercy.
- *Al-Rahim*: Sifat al-Mushabbaha — indicates a continuous, established, permanent quality of mercy.
- Meaning: *Al-ladhi wasi'at rahmatuhu kulla shay'in* — "He whose mercy encompasses all things."
- Allah's mercy includes: creating us, providing sustenance (*rizq*), and guiding us to *sa'adat al-darayn* — happiness in both this world and the next.
- Hadith point: All the mercy observable in this world — in human hearts, among animals, across all creation — constitutes only **1%** of Allah's mercy. The remaining **99%** will be released on the Day of Judgment. This underscores the meaning of *Da'im al-Ihsan* — Allah is ever-benevolent.
- The verse *wa in ta'uddu ni'mat Allahi la tuhsuha* (Quran: "If you try to count Allah's blessings you cannot enumerate them") was cited to reinforce this point.

### Ayah 3 — مَالِكِ يَوْمِ الدِّينِ

- **Maliki**: Owner, possessor — Ism al-Fa'il from *malaka*.
- **Yawm al-Din**: The Day of Recompense. *Din* here means compensation/reward — the day on which every person will be given the recompense for their deeds (reward for good, punishment for evil).
- Allah is *al-Mutasarrif fi Yawm al-Din* — He has complete, unrestricted authority on that day, as an owner (*malik*) has over his own dominion (*mulk*).
- In *dunya*, Allah gives people relative control as a test — do they think they are the true owners, or do they acknowledge Allah's ownership? On the Day of Judgment, even that relative human control is removed.
- Supporting ayah: *Yawma la tamliku nafsun linafsin shay'an wal amru yawma idhin lillah* — "On that day no soul shall have power to do anything for another, and all authority will belong to Allah."

### Ayah 4 — إِيَّاكَ نَعْبُدُ وَإِيَّاكَ نَسْتَعِينُ

- The pronoun *Iyyaka* (You alone) is placed **before** the verb — in Arabic this indicates *hasr* (exclusivity/restriction). The meaning: "You, and only You, we worship."
- **Ibadah** encompasses: *nakhda'u* (submission), *nastakinu* (humbling of the self), *nakhsha'u* (feeling awe and fear in the heart — khushu').
- We single out (*nakhussu*) Allah alone for worship: *La na'budu ahadan siwak* — "We worship no one except You."
- **Isti'anah** (seeking help): from *'Awn* (help) via the *istif'al* pattern, meaning to seek/request help. *Iyyaka nasta'in* = "Only from You do we seek help."
- We need Allah's help specifically to worship Him, to obey Him, and to please Him — because He alone has the power to enable us and accept our worship.
- *La hawla wa la quwwata illa billah* is the spirit of this ayah.

### Ayah 5 — اِهْدِنَا الصِّرَاطَ الْمُسْتَقِيمَ

- *Ihdi*: Command form (amr) of *hada yahdi hidayatan* — to guide, to show the path.
- *Al-Sirat al-Mustaqeem*: The straight, upright, exact and true path — a *Na't Sifat* construction.
- The full du'a meaning: "O Allah, guide us, make us steadfast (*thabbitna*) upon Islam — the Islam with which You sent Your Prophets and Messengers, and the final Messenger (*Khatam al-Mursaleen*)."
- Make us among *al-muqarrabin* — those closest to Allah.

### Ayah 6 — صِرَاطَ الَّذِينَ أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ

- This ayah defines who treads *Al-Sirat al-Mustaqeem* — those upon whom Allah has bestowed His bounty (*ni'mah*).
- These are identified from Surah An-Nisa (fifth juz): *"Ula'ika alladhina an'ama Allahu alayhim min al-Nabiyyina wa al-Siddiqina wa al-Shuhada'i wa al-Salihin"*
  - **Al-Nabiyyun**: The Prophets of Allah
  - **Al-Siddiqun**: The foremost truthful followers of the Prophets (e.g., Abu Bakr al-Siddiq — the first and foremost follower of the Prophet SAW, who attained the rank of *Siddiqiyyah*)
  - **Al-Shuhada'**: Those who attained martyrdom in the way of Allah
  - **Al-Salihin**: All righteous people
- These four groups are together in Jannah: *"wa hasuna ula'ika rafiqa"* — "and what excellent companions they are."
- Every believer in every raka'ah of every salah is making this supplication — asking Allah to be placed on the way of the Anbiya, Siddiqeen, Shuhada, and Salihin. This is profound.

### Ayah 7 — غَيْرِ الْمَغْضُوبِ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا الضَّالِّينَ

- A prayer for protection from two deviated groups.
- **Al-Maghdubi Alayhim** — Those upon whom is Allah's wrath: primarily the **Jews (Yahud)**.
  - Reason: They deviated from the right way **knowingly and deliberately** despite having many Prophets sent to them and having full knowledge of the truth.
  - Quranic evidence: *"Lu'ina alladhina kafaru min Bani Isra'il 'ala lisan Dawuda wa 'Isa ibn Maryam"* and *"ba'u bi ghadabin min Allah."* The words *ghadab*, *la'nah*, and *maghdub* are specifically used for the Jews in multiple Quranic ayat.
  - Hadith also specifically interprets *Al-Maghdubi Alayhim* as referring to the Jews.
- **Al-Dallin** — Those who have gone astray: primarily the **Christians (Nasara)**.
  - Reason: They deviated from the right way due to **ignorance and distance from the actual teachings** of 'Isa AS. After 'Isa AS departed and before the coming of the Prophet Muhammad SAW, they lost connection with the true teachings and went astray.
  - The word *Dallin* (from *dalla yadillu dalalan*) indicates straying/being lost, as opposed to willful defiance.
- **Important clarification (Q&A)**: These two groups are specifically named because they represent the most prominent examples — but *all* forms of deviation are included in these two ayat. Mushrikeen (polytheists), atheists, and all others who deviate from the straight path are encompassed in the meaning. The ayah does not name Jews and Christians explicitly — Allah left it general — but classical scholars and hadith identify these two groups as the primary referents.
- The full du'a meaning: *"Allahumma la taj'alna ya Allah min zumrati a'da'ik al-ha'idina 'an al-sirat al-mustaqim"* — "O Allah, do not make us from the group of Your enemies who deviate from the Straight Path."
- We close with *Ameen* — affirming and requesting acceptance of this entire supplication.

## Grammar and Language Notes

- **I'rab chain in Al-Fatiha**: *Lillahi* → *Rabbil Alameen* → *Al-Rahmani Al-Rahimi* → *Maliki Yawm al-Din*: all are majrur (genitive) because they are sifat linked to the majrur word *Lillahi* (harf al-jar = Lam). Each subsequent sifat carries the same kasra.
- **عَالَمُونَ / عَالَمِينَ**: Plural of *'Alam*; the plural form used is *Jama' Muzakkar Salim* (sound masculine plural).
- **اِسْمُ الْمُبَالَغَةِ vs. صِفَةٌ مُشَبَّهَةٌ**: *Al-Rahman* is Ism al-Mubalagha (superlative intensity of mercy). *Al-Rahim* is Sifat Mushabbaha (settled, continuous attribute of mercy). Both indicate the highest levels of the root meaning *rahima*.
- **مَالِكٌ**: Ism al-Fa'il (active participle) from *malaka yamliku*.
- **إِيَّاكَ نَعْبُدُ**: The object pronoun (*Iyyaka*) precedes the verb (*na'budu*) — this fronting (*taqdim*) in Arabic grammar signals *hasr* (restriction/exclusivity): worship is restricted to Allah alone.
- **اِسْتَعَانَ**: *Istif'al* pattern from *'Awn*. In the *istif'al* pattern, the meaning is always "to seek/request" the base action. So *ista'ana* = to seek help.
- **ضَالٌّ (pl. ضَالِّينَ)**: Ism al-Fa'il from *dalla yadillu dalalan*; the plural *Dallin* is used in the ayah.
- **Student note-taking guidance from teacher**: When studying Quranic words, know: (1) root word, (2) plural if applicable, (3) if a verb — its madhi, mudhari', and which bab it belongs to, (4) the exact Quranic/Arabic meaning.

## Lessons and Practical Takeaways

1. **Recite Al-Fatiha with meaning**: The teacher strongly emphasized that we should actively hold the meanings of each ayah in our mind while reciting, especially in nafl (voluntary) prayers. For instance, while saying *Ar-Rahman Ar-Rahim*, consciously reflect on all forms of mercy — Allah's mercy in creation, in provision, in guidance — and how 99% of that mercy is yet to be unveiled.

2. **Repeat ayat in reflection**: It is established from hadith that the Prophet SAW would repeat an ayah again and again — if it was an ayah about Jannah, he would repeat it with longing; if about Jahannam, with fear and seeking refuge. We should do the same, particularly in nafl prayers. This is permissible and praiseworthy.

3. **Understand the weight of Maliki Yawm al-Din**: Reciting this ayah should bring to mind the reality of the Day of Judgment — a day on which no soul can help another, and all authority belongs to Allah alone. This should produce humility, urgency, and motivation for good deeds.

4. **The salah as a recurring supplication**: Every single raka'ah of prayer contains this complete du'a. We are asking Allah, repeatedly every day, to put us on the path of the Anbiya, Siddiqeen, Shuhada, and Salihin — and to protect us from the path of those who earned Allah's wrath or went astray. We must be conscious of what we are asking.

5. **Differentiate khushu' from mere movement**: *Nakhsha'u* means internal humility and awe — a feeling in the heart, not just physical posture. This is the goal of salah.

6. **Gratitude is a duty**: Allah has bestowed blessings we cannot count (*wa in ta'uddu ni'mat Allahi la tuhsuha*). The response to this is constant *hamd* and *shukr* — which is itself taught to us by *Alhamdulillahi Rabbil Alameen*.

7. **Preparation for class**: Students are expected to prepare the tafseer portion of each lesson *before* the class and practice reading the Arabic *ibarah* with correct recitation. For Surah Al-Baqarah (beginning next class), come having read the *baina yaday al-surah* (introduction to the surah) and the tafseer text.

## Historical Context and Stories Mentioned

- **The Jews (Bani Isra'il)**: Allah sent an exceptionally large number of Prophets to Bani Isra'il, yet they repeatedly and knowingly disobeyed. This persistent, deliberate deviation is why the Quran associates *ghadab* (wrath) and *la'nah* (curse) with them in numerous ayat. Reference: *"Lu'ina alladhina kafaru min Bani Isra'il 'ala lisan Dawuda wa 'Isa ibn Maryam"* — the curse came through the tongues of two of their own Prophets (Dawud AS and 'Isa AS), indicating the severity of their transgression.

- **The Christians (Nasara)**: After the departure of 'Isa AS, a long period elapsed before the coming of Prophet Muhammad SAW. During this interval, the Christians gradually lost connection with the authentic teachings of 'Isa AS — through ignorance, neglect, and the corruption of their scriptures. This is distinguished from the Jews' case of willful rejection: the Christians' deviation was rooted more in *jahalah* (ignorance) and distance from the original message.

- **Abu Bakr al-Siddiq (RA)**: Mentioned as the prime example of a *Siddiq* — the first and foremost in following and believing the Prophet SAW, attaining the rank of *Siddiqiyyah* through his truthfulness and immediate acceptance of truth.

- **Hadith on Allah's Mercy**: The Prophet SAW stated that Allah divided His mercy into 100 parts — He sent down 1 part to this world, and by that 1 part all of creation shows mercy to one another (mother to child, animal to its young, etc.). The remaining 99 parts are reserved for the believers on the Day of Judgment. *(Referenced from Sahih Bukhari and Muslim.)*

## Q&A Highlights

**Q: What are the differences between نَخْضَعُ, نَسْتَكِينُ, and نَخْشَعُ?**
A: All three are synonyms related to worship and humility, but can be differentiated:
- *Nakhda'u* (خَضَعَ) — to submit, to bow down
- *Nastakinu* (اِسْتَكَانَ يَسْتَكِينُ) — to humble oneself, to submit completely
- *Nakhsha'u* (خَشَعَ يَخْشَعُ) — to feel internal humbleness and awe in the heart before Allah (*khushu'* is the noun). This is the inward, spiritual dimension of ibadah.

**Q: Do Al-Maghdubi Alayhim and Al-Dallin only refer to Jews and Christians, or do they include atheists and polytheists too?**
A: All forms of deviation are included in the broad meaning of these two ayat — mushrikeen, atheists, and all who stray are encompassed. However, classical scholars and the hadith of the Prophet SAW specifically interpret *Al-Maghdubi Alayhim* as the Jews and *Al-Dallin* as the Christians, because these are the highest and most prominent examples: the Jews' deviation was willful and knowledgeable, and the Christians' was due to ignorance and neglect. The ayah deliberately leaves the names out so the meaning remains universal and comprehensive.

**Q: Why specifically is *Maghdub* (wrath) used for Jews and *Dallin* (straying) for Christians?**
A: Because the Quran itself uses *ghadab* and *la'nah* specifically in connection with the Jews in multiple places (e.g., Surah Al-Ma'idah), and hadith confirm this interpretation. The Jews knew the truth and rejected it deliberately — hence *wrath* (an active divine response to willful transgression). The Christians lost the truth through ignorance and neglect of 'Isa AS's actual teachings — hence *straying* (a passive condition of being lost rather than actively defiant).

**Q: Is it permissible to write on a Quran that has blank lines (for annotation/tafseer notes)?**
A: The teacher clarified that the class tafseer book already contains the Quranic text with tafseer explanations. Students should record everything in their personal notebooks — especially the Arabic words, meanings, and grammatical analysis. The blank-lined Quran is only the Quranic text without tafseer, so the class book and notebook are the primary tools.

**Q: Is it permissible to repeat a single ayah multiple times during salah?**
A: Yes. It is established (*ja'iz*) from the sunnah — the Prophet SAW would stop at ayat mentioning Jannah and repeat them with longing, and at ayat mentioning Jahannam and repeat them while seeking refuge. In nafl prayers especially, repeating individual ayat while reflecting on their meaning is encouraged. In fard prayers in jama'ah, this is less practical since others are present, but in individual nafl prayers there is no restriction.
