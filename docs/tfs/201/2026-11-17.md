# Tafseer 201 - Study Notes
**Date:** 2026-11-17
**Surah Covered:** Surah Al-Baqarah, Ayat 104–110 (Page 85)

---

## Session Overview

This session covered Surah Al-Baqarah ayat 104–110, focusing on three major themes: (1) the prohibition of using the word "Ra'ina" and replacing it with "Unzurna," (2) the Qur'anic response to Jewish criticism of abrogation (naskh) in Islamic rulings, and (3) the broader context of Jewish and Christian hostility toward the Muslim community. The session opened with an explanation of the connection (munasabah) between this passage and the preceding ayat, which dealt with the evil characteristics of the Jews. Allah then addresses the believers directly with practical commands: guard your speech, do not ask excessive questions, pardon your enemies for now, and preserve your salah and zakah.

---

## Key Themes and Topics

1. **Munasabah (Contextual Link):** These ayat follow directly from the section exposing the evil traits of the Jews, extending the discussion to what they conceal in their hearts against the Prophet ﷺ and the Muslims.
2. **Prohibition of "Ra'ina":** The word was misused by Jews to insult the Prophet ﷺ; Allah commanded believers to use "Unzurna" instead.
3. **Jewish and Christian Envy (Hasad):** Disbelievers among Ahl al-Kitab and the Mushrikeen do not wish any good to come to Muslims — driven purely by hatred and envy.
4. **Naskh (Abrogation):** Allah's response to Jewish criticism that changes in Qur'anic rulings prove it is the speech of Muhammad ﷺ, not Allah — refuted by affirming Allah's wisdom, ownership, and power.
5. **Prohibition of Excessive Questioning:** Believers are warned not to ask their Prophet ﷺ frivolous or burdensome questions, as Bani Israel did with Musa عليه السلام.
6. **Practical Commands:** Pardon the disbelievers until the permission for Jihad comes; establish Salah; give Zakah; send forth good deeds.

## Main Tafseer Points and Explanations

### Ayah 104 — يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا لَا تَقُولُوا رَاعِنَا

- Allah directly addresses the believers: "O you who have believed, do not say 'Ra'ina.'"
- **Origin of "Ra'ina":** Believers used this word when addressing the Prophet ﷺ to mean "Watch over us and give us time to absorb what you are teaching us." It is a valid Arabic command form from the verb *Ra'a Yura'i Mura'atan* (Bab Ufa'ala), meaning to watch, to care for, to give someone time.
  - Grammatical breakdown: *Ra'i* = Fi'l (Amr), *Anta* = Fa'il (implied), *Na* = Maf'ul bih
- **Jewish Misuse:** The Jews of Madinah began using the same word "Ra'ina" but with a *Ya* inserted (Ra'i + Ya), transforming it into a Hebrew/Arabic pun meaning "our shepherd" — implying a fool or a dull-minded person — using it as a covert insult against the Prophet ﷺ.
- **Allah's Command:** Believers were told to drop this word entirely and replace it with **"Unzurna"** (from *Nazara Yanzuru* — to look, to attend), which carries the same intended meaning without any possibility of misuse.
- **Lesson:** Even the words we use matter. If a word becomes a vehicle for mischief, it must be abandoned in favor of a word free of ambiguity.
- Allah adds: **وَلِلْكَافِرِينَ عَذَابٌ أَلِيم** — For the disbelievers (specifically the Jews who insulted the Prophet ﷺ in this manner) there is a painful punishment.

---

### Ayah 105 — مَا يَوَدُّ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا مِنْ أَهْلِ الْكِتَابِ وَلَا الْمُشْرِكِينَ

- Neither the disbelievers among Ahl al-Kitab (Jews and Christians) nor the Mushrikeen wish that **any good** (*Khayr*) be sent down to the Muslims from their Lord.
- Their motive: *Bughdhan fikum wa hasadan lakum* — out of **hatred** for you and **envy** of you.
- **Allah's response:** *Wallahu yakhtassu bi-rahmatih man yasha'* — Allah singles out for His mercy and prophethood whomever He wills. It is not in the hands of the enemies of Islam to withhold the grace of Allah.
- **Allah's attribute here:** *Dhul fadhl al-'azim* — Possessor of immense grace and bounty.
- Key point: The blessing of the final Prophet ﷺ and the final Book was Allah's choice — not something gained or lost by human scheming.

---

### Ayah 106 — مَا نَنسَخْ مِنْ آيَةٍ أَوْ نُنسِهَا نَأْتِ بِخَيْرٍ مِّنْهَا أَوْ مِثْلِهَا

- **Context (Sabab al-Nuzul):** The Jews spread doubt among the people, saying: "Are you not amazed at this Muhammad? He commands his companions something, then forbids them from it. He says one thing today and reverses it tomorrow. This Qur'an is nothing but his own speech — he adds and removes as he wishes."
- **Allah's answer:** "Whatever verse We abrogate (*nansakh*) or cause to be forgotten (*nunsiha*), We bring in its place one **better** than it or **equal** to it."
- **Why does abrogation occur?**
  1. To **remove hardship** — an initially difficult ruling is eased.
  2. To **increase the believers' reward** — a new ruling brings greater *ajr* in the hereafter.
- **The key principle:** Allah is *Qadeer* (All-Powerful), *'Alim* (All-Knowing), *Hakeem* (All-Wise) — nothing He issues except it is **good and beneficial** for His servants. We do not always see the wisdom, but Allah knows what we do not.
- Closing affirmation: **أَلَمْ تَعْلَمْ أَنَّ اللَّهَ عَلَى كُلِّ شَيْءٍ قَدِيرٌ** — Do you not know that Allah is capable over all things?

---

### Ayah 107 — أَلَمْ تَعْلَمْ أَنَّ اللَّهَ لَهُ مُلْكُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ

- Allah owns *mulk al-samawati wal-ard* — the dominion of the heavens and the earth.
- He is *al-Malik al-Mutasarrif fi shu'un al-khalq* — the Sovereign who disposes the affairs of all creation.
- He rules as He wills (*yahkumu bima sha'*) and commands as He wills (*ya'muru bima sha'*).
- *Al-Amru wal-Nahyu* — the authority to command and to prohibit belongs exclusively to Allah.
- **Reminder of human ignorance:** *La tadri nafsun madha taksibu ghadan* — No soul knows what it will do tomorrow. If we do not even know our own future, how can we question Allah's wisdom in abrogating a ruling?
- **وَمَا لَكُم مِّن دُونِ اللَّهِ مِن وَلِيٍّ وَلَا نَصِيرٍ** — You have no protector and no helper apart from Allah. He is *ni'ma al-Nasir wal-Mu'een*.

---

### Ayah 108 — أَمْ تُرِيدُونَ أَن تَسْأَلُوا رَسُولَكُمْ كَمَا سُئِلَ مُوسَى مِن قَبْلُ

- **Prohibition of excessive questioning:** Believers are warned not to pepper the Prophet ﷺ with excessive, unnecessary questions as Bani Israel questioned Musa عليه السلام.
- **Example:** The story of the slaughtered cow (Surah Al-Baqarah's earlier passage) — Bani Israel asked question after question not out of genuine desire to understand but to find a way out of the obligation. Despite this, Allah granted them the ability to fulfil the command and they eventually slaughtered the cow.
- **Two types of questions distinguished:**
  1. Questions aimed at understanding and practising — **praiseworthy and encouraged**.
  2. Questions aimed at escaping or complicating a ruling — **prohibited**.
- The Sahaba رضي الله عنهم are praised implicitly: they listened carefully, sought to understand, and practised — they did not ask frivolous questions.
- **Warning:** Whoever exchanges *iman* for *kufr* — *faqad dalla sawa' al-sabil* — has gone completely astray from the straight path.

---

### Ayah 109 — وَدَّ كَثِيرٌ مِّنْ أَهْلِ الْكِتَابِ لَوْ يَرُدُّونَكُم مِّن بَعْدِ إِيمَانِكُمْ كُفَّارًا

- A great number of Ahl al-Kitab **wish** they could turn the Muslims back to disbelief after they have accepted faith.
- *Ayilaw yusayirunakum kuffaran* — to transform you into disbelievers (*Sayara Yusayiru* — to cause someone to become something).
- Their motive: **hasad** (envy) and the evil within their own souls — *hamalatuhum anfusuhum al-khabithah* — their own vile inner nature prompted them once it became clear to them that the Muslims held the truth.
- **Allah's command to believers:** *Fa'afu wasfahu* — Pardon them and overlook them **until** Allah brings His command (i.e., the permission for Jihad).
- Practical implication: Before the permission for military Jihad was given, Muslims were commanded to respond to hostility with patience and forbearance, not retaliation.
- **Allah's reassurance:** *Inna Allaha 'ala kulli shay'in Qadeer* — Allah is capable over all things; the time for justice will come.

---

### Ayah 110 — وَأَقِيمُوا الصَّلَاةَ وَآتُوا الزَّكَاةَ

- In the meantime — while awaiting the command — believers are directed to the two foundational pillars:
  - **Salah** — the physical act of worship
  - **Zakah** — the financial act of worship
- These represent the two dimensions of 'Ibadah: bodily and monetary.
- **وَمَا تُقَدِّمُوا لِأَنفُسِكُم مِّنْ خَيْرٍ تَجِدُوهُ عِندَ اللَّهِ** — Whatever good you send forward for yourselves — salah, charity, righteous deeds, obligatory or voluntary — you will find it preserved with Allah on the Day of Judgment.
- Closing: **إِنَّ اللَّهَ بِمَا تَعْمَلُونَ بَصِيرٌ** — Allah is fully aware (*Basir*, *Raqib*) of everything you do and will recompense you for it.

## Grammar and Language Notes

- **رَاعِنَا — Morphological Analysis:**
  - Root: ر-ع-ي (Ra-'Ayn-Ya)
  - Bab: Ufa'ala → *Ra'a Yura'i Mura'atan*
  - Form used: Amr (command) → *Ra'i* (with kasrah under the 'Ayn, no Ya)
  - *Na* = Maf'ul bih (object pronoun "us")
  - Full meaning: "Watch over us / Pay attention to us"

- **نَسَخَ يَنْسَخُ نَسْخًا:**
  - Mujarrad (base form)
  - Meaning: to abrogate, to cancel, to change a ruling

- **نَسِيَ يَنْسَى (Nasiya Yansa) vs. أَنْسَى يُنْسِي (Ansa Yunsi):**
  - *Nasiya Yansa* = to forget (intransitive, Mujarrad, same bab as *Samia Yasma'*)
  - *Ansa Yunsi Insa'an* = to cause someone to forget (transitive causative, Bab Ifa'al)
  - In the ayah *نُنْسِهَا* = "We cause it to be forgotten" — Bab Ifa'al, 1st person plural

- **عَفَا يَعْفُو عَفْوًا:** Bab Nasara/Fa'ala — to pardon, to forgive
- **صَفَحَ يَصْفَحُ صَفْحًا:** To overlook, to excuse — close in meaning to *'Afw* but emphasizes turning one's face away from the offence
- **وَدَّ يَوَدُّ:** To wish, to desire, to like — used here to describe the wishful thinking of the disbelievers

## Lessons and Practical Takeaways

1. **Guard your speech carefully.** Even a seemingly innocuous word can be weaponised. When a word becomes a tool of mockery or disrespect toward the Prophet ﷺ or Islam, it must be abandoned without hesitation.

2. **Do not imitate the enemies of Islam — even in speech.** The Jews changed and twisted words as they did with their own scriptures. We must be vigilant that we are following the Sunnah and not the habits of those who oppose Islam.

3. **Trust Allah's wisdom in all His rulings.** Abrogation is not evidence of contradiction — it is evidence of *Hikmah*. Allah knows our nature, our capacity, and what will benefit us most in this life and the next. The *lore* belongs to Him; we follow with trust and obedience.

4. **Ask questions to understand and practice, not to escape obligation.** The praiseworthy approach is to listen attentively, understand deeply, and act. Asking excessively to avoid a ruling leads to the same path as Bani Israel.

5. **The hatred of the disbelievers toward Muslims is real and ongoing.** It is not personal paranoia — it is confirmed by Allah in the Qur'an. This should make us firm in our faith rather than seeking their approval.

6. **Preserve Salah and Zakah especially in difficult times.** When faced with hostility, the believer's response is not despair or revenge — it is doubling down on worship and good deeds. Everything sent forward is preserved with Allah.

7. **Patience before the permission for action.** There is a time for forbearance and a time for action. Until the command comes, pardon and overlook — Allah is capable of dealing with the oppressors when the time is right.

8. **No protector or helper exists besides Allah.** No worldly alliance, no power, no nation can substitute for the *wilayah* of Allah. The believers' security rests entirely with Him.

## Historical Context and Stories Mentioned

- **Jewish Community in Madinah:** At the time of the Prophet ﷺ, the Jewish tribes in Madinah were Arabic-speaking and interacted with the Muslim community. They were aware of the linguistic nuance of words like *Ra'ina* and exploited this to insult the Prophet ﷺ covertly.

- **Sabab al-Nuzul of Ayah 106 (Naskh Ayah):** It is narrated that the Jews spread doubt by saying: "Are you not amazed at Muhammad? He commands his companions to do something then forbids it. He says something today and retracts it tomorrow. This Qur'an is nothing but Muhammad's own words — he adds and deletes as he wishes." In response, Ayah 106 was revealed, explaining the wisdom and divine authority behind abrogation.

- **Story of Bani Israel and the Cow (reference):** The session referenced the well-known story from earlier in Surah Al-Baqarah, where Bani Israel asked Musa عليه السلام an excessive series of questions about the cow they were commanded to slaughter — not out of sincere inquiry but to avoid compliance. This serves as the negative example against which the Muslims' conduct is measured.

- **Story of Bani Israel at Mount Tur (reference):** The session also briefly referenced the incident where a selected group of Bani Israel demanded to *see* Allah directly at Mount Tur — they escalated from wanting to hear Allah speak to demanding direct vision, an example of perpetually raising the bar of demands to escape submission.

## Q&A Highlights

- **Q: Were the Jews in Madinah speaking Arabic or Hebrew when they misused "Ra'ina"?**
  - **A:** They were speaking Arabic. The Jewish tribes living in Madinah at the time of the Prophet ﷺ were Arabic-speaking, which is precisely what made the wordplay possible — the same sound carried an insulting meaning they could exploit while claiming innocence.

- **Q (implicit from context): Why would Allah abrogate a ruling at all if the Qur'an is perfect?**
  - **A (from tafseer):** Abrogation is itself a sign of Allah's wisdom and mercy. Some rulings were replaced to remove hardship from the believers; others were replaced with rulings that earn the believer greater reward in the hereafter. The change reflects Allah's perfect knowledge of His creation's needs across time — it is not evidence of error but of ongoing divine wisdom.
