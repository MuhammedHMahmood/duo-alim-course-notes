## Word Patterns (Awzan) Covered

### Ism Fa'il (Active Participle / Doer Noun)
- Pattern: **فَاعِل** (Fa'ilun)
- Formed by: placing a Mad Alif after the first root letter, Kasra on the middle letter, Tanwin on the last
- Example: سَمِعَ → **سَامِع** (Sami'un) — one who hears

### Ism Maf'ul (Passive Participle / Receiver Noun)
- Pattern: **مَفْعُول** (Maf'ulun)
- Formed by: prefixing Meem with Fathah, Sukun on first root letter, Dhamma on second root letter, Alif Madd, Tanwin on last
- Example: فَعَلَ → **مَفْعُول** (Maf'ulun) — that which is acted upon

### Ism Tafdhil (Elative / Comparative-Superlative)
- Pattern: **أَفْعَل** (Af'alu)
- Expresses degrees of comparison: good/better/best, bad/worse/worst
- Example: كَبِيرٌ → **أَكْبَر** (Akbaru) — greater/greatest (as in Allahu Akbar)
- Has singular, dual, plural, and broken plural forms for male and female

### Sifah Mushabbahah (Adjective Resembling Ism Fa'il — permanent/intensive quality)
- Multiple patterns, not restricted to one scale; examples:
  - **حَسَن** (Hasanun) — handsome/excellent
  - **جُنُب** (Junubun) — in a state of ritual impurity
  - **سَجَن** — variant pattern
- The pattern **فَعِيل** (e.g., سَمِيع، عَلِيم) is used exclusively for Allah's attributes (indicating a permanent, perfect quality)
- Quranic example: إِنَّكَ أَنتَ السَّمِيعُ الْعَلِيمُ (Surah Al-Baqarah, 2:127)

### Ism Alah (Noun of Instrument)
- Patterns:
  - **مِفْعَل** — e.g., مِفْتَح (Miftah) — key (tool for opening)
  - **مِفْعَال** — e.g., مِرْوَاح (Mirwahah) — fan (tool for creating airflow)
  - **مِفْعَلَة** — e.g., مِخْيَطَة (Mikhyatah) — needle (tool for sewing)
- Broken plurals exist: e.g., مَفَاتِيح (Mafatih) — keys (plural of Miftah), appearing in Surah Al-Qasas

### Ism Zarf (Noun of Place or Time)
- Pattern: **مَفْعَل / مَفْعِل** (Maf'alun / Maf'ilun)
- Examples:
  - لَعِبَ → **مَلْعَب** (Mal'ab) — place of playing/playground
  - كَانَ يَكُونُ → **مَكَان** (Makan) — place (temporary)
  - سَجَدَ → **مَسْجِد** (Masjid) — place of prostration; broken plural: مَسَاجِد
  - حَشَرَ → **مَحْشَر** (Mahshar) — place of gathering
  - طَلَعَ → **مَطْلَع** (Matla') — place/time of rising (as of the sun)
  - خَرَجَ → **مَشْرِق** (Mashriq) — east (place of sunrise); plural: مَشَارِق
  - غَرَبَ → **مَغْرِب** (Maghrib) — west (place of sunset); plural: مَغَارِب
  - سَكَنَ → **مَسْكَن** (Maskan) — dwelling/residence
  - عَاشَ → **مَعِيشَة** (Ma'ishah) — livelihood/place of living

## Verb Conjugation Tables

### Forming the Amr (Imperative) — Step-by-Step

Starting from the Mudari' of the second person (Hadhir):

| Step | Action | Example (فَعَلَ) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Begin with Mudari' (2nd person) | تَفْعَلُ |
| 2 | Drop the sign of Mudari' (تَ) | فْعَلُ (begins with Sukun) |
| 3 | Apply Jazm (Sukun) on last letter | فْعَلْ |
| 4 | Add Hamzatul Wasl at beginning | اِفْعَلْ |
| 5 | Determine Hamzah's Harakat: if 2nd-to-last letter has Fathah or Kasra → Kasra on Hamzah; if Dhamma → Dhamma on Hamzah | اِفْعَلْ (Kasra) / اُفْعَلْ (Dhamma) |

**Examples:**

| Mudari' | Amr | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| تَفْتَحُ | اِفْتَحْ | Open! |
| تَضْرِبُ | اِضْرِبْ | Hit/Strike! |
| يَنْصُرُ → تَنْصُرُ | اُنْصُرْ | Help/Assist! |
| تَعْدُو | اُعْدُ | Run! (weak letter waw drops) |
| تَرْجِعُ | اِرْجِعْ | Return! |

**Six Amr forms (real 2nd person — Hadhir):**

| Form | Pattern | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Wahid Mudhakkar | اِفْعَلْ | Singular masculine |
| Wahid Mu'annath | اِفْعَلِي | Singular feminine |
| Muthanna | اِفْعَلَا | Dual (m/f) |
| Jama' Mudhakkar | اِفْعَلُوا | Masculine plural |
| Jama' Mu'annath | اِفْعَلْنَ | Feminine plural |

**Lam al-Amr (3rd person command):**

| Form | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Ghaib Wahid | لِيَفْعَلْ | Let him do it |
| Ghaib Jama' | لِيَفْعَلُوا | Let them do it |
| Mutakallim | لِأَفْعَلْ | Let me do it |
| Mutakallim Jama' | لِنَفْعَلْ | Let us do it |

### Laam and Noon of Emphasis (Tawkid) on Mudari'

Five ways to emphasize a Mudari' verb (six including the plain form):

| Method | Example (تَسْمَعُ — she hears) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Plain (no emphasis) | تَسْمَعُ | Default |
| Lam al-Tawkid only | لَتَسْمَعُ | Surely she hears |
| Noon Thaqilah only | تَسْمَعَنَّ | The letter before Noon Thaqilah takes Fathah |
| Noon Khafifah only | تَسْمَعَنْ | Simple Noon suffix |
| Lam + Noon Thaqilah | لَتَسْمَعَنَّ | Strongest emphasis |
| Lam + Noon Khafifah | لَتَسْمَعَنْ | Strong emphasis |

**Key rules for Noon Thaqilah attachment:**

| Entry | Base | With Noon Thaqilah | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wahid Mudhakkar Ghaib | يَفْعَلُ | لَيَفْعَلَنَّ | Letter before Noon gets Fathah (to distinguish from plural) |
| Jama' Mudhakkar Ghaib | يَفْعَلُونَ | لَيَفْعَلُنَّ | Original Noon and Waw drop before Noon Thaqilah |
| Dual | يَفْعَلَانِ | لَيَفْعَلَانِّ | Original Noon drops |
| Jama' Mu'annath | يَفْعَلْنَ | — | Some forms don't exist (awkward to pronounce; language disallows) |

**Noon Khafifah:** Same attachment points; several plural/dual forms do not exist in the language and are therefore simply not used.

### Al-Nahi (Prohibition — Negative Command)

Formed with **لَا + Mudari' + Jazm** on last letter. The sign of Mudari' is **not** dropped (unlike Amr).

| Amr (Do!) | Nahi (Don't!) | Meaning of Nahi |
|---|---|---|
| اِفْعَلْ | لَا تَفْعَلْ | Do not do it (m. singular) |
| اِفْعَلِي | لَا تَفْعَلِي | Do not do it (f. singular) |
| اِفْعَلُوا | لَا تَفْعَلُوا | Do not do it (m. plural) |
| اِفْعَلْنَ | لَا تَفْعَلْنَ | Do not do it (f. plural) — Noon of femininity never drops |

**Key distinction — Amr vs. Nahi:**

| | Amr | Nahi |
|---|---|---|
| Sign of Mudari' | Dropped | Kept |
| Beginning | Hamzatul Wasl added if needed | No change at beginning |
| End | Jazm on last letter | Jazm on last letter |
| Particle | None (or Lam al-Amr for 3rd person) | لَا (La Najiyah/Nahiyah) |

## Root Analysis

### Ism Fa'il vs. Fa'il in Jumlah Fi'liyah — Key Distinction

| Concept | Nature | Example |
|---|---|---|
| الْفَاعِل في الجُمْلَة (Fa'il in a sentence) | Grammatical subject/doer within a verbal sentence; can be any noun | يَجْلِسُ الرَّجُلُ — الرَّجُلُ is the Fa'il |
| اسْمُ الْفَاعِل (Ism Fa'il) | A derived noun built from the verb's root letters; means "one who regularly performs that action" | جَلَسَ → جَالِس (one who sits) |

The Fa'il in a sentence answers "who did the verb?" — it could be any noun. The Ism Fa'il is a specific word derived using the فَاعِل pattern from the root letters, carrying the meaning of a habitual or characteristic doer (like English: build → builder, hear → hearer).

### Similarly: Ism Maf'ul vs. Maf'ul Bihi

| Concept | Nature | Example |
|---|---|---|
| الْمَفْعُول بِه (Maf'ul Bihi) | Grammatical object in a sentence; any noun receiving the action | سَمِعَ الرَّجُلَ — الرَّجُلَ is Maf'ul Bihi |
| اسْمُ الْمَفْعُول (Ism Maf'ul) | Derived noun on مَفْعُول pattern; means "one upon whom the action is done" | أَرْسَلَ → مُرْسَل (one who is sent = messenger) |

### Arabic Nuance: Near-Synonyms Are Never Identical

Discussed in context of Ism Zarf examples — no two Arabic words for the same concept are 100% identical in meaning:

| Word | Root | Nuance |
|---|---|---|
| مَكَان (Makan) | كَانَ يَكُونُ | Place (temporary presence) |
| مَسْكَن (Maskan) | سَكَنَ | Dwelling (medium-term residence, e.g. rented apartment) |
| مَعِيشَة (Ma'ishah) | عَاشَ | Place of livelihood (permanent living + earning sustenance) |

## Key Takeaways

1. **Lam and Noon al-Tawkid produce five degrees of emphasis** beyond the plain Mudari', giving six total ways to express the same action — each with increasing communicative weight.

2. **The letter before Noon Thaqilah always takes Fathah** in the singular (Wahid) forms; this is what distinguishes singular from plural in emphasis constructions.

3. **Amr formation** = drop sign of Mudari' (تَ for 2nd person) + Jazm on last letter + add Hamzatul Wasl if needed; the Hamzah takes Kasra if the 2nd-to-last root letter has Fathah/Kasra, and Dhamma if it has Dhamma.

4. **Nahi = لَا + Mudari' + Jazm** — unlike Amr, you keep the sign of Mudari'; the Noon of femininity (نُون النِّسْوَة) never drops in Nahi.

5. **Six derived nouns (Asma' Mushtaqqah):** Ism Fa'il, Ism Maf'ul, Ism Tafdhil, Sifah Mushabbahah, Ism Alah, Ism Zarf — each has a specific pattern derived from the root letters of the verb.

6. **Critical distinction:** Ism Fa'il ≠ Fa'il in a sentence; Ism Maf'ul ≠ Maf'ul Bihi. The Ism versions are derived nouns (with their own built-in root letters); the grammatical Fa'il/Maf'ul can be any noun in a sentence.

7. **Sifah Mushabbahah on the pattern فَعِيل** (e.g., سَمِيع، عَلِيم) denotes a permanent and absolute quality, which is why this pattern appears in the Names and Attributes of Allah — e.g., إِنَّكَ أَنتَ السَّمِيعُ الْعَلِيمُ (Surah Al-Baqarah, 2:127).

8. **Ism Tafdhil on pattern أَفْعَل** — "Allahu Akbar" is a living example; أَكْبَر is Ism Tafdhil from كَبِيرٌ.

9. **Broken plurals are unpredictable** — e.g., مَسْجِد → مَسَاجِد, مَكَان → أَمْكِنَة/أَمَاكِن, مِفْتَاح → مَفَاتِيح. These must be learned per word; no single formula applies.

10. **No two Arabic words share 100% identical meaning** — illustrated by مَكَان/مَسْكَن/مَعِيشَة, and مَشْرِق/مَطْلَع. Arabic's precision is part of what makes the Qur'an linguistically miraculous.
