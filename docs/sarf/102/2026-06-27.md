---

## Word Patterns (Awzan) Covered

### 1. Sifat al-Mubalagha (صِفَة المُبَالَغَة) — Intensive Adjective
- Expresses performing an action at a **high degree, high frequency, or high intensity**
- Derived from a verb root but conveys the meaning of doing that action excessively or to an extreme
- **No gender distinction** in the base form — the same pattern can apply to male or female
  - Exception: when a gender distinction is intended, a feminine form is derived separately (e.g., ʿAlīm → ʿAlīma)
- Common patterns include: **فَعَّال، فَعِيل، فَعُول، فَعِل، مِفْعَال**
  - Examples: Ḍarāb, Khalāq, Jabbār, Ṣiddīq, Fahhām, Miskīn

### 2. Ismu al-Tafdhil (اسم التفضيل) — Comparative/Superlative Adjective
- Pattern: **أَفْعَل** (Afʿal)
- Conveys the meaning of *more* or *most* — equivalent to comparative/superlative in English (good → better → best)
- Expresses a quality **in comparison to others**
- Feminine form: **فُعْلَى** (Fuʿlā)
  - Example: Akbar (أكبر) → Kubrā (كُبْرَى)
- Example: *Allāhu Akbar* = Allāhu Akbaru min ayyi shayʾ (greater than anything)
- Example: *Huwa akullu al-qawm* = He is the greatest eater among the people

### 3. Thulathi Mazid Fih (الثلاثي المزيد فيه) — Augmented Triliteral Verbs
- The six basic categories of **Thulathi Mujarrad** (bare triliteral verbs) can be **augmented** by adding extra letters (at the front, middle, or end) to create new verb patterns with new or intensified meanings
- Additional letters are drawn from: **Hamza (ء), Alif (ا), Ta (ت), Nun (ن), Shadda (doubled letter)**
- These extra letters give the verb pattern its name **Mazid Fih** (مزيد فيه) — "with addition"

**Positions of added letters:**
| Added Letter | Position | Example Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| Hamza (ء) | Beginning | أَكْرَمَ — Akrama, Yukrimu (Bāb Ikrām) |
| Alif (ا) | After first root letter | فَاعَلَ — Nāṣara, Yunāṣiru |
| Shadda on middle letter | Middle (ʿAyn) | فَعَّلَ — Naṣṣara |
| Hamzatu'l-Wasl + Ta | Beginning | اِسْتَنْصَرَ — Istanṣara, Yastanṣiru |
| Hamzatu'l-Wasl + Nun | Beginning | اِنْفَتَرَ — Infatara, Yanfatiru |
| Hamzatu'l-Wasl + Shadda on last letter | Beginning + end | اِفْعَلَّ |

## Root Analysis

### Root ن-ص-ر (N-Ṣ-R) — Help / Support
- Base meaning: to help, to give victory
- Mazid Fih extensions show how **adding letters shifts the meaning** from simple helping → mutual support → seeking help
- Istanṣara follows the same pattern as **Istaghfara** (to seek forgiveness from Ghafara — to forgive)

### Root ق-ت-ل (Q-T-L) — Kill
- Base meaning: to kill (Qatala)
- With Shadda on ʿAyn (Qattala): massacre — intensification in degree and scale
- With Alif after Fāʾ (Qātala): mutual combat — bilateral action introduced
- With Hamzatu'l-Waṣl + Tāʾ (Iqtatala): fight one another — reflexive/collective action
- With Istafʿala pattern (Istaqtala): to risk one's life — seeking the action upon oneself

### Root خ-ل-ق (Kh-L-Q) — Create
- Khalaqa = to create
- Khallāq (فَعَّال pattern) = The One who creates abundantly
- Used as a Divine attribute in Quran (Surah Yāsīn referenced)

### Root ك-ر-م (K-R-M) — Honor / Generosity
- Akrama (أَفْعَلَ pattern with Hamza at beginning) = to honor, to be generous
- Maṣdar: Ikrām (إِكْرَام)
- Muḍāriʿ: Yukrimu (يُكْرِمُ)

## Practice Examples

1. **Sifat Mubalagha — Khalāq:**
   - *Allāhu Khallāqun* — Allah is the abundantly creating One (creates countless things unceasingly)

2. **Ismu Tafdhil — Akbar:**
   - *Allāhu Akbar* = *Allāhu Akbaru min ayyi shayʾ* — Allah is greater than anything/everything

3. **Ismu Tafdhil — comparative use:**
   - *Huwa akullu al-qawm* — He is the greatest eater among his people (comparing him to all others in his nation)

4. **Mazid Fih — Istanṣara:**
   - Parallel: Ghafara → Istaghfara (to seek forgiveness)
   - Naṣara → Istanṣara (to seek help/assistance)

5. **Mazid Fih — Qātala:**
   - *Qātalū fī sabīlillāh* — They fought in the path of Allah

6. **Mazid Fih — Infaṭara:**
   - Root: Faṭara (to split)
   - Infaṭara = to split apart, to be cleft (cf. Surah al-Infiṭār)

7. **Gender in Sifat Mubalagha:**
   - ʿAlīm (very knowledgeable man) / ʿAlīma (very knowledgeable woman) — feminine formed by adding Tāʾ Marbūṭa
   - Ḥamūl — used for the she-camel (the carrier), illustrating natural gender with this pattern

## Key Takeaways

1. **Sifat al-Mubalagha** intensifies the quality of a doer — it doesn't just say someone does X, it says they do X *excessively and at a high degree*. Common patterns are فَعَّال, فَعِيل, فَعُول, مِفْعَال, فَعِل.

2. **Ismu al-Tafdhil** (pattern: أَفْعَل / فُعْلَى) is the comparative/superlative and always implies comparison against others. It has no gender difference in its base form — the feminine is فُعْلَى.

3. **Thulathi Mujarrad** has six base Abwāb. These six are the foundation of all Arabic verb morphology.

4. **Thulathi Mazid Fih** adds letters (Hamza, Alif, Tāʾ, Nūn, Shadda) to those base patterns, generating new Abwāb. Each additional letter changes or intensifies the meaning in a predictable way — e.g., Alif after Fāʾ introduces mutuality (فَاعَلَ); Istafʿala introduces seeking or requesting the action.

5. **Learning the Abwāb unlocks the whole system** — once you know the base pattern, you can derive the Maṣdar, Fāʿil, Mafʿūl, Ismu Tafdhil, and all Mazid extensions systematically.

6. **This was the final session** of the Sarf 102 semester. The advanced verb paradigms (Thulathi Mazid Fih) are the natural continuation beyond Thulathi Mujarrad and should be reviewed as the next level of Sarf study.
