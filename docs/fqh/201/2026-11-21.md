## Session Overview

This session opened the chapter of Zakat (*Kitab uz-Zakat*), covering Imam Quduri's arrangement of topics, the linguistic and shari'i definitions of Zakat, its historical timeline of becoming Fard, the seven conditions for Zakat to become obligatory, items exempt from Zakat, the role of intention (*niyyah*) in Zakat, and a detailed walkthrough of the Zakat schedule for camels including the named age-categories of she-camels.

---

## Key Themes

1. **Why Zakat follows Salah in Imam Quduri's arrangement** — Salah is a bodily worship; Zakat is a wealth-related worship. Additionally, Salah and Zakat are mentioned together in 32 places in the Quran, establishing a strong textual and logical link.
2. **Definition and meanings of Zakat** — both linguistic (lughawi) and shari'i meanings explored.
3. **Seven conditions (shurut) for Zakat to become Fard** — the main doctrinal content of the session.
4. **Niyyah (intention) as a condition for valid Zakat** — when it must be made and edge cases.
5. **Scholarly differences** — Ahnaf vs. Imam Shafi'i/Malik/Ahmad on Zakat for children and the insane; Imam Zufur vs. the Ahnaf on a person who gives all wealth as Sadaqah without Zakat intention.
6. **Zakat on camels** — nisab, age-categories of she-camels, and the progressive Zakat schedule.

## Important Vocabulary

| Arabic Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Zakat (زكاة) | Linguistically: increase, growth, purification, blessing, reward (*ajr/thawab*). Shari'i: giving a fixed percentage of wealth (or its equivalent) to the poor by transferring ownership |
| Nisab (نصاب) | The minimum threshold of wealth on which Zakat becomes due |
| Hawlani Hawl (حولان الحول) | Passage of one complete (Islamic lunar) year over the wealth |
| Saima (سائمة) | A grazing animal that feeds itself outdoors for the majority of the year; Zakat-eligible |
| Alufa / Uloofa (عَلوفة) | An animal fed by its owner at home for more than half the year; not Zakat-eligible |
| Mali Nami (مال نامي) | "Growing wealth" — wealth with growth potential; a condition for Zakat eligibility |
| Muqatab / Mukatib (مكاتب) | A slave who has contracted to purchase his freedom from his master by installment payments |
| Mudabbar (مدبَّر) | A slave whose freedom is contingent on his master's death |
| Binti Makhaz (بنت مخاض) | One-year-old she-camel entering her second year (named for the pain of her mother's delivery) |
| Binti Laboon (بنت لبون) | Two-year-old she-camel entering her third year (named from *laban*, milk — her mother is producing milk again) |
| Hiqqa (حِقَّة) | Three-year-old she-camel entering her fourth year |
| Jiz'ah (جَذَعة) | Four-year-old she-camel entering her fifth year |
| Sha'ah (شاة) | A goat or sheep; the unit of Zakat due on the lower camel counts |
| Fard (فرض) | An obligation established by a *dalil qat'i* (definitive proof, i.e., Quran or mutawatir hadith) |
| Wajib (واجب) | An obligation established by a probable proof (khabar wahid); in Imam Quduri's style, sometimes used interchangeably with Fard |
| Istihsan (استحسان) | Juristic preference; departing from strict analogy (qiyas) for a stronger or more equitable ruling |
| Qiyas (قياس) | Analogical reasoning |
| Sahibi Nisab (صاحب نصاب) | One who possesses wealth at or above the nisab threshold |
| Dalil Qat'i (دليل قطعي) | Definitive proof (Quranic verse); establishes Fard |
| Siga al-Amr (صيغة الأمر) | Imperative grammatical form; used to derive obligation |

## Fiqh Rulings & Evidence

### Fardiyyat (Obligatory Status) of Zakat

Three categories of dalil establish Zakat as Fard:

1. **Quran** (*dalil qat'i*) — multiple explicit ayat; the imperative form (*siga al-amr*) is used, confirming Fardiyyat.
2. **Hadith** — The Prophet ﷺ said: *"Pay Zakat on your wealth and money."*
3. **Ijma' (Consensus)** — Every Muslim since the time of the Prophet ﷺ has acted upon it continuously.

> **Note on Imam Quduri's wording:** He uses the word *wajib* ("az-zakatu wajibatun") rather than *fard*, but Sahib al-Hidaya and Sahib al-Binaya explain this is Imam Quduri's stylistic interchangeability — it is in fact Fard by the consensus of the fuqaha. The dalil from the Quran alone establishes Fardiyyat.

---

### Historical Timing of Zakat Becoming Fard

| Opinion | View |
|---|---|
| Some fuqaha | Became Fard in the **1st year of Hijra** |
| Others | Became Fard in the **3rd year of Hijra** |
| Imam Ibn Khuzaymah رحمه الله | Became Fard **before the migration to Madina**; his dalil is the hadith of Umm Sulaym: when Ja'far رضي الله عنه described Islam to the Negus (Najashi), he mentioned Salah, Zakat, and fasting — meaning Zakat was already obligatory at that time |
| Imam Ibn Khuzaymah also noted | The Prophet ﷺ ordered Sadaqatul Fitr before the formal Zakat order came |

---

### Items Exempt from Zakat

The following are **not** subject to Zakat:
- The house in which a person resides
- Clothes (even if unused, as long as purchased for personal use — intention determines eligibility)
- Household items (*asas*)
- Animals used for personal transportation or riding
- Slaves used for personal service or business assistance
- Weapons owned for personal use

**Underlying principle:** These items are in active personal use and are not *mali nami* (growing/productive wealth).

---

### Zakat on Trade Goods — Intention Governs Eligibility

- Goods purchased with the **intention to sell** → Zakat due (trade goods).
- Goods purchased with the **intention of personal use** → No Zakat, even if never used.
- Example: A book purchased for one's own study → no Zakat. The same book purchased intending to resell → Zakat due.
- A cook who buys equipment to earn his livelihood (not to resell the equipment) → no Zakat on the equipment.

---

### Ruling on Debt and Zakat

| Scenario | Ruling |
|---|---|
| Debt equals or exceeds entire wealth | No Zakat due |
| Has debt but wealth exceeds the debt and the excess reaches nisab | Pays Zakat only on the excess |
| Excess does not reach nisab | No Zakat due |

---

### Ruling: When Must Zakat Be Paid After Conditions Are Met?

| Scholar | Opinion |
|---|---|
| **Imam Karkhi رحمه الله** | Must be paid immediately once conditions are met; delay is sinful. Dalil: the Quranic verse in siga al-amr. |
| **Imam Abu Bakr al-Jassas رحمه الله & Muhammad bin Shuja' al-Balkhi رحمه الله** | A person has his entire lifetime to pay; delay is not sinful, but payment before death is obligatory |
| **Preferred (Hanafi) position** | The second opinion — delay is permissible but one must pay before death |

**If a person becomes bankrupt before paying:** Zakat becomes *sakit* (waived). If he later regains wealth above nisab, the new wealth's Zakat begins afresh — the earlier waived Zakat does not return as a debt.

---

### Ruling: Giving All Wealth as Sadaqah Without Zakat Intention

**Question:** If a person gives away 100% of his wealth as Sadaqah without making intention of Zakat — is Zakat fulfilled?

| Position | View & Dalil |
|---|---|
| **Imam Zufur رحمه الله + Imam Shafi'i, Malik, Ahmad رحمهم الله** | Zakat is NOT fulfilled. Dalil: Sadaqah has two types — Nafl and Fard (Zakat). Intention is what distinguishes them. Without Zakat intention, only Nafl Sadaqah was performed and the Zakat obligation remains. |
| **Ahnaf (preferred)** | Zakat IS fulfilled by *istihsan*. Dalil: Zakat was only 2.5% of his wealth; since he gave 100%, the 2.5% was necessarily included. Additionally, by analogy with Ramadan fasting — if a person makes intention of Nafl fast during Ramadan, the Fard fast is still counted (and the Nafl becomes qada). Similarly here, even though he intended Sadaqah (Nafl), the Fard (Zakat) was included within what he gave. |

## Conditions & Categories

### Seven Conditions for Zakat to Become Fard

1. **Hurriyyah (Freedom)** — The person must be free; no Zakat on a Mukatab or Mudabbar slave (also because a slave does not have independent ownership of wealth — his wealth belongs to his master).
2. **'Aql (Sanity)** — Must not be insane.
3. **Bulugh (Adulthood)** — Must have reached the age of maturity.
4. **Islam** — Must be Muslim; no Zakat collected from non-Muslims even under Islamic governance.
5. **Sahibi Nisab** — Must possess wealth at or above the minimum threshold (varies by type of wealth).
6. **Tamam al-Milk (Complete Ownership)** — Must be the full and complete owner of the wealth.
7. **Hawlani Hawl (One Full Islamic Year)** — An entire Islamic lunar year must have passed over the wealth while it was at or above nisab.

> **Note on conditions 2 & 3 — Ahnaf vs. Jumhur:**
> - **Ahnaf:** No Zakat on children or the insane. Dalil: the hadith *"The pen (of accountability) is lifted from three: the sleeping person until he wakes, the child until puberty, and the insane until he recovers his sanity."* Zakat requires shari'i accountability, which is absent here.
> - **Imam Shafi'i, Malik, Ahmad رحمهم الله:** Zakat is still Fard on children and the insane. Dalil: Zakat is a right of the wealth itself (*farida muta'alliqa bil-mal*), not dependent on the owner's mental capacity.

---

### Six Conditions for an Animal to Be Zakat-Eligible

1. Must be of the three species: **Ibl (camel)**, **Baqar (cow/ox)**, or **Ghanam (sheep/goat)**.
2. Must be **Saima** — grazing freely outdoors for more than half the year (not fed by the owner).
3. Must **not** be raised primarily for breeding or milking (if raised for milk or trade, a different Zakat applies).
4. Must be **healthy** — animals with a physical defect (*'ayb*) are not Zakat-eligible.
5. Must reach the **minimum number** (nisab) — 5 for camels; details for cow and sheep/goat to follow.
6. Must **not be underage** — very young animals are excluded.

---

### Zakat Schedule for Camels

> Animals must be **Saima** (grazing) and one full year must pass. The due amount is paid as a she-camel of a specified age category, or its equivalent monetary value.

| Number of Camels | Zakat Due |
|---|---|
| 1 – 4 | Nothing |
| 5 – 9 | 1 Sha'ah (goat or sheep) |
| 10 – 14 | 2 Sha'ah |
| 15 – 19 | 3 Sha'ah |
| 20 – 24 | 4 Sha'ah |
| 25 – 35 | 1 Binti Makhaz (1-year she-camel) |
| 36 – 45 | 1 Binti Laboon (2-year she-camel) |
| 46 – 60 | 1 Hiqqa (3-year she-camel) |
| 61 – 75 | 1 Jiz'ah (4-year she-camel) |
| 76 – 90 | 2 Binti Laboon |
| 91 – 120 | 2 Hiqqa |
| 121 – 124 | 2 Hiqqa |
| 125 – 129 | 2 Hiqqa + 1 Sha'ah |
| (Continues with increments of 5 thereafter) | — |
| 275+ | Increments of 10 apply |

**She-camel age categories explained:**

| Name | Age | Etymology |
|---|---|---|
| Binti Makhaz | Completed 1 year, entering 2nd | From *makhad* (pain of delivery) — her mother recently gave birth to her |
| Binti Laboon | Completed 2 years, entering 3rd | From *laban* (milk) — her mother has resumed producing milk |
| Hiqqa | Completed 3 years, entering 4th | She has become strong enough to carry a rider or burden |
| Jiz'ah | Completed 4 years, entering 5th | She has reached full maturity |

**Key notes on the camel Zakat schedule:**
- The pattern is not uniform: increments of 5 (up to 24), then 10, then 15, then 30, then returns to 5.
- Male and female camels are counted equally for the nisab count; the Zakat payment itself is in she-camels (or monetary equivalent).
- If a person cannot produce the exact required she-camel age-category, he may pay a higher-category animal; the Zakat collector must return the price difference to the owner.
- Alternatively, Zakat on animals may always be paid in money equivalent to the value of the due animal(s).

### Niyyah (Intention) — Rules

| Scenario | Ruling |
|---|---|
| Pays Zakat at the time of payment with simultaneous intention | Valid — this is the ideal |
| Sets aside the Zakat amount in advance with clear intention, then distributes it | Valid — the prior intention at time of *'azl* (setting aside) is sufficient |
| Leaves home intending "the first £X I give to the poor today is my Zakat" | Valid |
| Gives money to the poor with absolutely no Zakat intention, then claims it was Zakat afterward | **Not valid** |
| Gives entire wealth as Sadaqah without Zakat intention | Valid by Ahnaf (*istihsan*) — see above |
| Pays more than the Zakat due | The extra is Sadaqah (Nafl); or he may specify it as advance Zakat for the following year |

> **Principle:** Niyyah is a condition for every *'ibadah* — Salah, Wudu, Ghusal, Sawm, and Zakat. Without it, the act does not count as the specified worship. Zakat is an 'ibadah; therefore intention is indispensable.

| Issue | Ahnaf | Shafi'i / Maliki / Hanbali |
|---|---|---|
| Zakat on wealth of children | Not obligatory (pen of accountability lifted) | Obligatory — Zakat is a right of the wealth |
| Zakat on wealth of the insane | Not obligatory | Obligatory |

## Lessons & Practical Application

1. **Zakat is Fard by the Quran alone** — its three-fold evidence (Quran, Hadith, Ijma') makes it among the most firmly established obligations. Denying it constitutes kufr; refusing it while believing it is Fard is a grave sin.

2. **Calculate Zakat on net wealth.** Debts must be subtracted from total wealth before determining whether nisab is met. Only the excess above the debt is counted.

3. **Personal-use items are always exempt.** Clothing, home, vehicle, tools of trade, and books purchased for personal use are never included in the Zakat calculation — regardless of their monetary value.

4. **Intention determines the nature of a charitable act.** The same payment of money can be Zakat, Kaffarah, Sadaqah al-Fitr, or Nafl Sadaqah depending solely on intention. Make a clear intention at or before the time of payment.

5. **Setting money aside in advance is a practical and valid approach.** A person may designate a sum as his Zakat and distribute it throughout the year — no fresh intention is needed at each individual disbursement.

6. **Animal Zakat can be paid in monetary equivalent.** Calculate the market value of the due she-camel (or goat/sheep) and pay that amount — especially relevant in modern contexts where paying in livestock is impractical.

7. **The *saima* condition is key for animal Zakat.** Animals kept at home and fed by the owner for more than half the year (*uloofa*) are entirely exempt — their upkeep cost is itself a financial burden on the owner.

8. **Intention of purchase determines trade-good Zakat.** An item bought for personal use is never subject to Zakat even if it appreciates in value; an item bought to resell is trade goods from the moment of purchase.

9. **Pay Zakat before death.** Even if one takes the view that delay is permissible, dying with Zakat still owed is spiritually dangerous. The obligation does not expire — it transfers to the estate if wealth exists; if one dies destitute, it is waived.

10. **Zakat paid each year applies to remaining wealth.** If Zakat is paid and the remaining balance is still above nisab, that balance is subject to Zakat again in the following year — Zakat recurs annually on held wealth.
