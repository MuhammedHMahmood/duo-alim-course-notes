# Fiqh 201 - Study Notes
**Date:** 2026-10-14

## Session Overview

This session covered two major topics: (1) actions that are disliked (makruh) during salah, with their evidences from hadith, and (2) the concept of **bana** — continuing one's salah after wudu breaks during the prayer. The class reviewed conditions under which bana is valid, the 13 conditions that must be met for bana to be permissible, and the special ruling for when hadas occurs after completing the sitting of tashahud in the final qa'dah. The session concluded with an introduction to the "masa'il of 12" to be covered in the next class.

---

## Key Themes

- **Makruhat in Salah**: A comprehensive list of disliked actions during prayer, each backed by hadith evidence
- **Facing the Qiblah**: The critical condition that one's chest/shoulders must not turn away from the qiblah — when this is violated, salah becomes invalid
- **Bana (Building upon one's salah)**: The Hanafi ruling that allows a worshipper to leave, make wudu, and return to continue salah from the point of interruption
- **13 Conditions for Valid Bana**: A detailed framework governing when bana is permissible
- **The Qa'dah Akhirah Rule**: A special principle — once a worshipper has sat for the duration of tashahud in the final sitting, subsequent violations (even deliberate ones) do not nullify the salah

## Fiqh Rulings & Evidence

### 1. Review: Status of Jama'at (Congregational Prayer)

Four scholarly positions:
- **Fard** — opinion of Imam Ahmad ibn Hanbal
- **Fard Kifayah** — opinion of Imam Shafi'i
- **Wajib** — majority of classical (mutaqaddimin) Hanafi scholars, supported by Quranic ayat
- **Sunnah Mu'akkadah** — opinion of Imam Quduri and later Hanafi scholars; the **dominant (mu'tamad) Hanafi position**

---

### 2. Review: Imam's Wudu Breaks After Salah

If an imam realizes after salah that he had no wudu, the salah of all muqtadis is invalid and must be repeated. The congregation does **not** repeat wudu — only the salah is to be repeated (with wudu, if they do not already have it).

---

### 3. Makruhat (Disliked Acts) in Salah

**Source text:** Imam Quduri states: *"It is makruh for the musalli to play with his clothes or his body..."*

| Disliked Act | Detail |
|---|---|
| Playing with clothes or body | Constantly adjusting garments or touching one's own body |
| Moving pebbles/stones (hasa) | Should be avoided; if unavoidable (e.g., for sajdah), do it **once only** |
| Cracking fingers | Forbidden during salah |
| Interlacing fingers (tashbik) | Should not be done; disrupts the proper hand position |
| Takhassur | Placing hands on hips/waist |
| Sadl | Draping a loose cloth (chador) over the head loosely so ends hang freely |
| Braiding/gathering hair (aqd al-sha'r) | Also applies to constantly touching or styling hair during salah |
| Tucking up garments (kaff al-thawb) | Tucking up clothes before/during ruku or sajdah |
| Turning right or left | Looking right or left during salah is disliked |
| Iqa' (sitting like a dog) | Sitting on hips with both knees raised and hands on ground |
| Responding to salam verbally | Speaking "wa alaykum al-salam" during salah |
| Responding to salam by hand | Raising hand/gesturing in response to salam |
| Crisscross sitting | Sitting cross-legged without a valid excuse (udhr) |
| Eating or drinking | Nullifies salah entirely |

**General principle for judging excessive movement:** If a bystander watching the musalli from a distance could reasonably think the person is **not in salah**, then the amount of movement is excessive.

---

### Evidences for Makruhat

**Playing/fidgeting in salah:**
> *"There are three things Allah dislikes: (1) playing inside salah (with the body or clothes), (2) dirty talk during fasting, and (3) laughing in the graveyard."*

**Moving pebbles:**
> Mu'ayqib (رضي الله عنه) narrated: *"I asked the Messenger of Allah ﷺ about smoothening the pebbles during salah, and he said: 'If you must do so, then let it be only once.'"* — **Tirmidhi**

**Cracking fingers:**
> Ali (رضي الله عنه) narrated that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: *"Do not crack your fingers during prayers."* — **Ibn Majah**

**Takhassur (hands on hips):**
> Abu Hurayrah (رضي الله عنه) reported that the Prophet ﷺ **forbade keeping one's hands on one's waist while praying.** — **Muslim** (also narrated by Abu Bakr)

**Sadl:**
> *"The Prophet ﷺ prohibited sadl in salah."* — **Tirmidhi**

**Tucking garments/gathering hair:**
> Ibn Abbas (رضي الله عنه) narrated that the Prophet ﷺ said: *"I have been ordered to prostrate on seven bones and not to tuck up the hair or the garments."* — **Bukhari**

**Iqa' (sitting like a dog):**
> *"The Prophet ﷺ forbade sitting like a dog during salah."* — **Bayhaqi**

**Responding to salam verbally:**
> A companion narrated: *"We used to talk while in prayer... until the verse was revealed: 'And stand before Allah in devout obedience' — then we were commanded to observe silence in prayer and forbidden to speak."* — **Muslim**

**Not responding to salam by hand:**
> A separate hadith also in **Muslim** further confirms this prohibition.

---

### 4. Salah Becomes Fasid vs. Vudu Breaks

**Important distinction:** The makruh acts listed above may render salah fasid (requiring repetition), but they do **not** break wudu. For example:
- Eating during salah → salah must be repeated, but wudu remains intact
- Breaking wudu → both salah and (where applicable) subsequent acts of worship are affected

---

### 5. Bana — Continuing Salah After Wudu Breaks

**Definition:** If wudu breaks during salah, the worshipper may leave, make wudu, and return to **continue from the point of interruption** rather than restarting entirely.

**Hanafi position:** Bana is valid and permitted, provided the 13 conditions are met.

**Imam Shafi'i's position:** Bana is **not** permitted. His reasoning: when the person walks toward the exit, their back turns to the qiblah — this violates a shart (condition) of salah, and when a shart is removed, salah becomes batil.

**Hanafi response:** The condition of facing qiblah is lifted for one who walks due to necessity (broken wudu); the act of walking is itself permitted under these circumstances.

**Dalil for Bana (Hanafi):**
> Aisha (رضي الله عنه) narrated that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: *"Whoever vomits, has a nosebleed, belches, or emits prostatic fluid should stop praying, perform ablution, then resume his prayer — and while he is in that state he should not speak."* — **Ibn Majah**
>
> A supporting hadith is also found in **Daraqutni**.

**Dalil for Istikhlaf (appointing substitute imam):**
> The practice of Abu Bakr (رضي الله عنه) leading salah and then yielding his spot to the Prophet ﷺ — **Bukhari** — establishes that an imam may appoint another to take his place mid-salah.

**Hanafi position on speech during salah:**
> If someone speaks during salah — **even accidentally/unknowingly** — the salah becomes **batil** (the Hanafi ruling). Imam Shafi'i holds that if speech was accidental (e.g., correcting the imam's recitation by mistake), the salah is not broken.

## Conditions & Categories

### 13 Conditions for Valid Bana

For a worshipper's bana to be valid after wudu breaks mid-salah, all of the following must be met:

1. **The hadas was involuntary** — the person had no control over it. If wudu was broken deliberately, bana is not allowed; salah must be restarted.

2. **The hadas originated from his own body** — not an external cause.

3. **The hadas does not require ghusl** — if ghusl (major purification) becomes obligatory (janabah), bana is not permissible; the time and acts required for ghusl are too extensive.

4. **He did not become unconscious or laugh loudly** — losing consciousness or laughing audibly in salah breaks both wudu and salah, and prevents bana.

5. **He did not complete a rukn while in the state of hadas** — if hadas occurred in a rukn (e.g., sajdah), he must not have finished that rukn in the state of impurity; he should simply rise and proceed to make wudu. There is a distinction between: rising to leave for wudu vs. continuing to perform the rukn.

6. **He did not speak** — no speech of any kind (voluntary or involuntary) to anyone.

7. **He did not take an unnecessarily distant route to make wudu** — if a closer wudu area was available and he chose to go farther (by personal preference), bana is invalidated.

8. **He did not perform an additional act of impurity** — e.g., if his wudu broke from passing gas but he then also urinated while making wudu, bana is no longer valid; he must restart salah.

9. **There was no unnecessary delay** — dawdling, waiting for warm water, or any unjustified delay invalidates bana.

10. **The hadas is not due to an expiry of a time-limited concession** — for example, masah (wiping) on leather socks is valid for 24 hours. If the 24-hour period expired during salah, the resulting hadas is external (related to a time-limit, not the body), and bana is not permissible. He must make proper wudu and restart.

11. **He is not a Sahib al-Tartib who suddenly remembers a missed salah** — Sahib al-Tartib (one with fewer than five missed prayers) who recalls a missed salah mid-prayer must first make up that missed salah before continuing, so bana is disrupted.

12. **He did not begin another salah in the interim** — if, after making wudu, he began a separate salah before returning, the original salah is forfeited.

13. **The substitute imam he appointed is qualified** — if the imam placed an unqualified person (e.g., someone who has not memorized any surah) in his spot, the salah of the entire congregation is invalidated.

---

### Procedure When Imam's Wudu Breaks

1. Imam turns to the most eligible person behind him and places them in the imam's spot (istikhlaf — no verbal salam is given, as this would confuse the congregation)
2. The original imam quietly withdraws and makes wudu
3. Upon returning, he **cannot** reclaim the imamate; he rejoins as a muqtadi
4. The substitute imam continues leading to completion

**Best practice in masajid:** Huffaz or knowledgeable individuals stand directly behind the imam so they understand what is happening if the imam must withdraw.

---

### Procedure for Muqtadi Returning After Making Wudu

- If the imam is still in salah: the returning muqtadi catches up to where the imam is and rejoins; he then completes any missed rukus at the end
- If the salah has concluded: the muqtadi continues his own salah from the point his wudu broke (e.g., if wudu broke in qa'dah of raka 3, he begins from qiyam of raka 3 and completes the remaining rakas)
- No new Takbir Tahrimah is needed — the original one remains valid
- No requirement to restart the specific surah from the beginning — resume from where the interruption occurred

---

### Special Ruling: Qa'dah Akhirah and Completion of Salah

**Principle:** Once a worshipper has sat for the duration of the tashahud in the **final qa'dah** (qa'dah akhirah), the fundamental arkan (pillars) of salah are fulfilled. Any subsequent act — even a deliberate violation — does not nullify the salah.

**In this state, if he:**
- Deliberately breaks his wudu (passes gas purposely) → salah is **still considered complete**
- Speaks to someone → salah is **still considered complete**
- Does any act incompatible with salah → salah is **still considered complete**

**Options available to him:**
1. Leave, make wudu, return, and make taslim (salam) to formally conclude the salah
2. His salah is considered complete even without returning — but returning and making taslim is the proper way to close it

**Contrast:** If any of the above happens **before** the tashahud duration is complete in the final qa'dah, the salah is nullified (since the arkan are not yet complete), and he must restart entirely.

**This principle also applies to laughter in this state:** Audible laughter at this point (after tashahud duration is complete in the final qa'dah) does not nullify the salah, though it still breaks wudu.

### Three Types of Laughter During Salah

| Type | Heard by | Effect on Salah | Effect on Wudu |
|---|---|---|---|
| Qahlaqah (قهقهة) — loud | Others nearby | Salah breaks (batil) | Wudu breaks |
| Qahqah — moderate | Only oneself | Salah breaks | Wudu breaks |
| Tabassum — smile/silent | No one | No effect | No effect |

## Lessons & Practical Application

1. **Minimize movement in salah.** A practical test: if an outside observer could mistake your movements for not being in salah, you are doing too much. Adjust clothing or move stones **once** and only when genuinely necessary.

2. **Cracking fingers is a common habit** that many carry into salah. Be mindful to stop this before beginning prayer.

3. **Sadl awareness:** Arab brothers wearing a keffiyeh or similar head-covering should be aware that letting it hang freely on both sides during salah falls under the sadl prohibition. If wearing a proper amamah (wrapped securely), it does not count as sadl.

4. **Braiding/gathering hair for men** — islamically discouraged as it resembles a female practice; should be avoided in general, not only in salah.

5. **If wudu breaks during salah, do not panic.** The Hanafi position provides a clear, structured procedure: leave quietly, make wudu without speaking or delay, and return to complete from the point of interruption. The 13 conditions are the checklist for whether this is valid.

6. **The imam should ensure qualified individuals stand directly behind him** so that if he must withdraw due to hadas, the transition is smooth and the congregation is not confused.

7. **Facing qiblah is critical.** As long as your chest and shoulders do not turn away from the qiblah, lateral movement (e.g., closing a gap in the saf) is permitted. The moment the chest/shoulders face away from the qiblah, salah is invalid.

8. **Do not hold the urge to urinate or defecate during salah.** This is makruh (it eliminates khushu). The correct action is to relieve oneself — even if it means missing jama'at — and then pray. Similarly, a very hungry person may eat before salah even at the cost of jama'at; some fuqaha say eat enough to focus, others say to complete the meal.

9. **The knowledge of fiqh is among the highest forms of Islamic knowledge.** As Imam Shafi'i stated, knowledge is of two types: knowledge of deen (ilm al-fiqh) and knowledge of dunya. The evidences of fiqh are grounded in the four usul — Quran, Sunnah, Ijma, and Qiyas (with qiyas of the Sahabah being the most authoritative form).
