## Session Overview

This session covered two topics: (1) a concluding discussion on Salat al-Tarawih, including the Hanafi ruling on its communal status, the structure of the five tarweehات, and the recommended resting intervals; and (2) a detailed treatment of Salat al-Khawf (the Prayer of Fear), including its Quranic basis, historical instances during the Prophet's ﷺ lifetime, and the step-by-step method of performance for two-raka'at and Maghrib prayers. ## Key Themes

1. **Tarawih as Sunnah Muakkadah / Sunnah Kifayah** — The Hanafi position that Tarawih is Sunnah Kifayah, meaning communal performance discharges the obligation for the whole community.
2. **Structure and etiquette of Tarawih** — Five tarweehat, two salams per tarweeh, and the recommended resting intervals between sets.
3. **The unbreakable obligation of Salah** — Islam's emphasis that Salah cannot be abandoned under any circumstance, including active combat or even drowning.
4. **Salat al-Khawf: method and legal status** — How the prayer is performed when two enemy forces face each other, with alternating groups praying behind the imam.
5. **Extreme-fear scenario** — When fighting is imminent, individuals may pray independently, face any direction, and perform ruku and sujud by ishara (gesture).

## Fiqh Rulings & Evidence

### Salat al-Tarawih

**Ruling:**
- Hanafi position: Tarawih is **Sunnah Kifayah** (communal Sunnah).
  - If some members of the community perform it in the masjid with jama'ah, the obligation is lifted from everyone else.
  - If the entire community neglects it (each assuming others will perform it), the **entire community is sinful**.
- Witr salah is led in congregation **only during Ramadan**; leading Witr with jama'ah outside of Ramadan is not done.

**Evidence:**
- The **Ijma' (consensus) of the Sahabah** on performing Tarawih in congregation — this is the basis for the Hanafi ruling of Sunnah Mu'akkadah/Kifayah.

---

### Salat al-Khawf

**Ruling:**
- There is a **difference of opinion** among scholars on whether Salat al-Khawf remains valid after the Prophet's ﷺ lifetime:
  - One opinion: it was specific to the Prophet ﷺ and is no longer performed.
  - Other opinion: it remains valid and should be performed when conditions apply.
- Engaging in actual fighting **while in the state of Salah invalidates the prayer** (*batalat salatuhum*).
- In an extreme-fear scenario (fighting likely to break out at any moment):
  - Each individual prays **alone** (without jama'ah).
  - They may **face any direction** (not necessarily Qibla), similar to the allowance for Nafl prayer while riding.
  - They perform ruku and sujud **by gesture (isharah)** rather than full physical movements, to avoid vulnerability.

**Evidence:**
- **Surah al-Nisa, Ayah 102** — the primary Quranic evidence; the entire method of Salat al-Khawf is derived from this single ayah.
- **Sahih al-Bukhari** — records at least **four Ghazawat** in which the Prophet ﷺ led Salat al-Khawf.

## Conditions & Categories

### Structure of Tarawih (Hanafi Method)

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| Total raka'at | 20 (five tarweehat × four raka'at each) |
| Units per tarweeh | 4 raka'at performed as 2+2 (two salams) |
| Rest interval | Between every two tarweehat: recommended duration equal to **one full tarweeh** (i.e., the time it takes to pray four raka'at) |
| Rest interval in common practice | Masajid typically rest for only 30 seconds to 1 minute — this falls short of the recommended miqdaar |
| Rest activity | Sitting quietly is permissible; performing **adhkar (dhikr)** is better and preferred |
| Final rest | After the fifth tarweeh, before Witr, a rest interval is also mustahab |
| Witr | Led in congregation in Ramadan only |

---

### Salat al-Khawf: Step-by-Step Method (Two-Raka'at Prayer)

Imam Quduri's described method:

**Setup:** The imam divides the Muslim soldiers into **two groups**.

**Round 1:**
1. Group 1 joins the imam and prays **Raka'at 1** together.
2. When the imam rises from Raka'at 1, Group 1 **leaves and stands guard** at the front/border.
3. Group 2 comes in and joins the imam for **Raka'at 2**.
4. After the imam completes Raka'at 2 and makes **salam** (imam's prayer is complete), Group 2 — instead of immediately completing their missed Raka'at 1 — **leaves and stands guard**.

**Round 2:**
5. Group 1 (which prayed Raka'at 1 with the imam) returns and **completes Raka'at 2** (their missed raka'at), makes salam, and returns to the border.
6. Group 2 returns and **completes Raka'at 1** (their missed raka'at), makes salam, and returns to the border.

**Result:** Each soldier has prayed two raka'at; the imam led the entire prayer without interruption.

---

### Salat al-Khawf: Method for Maghrib (Three-Raka'at Prayer)

**Setup:** Same two groups.

1. **Group 1** prays **two raka'at** with the imam, then goes to the border.
2. **Group 2** comes in and prays **one raka'at** with the imam (imam completes salam after Raka'at 3).
3. Group 2 goes to the border. **Group 1 returns** and completes their **one missed raka'at**, makes salam, returns to border.
4. **Group 2 returns** and completes their **two missed raka'at**, makes salam.

**Result:** Each soldier has prayed three raka'at total.

---

### Notes on Movement During Salat al-Khawf

- Soldiers walking back and forth and **turning their backs to the Qibla** during transition is **permissible** given the exigency.
- They remain **in the state of Salah** even while moving to and from the border.
- **Actual fighting invalidates** the prayer — they must try to avoid initiating combat while in this state.

---

### Army on Campaign: Qasr Ruling

- A Muslim army on campaign prays **Qasr (two raka'at)** for the four-raka'at prayers.
- Even if the army decides to **stay more than two weeks**, they continue praying Qasr because they are there for an important mission and the dispensation (*takhfif*) remains in effect.
- This is why Imam Quduri's method only references two-raka'at prayer — the four-raka'at prayers are already shortened to two.

## Scholarly Opinions

| Scholar | View |
|---|---|
| **Imam Quduri (Rahimullah)** | Described the method of Tarawih and Salat al-Khawf; specified the recommended miqdaar rest interval in Tarawih |
| **Sahib al-Hidayah** | Did not specify what to do during the rest intervals of Tarawih (silent on adhkar vs. silence) |
| **Sahib al-Inayah** | Clarified that during rest intervals one may either sit quietly or rest; best practice is adhkar |
| **Imam Tahtawi (Rahimullah)** | Held that Salat al-Khawf was performed a total of **24 times** by the Prophet ﷺ |
| **Imam Bukhari** | Recorded **four specific Ghazawat** in which Salat al-Khawf occurred with named locations and years |
| **Some Fuqaha** | Opinion that Salat al-Khawf was specific to the Prophet's ﷺ lifetime and is not performed after him |
| **Other Fuqaha** | Salat al-Khawf remains valid and should be performed when conditions apply |

## Lessons & Practical Application

1. **Salah is never abandoned.** The session opened with a powerful reminder: even a person who is drowning is obligated to make the intention for the current prayer — salah cannot be left under any circumstance. This should reinforce the priority every Muslim gives to their daily prayers regardless of circumstances.

2. **Tarawih rest intervals should be meaningful.** The common practice of resting for only 30 seconds between tarweehat is significantly shorter than what Imam Quduri recommends (the duration of one tarweeh). Worshippers and imams should be aware of this sunnah.

3. **Use the rest in Tarawih for dhikr.** Simply sitting and relaxing is acceptable, but performing adhkar during the rest intervals is the superior practice. This is an easy way to increase reward during Ramadan nights.

4. **Witr with jama'ah is Ramadan-specific.** Those who habitually perform Witr in congregation at other times of the year should be aware that this is specific to Ramadan according to the Hanafi position.

5. **Salat al-Khawf teaches flexibility within firm obligation.** The elaborate alternating system designed to ensure every soldier prays, even mid-battle, illustrates Islam's commitment to Salah while also demonstrating how the law accommodates genuine hardship. The fiqh maxim underlying this: necessity relaxes conditions without eliminating the obligation.
