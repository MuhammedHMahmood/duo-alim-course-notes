## Session Overview

This session opened Kitab al-Tahara (Book of Purification) from Imam al-Quduri's *Mukhtasar*, beginning with a review of the seven categories of Shari'a rulings, an explanation of how Islamic texts are organized (Kitab, Bab, Fasl), and a detailed study of the four fara'id (obligations) of wudu. The primary Qur'anic dalil for wudu was established (Surah al-Ma'idah 5:6), followed by discussion of two key masail: (1) whether the elbow and ankle bone are included in the washing, and (2) the minimum required area for masa of the head. Scholarly differences between the Ahnaf's three Imams and Imam Zufar, as well as between the four madhhabs on masa al-ra's, were covered in detail.

## Fiqh Rulings & Evidence

### The Qur'anic Basis for Wudu

**Dalil:** Surah al-Ma'idah 5:6  
*"O you who believe, when you stand for prayer, wash your faces and your hands up to the elbows, and wipe your heads, and wash your feet up to the two ankle bones."*

This single ayah is the foundational dalil for all four fara'id of wudu. The Sharah of Dur al-Mukhtar notes that this ayah points to **eight paired matters**: two types of tahara (wudu/ghusl), two means (water/tayammum), two obligations (ghusl of limbs/masa of head), two causes making tahara wajib (hadas/janabat), two mubah concessions (sickness/travel), two dalils for ghusl and wudu, two indications toward purpose, and two rewards (forgiveness of sins/completion of ni'mah).

---

### The Four Fara'id of Wudu

Per Imam Quduri and the Ahnaf, the obligatory elements of wudu are:

1. **Ghasl al-wajh** — Washing the face
2. **Ghasl al-yadayn ila al-murафiq** — Washing both arms up to (and including) the elbows
3. **Masa al-ra's** — Wiping the head (minimum: one-fourth)
4. **Ghasl al-rijlayn ila al-ka'bayn** — Washing both feet up to (and including) the ankle bones

---

### Masala 1: Are the Elbows and Ankle Bones Included in the Washing?

**Position of Aima Thalasa of Ahnaf** (Abu Hanifa, Abu Yusuf, Muhammad): **Yes, they are included.** The elbow must be washed as part of the arm; the ankle bone must be washed as part of the foot. If the elbow is not washed, wudu is invalid and salah is not accepted.

**Position of Imam Zufar Rahimullah:** They are **not** included. Washing up to the elbow is sufficient without washing the elbow itself.

**Dalil of Aima Thalasa:**
- Hadith in Sahih Muslim (narrated by Nu'aym ibn Abdullah al-Mujmir): Abu Hurairah رضي الله عنه performed wudu, washing his face and arms beyond the elbow, and his legs beyond the ankle bone (into the shank), saying: *"This is how I saw the Messenger of Allah ﷺ perform wudu."* He added that the Prophet ﷺ said believers will appear bright on the Day of Resurrection on the limbs washed in wudu, and whoever wishes may increase this brightness.
- Principle of **genus (jins)**: the elbow is part of the arm (same genus); the ankle bone is part of the foot (same genus). When a limit falls within the same genus as the thing being washed, it is included. This contrasts with the fast, where day and night are different genera.

**Dalil of Imam Zufar:**
- Qiyas on the Qur'anic ayah about fasting: *"Complete the fast until the night"* (Surah al-Baqarah). No scholar says the night is included in the fast — i.e., one stops at nightfall, not after it. By the same token, *"up to the elbows"* means one stops at the elbow.

**Answer to Imam Zufar's dalil:** The principle of **ghaya and mughayyah** — day and night are of different genera, so night is excluded. The arm and elbow are of the same genus, so the elbow is included. The fasting analogy does not hold across different-genus boundaries.

**Practical ruling:** If a person realizes their elbow was not washed, they need only re-wash the elbow and continue — they do not redo the entire wudu, provided no excessive time has elapsed.

---

### Masala 2: Minimum Required Area for Masa al-Ra's

**Dalil:** Hadith of Mughirah ibn Shu'bah رضي الله عنه (in Bukhari and Muslim): On a journey, Mughirah poured water for the Prophet ﷺ after he relieved himself. The Prophet ﷺ washed his face, arms, wiped his head (*masaha bi-ra'sihi*), and did masa over his two khuf. A version of this hadith uses the word *nasiya*, indicating he wiped over the forelock/frontal portion.

**Three opinions within the Ahnaf on what "nasiya" means:**

| Opinion | Area Required | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| 1st (relied-upon / muftabih) | One-fourth of the head | The usul: performing one-fourth of a thing equals performing the whole; Hadith mentions "nasiya" = forelock area = approximately one-fourth |
| 2nd | The forehead only (hairline area) | Narrower reading of "nasiya" — only the frontal strip at the hairline |
| 3rd | Three fingers' width | Narrowest reading; three fingers placed at the hairline |

**Ahnaf relied-upon position:** Wiping the **top/frontal quarter of the head** fulfills the fard. Wiping the entire head is sunnah.

**Usul underpinning the one-fourth principle:** *"One-fourth of a thing stands for the whole."* This same principle applies in: (1) woman's sattar in salah — if less than one-fourth is exposed briefly without completing a full rukun, salah is not invalidated; (2) Hajj — if less than one-fourth of the head is shaved without ihram permission, dam (major penalty) is not incurred (though a lesser penalty remains).

**Other madhhabs on masa al-ra's:**

| Imam | Minimum Required |
|---|---|
| Imam Shafi'i | Three hairs (from any part of the head) |
| Imam Malik | Entire head must be wiped (based on hadith where the Prophet ﷺ wiped his full head) |
| Ahnaf | One-fourth of the head |

---

### Masala 3: Wudu After Relieving Oneself

**Ruling:** Going for urination or relieving oneself breaks wudu (it constitutes a hadas). This is established by the same hadith of Mughirah — the Prophet ﷺ went to relieve himself and then made wudu upon returning. It is **mustahab** to perform wudu after relieving oneself even outside of prayer time.

---

### Masala 4: Performing Wudu When Already in a State of Wudu

**Ruling:** If a person already has wudu and wishes to perform wudu again for extra reward (mustahab wudu), they should **only perform the fara'id** (each limb once), not the sunnan (e.g., washing three times). Reasons:
1. Preservation of water — Islam prohibits wasteful use of water even in a river.
2. Since the fard is not due upon them (wudu is already present), only doing the minimum suffices for the nafl intention.

## Conditions & Categories

### Seven Categories of Shari'a Rulings (Review)

| Category | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Fard | Obligatory; denier becomes kafir | Salah, Sawm, Hajj |
| Wajib | Obligatory at a lower level; denier not kafir | Answering salam, responding to a sneeze |
| Mustahab | Recommended | Washing hands before eating |
| Mubah | Permissible | Traveling by car, plane, or train |
| Haram | Prohibited | Stealing |
| Makruh Tahrimi | Prohibitively disliked (treated as haram in aqidah; not kufr to deny) | Hoarding scarce goods to profit from shortage |
| Makruh Tanzihi | Mildly disliked | Drinking water while standing |

**Important distinction:** Calling something haram requires very solid proof from Qur'an, Sunnah, Ijma, or Qiyas. One should not label things haram arbitrarily.

**Wajib vs. Fard (aqidah perspective):** In aqidah, treat wajib like fard and makruh tahrimi like haram — but denial of wajib does not constitute kufr, unlike denial of fard.

### Structure of Islamic Legal Texts

| Level | Term | Equivalent | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| Highest | Kitab (كِتَاب) | Volume/Book | Covers an entire major subject area (e.g., Kitab al-Tahara covers all purity-related masail) |
| Middle | Bab (بَاب) | Chapter | Groups related masail under a specific topic (e.g., Bab al-Tayammum) |
| Lowest | Fasl (فَصْل) | Sub-section | Breaks a bab into a very specific subject area |

*Note: In al-Quduri, the Fasl level appears in some chapters but not all.*

---

### Linguistic Meanings of Tahara

| Vocalization | Term | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Fatha | Tahara (طَهَارَة) | Purification / the act of purifying (e.g., zakat = taharat al-mal) |
| Dhamma | Tuhur (طُهُور) | Water used for purification (leftover wudu water) |
| Kasra | Tihara (طِهَارَة) | Instrument of purification (the vessel/water itself) |

**Shari'a definition:** Tahara is purification — the removal of ritual impurity (hadas or janabat) through wudu, ghusl, or tayammum.

---

### Two Types of Tahara

| Type | Removes | Method |
|---|---|---|
| Wudu | Minor impurity (hadas) | Water applied to specified limbs |
| Ghusl | Major impurity (janabat) | Full body washing with water |
| Tayammum | Minor or major (when water unavailable) | Wiping face and hands with clean earth |

## Scholarly Opinions

### On the Inclusion of Elbows/Ankles in Wudu

| Scholar/Group | Position |
|---|---|
| Abu Hanifa, Abu Yusuf, Muhammad (Aima Thalasa of Ahnaf) | Elbow and ankle bone **are included** — must be washed |
| Imam Zufar Rahimullah | Elbow and ankle bone **not included** — washing up to them suffices |
| Imam Shafi'i, Imam Malik, Imam Ahmad (general) | Elbow and ankle included (broadly aligned with Aima Thalasa on this) |

---

### On the Minimum Masa al-Ra's

| Scholar | Minimum |
|---|---|
| Ahnaf (relied-upon) | One-fourth of the head |
| Imam Shafi'i | Three hairs |
| Imam Malik | Entire head |

### On Imam Zufar Rahimullah

Imam Zufar رحمه الله was among the forty senior fuqaha selected by Imam Abu Hanifa for his scholarly council (shura). His opinions, while sometimes differing from the Aima Thalasa of Ahnaf, were well-grounded and appear throughout Hanafi fiqh books. His ikhtilaf is respected and studied as part of the tradition.

---

### On Mughirah ibn Shu'bah رضي الله عنه

- Accepted Islam at the time of Ghazwah al-Ahzab (5th Hijri — well into the Madinah period)
- Despite accepting Islam relatively late, narrated approximately **136 ahadith**
- Passed away in Kufa — the city central to the development of Hanafi fiqh
- Passed away in 50th or 51st Hijri

## Lessons & Practical Application

1. **Maintain wudu as a constant state.** The Prophet ﷺ heard Bilal رضي الله عنه walking ahead of him in Jannah; when asked the reason, Bilal said he renews his wudu whenever it breaks and prays two raka'at. Wudu brings barakah in rizq, makes good deeds easier, and distances one from sin.

2. **Sleep in a state of wudu.** A hadith states that a person who dies in the state of wudu dies as a shahid. Even if wudu breaks after making the intention to sleep with it, the reward still applies — make the niyyah.

3. **Wudu is not re-obligatory if already in wudu.** If your wudu from Asr remains intact at Maghrib, you may pray without renewing. If you choose to renew it for extra reward, perform only the fara'id (each limb once) to avoid water waste.

4. **Do not call things haram without solid proof.** One of the most repeated cautions in this session: labeling something haram is a serious matter requiring Qur'an, Sunnah, Ijma, or valid Qiyas. Do not do it arbitrarily.

5. **Start everything with Bismillah.** Imam Quduri opens with Bismillah and a Qur'anic ayah — this is a means of barakah and completion. The hadith: anything begun with Bismillah has barakah in it.

6. **The one-fourth principle has wide application.** Beyond wudu, it applies to: a woman's sattar in salah (less than one-fourth exposed for less than a full rukun = salah intact), and shaving in Hajj (less than one-fourth = no dam, though a lesser penalty applies).

7. **Invest in physical books.** The teacher strongly encouraged students to purchase physical copies of al-Quduri and reference texts — for use while traveling, for retention, and for lifelong reference.
