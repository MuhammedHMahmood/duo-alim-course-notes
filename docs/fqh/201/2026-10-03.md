## Session Overview

This session continued the study of Salah from Kitab al-Salah, picking up from the end of the first Rakah. Topics covered included: the differences between the first and second Rakah; the two sitting positions (Iftirash and Tawarruq) with their evidence and scholarly disagreements; the ruling and significance of the Tashahud; recitation in the last two Rakahs; restrictions on Du'a in Salah; the ruling on ending Salah with Salam (Tasleem); how Sajda Sahu covers missed Wajibat; and a detailed enumeration of the 25 differences between men's and women's Salah. The session concluded with an introduction to Jahri (loud) and Sirri (quiet) Salah and the Quranic basis for the distinction.

---

## Key Themes

1. **Second Rakah distinctions** — What is omitted in the second Rakah versus the first (Sana, Ta'awwuz, Rafa' al-Yadayn) and the evidential basis for each.
2. **Sitting positions: Iftirash vs. Tawarruq** — Their descriptions, who does which, when, and the evidence behind the Hanafi preference for Iftirash in all sittings.
3. **Tashahud — its ruling, wording, and significance** — Why the Tashahud dialogue is held in such reverence; Wajib vs. Fard distinction; differences between Hanafi and Shafi'i positions.
4. **Recitation rules: first vs. last Rakahs** — Only Surah Fatihah in the last two Rakahs; the Wajib vs. Fard dispute.
5. **Du'a within Salah — permitted and prohibited forms** — Restricted to Quranic and Masnoon wordings; consequences of using one's own language.
6. **Tasleem — completing the Salah** — Wajib but not Fard; implications for missed Salam.
7. **Sajda Sahu — scope of coverage** — Covers any number of missed Wajibat with a single prostration; distinction from missed Fard.
8. **25 differences between men's and women's Salah** — Covering Takbir, Ruku, Sajda, Qa'da, Jama'ah, Jumu'ah, Eid, Adhan, and I'tikaf.
9. **Jahri and Sirri Salah** — The Quranic ayah from Surah Al-Isra that established the rule; night vs. day Salah distinction.

## Fiqh Rulings & Evidence

### 1. Differences in the Second Rakah
The second Rakah is performed identically to the first with three exceptions:
- **No Sana** (opening supplication)
- **No Ta'awwuz** (A'udhu billah) — recited only before the very first Qira'at of Salah
- **No Rafa' al-Yadayn** (raising of hands)

**Dalil against Rafa' al-Yadayn after the first Takbir:**
This was the established practice of many major Sahabah: Abu Bakr, 'Umar, 'Ali, 'Abdullah ibn 'Umar, 'Abdullah ibn Mas'ud, Abu Hurairah, Ka'b, Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, and Jabir ibn Samra (radiyallahu anhum).

Additionally, the Hadith of Ibn Abbas ؓ states that hands are raised on only **seven occasions**:
1. Takbir al-Tahreema
2. Qunud in Witr
3. Each additional Takbir of Eid prayers (Takbirat al-Eidain)
4. Istilam of Hajar al-Aswad (beginning/during Tawaf)
5. During Sa'i of Safa and Marwa
6. In 'Arafat
7. Rami al-Jamarat

*Opposing view:* Imam Shafi'i, Imam Ahmad, and Ishaq ibn Rahuyah hold that hands should be raised when going into Ruku and when rising from it.

---

### 2. No Istiraha (Rest-Sitting) Between Rakahs — Hanafi Position
After the second Sajda of the first Rakah, one rises directly to the standing position without pausing in a seated posture.

*Opposing view:* Imam Shafi'i (rahimahullah) holds that a brief sitting (Istiraha) between odd Rakahs is established Sunnah.

**Dalil for Imam Shafi'i:** Hadith narrated by Malik ibn Huwayrith ؓ in Bukhari: "I saw the Prophet ﷺ praying, and in the odd Rakah he used to sit for a moment before getting up."

**Hanafi response:** The Ahnaf accept this narration but interpret it as a concession made due to the Prophet's ﷺ old age. The consistent practice throughout most of his life was to rise directly. In matters of 'Ibadat that were performed throughout life (Mutawatir), the majority practice is taken as the ruling. The same principle is applied to Tawarruq (see below).

---

### 3. Sitting Position — Iftirash (Hanafi Position)
**Ruling:** Iftirash is the correct sitting position for **both** Qada al-Ula and Qada al-Akhira.

**Description of Iftirash:**
- Left foot is folded flat beneath the body; one sits on it
- Right foot remains upright, toes bent toward the Qibla
- Hands are placed flat on the thighs with fingers spread
- All fingers (including toes) are ideally directed toward the Qibla

**Dalil:** Hadith in Bukhari, Muslim, and Tirmizi (how the Prophet ﷺ used to sit)

*Opposing view:* Imam Malik and Imam Shafi'i hold that **Tawarruq** is correct, at minimum in the Qada al-Akhira.

**Dalil for Tawarruq:** Narration in Abu Dawud.

**Description of Tawarruq:**
- Left foot is extended out to the right side rather than tucked under the body
- The person sits on the ground, not on their foot
- Slightly easier position, particularly for the elderly

**Hanafi response:** As with Istiraha, the Prophet ﷺ adopted Tawarruq toward the end of his life. The Ahnaf take the majority practice throughout his life as the ruling, preferring Iftirash for all sittings.

**Note:** If a person is unable to sit in Iftirash due to pain, age, or injury, Tawarruq is permissible. If even Tawarruq is difficult, any comfortable sitting position is permitted — the Shari'ah grants ease in such cases.

### 4. Tashahud — Ruling and Wording
**Ruling:**
- The **recitation** of Tashahud is **Wajib**
- The **sitting** (Qa'da) itself is **Fard**

**The Tashahud of Ahnaf** is taken from the narration of **'Abdullah ibn Mas'ud ؓ**:
> *At-tahiyyatu lillahi wa salawatu wa tayyibat. As-salamu alayka ayyuhan-nabiyyu wa rahmatullahi wa barakatuhu. As-salamu alayna wa ala ibadillahi al-salihin. Ashhadu an la ilaha illallah wa ashhadu anna Muhammadan abduhu wa rasuluh.*

*Imam Shafi'i's Tashahud* is taken from the narration of Ibn Abbas ؓ and has slightly different wording.

**Spiritual significance:** The Tashahud re-enacts the dialogue of Mi'raj — Allah ﷻ greeting the Prophet ﷺ with the first portion, and the Prophet ﷺ responding with the second portion, notably including the entire Ummah ("As-salamu alayna") rather than keeping the blessing to himself.

### 5. What Is Done in Qada al-Ula vs. Qada al-Akhira

| | Qada al-Ula | Qada al-Akhira |
|---|---|---|
| Tashahud | ✓ Wajib (must be recited) | ✓ Wajib |
| Darud (Salawat) | ✗ Must stand up immediately (Hanafi) | ✓ Sunnah |
| Du'a | ✗ Not in Qada al-Ula | ✓ After Darud |
| Sitting position | Iftirash (Hanafi) | Iftirash (Hanafi) |

**Hanafi ruling:** In Qada al-Ula (in a three- or four-Rakah Salah), once Tashahud is completed the person **must rise immediately** for the next Rakah. Adding Darud here is not permitted and would constitute an error requiring Sajda Sahu.

*Imam Shafi'i:* May recite Darud even in Qada al-Ula.

**Minimum sitting time:** One must remain seated for at least the duration required to complete the Tashahud. Rising before this — even if only three seconds — means the Qa'da has not been fulfilled.

---

### 6. Recitation in the Last Two Rakahs
**Ruling (Hanafi):** In the third and fourth Rakahs, the Imam (or individual praying alone) recites **Surah Fatihah only** — no additional Surah is added.

Surah Fatihah in the last two Rakahs is **Wajib** (according to Ahnaf). If forgotten, Sajda Sahu compensates.

*Imam Shafi'i's position:* Surah Fatihah is **Fard** in every Rakah including the last two; missing it cannot be compensated by Sajda Sahu alone.

**Dalil (Hanafi):** Hadith in Bukhari narrated by 'Abdullah ibn Abi Qatada ؓ, who reported from his father: "The Prophet ﷺ used to recite Al-Fatihah followed by another Surah in the first two Rakahs of Zuhr and Asr, and used to recite only Al-Fatihah in the last two."

**On how the Sirri Salah Qira'at was known:** The Prophet ﷺ, as both Imam and teacher (Mu'allim), would sometimes recite in a very low but audible voice so that those standing immediately beside him could hear. This is how the Sahabah learned what was recited in Sirri Salah.

### 7. Du'a Within Salah — Restrictions
After completing the Darud in Qada al-Akhira, the Musalli may make Du'a with the following restrictions:

**Permitted:**
- Du'a from the Quran
- Masnoon/Ma'thur Du'as (transmitted from the Prophet ﷺ in authentic narrations)
- General requests (e.g., asking for halal provision, expanding sustenance) using established wordings

**Prohibited:**
- Du'a in one's own language (not from Quran or Sunnah) → **Salah becomes Fasid; must repeat**
- Naming specific worldly requests in one's own words (e.g., "give me this specific job today")
- Using expressions that resemble ordinary human speech (Kalam al-Nas)

**Note:** After completing Salam, one may make any Du'a desired in any language.

**Dalil:** Hadith in Bukhari: "Then select the invocation you like best and recite it." One example given is the Du'a narrated by 'A'ishah ؓ that the Prophet ﷺ frequently recited:
> *"Allahumma inni a'udhu bika min 'adhab al-qabri, wa min fitnat al-Masih al-Dajjal, wa min fitnat al-mahya wal-mamat. Allahumma inni a'udhu bika min al-ma'thami wal-maghram."*
("O Allah, I seek refuge with You from the punishment of the grave, the tribulation of the Dajjal, and the trials of life and death. O Allah, I seek refuge with You from sin and from debt.")

The Prophet ﷺ explained his frequent refuge from debt: a person in debt is prone to lying when he speaks and breaking promises when he makes them.

### 8. Tasleem (Salam) — Ruling on Completing Salah
**Ruling (Hanafi):** Tasleem is **Wajib**, not Fard.

**Method:** Turn to the right saying *"Assalamu alaikum wa rahmatullah"*, then to the left with the same. The Prophet ﷺ turned so completely that those seated in rows behind him could see the whiteness of his cheek — indicating a full head-turn is required.

**Implication of Wajib (not Fard):**
- If forgotten, Salah is still technically complete but is "deficient" (Salatul Nakas)
- One should repeat the Salah if possible, since a Wajib was missed
- If it were Fard, missing it would void the Salah entirely and repetition would be obligatory

**Dalil (Tasleem is Wajib):** Hadith in Abu Dawud narrated by Ibn Mas'ud ؓ: "The Prophet ﷺ used to give Salam to his right and left side until the whiteness of his cheek was seen."

Hadith in Tirmizi narrated by Abu Sa'id ؓ: "The key to Salah is purification (*Tahara*), its *Tahreem* is the Takbir, and its *Tahleel* is the Tasleem."

**Dalil (Tasleem is not Fard):**
1. Hadith in Abu Dawud (Ibn Mas'ud narration): After teaching the Tashahud, the Prophet ﷺ said: "When you have said this, you have completed your Salah. If you want to stand up then stand up, and if you want to remain sitting then remain sitting." — This indicates completion of Salah is tied to Tashahud, not Tasleem.
2. Hadith in Tirmizi ('Abdullah ibn 'Amr ؓ): "When a man breaks his wudu while sitting in the end of Salah before saying Tasleem, his Salah is acceptable." — Since wudu breaking ends one's Salah, this shows Salah was already complete at the Tashahud stage.

---

### 9. Sajda Sahu — Scope of Coverage
**Ruling:**
- **Any number of missed Wajibat** (whether omitted or incorrectly added) can be compensated through **a single Sajda Sahu** — multiple Sajdat are not required.
- **Missed Fard:** Must be performed the moment it is remembered (even mid-Salah), then Sajda Sahu is performed at the end.
- **Intentional omission of Wajib:** Cannot be compensated by Sajda Sahu; Salah must be repeated.

**What triggers Sajda Sahu:**
- Omitting a Wajib act unintentionally
- Adding an extra Wajib or Fard act by mistake (e.g., performing three Sajdas)

**Source:** Fatawa al-Tatarkhaniyya (a primary authoritative Hanafi Fatwa reference)

### 10. Jahri vs. Sirri Salah

| Salah | Recitation |
|---|---|
| Fajr | Loud (Jahri) — both Rakahs |
| Maghrib | Loud (Jahri) — first two Rakahs; quiet (Sirri) — third Rakah |
| Isha | Loud (Jahri) — first two Rakahs; quiet (Sirri) — last two |
| Zuhr | Quiet (Sirri) — all Rakahs |
| Asr | Quiet (Sirri) — all Rakahs |

**Dalil:** Quran, Surah Al-Isra:
> *"Wa la tajhar bi salatika wa la tukhafid biha wabtaghi bayna dhalika sabila"* — "Do not recite your prayers too loudly or silently, but seek a way in between."

**Historical context:** In the early Makkan period, the Prophet ﷺ recited all Salah loudly. The Mushrikeen of Makkah would harass him, insult Allah and the Prophet ﷺ, and try to disrupt the recitation. This ayah was then revealed, instructing that night Salah be recited loudly and day Salah quietly — the three night-time Salah (Fajr, Maghrib, Isha) are Jahri; the two daytime Salah (Zuhr, Asr) are Sirri.

**Note on Sirri recitation:** Sirri does not mean reciting in the heart — the lips and tongue must move, and the person must be able to hear themselves. Completely sealing the mouth is incorrect, and some scholars hold that such a Salah must be repeated.

## Conditions & Categories

### Conditions for Valid Sitting (Qa'da)
1. Must sit for at minimum the duration of completing the Tashahud
2. Must sit in Iftirash (Hanafi) — left foot folded under body, right foot upright
3. Hands placed flat on thighs, fingers spread (men); fingers closed (women)
4. Toes directed toward Qibla where possible

### Categories of Sitting in Salah
| Sitting | When | Ruling |
|---|---|---|
| Qada al-Ula | After 2nd Rakah in 3- or 4-Rakah Salah | Fard |
| Qada al-Akhira | Final sitting of any Salah | Fard |

### 25 Differences Between Men's and Women's Salah (Hanafi)

**Takbir al-Tahreema:**
1. Women raise hands only to shoulder level (not to earlobes as men do)
   - *Dalil:* Mojam Kabir al-Tabrani; four narrations in Musannaf ibn Abi Shaybah
2. Women do not take hands out of sleeves or burqa — they perform Takbir inside their outer garment

**Qiyam (Standing):**
3. Women place right palm on left wrist without wrapping (no holding); men wrap the wrist with thumb and pinky
   - *Dalil:* Maraqi al-Falah
4. Women place hands on **chest** (not below navel as men do)
   - This is **Muttafaqun 'alayhi** — agreed upon by all four Imams

**Ruku:**
5. Women bow only slightly — enough for hands to touch the knees; not required to straighten the back like men
   - *Dalil:* Abu Dawud
6. Women do not support themselves on the knees
7. Women do not grasp the kneecap/patella
   - *(6 & 7 Dalil: al-Durr al-Mukhtar)*
8. Women keep fingers **closed together** on the knee (men spread fingers)
9. Women's knees remain **slightly bent** in Ruku (men's legs are straight)
10. Women **tuck elbows to their sides** in Ruku (men's armpits are open/raised)

**Sajda:**
11. Women **contract the body completely**: thighs touching stomach, arms against sides, legs extended to the right
    - *Dalil:* Ila al-Sunnah; Sunan al-Kubra al-Bayhaqi; Musannaf ibn Abi Shaybah
12. Women's arms and elbows should be **on the ground** (men keep elbows raised)
    - *Dalil:* Marasil Abu Dawud (note: a different book from Sunan Abu Dawud)

**Qa'da (Sitting):**
13. Women sit on **left buttock** with feet extended to the right side, right thigh resting on left thigh, feet horizontal — not upright as in men's Iftirash
    - *Dalil:* Musannaf 'Abd al-Razzaq; Fath al-Qadir
14. Women raise the index finger **slightly** (lighter than men) when saying "Ashhadu an la ilaha illallah"

**Jama'ah (Congregational Prayer):**
15. To alert the Imam of an error: women **clap** (right palm striking the back of the left hand); men say Subhanallah — because a woman's voice is 'Awrah/under Purdah
16. Women **cannot lead men** in prayer
17. A congregation of women alone is **Makrooh** (disliked, not forbidden)
18. If women do hold a congregation, their Imam stands **in the same row** as the rest of the women (not ahead as a male Imam would)
19. **Gathering solely for the purpose** of women's Jama'ah is also Makrooh
20. Women **cannot stand in the same row as men** in congregational prayer; violating this invalidates the entire row's Salah (subject to specific conditions — to be detailed in Bab al-Salat al-Jama'ah)

**Special Obligations:**
21. **Jumu'ah (Friday prayer) is not obligatory** for women
22. **Eid prayers are not Wajib** for women
23. Eid Takbirat should be recited **silently** by women (men recite loudly)
24. **Takbirat al-Tashriq** should be recited silently by women

**Time of Prayer:**
25. It is **Mustahab** for women to pray Fajr while it is still dark (men pray when light begins; women have additional encouragement to pray in darkness)

**Additions by Imam Tahtawi (rahimahullah):**
- Women should **not call the Adhan** (the voice's Purdah would be violated)
- Women's **I'tikaf is best performed in their own home**, not in the Masjid

*Note on women's Sajda posture:* Some Salafi/Ahle Hadith schools teach that women should pray exactly like men. The above rulings have specific evidential support and students should be aware of these dalail to address such claims.

## Scholarly Opinions

| Issue | Hanafi Position | Imam Shafi'i / Others |
|---|---|---|
| Istiraha between odd Rakahs | Not established — was due to Prophet's ﷺ old age | Sunnah, based on Hadith of Malik ibn Huwayrith (Bukhari) |
| Rafa' al-Yadayn after first Takbir | No raising in subsequent Rakahs — supported by many major Sahabah and Hadith of Ibn Abbas | Imam Shafi'i, Imam Ahmad, Ishaq ibn Rahuyah: raise hands before Ruku and rising from it |
| Sitting position | Iftirash in **both** Qada al-Ula and Qada al-Akhira | Imam Malik and Imam Shafi'i: Tawarruq (especially in Qada al-Akhira); Masnoon per them; Dalil: Abu Dawud |
| Darud in Qada al-Ula | Must stand up immediately after Tashahud — no Darud | Imam Shafi'i: may recite Darud in Qada al-Ula |
| Surah Fatihah in last two Rakahs | Wajib — Sajda Sahu compensates if missed | Imam Shafi'i: Fard — Salah incomplete without it |
| Darud in Qada al-Akhira | **Sunnah** — no Sajda Sahu required if missed | Imam Shafi'i: **Fard** in Qada al-Akhira |
| Tasleem (Salam) | **Wajib** (not Fard) — Salah is valid but deficient without it | Generally: Tasleem is obligatory; some hold it as Fard |
| Sending Salutations once in a lifetime | Fard (established from Quran, Surah Al-Ahzab) | Same |

## Lessons & Practical Application

1. **Complete your head-turns in Salam.** The Prophet ﷺ turned so completely that those behind him could see his cheek. A slight tilt of the head is insufficient.

2. **Do not pause to sit between Rakahs.** Rising directly from the second Sajda to the standing position is the Hanafi ruling. The rest-sitting some people observe is not established as a general practice.

3. **Work on the Iftirash sitting position.** It can feel difficult at first — especially bending the right foot's toes toward the Qibla — but with consistent effort it becomes natural. Attempting the correct position, even imperfectly, is better than defaulting to Tawarruq without need.

4. **Do not add Darud in Qada al-Ula** (in three- or four-Rakah Salah). Pause only as long as needed to complete the Tashahud, then rise.

5. **Keep Du'a within Salah to Quranic and Masnoon wordings.** If you wish to ask for something specific (a job, exam success, health), find a Hadith or Quranic Du'a with a similar meaning. After Salam, you may make any Du'a in any language freely.

6. **Do not make Sajda in your heart.** Sirri recitation still requires moving the lips and tongue — the person must be able to hear themselves. Completely sealing the mouth is incorrect.

7. **Memorize the seven occasions for Rafa' al-Yadayn** (from the Hadith of Ibn Abbas ؓ). The first three (Takbir al-Tahreema, Qunud in Witr, Takbirat al-Eidain) are directly relevant to daily practice.

8. **Women should be aware of the 25 differences** and be prepared to explain their evidential basis, as questions sometimes arise from other prayer traditions that hold women should pray identically to men.

9. **Sajda Sahu is comprehensive.** If multiple Wajibat are missed unintentionally in a single Salah, one Sajda Sahu at the end suffices — you do not perform multiple. However, intentional omission removes this safety net entirely.

10. **The Tashahud is sacred.** Reflecting on its context — the Prophet ﷺ before Allah ﷻ during Mi'raj choosing to include the entire Ummah in his response — should deepen one's focus and presence during this portion of every Salah.
