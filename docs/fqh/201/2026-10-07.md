## Session Overview

This session covered two main areas. The first was a brief recap of the rules distinguishing loud (jahri) from quiet (sirri) recitation in salah, including the historical context and required volume levels. The second and more extensive portion covered Salatul Witr in detail — its ruling (wajib according to Imam Abu Hanifa), its structure (three continuous rakahs), and the dalail for its obligations. The session also addressed Qunut: its timing, its distinction from Qunut al-Nazila, and the madhab differences. The final portion covered rules on which surahs to recite in salah, minimum recitation requirements, and the rules governing a muqtadi praying behind an imam, including the prohibition on recitation behind the imam and the two required intentions.

## Fiqh Rulings & Evidence

### 1. Jahri vs. Sirri Recitation

**Rule:** Zuhr and Asr are sirri (quiet); Fajr, Maghrib, Isha (first two rakahs), Jumu'ah, and Idain are jahri (loud).

**Historical context:** In Makkah, the mushrikeen would disturb the Prophet ﷺ and insult Allah during loud prayer, so recitation was quieted during daytime. The ayah of Surah al-Isra ("Do not recite too loudly or silently, but seek a way in between") established the balanced approach. Jumu'ah and Idain became obligatory in Madinah where the hostile environment no longer applied, hence they remain jahri even though performed in daytime.

**Dalail:**
- Ruling for Fajr recitation: Sahih al-Bukhari
- Ruling for Maghrib recitation: Sahih al-Bukhari
- Ruling for Isha recitation: Sahih al-Bukhari
- Dalil for sirri recitation in the last two rakahs: Sahih al-Bukhari

**Volume thresholds (per Abu al-Hasan al-Karkhi رحمه الله):**
- Sirri: the person must at minimum be able to hear himself; lips must move
- Jahri: at least two people standing next to or directly behind the reciter must be able to hear

**Ruling for a person praying alone:**
- Sirri salah: must remain quiet — no option to raise voice even when alone at home
- Jahri salah: has the option to recite loudly or quietly; it is better (mustahab) to recite as the imam would have

**Penalty:** If someone recites loudly in a sirri salah or quietly in a jahri salah, doing so makes the action wajib to correct (i.e., it is a wajib-level violation).

### 2. Status of Witr Salah

**Hanafi ruling:** Witr is **wajib**. It is above sunnah in rank but does not reach fard because there is no Dalil Qat'i (no Quranic ayah).

**Reasoning for not being fard:** The Miraj established five obligatory salah. To classify Witr as fard would effectively create a sixth fard, which is textually unsupported. Since there are many ahadith with strong taqeed but no Quranic text, it settles at wajib.

**Practical consequence of wajib status:**
- If Witr is missed, qadha is required (unlike sunnah, which has no qadha)
- Must be treated with the same seriousness as fard in acts of worship

**Dalil for wujub of Witr** (Abu Dawud): Rasulullah ﷺ said: "Allah the Exalted has given you an extra prayer which is better for you than red camels — that is the Witr salah, which Allah has appointed for you between the night prayer and the daybreak."

**Sahiban's position:** Imam Abu Yusuf and Imam Muhammad (Sahibain) consider Witr to be sunnah, which is also the position of Imam Shafi'i رحمه الله. The **muftabih fatwa** is with Imam Abu Hanifa رحمه الله that it is wajib.

### 3. Structure of Witr — Three Continuous Rakahs

**Hanafi ruling:** Witr is three rakahs performed continuously without an intervening salam, similar in structure to Maghrib salah.

**Differences from Maghrib:**
- In the third rakah of Witr, the imam recites aloud (unlike the third rakah of Maghrib where the imam is quiet)
- A surah must be added after Surah al-Fatihah in every rakah of Witr (in fard salah only Surah al-Fatihah is recited after the second rakah)
- Before going into ruku in the third rakah, a takbir is made and Dua al-Qunut is recited

**Dalil for three rakahs** (Tirmizi): Narrated from Ali رضي الله عنه that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ would perform Witr with three rakahs, reciting nine surahs from the Mufassal — three surahs per rakah.

**Recommended surahs for Witr** (Hadith al-Nasa'i, narrated by Ubay ibn Ka'b رضي الله عنه):
- 1st rakah: Surah al-A'la (سَبِّحِ اسْمَ رَبِّكَ الْأَعْلَى)
- 2nd rakah: Surah al-Kafirun
- 3rd rakah: Surah al-Ikhlas

**Imam Shafi'i and Imam Malik's position:** Two rakahs with a salam, then one separate rakah (2+1 structure). Their dalil is a hadith in Sahih al-Bukhari.

**Ruling for following a Maliki/Shafi'i imam who does 2+1:**
- Make the intention of Witr and pray behind them — this is valid
- One exception: if the imam performs only a single standalone rakah (1 alone, with no preceding two), do not follow — there is no dalil for a single-rakah Witr, and there is a severe prohibition against three-rakah nafil

### 4. Qunut in Witr

**Hanafi ruling:** Qunut is recited in the third rakah of Witr **before ruku**, and this is done **throughout the entire year**.

**Procedure:** After completing recitation of the surah in the third rakah, raise hands and say Allahu Akbar (takbir), then recite Dua al-Qunut, then proceed to ruku.

**Dalil** (Ibn Majah, narrated from Ubay ibn Ka'b رضي الله عنه): Rasulullah ﷺ used to pray Witr and would recite Qunut before the ruku.

**Imam Shafi'i's position:** Qunut is only in the last half of Ramadan (last 15 nights). Outside Ramadan, Shafi'i masajid perform Qunut al-Nazila in Fajr after rising from ruku.

### 5. Qunut al-Nazila (Supplication During Calamity)

**Definition:** A special supplication recited during times of hardship or affliction upon the Ummah, distinct from the regular Witr Qunut.

**Hanafi ruling:** Permissible and practiced — but only during actual times of hardship. Not a permanent fixture in any salah.

**Historical dalil** (Abu Dawud, narrated by Abdullah ibn Abbas رضي الله عنه): Rasulullah ﷺ recited the supplication daily for one month in Zuhr, Asr, Maghrib, Isha, and Fajr — in the last rakah after rising from ruku — cursing the tribes of Banu Sulaym, Ri'l, Dhakwan, and 'Usayyah. He then stopped after one month.

**Why Ahnaf don't do Qunut in Fajr permanently:**
1. The Prophet ﷺ did it for only one month (for a specific necessity against those three tribes), then stopped — his action being limited in time is itself a dalil
2. Hadith in Ibn Majah (narrated from Umm Salamah رضي الله عنها): the Prophet ﷺ prohibited Qunut in Fajr salah
3. Tabarani: Ghalib رحمه الله said he stayed two months with Anas رضي الله عنه and never saw him perform Qunut in Fajr

**Dalil for year-round Qunut in Witr** (Musannaf Ibn Abi Shaybah): "Qunut is a must in Witr."

**Dalil supporting continuity of Witr** (Bukhari, narrated by Aisha رضي الله عنها): Rasulullah ﷺ would offer Witr at various hours throughout the night, always ending the tahajjud with Witr — sometimes extending all the way to the last hour of the night.

**Hanafi position on following a Shafi'i imam who does Qunut in Fajr:**
- Do not raise hands during their Qunut as a form of silent objection
- Still say Ameen, because it is still dua and dua during salah is highly meritorious
- This is the only case in Hanafi fiqh where an action of another madhab's imam is explicitly objected to in this manner

### 6. Recitation in Salah — Prohibited Fixation

**Rule:** It is makruh to designate a specific surah for a specific salah such that no other surah is considered permissible in that slot (muta'ayyan).

**What is permitted:** Habitually reciting the same surahs without that inner conviction of exclusivity is not makruh — even if it happens consistently. The criterion is the internal fixation, not the external pattern.

**Reason for the prohibition:**
1. It implies the ignored surahs are lesser, which is incorrect — all parts of the Quran carry equal sanctity
2. It implies one surah is superior to all others in an absolute sense
3. It leads to neglect of memorization

**Application to Jumu'ah Fajr surahs (Alif Lam Mim Sajdah + Surah al-Dahr/al-Insan):**
- If recited with the intention of following the sunnah of Rasulullah ﷺ: rewarded and correct
- If recited with the conviction that nothing else is permissible: makruh
- Imam al-Jibawi رحمه الله and Imam al-Tahawi رحمه الله both noted this distinction

### 7. Minimum Recitation in Salah

**Imam Abu Hanifa's position:** Any portion of the Quran that qualifies as "ismul Quran" (a recognizable word of the Quran) is technically sufficient. His istidlal: the Quranic ayah فَاقْرَأُوا مَا تَيَسَّرَ مِنَ الْقُرْآنِ ("read whatever is easy from the Quran") prescribes no minimum.

**Discussion:** This raises the question of single-worded ayat (e.g., مُدْهَامَّتَانِ) or even single-letter ayat (ق، ص) — are these sufficient? Imam Abu Hanifa's position implies yes; other fuqaha say no.

**Sahibain's position (Abu Yusuf and Muhammad):** Minimum is three short ayat or one long ayah. This is also the position referenced in Mukhtasar al-Quduri.

### 8. Muqtadi — No Recitation Behind the Imam

**Hanafi ruling:** A muqtadi (follower) recites neither Surah al-Fatihah nor any other surah behind the imam — whether the salah is jahri or sirri.

**Dalil 1 — Quranic (Nass Qat'i):** Surah al-A'raf, Ayah 204: "When the Quran is recited, listen to it attentively and be silent so that you may be shown mercy." (وَإِذَا قُرِئَ الْقُرْآنُ فَاسْتَمِعُوا لَهُ وَأَنصِتُوا لَعَلَّكُمْ تُرْحَمُونَ)

**Dalil 2 — Hadith** (Ibn Majah): Rasulullah ﷺ said: "The imam is appointed to be followed — when he recites, listen attentively..."

**Imam Malik's position:** Muqtadi is silent only in jahri salah; in sirri salah he may recite.

**Imam Shafi'i's position:** Muqtadi must recite Surah al-Fatihah in all prayers. His dalil (Bukhari): "There is no salah for one who did not recite Surah al-Fatihah." No restriction is specified in this hadith, so it applies to muqtadi as well.

**Imam Muhammad's (Hanafi) concession:** As a precaution due to this strong hadith, one may recite Surah al-Fatihah in sirri salah — but the **muftabih fatwa** is that the muqtadi remains completely silent in all cases.

**Saying Ameen:** Should be said very quietly (or only in the heart) — saying it loudly is not correct according to Ahnaf.

**Mouth/lip movement:** Not permitted — even moving lips silently is not acceptable, as it will inevitably lead to actual recitation.

---

### 9. Two Intentions Required for Muqtadi

**Rule:** To pray in jama'ah behind an imam, a muqtadi must make two intentions:
1. **Niyyat al-Salah** — the intention of the specific salah being performed
2. **Niyyat al-Mutaba'ah** — the intention to follow this imam

Without both, the prayer does not count as jama'ah prayer.

**Dalil** (Sahih Muslim, narrated by Abu Hurayrah رضي الله عنه): "The imam is appointed so that he should be followed — do not be at variance with him..."

**Catching a rakah in ruku:** If a person left home with the intention of praying behind the imam and joins during ruku, the intention is valid from when he left. The rakah is caught if his hands touch his knees before the imam says سَمِعَ اللهُ لِمَنْ حَمِدَهُ.

## Conditions & Categories

### Five Categories of Recitation in Salah (per Sahib al-Muhit رحمه الله)

| Category | Description |
|---|---|
| **Fard** | Recitation itself (any valid recitation that fulfills the obligation) |
| **Wajib** | Surah al-Fatihah + one additional surah (minimum required combination) |
| **Masnoon** | Reciting from the appropriate section of the Mufassal for each salah (see below) |
| **Mustahab** | 30–40 ayat in the first rakah of Fajr; 20–30 ayat in the second rakah of Fajr |
| **Makruh** | Reciting without Surah al-Fatihah; less than one ayah after Fatihah; reciting surahs out of Quranic order; leaving Fatihah incomplete before ruku |

### Masnoon Recitation by Salah

| Salah | Category | Range |
|---|---|---|
| Fajr | Tiwal al-Mufassal | Surah al-Hujurat → Surah al-Buruj |
| Zuhr | Tiwal al-Mufassal | Surah al-Hujurat → Surah al-Buruj |
| Asr | Awsat al-Mufassal | Surah al-Buruj → Lam Yakuni (Surah al-Bayyinah) |
| Maghrib | Qisar al-Mufassal | Surah al-Zalzalah → Surah al-Nas |
| Isha | Awsat al-Mufassal | Surah al-Buruj → Lam Yakuni (Surah al-Bayyinah) |

### Rules for Reciting Out of Order

- Reciting a surah in the first rakah and then a surah that precedes it in the Quran in the second rakah is makruh
- If the same surah was recited in both rakahs: begin again from the beginning of the Quran for the second rakah choice, or recite a different surah

### Conditions for Witr to be Valid as Jama'ah Prayer (Hanafi)

- Must be three continuous rakahs (no salam between rakah 2 and 3)
- Imam recites aloud in all three rakahs
- A surah must be added after Fatihah in every rakah
- Qunut recited before ruku in the third rakah
- Single-rakah Witr alone is not valid and should not be followed

### Catching a Ruku — Conditions

- The muqtadi's hands must reach his knees before the imam says سَمِعَ اللهُ لِمَنْ حَمِدَهُ
- Being delayed by more than two rukns from the imam risks the salah becoming fasid
- Falling behind in one rukn due to child/external obstruction and catching up before the next major position: valid, alhamdulillah

## Lessons & Practical Application

1. **Witr must be taken seriously.** Unlike sunnah prayers, Witr requires qadha if missed. Treat it as you would a fard — ensure it is prayed every night, and make it up if omitted.

2. **Three rakahs, unbroken.** When praying Witr behind an imam who performs 2+1, make the intention of Witr and follow. It is valid. But never follow an imam who performs only a single standalone rakah.

3. **Qunut al-Nazila is not restricted to Ramadan.** It is a tool for the Ummah during hardship. When calamity strikes (as with Gaza), it is established sunnah to perform it across all five daily prayers for the duration of the hardship — not just in Witr.

4. **Do not object to other madhabs unnecessarily.** The one exception in Hanafi fiqh is not raising hands during a Shafi'i imam's Qunut in Fajr. Beyond that, follow the imam and say Ameen to his dua.

5. **Avoid fixing surahs.** Habitually reciting the same surahs is fine if done without internal rigidity. The moment you believe nothing else is acceptable, it becomes makruh. Vary your recitation to preserve the full Quran's honor and to maintain memorization.

6. **As a muqtadi: close your mouth, open your ears.** No recitation of any kind — not Fatihah, not surahs, not even silent lip movement. The Quranic command to listen when the Quran is recited is a Nass Qat'i (absolute text). The imam's recitation covers the congregation.

7. **Two intentions for jama'ah are non-negotiable.** When joining the imam, consciously intend both the salah and the following. If you left the house with both intentions in your heart, joining during ruku is valid.

8. **Do not delay behind the imam.** Stay within one rukn of the imam's position. Being more than two rukns behind risks the salah becoming fasid, requiring a full redo.

9. **Tasbi between the two sajdahs is not required.** The only requirement in the jalsa between the two prostrations is itminan (a moment of stillness). Forgetting Tasbi or reciting Subhana Rabbi al-A'la only once does not require Sajdat al-Sahw.
