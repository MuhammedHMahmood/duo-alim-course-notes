## Session Overview

This session covered the **sunan (recommended acts) and mustahabbat (desirable acts) of wudu**, following the completion of the fara'id (obligatory acts) of wudu in the previous session. The session also introduced the opening discussion on **nawaqid al-wudu** (acts that break wudu), covering the categories of things that nullify wudu — specifically what exits the body, what enters the body, and states related to the body. The class used *Mukhtasar al-Quduri* as the primary text, with discussion referencing *Hidayah* and other classical sources.

---

## Key Themes

1. **Linguistic and legal definitions of Sunnah** — distinguishing Sunnah al-Huda from Sunnah al-Zawa'id
2. **Sunan of wudu** — nine acts the Prophet ﷺ performed consistently in wudu
3. **Mustahabbat of wudu** — six additional desirable acts, with some scholarly disagreement on categorisation
4. **Absence of wajibat in wudu** — unlike other acts of worship, wudu has no intermediate wajib category; it is either fard, sunnah, or mustahab
5. **Nawaqid al-wudu** — three categories of nullifiers: what exits the body, what enters the body, and states of the body
6. **Blood and pus** — detailed rulings on when flowing blood breaks wudu and the concept of the *mazur*
7. **Scholarly differences** — how Hanafi, Shafi'i, and Maliki positions diverge on multiple masail

## Fiqh Rulings & Evidence

### Sunan of Wudu

**1. Washing the hands three times before placing them into a vessel (upon waking)**
- Only applies when waking from sleep; at other times this rule does not apply
- Reason: a person does not know what their hands may have touched while asleep — if placed directly into a water vessel, the water could become impure
- If a person is certain their hands are clean, they may place them directly into the vessel
- *Dalil:* Various ahadith; Hadith of Mughirah ibn Shu'ba (radiAllahu anhu) among the supporting evidences

**2. Saying Bismillah at the beginning of wudu (Tasmiyah)**
- Imam Quduri (rahimahullah): **Sunnah mu'akkadah**
- Sahibi Hidayah: **Mustahab**
- 'Allama (rahimahullah): **Sunnah** (without specifying subcategory)
- Third opinion (some scholars): **Wajib** — based on a hadith in Abu Dawud, Tirmidhi, and Ibn Majah: *"The one who does not say Bismillah at the start of wudu, his wudu is not complete."*
- Hanafi position: Sunnah — the hadith evidence is considered but does not meet the threshold for establishing a wajib ruling in isolation
- If a person enters the bathroom where saying Bismillah aloud is discouraged, and they had already made the intention before entering, they may continue without repeating it

**3. Using the Siwak (Miswak)**
- Using siwak increases the reward of salah 70 times over salah without it
- If siwak is unavailable: use fingers with the intention of siwak (same reward applies); alternatively use a toothbrush with the same intention
- Key scholarly difference:
  - **Ahnaf (Hanafi):** Siwak is a *sunnah of wudu* (performed at time of wudu) — this is the mu'tamad (relied-upon) opinion
  - **Shafi'i:** Siwak is a *sunnah of salah* (performed just before beginning salah) — hence some worshippers are seen using siwak right before salah begins

**4. Rinsing the mouth (Madmada)**
- Done three times, with new water each time
- **Important:** Madmada should be avoided or done very carefully during fasting (do not gargle deeply)
- *Dalil:* Hadith in Abu Dawud and a variant wording in Sahih Muslim specifying three times with new water each time
- **Imam Malik:** Considers madmada a *fard* of wudu — if omitted, wudu is incomplete
- **Ahnaf and Shafi'i:** Sunnah

**5. Cleaning the nostrils (Istinshaq)**
- Water is drawn into the soft part of the nose
- Done three times with new water each time (Hanafi position)
- **Shafi'i:** Madmada and istinshaq are done together three times — the water from the mouth is used for the nostrils in the same scoop (i.e., six actions become three)
- *Dalil:* Same hadith as madmada (Abu Dawud, Muslim)
- **Imam Malik:** Considers istinshaq a *fard* of wudu as well

**6. Wiping the ears (Masah of ears)**
- Done after wiping the head; no need to wet hands again — use the remaining moisture from the masah of the head
- Both the outer (upper) and inner (lower) parts of the ears are wiped
- **Imam Shafi'i:** New water should be taken for the masah of the ears (separate from the head masah)

**7. Interlacing the beard (Takhlil al-lihyah)**
- Wet hand is run through the beard to ensure water reaches the skin beneath
- If the beard is very thick and it is not physically possible to reach the skin, one does as much as possible without pulling or breaking hairs — the hair itself stands in place of the skin in this case
- If beard is sparse and skin is visible, one should wash that area properly to ensure water reaches the skin
- Running fingers from underneath (back of hand going inward) may allow better penetration
- *Dalil:* Hadith in Tirmidhi and Abu Dawud

**8. Interlacing the fingers (Takhlil al-asabi')**
- Done after masah of head, ears, and beard — using remaining moisture on the hands (no need to re-wet)
- Fingers are spread wide and the fingers of one hand are run between the fingers of the other
- Purpose: ensures water reaches the gaps between fingers that may have been missed when fingers were held together during washing
- *Dalil:* Hadith in Tirmidhi and Nasa'i
- Note: In Imam Quduri's text, takhlil of beard and fingers are listed together as one item, but they can be counted separately

**9. Washing each limb three times (Tikrar al-ghasl)**
- Washing each limb once is the fard (established by the Quranic ayah); three times is the sunnah
- The basis for "three times" comes from hadith, not the Quran
- *Dalil:* Hadith in Bukhari and a variant in Abu Dawud
- Important distinction: *masah* (wiping) of the head is NOT included here — the Quranic word used for the head is masah, not ghasl. Masah of the head is done once (see mustahabbat below)

### Mustahabbat of Wudu

**1. Making intention (Niyyah)**
- Intention is made in the heart; saying it aloud is better
- Intention can be made before entering the bathroom (e.g., "I intend to perform wudu and pray Isha")
- **Hanafi position (Imam Quduri):** Niyyah in wudu is *mustahab* — wudu is valid without it, though the person misses reward
- **Imam Shafi'i:** Niyyah is *fard* in wudu — wudu is not valid without it
- *Dalil:* Hadith in Bukhari: *"Actions are by intentions"* — basis of Shafi'i position; Hanafis accept the hadith but classify niyyah as mustahab in this specific context
- Contrast with zakat: intention at the time of payment is essential for the zakat to count — the ruling varies by act of worship

**2. Wiping the entire head (Masah of full head)**
- The fard of masah (from the Hanafi Usul) is wiping one-quarter of the head — this fulfils the obligation
- Wiping the entire head is *mustahab* — rewarded but not obligatory
- Basis: The Prophet ﷺ wiped the entire head in some narrations and one-quarter in others; Hanafi usul reconciles both by making full head masah mustahab
- Masah of the head is done **once** — the word in the Quran is *masah*, not *ghasl* (washing), so the rule of "three times" for limbs does not apply
- *Dalil:* Hadith in Bukhari and Abu Dawud
- **Imam Shafi'i:** Masah should be done three times with new water each time; also holds that masah of even three hairs suffices for the obligatory masah

**3. Performing wudu in order (Tartib)**
- The Quranic sequence: face → hands (to elbows) → head → feet (to ankles)
- **Hanafi position:** Tartib is *mustahab* — if someone accidentally washed out of order, the wudu is valid; they do not need to restart, but should not do this habitually or deliberately
- **Imam Shafi'i:** Tartib is *fard* — departing from the Quranic order invalidates the wudu

**4. Washing limbs consecutively without long gaps (Muwalaah)**
- Ideal: proceed from one limb to the next without interruption
- If interrupted briefly and the limb has not fully dried: continue from where one left off
- If a long time has passed and the limbs have dried without a valid excuse: restart wudu from the beginning
- The distinction is between unavoidable urgent interruptions and casual negligence

**5. Starting from the right side (Bada' bil-yamin)**
- The Prophet ﷺ always began actions from his right side — distributing food, wearing shoes, etc.
- Applied in wudu: right hand before left hand, right foot before left foot
- *Dalil:* Hadith in Bukhari
- Some fuqaha consider this the *only* true mustahab of wudu, classifying all others as sunan

**6. Wiping the neck (Masah of neck)**
- Not mentioned in Imam Quduri's text but established by other fuqaha
- *Dalil:* Mentioned in *'Ala' al-Sunan* and *Musnad Ahmad*
- Included as an additional mustahab making the total six (or seven depending on counting)

### Nawaqid al-Wudu — Acts That Break Wudu

**Three categories framework:**

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| What exits the body | Blood, pus, urine, stool, gas, mazi, wadi, mani, hayd, nifas, istihada, stomach worms |
| What enters the body | Food, drink, intravenous fluids, suppositories — generally do NOT break wudu |
| States of the body | Sleep, loss of consciousness/insanity — break wudu |

**From the two passages (frontal and back):**
- Anything exiting from either passage breaks wudu
- This includes: gas, liquid, solid, semi-solid, and even stomach worms
- *Dalil:* Quranic ayah (Surah al-Ma'idah 5:6) mentioning going to relieve oneself; Hadith in Tirmidhi and Nasa'i

**Seven things from the frontal passage:**
1. Urine (men and women)
2. Mazi (pre-sexual fluid)
3. Wadi (thick white fluid after urination)
4. Mani (semen — requires ghusl)
5. Hayd (menstrual blood — women)
6. Nifas (post-natal blood — women)
7. Istihada (irregular bleeding — women)

**Three things from the back passage:**
1. Defecation
2. Gas/wind
3. Stomach worm

**Ruling on stomach worm:**
- **Ahnaf, Shafi'i, Hanbali:** Breaks wudu
- **Maliki:** Does not break wudu

**Blood and pus from the skin (not from the passages):**
- Blood/pus that oozes out of a wound but **does not flow** from its original location does NOT break wudu
- Once blood/pus begins to **flow** from its original location, wudu is broken
- Wiping it multiple times: if the combined amount across multiple wipes exceeds the threshold, wudu is broken
- If blood is visible on clothing or spreads beyond the wound site, wudu is likely broken and must be remade
- A band-aid that shows no external sign of bleeding: wudu remains intact; do not open unnecessarily
- The exact size threshold (possibly the size of a dirham or a chickpea) — to be confirmed in a subsequent class
- *Dalil:* Specific hadith on this; Quranic ayah (5:6) provides the general framework

**Imam Malik and Imam Shafi'i** on blood/pus: even if it flows from the wound, wudu is **not** broken — their own dalail support this position

**Sleep and insanity:**
- Neither sleep nor insanity inherently produces an impurity
- They break wudu because the person's rational faculty (*'aql*) is suspended — they have no awareness of what their body may have done (passed wind, touched impurity, etc.)

### The Mazur (Person with a Chronic Excuse)

- If blood or another nullifier flows **continuously** for a full day and night (24 hours) without stopping, the person enters the status of *mazur*
- A mazur may make wudu and pray even while the blood continues to flow — the wudu is not considered broken by the ongoing condition
- If the condition lasts less than 24 hours: the person waits; salah becomes qada' (to be made up) during the period of unbroken flow
- If flow stops before a prayer time ends: that salah should be prayed in its time
- If five prayers accumulate as missed, the person transitions to mazur status and may then pray qada' with wudu despite ongoing flow

**Practical guidance during the waiting period:**
- Some Hanafi scholars say one may pray anyway with the intention to repeat later; others say to wait and make qada' — to be confirmed

## Scholarly Opinions

**Imam Abu Hanifa (Rahimahullah):**
- No wajibat exist in wudu — only fara'id, sunan, and mustahabbat
- Siwak is sunnah of wudu
- Niyyah is mustahab (not fard)
- Tartib is mustahab
- Masah of one-quarter of head fulfils the fard; full head is mustahab
- Blood that flows from its location breaks wudu

**Imam Shafi'i (Rahimahullah):**
- Niyyah is fard in wudu
- Tartib is fard
- Masah of the head: done three times with new water; even three hairs are sufficient for the fard portion
- Siwak is sunnah of salah (not of wudu)
- Madmada and istinshaq combined in one scoop (three times total, not six)
- Blood/pus flowing from the skin does NOT break wudu

**Imam Malik (Rahimahullah):**
- Madmada and istinshaq are fard in wudu
- Blood/pus flowing from the skin does NOT break wudu
- Worm exiting the back passage does NOT break wudu

**Sahibi Hidayah:**
- Classifies Bismillah (tasmiyah) as mustahab (vs. Imam Quduri's classification as sunnah mu'akkadah)

**'Allama (rahimahullah) mentioned in class:**
- Classifies Bismillah as sunnah without specifying subcategory

## Lessons & Practical Application

**On performing wudu:**
- When waking from sleep, always wash hands before placing them into a water vessel — do not assume cleanliness
- Say Bismillah at the start of wudu; if entering the bathroom before having said it, make the intention beforehand
- Use siwak (or fingers/toothbrush with the intention of siwak) — the 70× reward for salah is significant
- Rinse mouth and clean nostrils three times each, with fresh water each time (Hanafi method), noting this should be avoided during fasting
- When wiping ears, use the remaining moisture from the masah of the head — no need to re-wet
- For thick beards: do takhlil as deeply as possible without causing pain; the hair replaces the skin
- For sparse beards/goatees: wash the visible skin directly to ensure water reaches it
- Spread fingers during takhlil to guarantee water reaches between the joints
- Wash each limb three times — once is fard, three times is sunnah

**On achieving Wudu Kamil (complete/perfect wudu):**
- Fulfilling all fara'id, sunan, and mustahabbat together constitutes a "kamil wudu"
- Praying with kamil wudu gradually changes the quality of salah — the *nur* of it affects the heart over time, though this may not be felt immediately
- Comparison: just as perfecting a skill in worldly pursuits (sport, study) transforms performance, perfecting wudu transforms the quality of prayer

**On blood and wounds:**
- Monitor wounds during salah time: if blood stays at the wound site and does not flow, wudu is intact
- If blood seeps through clothing, wudu is likely broken — remake it
- A band-aid with no visible blood seeping through: assume wudu is intact; do not open the wound to check unnecessarily
- For continuous bleeding: consult a scholar about the mazur ruling for one's specific situation
- Approximate size threshold for when flowing blood breaks wudu: to be confirmed (possibly size of a dirham or chickpea)

**On asking questions:**
- Asking genuine questions of scholars and teachers is an act of honour toward them and a means of one's own learning
- The Sahaba would wait for Bedouins to ask direct questions to the Prophet ﷺ because their blunt, clear questions produced detailed beneficial explanations
- One category of question — asked with sincere intention to learn — is of great benefit in this world and the next
- Excessive or purposeless questioning should be avoided (established in hadith in Bukhari and Muslim)
- Students are encouraged to post questions to the class chat if the teacher does not follow up

**On memorising ahadith:**
- Students encouraged to begin memorising short ahadith in Arabic — starting with 10–20 smaller narrations
- Rationale: all of fiqh and deen rests on the ahadith of the Prophet ﷺ; being able to cite them strengthens conviction and enables one to share knowledge with family, friends, and community
- The teacher offered to curate a short list of relevant ahadith to share with the class
