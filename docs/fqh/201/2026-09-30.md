# Fiqh 201 - Study Notes
**Date:** 2026-09-30

---

## Session Overview

This session covers **Bab Sifat al-Salah** (Chapter on the Attributes/Description of Prayer), beginning at page 68 of the text (Mukhtasar al-Quduri). Imam al-Quduri outlines the six Fara'id (obligatory pillars) of Salah and then proceeds through a detailed explanation of each pillar — from Takbir al-Tahreemah through to the second Sajdah — including the correct method of performance, supporting evidence from Quran and Hadith, and points of scholarly difference between the four madhabs and within the Hanafi school itself.

---

## Key Themes

1. **Fara'id vs. Sunan of Salah** — The six pillars without which Salah is invalid, distinguished from Wajibat (whose omission requires Sajdah Sahu) and Sunan (whose omission does not invalidate)
2. **Dalil-based Fiqh** — Every ruling is grounded in Qur'anic ayah or specific Hadith; Ahnaf, Shafi'i, Maliki, and Hanbali positions each have valid textual basis
3. **Ta'deel al-Arkan** — The requirement of stillness and measured pausing between postures, classified as Wajib by Ahnaf and Fard by Imam Shafi'i
4. **Difference within Hanafi school** — Imam Abu Hanifah, Imam Abu Yusuf, and Imam Muhammad (Sahibayn) frequently differ on subsidiary rulings; the Mufta bihi (operative fatwa) position is noted throughout
5. **Respecting other madhabs** — Students warned against criticizing other schools, as each draws from Quran and Sunnah

## Fiqh Rulings & Evidence

### 1. The Six Fara'id of Salah

Imam al-Quduri states: *"Fara'id al-Salah sittah: Takbir al-Tahreemah, al-Qiyam, al-Qira'at, al-Ruku, al-Sujood, al-Qad'ah al-Akhirah miqdar al-Tashahud."*

> Note: Imam al-Quduri lists only six, omitting the Salam (which some count as a seventh). The reason will be discussed in a future session.

| Pillar | Dalil |
|---|---|
| Takbir al-Tahreemah | Qur'an, Surah al-Muddaththir (74:3): "وَرَبَّكَ فَكَبِّرْ" — interpreted by Mufassirun as referring to Takbir al-Tahreemah |
| Qiyam | Qur'an, Surah al-Baqarah (2:238): "وَقُومُوا لِلَّهِ قَانِتِينَ"; also Hadith: "Pray standing, and if unable, then sitting" |
| Qira'at | Qur'an, Surah al-Muzzammil (73:20): "فَاقْرَأُوا مَا تَيَسَّرَ مِنَ الْقُرْآنِ"; also Jumhur al-Ulama consensus |
| Ruku & Sujood | Qur'an, Surah al-Hajj (22:77): "يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا ارْكَعُوا وَاسْجُدُوا" |
| Qad'ah Akhirah | Hadith narrated by Abdullah ibn Mas'ud (r.a.) in Abu Dawud: "If you complete this [final sitting], your Salah is complete" |

---

### 2. Takbir al-Tahreemah

**Status:** Imam Abu Hanifah and Imam Abu Yusuf (Shaykhain) consider it a **Shart** (precondition) of Salah, because it connects the external (what is outside Salah) to the internal (the Arkan of Salah). Imam al-Quduri himself classifies it as a Rukn.

**Raising the Hands:**
- **Fard:** Raising the hands itself is Fard (obligatory).
- **Sunnah:** The manner and exact position of raising are Sunnah.
- **Ahnaf position:** Thumbs level with the earlobes; palms open; fingers pointing upward; facing Qiblah.
- **Imam Shafi'i:** Up to the shoulders is acceptable.
- **Imam Malik:** Even above the earlobes up to the head is acceptable.
- **Dalil for Ahnaf:** Hadith of Harith ibn Huwairaz (r.a.) in Bukhari and Muslim; Hadith of Wail ibn Hujr (r.a.) in Muslim.
- **For women:** Hands raised only to chest or shoulder level, to preserve modesty.

**Order of raising hands and saying Takbir:**
- **Ahnaf:** Raise hands first, then say Allahu Akbar. Dalil: Hadith in Muslim.
- Both sequences appear in Hadith; either is acceptable per the narrations.

**Wording of Takbir:**

| Opinion | Acceptable Wordings |
|---|---|
| Imam Abu Hanifah & Imam Muhammad | Allahu Akbar, Allahu Ajall, Allahu A'zam, Ar-Rahmanu Akbar — all valid |
| Imam Abu Yusuf | Only: **Allahu Akbar** or **Allahu Kabir** (must contain the root *k-b-r*) |
| Imam Shafi'i | Only: **Allahu Akbar** |
| Imam Malik | Only: **Allahu Akbar** |

### 3. Hand Placement in Qiyam

**Method (for men):**
1. Place the left palm below the navel.
2. Place the open right palm over the back of the left hand.
3. Encircle the left wrist with the pinky finger and thumb of the right hand.
4. Place the remaining three fingers of the right hand on top.

**Dalil:** Two Hadiths — one in Tirmizi, one in Muslim — both establish that the right hand holds the left.

**Position (below navel — Ahnaf):**
- **Dalil:** Hadith in Abu Dawud.
- **Imam Shafi'i:** Hands on the chest. His Dalil is also from Abu Dawud — the same three Hadiths Ahnaf use.
- The preference of one over the other is discussed in detail in Usul al-Fiqh (particularly Usul al-Karkhi) and in Kitab al-Salah of Abu Dawud.

---

### 4. Sana (Opening Supplication)

**Text:** *"Subhanakallahumma wa bihamdika wa tabarakasmuka wa ta'ala jadduka wa la ilaha ghayruk"*

**Dalil:** Hadith in Tirmizi.

**Imam Shafi'i's Sana:** The Quranic ayah from Surah al-An'am: *"Inni wajjahtu wajhiya lilladhi fataras-samawati wal-ard hanifan wa ma ana min al-mushrikin."*

**Order:** Sana → Ta'awwudh → Bismillah → Surah al-Fatihah. Ahnaf explain that Ta'awwudh is the Sunnah for beginning Qira'at (not for Sana), which is why Sana comes first.

---

### 5. Ta'awwudh (A'udhu billah)

**Status (Ahnaf):** Sunnah of Qira'at of the Quran.
**Dalil:** Qur'an, Surah al-Nahl (16:98): "فَإِذَا قَرَأْتَ الْقُرْآنَ فَاسْتَعِذْ بِاللَّهِ مِنَ الشَّيْطَانِ الرَّجِيمِ"; also Hadith in Tirmizi.
**Recitation:** Silently (softly), not aloud.

---

### 6. Bismillah

**Ahnaf:** Recited **silently** before Surah al-Fatihah.
**Dalil:** Hadith in Nasa'i narrated by Anas ibn Malik (r.a.): "I prayed behind the Prophet ﷺ, Abu Bakr, Umar, and Uthman — none of them said Bismillah loudly."
**Imam Shafi'i:** Recited loudly, as he considers Bismillah part of Surah al-Fatihah.
**Imam Malik:** Bismillah is not recited in Salah at all; even omitting it is acceptable.

---

### 7. Qira'at (Recitation)

**Surah al-Fatihah:**
- **Status (Ahnaf): Wajib** — its omission requires Sajdah Sahu.
- **Imam Shafi'i and Imam Malik:** Fard — its omission invalidates the Salah entirely.
- **Dalil 1:** Hadith in Bukhari (narrated by 'Ubadah ibn al-Samit r.a.): *"Whoever does not recite Surah al-Fatihah in his prayer, his prayer is invalid."*
- **Dalil 2:** Hadith in Muslim (narrated by Abu Hurairah r.a.): *"He who said his prayer but did not recite the Opening Chapter, his prayer is incomplete"* — said three times.

**Adding a Surah after Fatihah:**
- **Status (Ahnaf): Wajib** in the first two rak'ahs of Fard Salah.
- **Minimum:** Three short ayahs OR one long ayah from any Surah.
- **Dalil:** Hadith in Tirmizi (narrated by Abu Sa'id al-Khudri r.a.): *"There is no Salah for one who did not recite al-Hamd and a Surah in the obligatory prayer or other prayers."*
- **Dalil for reading from any portion of Quran:** Surah al-Muzzammil (73:20): "فَاقْرَأُوا مَا تَيَسَّرَ مِنَ الْقُرْآنِ"

**Practical rule on missed Fard vs. Wajib:**
- If a **Fard** (e.g., a Sajdah) is missed, it must be performed within the Salah; if remembered only after Salah, the Salah must be repeated.
- If a **Wajib** (e.g., Surah al-Fatihah) is missed, Sajdah Sahu at the end compensates. Missing a Wajib and deliberately finishing without Sajdah Sahu also requires repetition.
- Multiple missed Wajibs: One set of Sajdah Sahu compensates (up to approximately three; beyond a certain number the Salah must be repeated — exact limit to be confirmed).

### 8. Saying Ameen

- **Both Imam and Muqtadi say Ameen (Ahnaf): Silently.**
- **Dalil for saying Ameen:** Hadith in Bukhari: *"Say Ameen when the Imam says it; if your Ameen coincides with that of the angels, all past sins will be forgiven."*
- **Dalil for saying it silently (Ahnaf):** Hadith in Tirmizi — Ibn Shihab states the Prophet ﷺ lowered his voice when saying Ameen.
- **Imam Shafi'i and Imam Malik:** Ameen is said **loudly**. Dalil: Hadith in Ibn Majah narrated by Abu Hurairah (r.a.).
- **Notable:** Abu Hurairah (r.a.) himself — whose Hadith Shafi'i and Malik use — stopped saying Ameen loudly in the later part of his life, indicating a subsequent narration influenced his practice.

### 9. Ruku (Bowing)

**Correct Position:**
- Say Allahu Akbar while moving into Ruku.
- Place both palms on the kneecaps with fingers **spread open**.
- Keep the back **level and straight** — head neither raised nor lowered relative to the back.
- Eyes directed downward (not looking forward).

**Dalil (back level):** Hadith in Nasa'i: *"When the Prophet ﷺ bowed, he was balanced — he did not raise his head higher or lower than his back, and he placed his hands on his knees."*
**Dalil (spreading fingers on kneecaps):** Hadith in Tirmizi.

**Tasbih in Ruku:**
- *"Subhana Rabbiy al-'Azim"* — **minimum three times** (also the Sunnah amount).
- May be increased; should be recited in **odd numbers** (Dalil: Tirmizi).
- **Ahnaf:** Tasbih is Sunnah; omitting it does not require Sajdah Sahu.
- **Imam Ahmad:** Doing it even once is Wajib; omitting requires Sajdah Sahu.

**Common error:** Some people hold their clothing (izar/pants) instead of placing palms on kneecaps. This is incorrect and should be corrected.

---

### 10. Rising from Ruku & Ta'deel al-Arkan

**Imam says:** *"Sami'allahu liman hamidah"* while rising.
**Muqtadi says:** *"Rabbana lakal hamd"* — does **not** say Sami'allahu liman hamidah.

| Opinion | What the Imam says | Dalil |
|---|---|---|
| Imam Abu Hanifah (Mufta bihi) | Imam: Sami'allahu liman hamidah only; Muqtadi: Rabbana lakal hamd only | Bukhari, Tirmizi |
| Imam Shafi'i & Imam Abu Yusuf | Imam says **both** | Bukhari |
| Praying alone / women at home | Must say **both** | — |

**Ta'deel al-Arkan (pausing between postures):**
- After rising fully from Ruku, the musalli must **stand still for a moment** before going into Sajdah.
- **Ahnaf:** This pause is **Wajib**. Missing it may require Sajdah Sahu. Dalil: Hadith in Bukhari.
- **Imam Shafi'i and Imam Abu Yusuf:** This is **Fard** — missing it invalidates the Salah.
- Critical note for Imams: If the Imam skips this pause, the Salah of all those praying behind him may also be affected.

### 11. Sajdah (Prostration)

**Entry:** Say Allahu Akbar, place hands on ground first, then place the face between the two palms with **both the forehead and nose on the ground**.

**What must touch the ground (7 bones):**
Based on Hadith in Bukhari (Ibn Abbas r.a.): *"I have been ordered to prostrate on seven bones: the forehead (along with the tip of the nose), both hands, both knees, and the toes of both feet — and not to gather the clothing or hair."*

The seven are: forehead + nose tip, 2 hands, 2 knees, toes of 2 feet.

**Scholarly difference on forehead and nose:**

| Opinion | Ruling |
|---|---|
| Imam Abu Hanifah | Either forehead **or** nose is sufficient (one of the two) |
| Imam Abu Yusuf & Imam Muhammad (Sahibayn) — **Mufta bihi** | **Both** must touch the ground without a valid excuse |
| Imam Shafi'i | Both forehead and nose must touch the ground (Fard) |
| All scholars | At minimum, the **soft tip of the nose** must touch the ground |

**Dalil for Sahibayn:** Hadith in Bukhari (Ibn Abbas r.a.) — Prophet ﷺ pointed to his nose when listing the seven bones.

**Ahnaf's interpretation of Imam Shafi'i's Hadith:** "No Salah for the one who does not put his forehead on the ground" — Ahnaf interpret this as pointing to the excellence of Sajdah, analogous to the Hadith that a person living near the Masjid has "no Salah" except in the Masjid (meaning his excellence is diminished, not that it is invalid absolutely).

**Position in Sajdah (for men):**
- Armpits open (away from the body) — Prophet ﷺ held his arms so far that the whiteness of his armpits was visible (Bukhari).
- Gap between stomach and thighs.
- All **20 fingers** (hands and feet) pointing toward the **Qiblah** — requires practice; achievable within 2–3 months with effort.
- Forearms raised off the ground (not resting flat).
- **Dalil:** Hadith narrated by Abu Humayd al-Sa'idi (r.a.) in Bukhari.

**For women:** Body kept close and compact (opposite of men) — detailed discussion deferred to a separate session on women's Salah.

**Tasbih in Sajdah:**
- *"Subhana Rabbiy al-A'la"* — **minimum three times**.
- Same ruling as Ruku tasbih: Sunnah per Ahnaf; should be in odd numbers.

**Sajdah on cloth of Amama:**
- Permissible if a fold of the turban or its trailing end falls between the forehead and the ground. Dalil: Hadith in Bukhari (Anas ibn Malik r.a.) — Sahaba used to place the ends of their clothes at the place of prostration due to the scorching heat, and the Prophet ﷺ did not object (**Muqarrar Hadith**).
- **Condition:** The head must still be able to **feel the hardness of the ground**. If the Amama is wrapped so thickly that the forehead cannot make contact with the ground at all, the Sajdah is invalid.
- This same principle applies to prayer mats — acceptable, even if thick, as long as the forehead is pressing against the surface.

**When is Sajdah considered complete?**

| Opinion | Ruling | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| Imam Abu Yusuf | When the **head is placed** on the ground | If wudu breaks after placement, Sajdah is complete; need not repeat |
| Imam Muhammad — **Mufta bihi** | When the person **begins to raise the head** | If wudu breaks after placement but before raising, must repeat the Sajdah |

---

### 12. Sitting Between the Two Sajdahs (Jalsa)

- After the first Sajdah, say Allahu Akbar and **sit peacefully** (Itminan) before the second Sajdah.
- **Dalil:** Hadith in Muslim — the Prophet ﷺ used to sit between the two Sajdahs for so long that people thought he had forgotten.
- This sitting is part of **Ta'deel al-Arkan** (Wajib per Ahnaf) — minimum duration: enough to say *"Alhamdulillah"* two or three times.

---

### 13. Second Sajdah

**Status:** Fard — a distinct obligatory pillar.
**Dalil:** Hadith in Bukhari (Abu Hurairah r.a.) — the famous narration of the man who prayed incorrectly three times. The Prophet ﷺ instructed: *"...then prostrate calmly until you feel at ease, then raise your head and sit with calmness, then prostrate with calmness until you feel at ease..."* — pointing to two distinct Sajdahs.

---

### 14. Rising to the Second Rak'ah

- After the second Sajdah, say Allahu Akbar and rise **straight up** without sitting.
- **Ahnaf:** No brief sitting between the first and second rak'ah (unlike some other madhabs who pause momentarily).
- When rising, **do not place hands on the ground** if one is healthy; instead, push up using the leg muscles, or place hands on thighs/knees if needed.
- Placing hands on the ground is only acceptable for the elderly, the injured, or those with a valid excuse.

## Conditions & Categories

### Classification of Acts in Salah

| Category | Definition | Consequence of Omission |
|---|---|---|
| **Fard (Pillar)** | Absolute requirement; no Salah exists without it | Salah is invalid; must be repeated. If missed mid-Salah and remembered, must be performed before completing |
| **Wajib** | Necessary act; below Fard | Omission requires Sajdah Sahu. If deliberately omitted without Sajdah Sahu, Salah must be repeated |
| **Sunnah** | Established practice of the Prophet ﷺ | Omission is disliked but does not invalidate; no Sajdah Sahu required |

### Sajdah Sahu Capacity
- One set of Sajdah Sahu compensates for multiple missed Wajibs (up to a number — approximately three; beyond this limit the Salah must be repeated; exact number to be confirmed in a future session).

### Making Up a Missed Fard Mid-Salah
- If a Fard pillar (e.g., a Sajdah) is missed and remembered in a later rak'ah, the musalli should perform that missed Fard immediately, then continue from where they left off, and perform Sajdah Sahu at the end.

## Lessons & Practical Application

1. **Takbir al-Tahreemah technique:** For men — open palms, thumbs level with earlobes, fingers pointing upward. Raise hands *before* saying Allahu Akbar. For women — raise hands only to chest or shoulder height. This distinction preserves modesty.

2. **Hand placement in Qiyam:** Practice the correct grip — right pinky and thumb encircling the left wrist, three fingers resting on top. Place below the navel. Do not hold clothing; do not allow this habit to persist.

3. **Qira'at obligations:** Surah al-Fatihah is Wajib per Ahnaf — its omission is recoverable via Sajdah Sahu if caught within Salah. However, adding a Surah after Fatihah is also Wajib in the first two rak'ahs. Minimum: three short ayahs or one long ayah.

4. **Ameen:** In a Hanafi congregation, both the Imam and all followers say Ameen quietly. Do not be confused by other congregations doing otherwise — both practices have valid Hadith-based grounds.

5. **Ruku posture check:** Back must be flat and level; fingers spread on kneecaps (not closed, not holding clothing). Tasbih minimum is three times in odd numbers.

6. **Ta'deel al-Arkan — the most overlooked Wajib:** After rising from Ruku, pause and stand fully upright before going into Sajdah. Between the two Sajdahs, sit with Itminan. These pauses are Wajib. An Imam who skips them may inadvertently affect the validity of the entire congregation's Salah.

7. **Sajdah position:** All 20 fingers (hands and feet) pointing toward the Qiblah. This takes practice — begin now and it becomes natural within a few months. Armpits open; stomach away from thighs.

8. **Amama/cloth in Sajdah:** A turban or prayer mat between the forehead and ground is permissible as long as the forehead still makes firm contact with the surface. An excessively thick wrapping that prevents contact is not permissible.

9. **Rising to the next rak'ah:** Rise straight from the second Sajdah without a sitting pause, and without placing hands on the ground (unless elderly, injured, or otherwise excused).

10. **Respecting madhab differences:** When a fellow Muslim from another madhab does something differently (e.g., places hands on chest, says Ameen loudly, raises hands to shoulder), recognize that their practice traces back to valid Hadith. Criticizing another madhab's Dalil amounts to criticizing Quran and Sunnah — a serious matter. Follow the Hanafi method with confidence in its Dalil, not with dismissal of others.
