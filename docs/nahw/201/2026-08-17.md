## Session Overview

This session covered a review of Kalam (complete vs. incomplete speech) and the alāmāt (distinguishing signs) of fi'l, then launched into a new chapter: **Faslun fī Asnāfi I'rābi l-Ism** — the nine categories of i'rāb for nouns. Four of the nine categories were covered in detail, with extensive discussion of the conditions governing each type.

---

## Grammar Rules Covered

### Review: Kalam Tām vs. Kalam Nāqis

- **Kalam Nāqis:** Incomplete speech — e.g., *Zaydun al-bā'idu* (two words, no complete meaning conveyed).
- **Kalam Tām:** Complete speech — e.g., *Zaydun fil-bayti*. Its alāma: *yasiḥḥu l-sukūtu 'alayhā* — it is permissible to stop/pause on it (the listener has no remaining questions).

### Review: Alāmāt of Fi'l

- **Sawfa:** Strongest alāma exclusive to fi'l — only fi'l accepts *sawfa*, making it the most definitive distinguishing sign.
- **Yā':** Shared with other categories, so weaker as an alāma.

---

### New Chapter: Faslun fī Asnāfi I'rābi l-Ism (The Nine Categories of Noun I'rāb)

The three primary i'rāb states are **Raf', Naṣb, and Jarr**. However, because secondary i'rāb (ḥurūf: wāw, alif, yā) can substitute for primary i'rāb (ḥarakāt: ḍamma, fatḥa, kasra), and because some nouns deviate from the default pattern (e.g., ghayr munsarif uses fatḥa in place of kasra), there are **nine distinct categories** of i'rāb for nouns.

---

### Category 1 — Raf' with Ḍamma, Naṣb with Fatḥa, Jarr with Kasra (Primary I'rāb)

**This is the default/standard i'rāb.**

**Conditions** — the noun must be:
1. **Mufrad** (singular)
2. **Munsarif** (not ghayr munsarif — i.e., it accepts tanwīn and kasra)
3. **Saḥīḥ** — defined in naḥw as: *mā lā yakūnu fī ākhirihi ḥarfu 'illah* — a noun whose final letter is not a ḥarf 'illah (ا، و، ي)

> Note: The definition of **ṣaḥīḥ** differs between naḥw and ṣarf. In ṣarf, a noun is ma'tall if it has a ḥarf 'illah *anywhere* (beginning, middle, or end). In naḥw, only the **final letter** matters — if it is free of ḥarf 'illah, the noun is ṣaḥīḥ.

**Also included in Category 1** (same i'rāb pattern):

- **Jārī majrā l-ṣaḥīḥ**: A noun ending in wāw or yā' with a sākin (sukūn) on the letter before it.
  - Examples: *Dalwun* (دَلْوٌ — wāw at end, mā qabl sākin), *Ẓabyun* (ظَبْيٌ — yā' at end, mā qabl sākin)
- **Jam' mukassar munsarif** (broken plural that is munsarif): e.g., *Rijālun* (رِجَالٌ)
  - Note: *Madāris* is also a broken plural but is **ghayr munsarif**, so it does not fall here.

**Examples:**
| Case | Example |
|---|---|
| Raf' | جَاءَنِي زَيْدٌ وَدَلْوٌ وَظَبْيٌ وَرِجَالٌ |
| Naṣb | رَأَيْتُ زَيْدًا وَدَلْوًا وَظَبْيًا وَرِجَالًا |
| Jarr | مَرَرْتُ بِزَيْدٍ وَدَلْوٍ وَظَبْيٍ وَرِجَالٍ |

*(Zayd = mufrad ṣaḥīḥ; Dalw and Ẓaby = jārī majrā l-ṣaḥīḥ; Rijāl = jam' mukassar munsarif)*

---

### Category 2 — Raf' with Ḍamma, Naṣb with Kasra, Jarr with Kasra

**Deviation:** Naṣb does not take fatḥa; instead **kasra substitutes for fatḥa** in both Naṣb and Jarr.

**This i'rāb is specific to: Jam' Mu'annas Sālim** (sound feminine plural — every mu'annas ending in alif + tā' marbūṭa in plural form, e.g., *Muslimāt*).

**Qur'ānic Example:**
> إِنَّ الْمُسْلِمِينَ وَالْمُسْلِمَاتِ

*Inna* normally causes fatḥa (naṣb). However, since *Muslimāt* is jam' mu'annas sālim, it **does not accept fatḥa** — kasra substitutes for fatḥa. So the kasra on *Muslimāti* is not a kasra of jarr but a kasra **representing fatḥa of naṣb**.

---

### Category 3 — Raf' with Ḍamma, Naṣb with Fatḥa, Jarr with Fatḥa

**Deviation:** Jarr does not take kasra; instead **fatḥa substitutes for kasra**.

**This i'rāb is specific to: Ghayr Munsarif** — nouns that do not accept tanwīn or kasra.

> Ghayr munsarif has **nine asbāb (causes)**; if any **two** of them are present in a noun, it becomes ghayr munsarif. (To be studied in detail in upcoming sessions.)

**Example — *'Umar* (عُمَرُ):**
| Case | Form | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Raf' | جَاءَنِي عُمَرُ | Ḍamma (no tanwīn) |
| Naṣb | رَأَيْتُ عُمَرَ | Fatḥa |
| Jarr | مَرَرْتُ بِعُمَرَ | Fatḥa (not kasra — ghayr munsarif rejects kasra) |

---

### Category 4 — Raf' with Wāw, Naṣb with Alif, Jarr with Yā' (Secondary I'rāb with Ḥurūf)

**Here ḥurūf (letters) replace ḥarakāt entirely:**
- Wāw → in place of Ḍamma (Raf')
- Alif → in place of Fatḥa (Naṣb)
- Yā' → in place of Kasra (Jarr)

**This i'rāb is specific to: Asmā' al-Sitta** (The Six Nouns), with **four conditions**:

#### The Six Nouns (Asmā' al-Sitta):
أَبٌ، أَخٌ، حَمٌ، فَمٌ، هَنٌ، ذُو

#### Four Conditions:
1. **Must be one of the six nouns** (asmā' al-sitta)
2. **Mukabbara** — in their base/regular form (not muṣagghar/taṣghīr form)
   - e.g., *Akhun* is mukabbara; its muṣagghar is *Ukhayyun* — if muṣagghar, different i'rāb applies
3. **Muwāḥada** — singular (not muṯhannā or jam')
   - If dual or plural, different i'rāb applies
4. **Muḍāf to other than Yā' al-Mutakallim**
   - If muḍāf to *Yā' al-Mutakallim* (e.g., *Akhī* — my brother), all three cases look identical → no i'rāb distinction → different rule applies

**Example — *Akhun* (أَخٌ) muḍāf to *Ḥāmid*:**
| Case | Form |
|---|---|
| Raf' | جَاءَ أَخُو حَامِدٍ |
| Naṣb | رَأَيْتُ أَخَا حَامِدٍ |
| Jarr | مَرَرْتُ بِأَخِي حَامِدٍ |

**Contrast — when muḍāf to Yā' al-Mutakallim (no i'rāb change):**
| Case | Form |
|---|---|
| Raf' | جَاءَ أَخِي |
| Naṣb | رَأَيْتُ أَخِي |
| Jarr | مَرَرْتُ بِأَخِي |

*(All three identical — therefore a separate rule governs this situation.)*

> **Remaining five categories** will be covered in the next session (inshā'Allāh).

## Important Vocabulary

| Arabic Term | Meaning / Definition |

| كَلَامٌ تَامٌّ | Complete speech — listener has no remaining questions; pausing on it is valid |
| كَلَامٌ نَاقِصٌ | Incomplete speech — does not convey a full meaning on its own |
| يَصِحُّ السُّكُوتُ عَلَيْهَا | "It is permissible to stop/pause on it" — alāma of kalam tām |
| سَوْفَ | Particle exclusive to fi'l; strongest alāma of fi'l |

| غَيْرُ مُنْصَرِفٌ | A noun that does not accept tanwīn or kasra; fatḥa substitutes for kasra in jarr |
| صَحِيحٌ (in naḥw) | A noun whose final letter is not a ḥarf 'illah |
| حَرْفُ عِلَّةٍ | The weak letters: ا، و، ي |
| مَعْتَلٌّ | A noun containing a ḥarf 'illah (definition differs: naḥw = at end; ṣarf = anywhere) |
| جَارٍ مَجْرَى الصَّحِيحِ | "Acting like ṣaḥīḥ" — noun ending in wāw or yā' with sukūn on the preceding letter |

| جَمْعُ مُكَسَّرٌ | Broken plural — singular form is structurally broken in the plural |
| جَمْعُ مُؤَنَّثٍ سَالِمٌ | Sound feminine plural — ends in ـَاتٌ; does not accept fatḥa in naṣb |
| أَسْبَابُ مَنْعِ الصَّرْفِ | The nine causes that make a noun ghayr munsarif (two must be present) |
| أَسْمَاءُ السِّتَّةِ | The six special nouns: أَبٌ، أَخٌ، حَمٌ، فَمٌ، هَنٌ، ذُو |
| مُكَبَّرٌ | Base/regular form of a noun (not diminutive) |
| مُصَغَّرٌ / تَصْغِيرٌ | Diminutive form of a noun (e.g., وُلَيْدٌ from وَلَدٌ; رُجَيْلٌ from رَجُلٌ) |
| مُوَحَّدٌ | Singular (as a condition for asmā' al-sitta i'rāb) |
| مُضَافٌ | A noun in an iḍāfa (possessive/genitive) construction |
| يَاءُ الْمُتَكَلِّمِ | The first-person possessive yā' (ـِي — "my") |
| مُضَافٌ إِلَيْهِ | The second noun in an iḍāfa; the one being possessed/specified |
| إِعْرَابٌ أَصْلِيٌّ | Primary i'rāb — by ḥarakāt (ḍamma, fatḥa, kasra) |
| إِعْرَابٌ فَرْعِيٌّ | Secondary i'rāb — by ḥurūf (wāw, alif, yā') or substitution of ḥarakāt |

## I'rāb (Sentence Analysis) Examples

### جَاءَنِي زَيْدٌ
- **زَيْدٌ** — Fā'il, marfū', alāmatu raf'ihi l-ḍamma l-ẓāhira. Mufrad, munsarif, ṣaḥīḥ → Category 1.

### رَأَيْتُ عُمَرَ
- **عُمَرَ** — Maf'ūl bihi, manṣūb, alāmatu naṣbihi l-fatḥa l-ẓāhira. Category 3 (ghayr munsarif).

### مَرَرْتُ بِعُمَرَ
- **بِ** — Ḥarf jarr.
- **عُمَرَ** — Majrūr, alāmatu jarrihi l-fatḥa niyābatan 'an il-kasra li-annahu ghayr munsarif. (Fatḥa stands in for kasra because ghayr munsarif rejects kasra.) Category 3.

### إِنَّ الْمُسْلِمَاتِ
- **الْمُسْلِمَاتِ** — Ism Inna, manṣūb, alāmatu naṣbihi l-kasra niyābatan 'an il-fatḥa li-annahu jam' mu'annas sālim. Category 2.

### جَاءَ أَخُو حَامِدٍ
- **أَخُو** — Fā'il, marfū', alāmatu raf'ihi l-wāw niyābatan 'an il-ḍamma li-annahu min al-asmā' al-sitta. Category 4.
- **حَامِدٍ** — Muḍāf ilayhi, majrūr, alāmatu jarrihi l-kasra l-ẓāhira.

### مَرَرْتُ بِأَخِي حَامِدٍ
- **أَخِي** — Majrūr bi-l-bā', alāmatu jarrihi l-yā' niyābatan 'an il-kasra li-annahu min al-asmā' al-sitta. Category 4.

## Key Takeaways

1. **There are nine categories of i'rāb for nouns**, arising from the three base states (Raf', Naṣb, Jarr) combined with variations in how those states are marked (primary ḥarakāt, substitute ḥarakāt, or ḥurūf).

2. **ṣaḥīḥ in naḥw ≠ ṣaḥīḥ in ṣarf.** In naḥw, only the final letter matters; in ṣarf, a ḥarf 'illah anywhere in the word makes it ma'tall. Do not conflate the two definitions.

3. **Ghayr munsarif rejects both tanwīn and kasra.** When a ghayr munsarif noun is majrūr, the fatḥa you see is not naṣb — it is fatḥa standing in for kasra. Answer accordingly in i'rāb: *alāmatu jarrihi l-fatḥa niyābatan 'an il-kasra.*

4. **Jam' mu'annas sālim rejects fatḥa in naṣb.** When you see *Inna + muslimāt*, the kasra is not jarr — it is kasra standing in for the expected fatḥa of naṣb.

5. **Asmā' al-sitta have four strict conditions** for their wāw/alif/yā' i'rāb to apply: must be one of the six nouns, mukabbara, muwāḥada (singular), and muḍāf to other than Yā' al-Mutakallim. If muḍāf to Yā' al-Mutakallim, all cases are identical (ـِي) and a different rule governs.

6. **Reading i'rāb correctly matters more than memorizing definitions.** The teacher's analogy: a skilled driver no longer thinks consciously about gears — but the student *must* be deliberate in the learning phase. Apply each condition consciously when parsing a text until the correct i'rāb becomes instinctive.

7. **Jārī majrā l-ṣaḥīḥ has nothing to do with Jār-Majrūr.** The term means "functioning like a ṣaḥīḥ noun" — a purely morphological classification based on the final letters wāw/yā' with sukūn before them.
