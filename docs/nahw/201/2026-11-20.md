## Grammar Rules Covered

### 1. Definition of Idafa (إضافة)

Idafa is the grammatical annexation of one noun (**Mudaf** — مضاف) to another noun (**Mudaf Ilayh** — مضاف إليه). The Mudaf Ilayh is always **Majroor** (مجرور). The relationship between them is mediated by a Harf Jarr — either:
- **Explicit (Mawjood):** e.g., مَرَرْتُ بِزَيْدٍ — here the verb (فعل) is linked to Zaid via the explicit Ba (بـ); this construction is called **Jarr Majroor** (جار ومجرور).
- **Hidden (Taqdiri):** e.g., غُلَامُ زَيْدٍ — the Lam is implied; this is proper Idafa.

---

### 2. Rules for the Mudaf (مضاف)

The Mudaf must be stripped of:
1. **Tanween (تنوین):** You cannot say غُلَامٌ زَيْدٍ — tanween is dropped.
2. **Noon of Tasniya (نون التثنية):** e.g., مُسْلِمَا زَيْدٍ not مُسْلِمَانِ زَيْدٍ.
3. **Noon of Jam' Muzakkar Salim (نون الجمع):** e.g., مُسْلِمُو الْهِنْدِ not مُسْلِمُونَ الْهِنْدِ.

> These noons stand in place of Tanween, so they follow the same rule.

---

### 3. Types of Idafa

#### A. Idafa Ma'anawiyya (إضافة معنوية)
- Mudaf is **not** a Sigha Sifat (صيغة صفت — i.e., not an Ism Fa'il, Ism Maf'ool, or Sifat Mushabbaha).
- Mudaf Ilayh is **not** the Ma'mool (معمول) of that Mudaf.
- A hidden Harf Jarr exists between Mudaf and Mudaf Ilayh.
- Examples: غُلَامُ زَيْدٍ، خَاتَمُ فِضَّةٍ، صَلَاةُ اللَّيْلِ

**How to identify the hidden Harf Jarr:**

| Hidden Harf Jarr | Condition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **مِنْ** | Mudaf Ilayh shares the same **Jins** (category/material) as Mudaf | خَاتَمُ فِضَّةٍ (ring **of** silver — same material) |
| **فِي** | Mudaf Ilayh is a **Zarf** (ظرف — container or time) for the Mudaf | صَلَاةُ اللَّيْلِ (prayer **in** the night) |
| **لَام** | Neither of the above applies | غُلَامُ زَيْدٍ (slave **of** Zaid) |

> Note: Knowing which hidden Harf Jarr applies does not change the meaning in translation; it is a theoretical distinction.

**Benefits of Idafa Ma'anawiyya:**
1. **Ta'reef (تعريف):** If Mudaf Ilayh is a **Ma'rifa** (definite), the Mudaf also becomes Ma'rifa.
   - e.g., قَلَمُ زَيْدٍ — قَلَم was Nakira; by being annexed to the proper noun زَيْد (Ma'rifa), it becomes definite ("Zaid's pen" — a specific, known pen).
2. **Takhsees (تخصيص):** If Mudaf Ilayh is a **Nakira** (indefinite), the Mudaf becomes "Khas" — a middle state between Nakira and Ma'rifa, gaining specification without full definiteness.
   - e.g., قَلَمُ رَجُلٍ — no longer completely unknown; narrows the owner to the category of men. This is why a Takhseez phrase can begin a nominal sentence despite being Nakira.

---

#### B. Idafa Lafziyya (إضافة لفظية)
- Mudaf **is** a Sigha Sifat (Ism Fa'il, Ism Maf'ool, or Sifat Mushabbaha).
- Mudaf Ilayh **is** the Ma'mool of that Mudaf (either its Fa'il or Maf'ool).
- The separation is **taqdiri** (conceptual only) — they are still logically two separate items.
- Example: ضَارِبُ زَيْدٍ (striker of Zaid) — ضَارِب is Ism Fa'il (Sifat), زَيْد is its Maf'ool.
- Another example: حَسَنُ الْوَجْهِ

**Benefit of Idafa Lafziyya:**
- **Takhfif (تخفيف) — lightness of pronunciation.** Dropping Tanween or Noon makes the phrase easier to articulate.
  - ضَارِبُ زَيْدٍ flows more smoothly than ضَارِبٌ زَيْدًا.

### 4. Mudaf toward Yaa' Mutakallim (یاء المتکلم)

When a Mudaf is annexed to the first-person Yaa' (my), specific morphological rules apply:

| Case | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Regular Ism Sahih** | Last letter of Mudaf becomes Maqsoor (ساکن) | وَلَدِي، قَلَمِي، بَيْتِي |
| **Ism Maqsoor** (ends in Alif) | Retain the Alif; give Fatha to the Yaa | عَصَايَ (preferred opinion) |
| **Ism Maqsoor — Huzail dialect** | Drop Alif, change to Yaa, merge | عَصَيَّ (not preferred) |
| **Ism Manqoos** (ends in Yaa, preceded by Kasra) | Two Yaas meet (both Sakin) → merge them, give Fatha to second Yaa | قَاضِيَّ (from قَاضِي + ي) — لِئَلَّا يَلْتَقِيَ السَّاكِنَانِ |
| **Jam' Muzakkar Salim** | 1) Drop Noon-e-Jam'. 2) Change Waw to Yaa. 3) Merge the two Yaas. | مُسْلِمِيَّ (from مُسْلِمُونَ: drop noon → مُسْلِمُو → change Waw to Yaa → merge + Kasra before Yaa) |
| **Asma' Sitta** (أخ، أب، حم، هن، فم) | Use short forms: أَخِي، أَبِي، حَمِي | أَخِي، أَبِي |
| **فَم (Famun)** | Waw (محذوف) is restored, then changed to Yaa, then merged | فِيَّ (فَم → فُو + ي → فَوَّ → فِيَّ) — because فَم is a two-letter root (ف + م); the Waw returns like it does in other deficient two-letter words |
| **ذو (Dhoo)** | Normally **cannot** be Mudaf toward a Zamir (pronoun) — only toward Ism Zahir | ذُو عِلْمٍ، ذُو مَالٍ (correct); ذُو + Zamir is rare/شاذ |

> **Why does Waw return for فَم but not for أَب/أَخ?** Because فَم is a two-letter root (the Waw was dropped to avoid a word of only two letters); أَب and أَخ are three-letter roots — their Waw does not come back in the same way.

## I'rab (Sentence Analysis) Examples

**Example 1: غُلَامُ زَيْدٍ**
- غُلَامُ: Mudaf, Marfoo' (subject context), no Tanween.
- زَيْدٍ: Mudaf Ilayh, Majroor, sign of Jarr is Kasra.
- Hidden Harf Jarr: **لَام** (since Zaid is not the same material/jins as Ghulam, and Zaid is not a Zarf).
- Type: **Idafa Ma'anawiyya**; benefit: Ta'reef (Ghulam becomes definite).

**Example 2: خَاتَمُ فِضَّةٍ**
- خَاتَمُ: Mudaf; فِضَّة: Mudaf Ilayh, Majroor.
- Hidden Harf Jarr: **مِنْ** (Fidda/silver is the same material/jins as a ring — the ring is made of it).
- Meaning: "a ring of silver" (made from silver).

**Example 3: صَلَاةُ اللَّيْلِ**
- صَلَاةُ: Mudaf; اللَّيْل: Mudaf Ilayh, Majroor.
- Hidden Harf Jarr: **فِي** (the night is a Zarf — the prayer occurs *in* the night).
- Meaning: "the night prayer."

**Example 4: ضَارِبُ زَيْدٍ**
- ضَارِبُ: Mudaf, Ism Fa'il (Sigha Sifat).
- زَيْدٍ: Mudaf Ilayh, Majroor; it is the **Maf'ool Bihi** (Ma'mool) of ضَارِب.
- Type: **Idafa Lafziyya**; benefit: Takhfif (lighter than saying ضَارِبٌ زَيْدًا).
- The separation is Taqdiri — conceptually: (ضَارِبٌ زَيْدًا).

**Example 5: قَلَمُ رَجُلٍ**
- قَلَمُ: Mudaf (was Nakira); رَجُلٍ: Mudaf Ilayh, Nakira, Majroor.
- Type: **Idafa Ma'anawiyya**; benefit: **Takhsees** (not fully definite, but no longer entirely unknown — narrows owner to men).

**Example 6: مُسْلِمُو الْهِنْدِ**
- مُسْلِمُو: Jam' Muzakkar Salim, Mudaf — Noon of Jam' dropped (نون الجمع محذوف).
- الْهِنْدِ: Mudaf Ilayh, Majroor.

**Example 7: قَاضِيَّ (from قَاضِي + ي)**
- قَاضِي: Ism Manqoos; Yaa at end (Sakin) + Yaa of Mutakallim (Sakin) → two Sakins meet → merge + Fatha → قَاضِيَّ.

## Key Takeaways

1. **Mudaf is always stripped** of Tanween, Noon-e-Tasniya, and Noon-e-Jam' Muzakkar Salim — these all stand in for Tanween and cannot coexist with Idafa.

2. **Two types of Idafa:** Ma'anawiyya (Mudaf is not Sifat; hidden Lam/Min/Fi) and Lafziyya (Mudaf is Sigha Sifat annexed to its own Ma'mool; benefit is pronunciation ease).

3. **Identifying the hidden Harf Jarr in Ma'anawiyya:** Same Jins → مِنْ; Mudaf Ilayh is Zarf → فِي; otherwise → لَام. The distinction is theoretical and does not change meaning.

4. **Idafa makes Nakira into Ma'rifa** (when Mudaf Ilayh is Ma'rifa) or **Takhsees** (when Mudaf Ilayh is Nakira). This is why Takhsees nouns can begin Jumlah Ismiyya — they have enough specification.

5. **Yaa' Mutakallim triggers morphological changes:** Ism Manqoos merges double Yaas → Fatha; Jam' Muzakkar Salim drops Noon then converts Waw to Yaa then merges; فَم restores its dropped Waw before the merger because it is a two-letter root.

6. **ذُو (Dhoo) cannot take a Zamir as Mudaf Ilayh** — it is always annexed to an Ism Zahir. Its rare use with a Zamir in poetry is Shaadh (irregular).
