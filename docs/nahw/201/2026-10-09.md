## Session Overview

This session served as a review of the eight categories of **Marfu'at** (nominative nouns), followed by an introduction to the twelve categories of **Mansubat** (accusative nouns). The bulk of the class focused on two specific Mansubat in detail: **Maf'ul Mutlaq** (the absolute/cognate object) and **Maf'ul Bihi** (the direct object). Key topics included the purposes of each grammatical form, rules for dropping the governing verb (fi'l), and conditions for reordering sentence elements.

### Maf'ul Mutlaq (المَفْعُول المُطْلَق)

**Definition:** A masdar (verbal noun) that carries the meaning of the verb mentioned before it.

**Three purposes:**

1. **Taqid (Emphasis)** — to emphasize the action
   - Example: ضَرَبْتُهُ ضَرْبًا — *I hit him (a real hitting)*

2. **Bayan al-Naw' (Describing the type/manner of action)**
   - جَلَسْتُ جِلْسَةَ الْمَلِكِ — *I sat like a king*
   - جَلَسْتُ جِلْسَةَ الطِّفْلِ — *I sat like a child*

3. **Bayan al-'Adad (Indicating the number of occurrences)**
   - جَلَسْتُ جَلْسَةً — *I sat once*
   - جَلَسْتُ جَلْسَتَيْنِ — *I sat twice*
   - جَلَسْتُ جَلَسَاتٍ — *I sat multiple times*

**Key rule — Maf'ul Mutlaq from a different root word:**
The masdar and the verb may come from different root words, provided the *meaning* is the same.
- Example: أَنْبَتَهُ نَبَاتًا — *He caused it to grow (a growing)*
  - أَنْبَتَ is a fi'l mazid (augmented verb); نَبَات is from the fi'l mujarrad — different words, same core meaning.

**Dropping the governing verb (fi'l):**

The verb can be dropped either:
- **Jawāzan (optionally)** — when context (qareena) makes the meaning clear
  - You see someone reading for a long time → you say: قِرَاءَةً طَوِيلَةً (the full sentence is implied)
  - When responding to a situation already understood: أَكْلًا كَثِيرًا
  - Saying خَيْرَ مَقْدَمٍ — actually means قَدِمْتَ قُدُومًا خَيْرًا; the verb and original masdar are dropped, and only the *sifa* (adjective) of the masdar remains

- **Wujūban (obligatorily)** — in specific sam'i (transmitted) cases where Arabs established the usage without the verb:
  - سَقْيًا — *May you be given to drink (rain/relief)*
  - شُكْرًا — *Thanks* (from شَكَرْتُكَ شُكْرًا — the verb is always dropped in use)

### Maf'ul Bihi (المَفْعُول بِهِ)

**Definition:** The noun upon which the action of the verb falls — the receiver of the action (the direct object).

- **Fa'il** = the doer of the action
- **Maf'ul Bihi** = the receiver of the action

**Example:** ضَرَبَ زَيْدٌ عَمْرًا — *Zaid hit Amr*
- زَيْدٌ = Fa'il (doer), مَرْفُوع
- عَمْرًا = Maf'ul Bihi (receiver), مَنْصُوب

**Inversion of word order (Taqdim al-Maf'ul):**
Normally the order is: verb → fa'il → maf'ul bihi. It is permissible to place the maf'ul before the fa'il:
- ضَرَبَ عَمْرًا زَيْدٌ (permissible)

**Condition:** This is only allowed when there is **clear i'rab** marking on both nouns — so the reader/listener cannot be confused about who did what. If i'rab is absent or ambiguous, the inversion is **not permitted**.

**Reasons for inversion:**
- Emphasis on the receiver (the maf'ul is the more important information)
- Natural flow of speech (the word came to tongue first)

**Dropping the governing verb (fi'l) for Maf'ul Bihi:**

Similar to Maf'ul Mutlaq, the verb can be dropped:

- **Jawāzan:** In response to a question when context is clear
  - Question: مَنْ أَضْرِبُ؟ (*Who should I hit?*)
  - Response: زَيْدًا (*[Hit] Zaid*)

- **Wujūban (Obligatorily):** Four specific situations (first two covered in this session):

  1. **Sam'i phrases** — phrases transmitted from the Arabs where the verb is always absent:
     - امْرَأً وَنَفْسَهُ — *Leave a man to himself* (meaning: اتْرُكْ امْرَأً وَنَفْسَهُ; the verb اتْرُكْ is never stated)

  2. **Quranic/Transmitted usage:**
     - وَانْتَهُوا خَيْرًا لَكُمْ — The full meaning is وَاقْصِدُوا خَيْرًا لَكُمْ; the verb وَاقْصِدُوا is dropped, and خَيْرًا is the maf'ul
     - خَيْرًا = tawḥid; meaning: *opt for tawḥid, it is better for you*

  3–4. (To be covered in the next session)

## I'rab (Sentence Analysis) Examples

**Example 1:** إِنَّ الْكِتَابَ جَمِيلٌ
- إِنَّ: Harf nasb wa tawkid (particle of emphasis), mabni
- الْكِتَابَ: Ism of inna, **mansoob**, sign: fatha
- جَمِيلٌ: Khabar of inna, **marfu'**, sign: damma

**Example 2:** كَانَ خَالِدٌ مَرِيضًا
- كَانَ: Fi'l mad, mabni
- خَالِدٌ: Ism of kana, **marfu'**, sign: damma
- مَرِيضًا: Khabar of kana, **mansoob**, sign: fatha

**Example 3:** ضَرَبَ زَيْدٌ عَمْرًا
- ضَرَبَ: Fi'l madi, mabni
- زَيْدٌ: Fa'il, **marfu'**, sign: damma
- عَمْرًا: Maf'ul bihi, **mansoob**, sign: fatha

**Example 4:** جَلَسْتُ جِلْسَةَ الْمَلِكِ
- جَلَسْتُ: Fi'l + fa'il (conjugated)
- جِلْسَةَ: Maf'ul mutlaq (bayan al-naw'), **mansoob**, sign: fatha (mudaf)
- الْمَلِكِ: Mudaf ilayhi, **majrur**, sign: kasra

**Example 5:** أَنْبَتَهُ اللَّهُ نَبَاتًا حَسَنًا (Quranic reference)
- أَنْبَتَهُ: Verb + maf'ul bihi (pronoun)
- اللَّهُ: Fa'il, **marfu'**
- نَبَاتًا: Maf'ul mutlaq from a different root (أَنْبَتَ / نَبَات), **mansoob**
- حَسَنًا: Sifa (adjective) of نَبَاتًا, **mansoob**

**Example 6:** وَانْتَهُوا خَيْرًا لَكُمْ (Quran 4:171)
- Full implied meaning: وَاقْصِدُوا خَيْرًا لَكُمْ
- خَيْرًا: Maf'ul bihi with its governing verb (وَاقْصِدُوا) dropped — **wujuban**
- Meaning: *Seek what is better for you (i.e., tawḥid)*

## Practice Examples

1. Identify the maf'ul mutlaq and its purpose (taqid / bayan naw' / bayan 'adad):
   - ضَرَبْتُهُ ضَرْبًا → **Taqid** (I hit him, a real hitting)
   - جَلَسْتُ جِلْسَةَ الطِّفْلِ → **Bayan al-Naw'** (I sat like a child)
   - زُرْتُ الْكَعْبَةَ زِيَارَتَيْنِ → **Bayan al-'Adad** (I visited the Ka'ba twice)

2. Identify whether the dropped verb is jawāzan or wujūban:
   - Someone is eating a lot; you remark: أَكْلًا كَثِيرًا → **Jawāzan** (context makes it clear)
   - شُكْرًا → **Wujūban Sam'i** (Arabs never state the verb with this masdar)
   - امْرَأً وَنَفْسَهُ → **Wujūban Sam'i** (the verb اتْرُكْ must be omitted)

3. Is this inversion permissible? ضَرَبَ عَمْرًا زَيْدٌ
   - Yes — both nouns have clear i'rab markers (عَمْرًا is mansoob = maf'ul; زَيْدٌ is marfu' = fa'il), so no ambiguity.

4. Explain أَهْلًا وَسَهْلًا grammatically:
   - Full meaning: أَتَيْتَ أَهْلًا وَبَاطَأْتَ سَهْلًا
   - أَهْلًا = Maf'ul bihi (mansoob), verb أَتَيْتَ dropped — wujūban (sam'i phrase)
   - سَهْلًا = Maf'ul bihi (mansoob), verb بَاطَأْتَ dropped
   - Meaning: *You have arrived among family, and you have walked on soft ground (ease)*

## Key Takeaways

1. **All eight Marfu'at completed.** Any noun in raf' belongs to one of eight categories: fa'il, na'ib al-fa'il, mubtada', khabar, ism of inna, ism of kana, ism of ma/la, or khabar of inna/ma/la.

2. **Twelve Mansubat to learn.** Four were covered in earlier sessions; this session introduced Maf'ul Mutlaq and Maf'ul Bihi in detail. The remaining six will follow.

3. **Maf'ul Mutlaq is always a masdar.** It can come from the same root as its verb or from a different root — the shared *meaning* is what matters. Its three functions are taqid, bayan al-naw', and bayan al-'adad.

4. **Dropping the fi'l** follows a two-tier rule: jawāzan (when qareena is present) or wujūban (in specific sam'i constructions or Quranic patterns). Understanding which cases require the drop — and which merely allow it — is essential.

5. **Maf'ul Bihi word order can be inverted,** but only when clear i'rab eliminates any ambiguity about who is the doer and who is the receiver.

6. **Sam'i phrases must be memorized as-is.** Constructions like شُكْرًا, سَقْيًا, and امْرَأً وَنَفْسَهُ are transmitted usage — you cannot re-insert the dropped verb.

7. **Next session:** Remaining two cases of wujubi hazf al-fi'l for Maf'ul Bihi, then onward through the remaining Mansubat.
