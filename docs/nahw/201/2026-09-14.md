## Grammar Rules Covered

### 1. Wazan al-Fi'l (وزن الفعل) as a Sabab — Review

The wazan of a word must be **khāṣṣ (exclusive) to the fi'l** for it to count as a sabab of Ghayr Munṣarif.

- When an ism (noun/name) is placed on a wazan that belongs exclusively to the fi'l (e.g., فَعَّل as in *Shammara*, or فُعِل as in *Dhuriba*), it carries **two asbāb**:
  1. **Wazan al-Fi'l** (وزن الفعل)
  2. **'Alamiyyah** (علمية — proper noun status)

### 2. Awzān Shared Between Fi'l and Ism

Some awzān are not exclusive to fi'l — they occur in both verbs and nouns. For the wazan to still qualify as a sabab, **two conditions** must be met simultaneously:

1. **The word must begin with one of the ḥurūf al-mudāra'ah** (أ / ي / ت / ن)
2. **The word must NOT accept tā' marbūṭah (ة)**

| Ḥarf Mudāra'ah | Example |
|---|---|
| Hamzah (أ) | أَحْمَد |
| Yā' (ي) | يَشْكُر |
| Tā' (ت) | تَغْلِي |
| Nūn (ن) | نَرْجِز |

**Counter-example:** *نَاقَةٌ يَعْمَلَةٌ* — this word begins with yā' but **accepts tā' marbūṭah**, therefore it is **munṣarif**.

### 3. Waṣf on the Pattern of Af'al (أفعل) — Colors and Permanent Qualities

- Adjectives (waṣf) on the pattern **أَفْعَل** — particularly colors — are **Ghayr Munṣarif**.
- Two asbāb: **Waṣf** + **Wazan Af'al**
- Example: أَبْيَض (white), أَحْمَر (red) — these represent a **waṣf dā'im** (permanent quality/color).
- Such a word cannot receive tanwīn.

### 4. Jam' Muntahal Jumū' (جمع منتهى الجموع) — Broken Plural

- Plurals on patterns such as **مَفَاعِل** are Ghayr Munṣarif.
- This is a **single sabab that counts as two** (it is itself the equivalent of a double cause).
- Example: مَصَابِيح (on the pattern of مَفَاعِيل) — Ghayr Munṣarif.
- Qur'anic evidence: **وَلَقَدْ زَيَّنَّا السَّمَاءَ الدُّنْيَا بِمَصَابِيحَ** — the word appears without kasrah or tanwīn, confirming it is Ghayr Munṣarif.

### 5. Alif Nūn Zā'idatān (ألف نون زائدتان)

- When a proper noun contains an **alif-nūn that are extra letters** (not part of the original root), this counts as a sabab.
- Combined with **'Alamiyyah**, it produces two asbāb → Ghayr Munṣarif.
- Example: سَلْمَان — root is sin-lam (سلم); the alif-nūn are zā'idah (extra).
- The word is in a majrūr state grammatically, but since it is Ghayr Munṣarif, it shows **fatḥah** instead of kasrah.

### 6. Ta'nīth Lafẓī vs. Ta'nīth Ma'nawī in Proper Nouns

- **Ta'nīth lafẓī**: femininity by form (e.g., the word carries tā' marbūṭah or alif maqṣūrah) even if the referent is masculine.
- **Ta'nīth ma'nawī**: femininity by meaning (the referent is female).
- **Ṭalḥah (طلحة)**: carries tā' marbūṭah → ta'nīth lafẓī; by meaning (*ma'nan*) it refers to a male → **mudhakkar**.
  - Verb agreement follows the meaning: **جَاءَ طَلْحَةُ** (not جَاءَتْ), matching the mudhakkar referent.
  - This parallels how **مَلَائِكَة** is used in the Qur'an with a mudhakkar verb despite the tā' marbūṭah.
- When assessing Ghayr Munṣarif, **ta'nīth lafẓī still counts** as a sabab even when the meaning is mudhakkar.

### 7. 'Ajam ('عجمة) vs. Ta'nīth as Asbāb for Maryam

- **مَرْيَم** carries two asbāb: **'Alamiyyah** + **Ta'nīth** (ma'nawī — it refers to a female).
- Optionally, **'Ujmah** (عجمة — non-Arabic/foreign origin; Maryam is a Hebrew word) can also be cited.
- However, **ta'nīth is considered stronger** than 'ujmah in this case, so ta'nīth is the preferred second sabab.
- Note: citing 'ujmah is not incorrect, but ta'nīth takes precedence.

### 8. Effect of Alif-Lam on Ghayr Munṣarif

- When **alif-lam (ال)** is added to a Ghayr Munṣarif word, it **removes the Ghayr Munṣarif status** — the word then accepts kasrah normally.
- Example: **الْمَدَارِس** — with alif-lam, it takes kasrah in majrūr position: **بِالْمَدَارِسِ الإِسْلَامِيَّةِ** ✓

### 9. Upcoming Chapter Preview — Marfū'āt, Manṣūbāt, Majrūrāt

- Next sessions will cover all the positions in which an ism becomes:
  - **Marfū'** (مرفوع) — nominative
  - **Manṣūb** (منصوب) — accusative
  - **Majrūr** (مجرور) — genitive

## I'rab (Sentence Analysis) Examples

### Example 1: مَرَرْتُ بِالْمَدَارِسِ الإِسْلَامِيَّةِ
- **مَدَارِس** is Jam' Muntahal Jumū' → normally Ghayr Munṣarif.
- However, with **ال (alif-lam)** attached, it reverts to full declension and accepts kasrah.
- **بِالْمَدَارِسِ** is correct — majrūr with kasrah because of alif-lam.
- Verdict: ✓ Correct

### Example 2: (A sentence containing مَرْيَم)
- **مَرْيَم** — Ghayr Munṣarif
- Asbāb:
  1. **'Alamiyyah** (proper noun)
  2. **Ta'nīth ma'nawī** (refers to a female — primary and stronger sabab)
  3. **'Ujmah** may also be cited (Hebrew origin) — valid but secondary to ta'nīth
- In majrūr position, takes fatḥah instead of kasrah.

### Example 3: (A sentence containing أَحْمَر / أَبْيَض)
- **أَبْيَض / أَحْمَر** — Ghayr Munṣarif
- Asbāb:
  1. **Waṣf** (adjective)
  2. **Wazan Af'al** (on the pattern أَفْعَل)
- These are colors → **waṣf dā'im** → confirmed Ghayr Munṣarif.
- No tanwīn is given.

### Example 4: (A sentence containing طَلْحَة)
- **طَلْحَة** — Ghayr Munṣarif
- Asbāb:
  1. **'Alamiyyah** (proper noun)
  2. **Ta'nīth lafẓī** (has tā' marbūṭah, even though the meaning is mudhakkar)
- Verb agreement: **جَاءَ طَلْحَةُ** (mudhakkar verb, not jā'at) — meaning governs the verb.

### Example 5: اِلْتَقَيْتُ بِسَلْمَانَ فِي الْمَسْجِدِ قَبْلَ أُسْبُوعٍ
- **سَلْمَان** — Ghayr Munṣarif
- Asbāb:
  1. **'Alamiyyah** (proper noun)
  2. **Alif Nūn Zā'idatān** (the alif-nūn are extra; root = سلم)
- **بِسَلْمَانَ**: majrūr position, but takes **fatḥah** (بِسَلْمَانَ), not kasrah — due to Ghayr Munṣarif.
- Verdict: بِسَلْمَانَ ✓ (not بِسَلْمَانِ ✗)

### Example 6: (A sentence containing مَصَابِيح)
- **مَصَابِيح** — Ghayr Munṣarif
- Pattern: **مَفَاعِيل** → Jam' Muntahal Jumū'
- One sabab = equivalent of two → Ghayr Munṣarif.
- Qur'anic confirmation: **بِمَصَابِيحَ** (Sūrat al-Mulk) — fatḥah, no tanwīn.

### Example 7: (A sentence containing سَلْمَى)
- **سَلْمَى** — Ghayr Munṣarif
- Asbāb:
  1. **'Alamiyyah** (proper noun)
  2. **Ta'nīth lafẓī** — by **alif maqṣūrah** (ى at end), which is a marker of femininity by form

## Key Takeaways

1. **Wazan al-Fi'l as sabab** requires the wazan to be exclusive to fi'l. If shared with ism, the word must begin with a ḥarf mudāra'ah **and** not accept tā' marbūṭah for the wazan condition to hold.

2. **Alif-lam overrides Ghayr Munṣarif** — any Ghayr Munṣarif word preceded by ال becomes fully declinable and accepts kasrah in majrūr position.

3. **Jam' Muntahal Jumū' is self-sufficient** — it is a single sabab that counts as two, so no second sabab is needed.

4. **Alif Nūn Zā'idatān** must be confirmed as truly extra letters (not part of the root) before applying this condition.

5. **Ta'nīth lafẓī counts for Ghayr Munṣarif** even when the meaning is mudhakkar — the form of the word is decisive for this rule, even if the verb agreement follows the meaning.

6. **When identifying asbāb for 'ajam names**, both 'ujmah and ta'nīth may apply, but ta'nīth is typically the stronger and preferred second sabab for female names.

7. **Alif maqṣūrah (ى) is a form of ta'nīth lafẓī** — proper nouns ending in alif maqṣūrah (e.g., سَلْمَى، لَيْلَى) are Ghayr Munṣarif on the asbāb of 'alamiyyah + ta'nīth lafẓī.
