## Session Overview

This session covered two major topics in Arabic grammar: (1) a review and practice of **Fi'l Majhul** (passive voice) with conversion exercises, and (2) the beginning of **Fasl al-Mubtada wal-Khabar** — the chapter on the nominal sentence. Students practiced converting active sentences to passive, and the teacher introduced the rules governing Mubtada and Khabar, including the conditions under which a Nakira (indefinite noun) may serve as Mubtada.

---

## Grammar Rules Covered

### 1. Fi'l Majhul (Passive Voice) — Review & Practice

**Rule:** To convert a Fi'l Ma'roof (active verb) into Fi'l Majhul (passive verb):
- Drop the Fa'il (subject/doer)
- Bring the Maf'ul (object) into the place of the Fa'il
- Give the Fa'il's I'rab (Raf') to the Maf'ul — it is now called **Na'ib al-Fa'il**
- Na'ib al-Fa'il is always **Marfu'**

**Agreement Rule:** The Fi'l Majhul must agree in gender with the Na'ib al-Fa'il:
- If Na'ib al-Fa'il is Mudhakkar → Fi'l is Mudhakkar
- If Na'ib al-Fa'il is Mu'annath → Fi'l is Mu'annath

**Example:** *Akalat* (Mu'annath verb — active) becomes *Ukila* (Mudhakkar verb — passive), because the Na'ib al-Fa'il (*at-Ta'am*) is Mudhakkar.

---

### 2. Marfu'at — Overview

The session transitioned to Marfu'at (nouns in the nominative state). Previously covered:
1. **Fa'il** — always Marfu'
2. **Na'ib al-Fa'il** — always Marfu'

Now beginning:
3. **Mubtada**
4. **Khabar**

> Unlike Fa'il and Na'ib al-Fa'il, Mubtada and Khabar have no **Lafzi 'Amil** (explicit grammatical agent causing Raf'). Their 'Amil is **Ma'nawi** — it is **Al-Ibtida'** (the act of beginning a nominal sentence) itself.

---

### 3. Fasl al-Mubtada wal-Khabar

**Definitions:**
- **Mubtada** = the first of two nouns in a nominal sentence; it is **Musnad Ilayh** (what is being spoken about)
- **Khabar** = the second noun; it is **Musnad** (what is being said about the Mubtada)
- They are **Lazim-Malzum** (inseparable counterparts): every Mubtada has a Khabar and vice versa

**General Rule:**
- Mubtada should be **Ma'rifa** (definite)
- Khabar should be **Nakira** (indefinite)

*Example:* زَيْدٌ إِمَامٌ — Zaidun is Mubtada (Ma'rifa), Imamun is Khabar (Nakira)

---

### 4. Conditions Allowing Nakira to Become Mubtada

Normally Mubtada must be Ma'rifa, but Nakira may serve as Mubtada under the following conditions:

**Condition 1: Nakira receives a Sifa (adjective/description)**
- When a Nakira noun is described (has a Sifat), it becomes **Nakira Mukhassasa** — closer to Ma'rifa
- *Example:* وَلَعَبْدٌ مُؤْمِنٌ خَيْرٌ مِنْ مُشْرِكٍ
  - *Abd* is Nakira, but with Sifat *Mu'min*, it becomes Mubtada
  - *Abd* = Mawsuf, *Mu'min* = Sifat, together they form the Mubtada

**Condition 2: Nakira comes between Hamza Istifham and *Am***
- *Example:* أَرَجُلٌ فِي الدَّارِ أَمِ امْرَأَةٌ
  - *Rajulun* = Ma'tuf 'Alayh, *Imra'atun* = Ma'tuf
  - Nakira framed by interrogative *Hamza* and *Am* becomes Nakira Mukhassasa

**Condition 3: Nakira comes after *Ma Nafiya***
- *Example:* مَا أَحَدٌ خَيْرٌ مِنْكَ
  - *Ma* = Nafiya, *Ahadun* = Mubtada (Nakira), *Khairun minka* = Khabar

**Condition 4: Nakira has a hidden (Mahdhuf) Sifat**
- *Example:* شَرٌّ أَهَرَّ ذَا نَابٍ
  - *Sharrun* has an implied Sifat (*'Azim*), making it Nakira Mukhassasa → Mubtada
  - *Aharra* = Khabar (Fi'l + Fa'il + Maf'ul together functioning as Khabar)

**Condition 5: Khabar is a Zarf (adverb of place/time) and Muqaddam (fronted)**
- When Khabar is a Zarf and placed before the Mubtada, and the Mubtada is Nakira — this is valid
- *Example:* وَفِي الدَّارِ رَجُلٌ
  - *Fiddar* = Khabar (Zarf, Muqaddam), *Rajulun* = Mubtada (Nakira, Mu'akhkhar)
- The default rule: when Khabar is a Zarf and Mubtada is Nakira, Khabar **must** come first (Muqaddam) and Mubtada comes last (Mu'akhkhar)

**Condition 6: Nakira is Mudhaf to a Ma'rifa (implied)**
- *Example:* سَلَامِي عَلَيْكَ (*Salaamun 'Alayk* → *Salami 'Alayk*)
  - *Salami* = Mudhaf to *Ya* (Adiyya), and Mudhaf is Ma'rifa — so it is eligible for Mubtada

---

### 5. Choosing Mubtada/Khabar When Both Nouns Are the Same Type

**When one is Ma'rifa and one is Nakira:**
- Fixed: Ma'rifa = Mubtada, Nakira = Khabar (no choice)
- *Example:* مُحَمَّدٌ ﷺ نَبِيٌّ — Muhammad (Ma'rifa) = Mubtada, Nabiyyun (Nakira) = Khabar

**When both are Ma'rifa:**
- Either may be Mubtada, either may be Khabar — it is the speaker's choice
- *Examples:*
  - اللهُ إِلٰهُنَا — Either *Allah* or *Ilahuna* can be Mubtada
  - آدَمُ أَبُونَا — Either can be Mubtada

## I'rab (Sentence Analysis) Examples

### Example 1: أُكِلَ الطَّعَامُ
*Ukilat-ta'amu* (The food was eaten)

| Word | I'rab |
|---|---|
| أُكِلَ | Fi'l Majhul, Madhi |
| الطَّعَامُ | Na'ib al-Fa'il, Marfu', 'Alamat Raf' Damma |

---

### Example 2: نُصِحَتْ عَائِشَةُ
*Nusihat 'A'ishatu* (Aisha was advised)

| Word | I'rab |
|---|---|
| نُصِحَتْ | Fi'l Majhul, Madhi, Mu'annath (because Na'ib al-Fa'il is Mu'annath) |
| عَائِشَةُ | Na'ib al-Fa'il, Marfu', 'Alamat Raf' Damma |

> *Note:* Original active was *Naṣaḥa Khālidun 'Ā'ishata*. When made passive, Khalidun (Fa'il) is dropped, 'A'isha moves to Na'ib al-Fa'il position and takes Raf', verb becomes Mu'annath to match.

---

### Example 3: طُبِخَ اللَّحْمُ
*Tubiḥa al-laḥmu* (The meat was cooked)

| Word | I'rab |
|---|---|
| طُبِخَ | Fi'l Majhul, Madhi |
| اللَّحْمُ | Na'ib al-Fa'il, Marfu' |

---

### Example 4: بُنِيَتِ الْمَسَاجِدُ
*Buniyat al-masājidu* (The mosques were built)

| Word | I'rab |
|---|---|
| بُنِيَتْ | Fi'l Majhul, Madhi, Mu'annath (Jama' Muqassar of Ghayr 'Aqil treated as Mu'annath Wahida — though Mudhakkar also valid) |
| الْمَسَاجِدُ | Na'ib al-Fa'il, Marfu' |

---

### Example 5: زَيْدٌ إِمَامٌ
*Zaidun imāmun* (Zayd is an imam)

| Word | I'rab |
|---|---|
| زَيْدٌ | Mubtada, Marfu', Ma'rifa |
| إِمَامٌ | Khabar, Marfu', Nakira |

---

### Example 6: وَلَعَبْدٌ مُؤْمِنٌ خَيْرٌ مِنْ مُشْرِكٍ
*Wa la-'abdun mu'minun khairun min mushrikin*

| Word | I'rab |
|---|---|
| عَبْدٌ | Mubtada, Nakira Mukhassasa (due to Sifat *Mu'min*), Marfu' |
| مُؤْمِنٌ | Sifat of 'Abd |
| خَيْرٌ | Khabar, Marfu' |
| مِنْ مُشْرِكٍ | Jar-Majrur, Muta'alliq bil-Khabar |

---

### Example 7: شَرٌّ أَهَرَّ ذَا نَابٍ
*Sharrun aharra dhā nābin* (A great evil provoked the fanged one — i.e., the dog)

| Word | I'rab |
|---|---|
| شَرٌّ | Mubtada, Nakira (Sifat *'Azim* is hidden/Mahdhuf) |
| أَهَرَّ | Khabar (Fi'l) |
| هُوَ (hidden) | Fa'il (Damir) |
| ذَا نَابٍ | Maf'ul Bihi |

---

### Example 8: وَفِي الدَّارِ رَجُلٌ
*Wa fid-dāri rajulun* (And in the house is a man)

| Word | I'rab |
|---|---|
| فِي الدَّارِ | Khabar Muqaddam (Zarf, fronted) |
| رَجُلٌ | Mubtada Mu'akhkhar, Nakira (valid because Khabar is Zarf and Muqaddam) |

## Key Takeaways

1. **Na'ib al-Fa'il is always Marfu'** — when converting active to passive, the object replaces the subject and takes Raf'.

2. **Fi'l Majhul must agree in gender with Na'ib al-Fa'il**, not the original Fa'il — this can change the gender of the verb from the active form.

3. **Mubtada and Khabar are Marfu' without any Lafzi 'Amil** — their cause of Raf' is Ma'nawi: the *Ibtida'* (the state of beginning a nominal sentence) itself.

4. **Default rule: Mubtada = Ma'rifa, Khabar = Nakira** — but Nakira can become Mubtada under six specific conditions (Sifat, Hamza + Am, Ma Nafiya, hidden Sifat, Muqaddam Zarf Khabar, Mudhaf structure).

5. **When both nouns are Ma'rifa, either may be Mubtada** — there is no fixed rule; the speaker chooses based on what they intend to speak *about*.

6. **When Khabar is a Zarf and Mubtada is Nakira, the Khabar must come first** (Muqaddam) — the Mubtada comes last (Mu'akhkhar). This is the standard word order in such sentences.

7. **Nahw requires sustained practice above all other subjects** — the teacher strongly emphasized spending more time on Nahw than other subjects. Mastery of Nahw enables independent reading of any Islamic text (Tafsir, Fiqh, Hadith), while weakness in Nahw creates permanent dependence on explanatory notes and teachers.
