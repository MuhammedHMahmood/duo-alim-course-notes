## Grammar Rules Covered

### 1. Kalima vs. Kalam

- **Kalima** (كَلِمَة) — a single word. It is a lafz (utterance) designed to denote a specific, singular meaning.
- **Kalam** (كَلَام) — two or more words that come together in proper order to form a meaningful sentence/phrase.
- Example: *Zaidun* alone = Kalima. *Zaidun maridun* (Zaid is sick) = Kalam.
- Kalam is **murakkab** (compound); Kalima is **mufrad** (single).
- The entire discipline of Nahw revolves around rules pertaining to these two things.

---

### 2. The Three Types of Kalima

Every single word (Kalima) falls into one of three categories:

| Type | Arabic | Characteristic |
|---|---|---|
| Ism | اِسْم | Denotes meaning on its own; no tense |
| Fi'l | فِعْل | Denotes meaning on its own; tense is embedded |
| Harf | حَرْف | Does NOT denote meaning on its own; requires support |

---

### 3. Definition of Harf (حَرْف)

- **لِأَنَّهَا إِمَّا أَلَّا تَدُلَّ عَلَى مَعْنًى فِي نَفْسِهَا وَهُوَ الْحَرْفُ**
- Harf is that which does **not** convey its meaning on its own — it always requires an accompanying word.
- Example: *Ilā* (إِلَى) alone is meaningless. *Ilā al-masjid* conveys meaning.

---

### 4. Definition of Fi'l (فِعْل)

- **أَوْ تَدُلَّ عَلَى مَعْنًى فِي نَفْسِهَا وَيَقْتَرِنُ مَعْنَاهَا بِأَحَدِ الْأَزْمِنَةِ الثَّلَاثَةِ وَهُوَ الْفِعْلُ**
- Fi'l denotes meaning on its own AND carries one of the three tenses embedded within it.
- Three tenses: **Māḍī** (past) | **Ḥāl** (present) | **Istiqbāl** (future)
- Examples: *Dhahaba* (ذَهَبَ) = past | *Yadhabu* (يَذْهَبُ) = present/future

---

### 5. Definition of Ism (اِسْم)

- **فَحَدُّ الِاسْمِ أَنَّهُ كَلِمَةٌ تَدُلُّ عَلَى مَعْنًى فِي نَفْسِهَا غَيْرُ مُقْتَرِنٍ بِأَحَدِ الْأَزْمِنَةِ الثَّلَاثَةِ**
- Ism is a word that denotes meaning on its own but is **not** associated with any of the three tenses (past, present, future).
- It evokes an image or concept in the mind without implying when.
- Examples: *Al-kitāb* (book), *Al-masjid* (mosque), *Al-madrasah* (school), *Al-walad* (boy), *Al-sayyāra* (car)

---

### 6. Alamat of Ism (Signs that Identify a Noun)

These are markers — if any one of them is present on a word, it is confirmed to be an Ism. Fi'l and Harf **never** carry these markers.

| # | Alamah (Sign) | Explanation | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Al-Ikhbār** (الإِخْبَار) | Can have information given *about* it (mubtada) OR can itself *be* information (khabar) | *Zaidun qā'imun* — Zaid = mubtada; qā'im = khabar |
| 2 | **Iḍāfah** (الإِضَافَة) | Can serve as Muḍāf (the first word in a possessive construction) | Only Ism can be Muḍāf |
| 3 | **Alif-Lām** (ال) | The definite article attaches only to Ism | *Al-rajul*, *al-kitāb* |
| 4 | **Jarr / Kasra** (الجَرّ) | Can take kasra (the i-vowel) due to a preposition or iḍāfah | Fi'l *never* takes kasra or tanwīn |
| 5 | **Tanwīn** (التَّنْوِين) | The nunation suffix (-un, -an, -in) appears only on Ism | *Sayyāratun*, *kitābun* |
| 6 | **Taṣniyah** (التَّثْنِيَة) | Can be made dual | *Kitābāni* (two books) |
| 7 | **Jamʿ** (الجَمْع) | Can be made plural | *Kutub* (books) |
| 8 | **Ṣifah / Naʿt** (الصِّفَة) | Can function as or receive an adjective | Only Ism can be described |
| 9 | **Taṣghīr** (التَّصْغِير) | Can be diminutivized (made to mean "small X") | *Waladun* → *Wulaid* (little boy); *Rajulun* → *Rujail* (small man) |
| 10 | **Nidāʾ** (النِّدَاء) | Can be called upon with *Yā* (يَا) | *Yā Zaid*, *Yā Bakr* — *Yā* never precedes Fi'l |

> **Key point:** Fi'l never carries jarr, kasra, or tanwīn. You will never read *dhahabun*, *dhahaban*, or *dhahabin*. This alone can confirm a word is Ism.

## Iʿrāb (Sentence Analysis) Examples

### Example 1: Kalima vs. Kalam
- *Zaidun* — **Kalima** (single word, one unit)
- *Zaidun maridun* — **Kalām** (complete sentence: "Zaid is sick")
  - *Zaidun* = Mubtadaʾ (subject), marfūʿ, sign: ḍammah
  - *Maridun* = Khabar (predicate), marfūʿ, sign: ḍammah

### Example 2: Identifying Ism via Alif-Lām
- *Al-qiṭār* (القِطَار) — has Alif-Lām → confirmed **Ism** (no further analysis needed)

### Example 3: Identifying Ism via Kasra and Tanwīn
- *Sayyāratun* (سَيَّارَةٌ) — has tanwīn → confirmed **Ism**
- *Sayyāratin* (سَيَّارَةٍ) — has kasra → confirmed **Ism** (Fi'l never takes kasra)

### Example 4: Ikhbār (information about and with)
- *Zaidun qāʾimun* (زَيْدٌ قَائِمٌ) — "Zaid is standing"
  - Information is given **about** Zaid → Zaid is Ism (mubtadaʾ)
  - *Qāʾimun* is the information itself → Qāʾim is also Ism (khabar)

### Example 5: Harf needing support
- *Ilā* alone — no meaning conveyed
- *Ilā al-masjid* — "to the mosque" — meaning now conveyed with support

## Practice Examples

**Identify whether each word is Ism, Fiʿl, or Ḥarf, and state the evidence:**

1. **ذَهَبَ** (*Dhahaba*) — **Fiʿl** (past tense embedded; conveys meaning alone)
2. **يَذْهَبُ** (*Yadhabu*) — **Fiʿl** (present/future tense)
3. **إِلَى** (*Ilā*) — **Ḥarf** (cannot convey meaning alone)
4. **الْكِتَابُ** (*Al-kitābu*) — **Ism** (has Alif-Lām)
5. **وَلَدٌ** (*Waladun*) — **Ism** (has tanwīn)
6. **وُلَيْد** (*Wulaid*) — **Ism** (is a taṣghīr form; only Ism can be diminutivized)
7. **كِتَابَيْنِ** (*Kitābaini*) — **Ism** (dual form / taṣniyah)
8. **كُتُب** (*Kutub*) — **Ism** (jamʿ / plural)
9. **يَا زَيْد** (*Yā Zaid*) — *Yā* is Ḥarf Nidāʾ; *Zaid* is **Ism** (Yā only precedes Ism)
10. **سَيَّارَةٍ** (*Sayyāratin*) — **Ism** (kasra; Fi'l never takes kasra)

## Key Takeaways

1. **Nahw's core subject matter is Kalima and Kalām** — every grammatical rule connects back to either a single word or a compound sentence.

2. **Three and only three types of Kalima exist:** Ism (noun), Fiʿl (verb), Ḥarf (particle). Every Arabic word belongs to one of these.

3. **The defining difference between Ism and Fiʿl** is tense — both convey meaning independently, but Fiʿl carries tense (past/present/future) while Ism does not.

4. **Ḥarf is structurally dependent** — it cannot convey meaning without attaching to another word.

5. **Alamāt are your shortcut** — you do not need to know a word's dictionary meaning to classify it. Seeing Alif-Lām, tanwīn, kasra, taṣniyah, jamʿ, taṣghīr, or Yā before a word instantly confirms it is Ism. No analysis required.

6. **Fi'l never takes jarr, kasra, or tanwīn** — this is an absolute rule. If any of these appear on a word, it is definitively Ism.

7. **Yā (Ḥarf Nidāʾ) only precedes Ism** — if you see Yā, the next word is always Ism.

8. **Digestion over speed** — the ustadh emphasized reviewing and memorizing these alamāt thoroughly before advancing, as everything in Nahw builds on this foundation.
