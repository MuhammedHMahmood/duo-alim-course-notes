    Nahw 201 - Study Notes

    Date: 2026-08-10

    Session Overview

    This session covered the definitions and distinguishing markers (alamat) of fi'l (verb) and harf (particle), completing the chapter on the three parts of speech (kalima). The session concluded with an introduction to kalam (meaningful sentence), covering the concepts of isnad, musnad, and musnad ilayh, and the distinction between jumlah ismiyyah and jumlah fi'liyyah.

    Grammar Rules Covered

    1. Definition of Fi'l (الفعل)

    الفعل كلمة تدل على معنى في نفسها

    A fi'l is a word that: 1. Denotes a meaning on its own, AND 2. Has a tense (زمان) associated with it

    The three tenses of fi'l: - ماضي (past) — e.g. ضَرَبَ - مضارع (present/future) — e.g. يَضْرِبُ - أمر (command) — e.g. اضْرِبْ

    2. Alamat (Distinguishing Markers) of Fi'l

    A. أن يصح الإخبار به لا عنه A fi'l can only be used as ikhbar bihi (giving information WITH it), never ikhbar anhu (giving information ABOUT it). - Ism can be both: e.g. زَيْدٌ نَائِمٌ (information is given about Zaid) - Fi'l is always information itself: e.g. ضَرَبَ زَيْدٌ — here ضَرَبَ conveys the information, it is not what information is being given about

    B. دخول قد قد enters only upon fi'l, never upon ism. - قَدْ ذَهَبَ — confirms the word is fi'l - Quranic example: قَدْ أَفْلَحَ مَن تَزَكَّى (Al-A'la) — قد here adds taqid (emphasis/certainty) - On mudari': قد gives the meaning of sometimes/occasionally (e.g. "Do you visit Hamid every day?" → "Sometimes.")

    C. دخول السين وسوف - سَيَعْلَمُ — سين upon a word confirms it is fi'l (future) - سَوْفَ يَعْلَمُ — same function

    D. الجزم - Jazm (sukun or deletion of nun) comes only upon fi'l - e.g. لَمْ يَذْهَبْ — jazm confirms the word is fi'l

    E. التصريف إلى الماضي والمضارع - The ability to change a word between madhi and mudari' forms is exclusive to fi'l - Ism does not undergo this type of change

    F. كونه أمرًا أو نهيًا - Only fi'l becomes amr (command) or nahi (prohibition) - e.g. كُلْ، اذْخُلْ، اسْجُدْ، لَا تَسْجُدْ، لَا تَأْكُلْ

    G. اتصال الضمائر البارزة المرفوعة - Attached subject pronouns (تاء المتكلم، تاء المخاطب، etc.) connect only to fi'l - e.g. ضَرَبْتَ، ضَرَبْتُ، ضَرَبْتِ، أَكَلْتَ، أَكَلْتُ

    H. تاء التأنيث الساكنة - The silent feminine ta (ـَتْ) attaches only to fi'l - e.g. ضَرَبَتْ، أَكَلَتْ

    I. نون التوكيد - Nun of emphasis attaches only to fi'l, adding taqid - Two types: - ثقيلة (heavy/mushaddad): لَأَزِيدَنَّكُمْ - خفيفة (light): لَيَذْهَبَنْ - Quranic example: لَئِن شَكَرْتُمْ لَأَزِيدَنَّكُمْ (Ibrahim:7) — the shadda nun adds certainty to Allah's promise

    3. Definition of Harf (الحرف)

    الحرف كلمة لا تدل على معنى في نفسها بل تدل على معنى في غيرها

    A harf is a word that does not denote a meaning on its own; it only conveys meaning through another word. - e.g. مِن — its meaning is ibtida' (starting point), but this meaning is only realized when the starting point is mentioned: مِنَ البَصْرَةِ

    Alamat of Harf: - لا يصح الإخبار عنه ولا به — you cannot give information about it, nor with it - لا يقبل علامات الأسماء — does not accept the markers of ism - لا يقبل علامات الفعل — does not accept the markers of fi'l

    Why is it called harf? Harf means tarf (edge/corner) — it is at the periphery of kalam, not a primary pillar. It is not maqsood bidhatihi (independently intended); it supports and connects, but ism and fi'l are the pillars.

    Roles of Harf in Arabic (فوائده): 1. الربط بين الاسمين — connecting two nouns: e.g. زَيْدٌ فِي الدَّارِ (fi connects Zaid and the house) 2. الربط بين الفعلين — connecting two verbs: e.g. with عَطْف particles 3. الربط بين الجملتين — connecting two sentences: e.g. إِن جَاءَنِي زَيْدٌ أَكْرَمْتُهُ — without إِن, these are two disconnected statements; with إِن, they form one conditional meaning

    4. Definition and Nature of Kalam (الكلام)

    Kalam is a meaningful utterance that contains isnad — the attribution of something (musnad) toward someone or something (musnad ilayh).

    الإسناد: نسبة يُحدث الكلمتين إلى الأخرى — the connection established between two words whereby one is attributed to the other

    Key principle: Simply placing two words together does not form kalam. What makes kalam is isnad.

    Musnad (المسند): That which is attributed — the predicate/verb (the information being conveyed)

    Musnad Ilayh (المسند إليه): That toward which something is attributed — the subject (the one being spoken about)

    Examples: | Sentence | Musnad | Musnad Ilayh | |---|---|---| | ضَرَبَ زَيْدٌ | ضَرَبَ | زَيْدٌ | | أَكَلَتْ فَاطِمَةُ | أَكَلَتْ | فَاطِمَةُ | | قَرَأَ حَامِدٌ | قَرَأَ | حَامِدٌ | | زَيْدٌ جَالِسٌ | جَالِسٌ | زَيْدٌ |

    Alamat that isnad is present: The listener (mukhatab) receives a complete message (fa'idah tammah) and the speaker's silence afterward is valid (yasihhu as-sukut alayha). If the listener is still waiting for more information, isnad is incomplete.

    Counter-example: زَيْدٌ حَامِدٌ — if the speaker goes silent, the listener waits: what about them? No musnad is established → not valid kalam until a khabar is added: زَيْدٌ حَامِدٌ نَائِمٌ.

    5. Types of Jumlah (الجملة)

    Kalam (meaningful sentence) forms in two ways:

    Type Structure Example
    جملة اسمية ism + ism (musnad ilayh + musnad) زَيْدٌ قَائِمٌ
    جملة فعلية fi'l + ism قَامَ زَيْدٌ

    Musnad and musnad ilayh exist only within these two structures. Every valid kalam must contain both.

    6. Objection and Response: يا زيد

    Objection: "Ya Zayd" (يَا زَيْدُ) is a valid sentence but contains only a harf (يا) and an ism (زيد) — where is musnad and musnad ilayh?"

    Answer: حرف النداء قائم مقام الفعل — the harf of nida' stands in the place of a fi'l. يَا زَيْدُ in meaning is: أَدْعُو زَيْدًا or أَطْلُبُ زَيْدًا ("I am calling Zaid" / "I am seeking Zaid"). So by meaning, يَا is a fi'l. The rule is not broken — one fi'l (in meaning) + one ism = valid kalam.

    I'rab (Sentence Analysis) Examples

    1. ضَرَبَ زَيْدٌ - ضَرَبَ: fi'l madi, musnad - زَيْدٌ: ism, fa'il (agent), musnad ilayh, marfu' with damm

    2. زَيْدٌ جَالِسٌ - زَيْدٌ: ism, mubtada', musnad ilayh, marfu' - جَالِسٌ: khabar, musnad, marfu'

    3. قَرَأَ حَامِدٌ - قَرَأَ: fi'l madi, musnad - حَامِدٌ: fa'il, musnad ilayh, marfu'

    4. لَمْ يَذْهَبْ - لَمْ: harf jazm (nafi) - يَذْهَبْ: fi'l mudari' majzum — the jazm proves it is fi'l

    5. أَكَلَتْ فَاطِمَةُ - أَكَلَتْ: fi'l madi — the ـَتْ (ta ta'nith sakina) confirms it is fi'l; musnad - فَاطِمَةُ: fa'il, musnad ilayh, marfu'

    6. قَدْ أَفْلَحَ مَن تَزَكَّى (Al-A'la: 14) - قَدْ: harf taqid — confirms أَفْلَحَ is fi'l - أَفْلَحَ: fi'l madi, musnad - مَن: ism mawsul, musnad ilayh, fa'il

    7. لَئِن شَكَرْتُمْ لَأَزِيدَنَّكُمْ (Ibrahim: 7) - لَأَزِيدَنَّكُمْ: fi'l mudari' — the nun thaqilah (نَّ) confirms it is fi'l and adds taqid to Allah's promise of increase

    8. زَيْدٌ فِي الدَّارِ - زَيْدٌ: mubtada', musnad ilayh - فِي: harf jar — connects zaid (ism) with the following ism - الدَّارِ: majrur by في — harf playing its role of connecting two nouns

    Practice Examples

    Identifying fi'l by its alamat:

    Word Alamat Present Conclusion
    قَدْ ذَهَبَ قد entered upon it Fi'l confirmed
    سَيَعْلَمُ سين entered upon it Fi'l confirmed
    لَمْ يَذْهَبْ Jazm present (sukun) Fi'l confirmed
    ضَرَبْتُ Damin mutakallim attached Fi'l confirmed
    ضَرَبَتْ Ta ta'nith sakinah attached Fi'l confirmed
    لَأَزِيدَنَّكُمْ Nun tawkid attached Fi'l confirmed

    Distinguishing kalam from non-kalam:

    Utterance Kalam? Reason
    زَيْدٌ قَائِمٌ Yes Musnad (قائم) + musnad ilayh (زيد) present; silence is valid
    قَامَ زَيْدٌ Yes Fi'l (musnad) + ism fa'il (musnad ilayh); silence is valid
    زَيْدٌ حَامِدٌ Incomplete Listener waits — no clear khabar/musnad established without context
    الكِتَابُ الكَلَامُ No Two musnad ilayh, no musnad
    يَا زَيْدُ Yes يا = fi'l in meaning (أَدْعُو); zaid = maf'ul/musnad ilayh

    Key Takeaways

    1. Fi'l is defined by two things: carrying meaning independently AND having an associated tense (madhi, mudari', amr).

    2. Nine alamat of fi'l were covered: ikhbar bihi (not anhu), acceptance of قد/سين/سوف, acceptance of jazm, tasrif between madhi and mudari', becoming amr/nahi, attachment of marfu' pronouns, attachment of ta ta'nith sakinah, and attachment of nun tawkid.

    3. Harf has no independent meaning — it only conveys meaning in relation to other words. It accepts neither the markers of ism nor the markers of fi'l. Despite this, it plays critical structural roles: connecting nouns, connecting verbs, and connecting sentences.

    4. Kalam requires isnad — merely placing words together is not enough. One element must be musnad (predicate) and one must be musnad ilayh (subject). Without this attribution-connection, there is no kalam.

    5. Two and only two structures produce valid kalam: jumlah ismiyyah (ism + ism) and jumlah fi'liyyah (fi'l + ism). Musnad and musnad ilayh are found exclusively in these two.

    6. Soundness of silence (يصح السكوت) is the practical test for kalam — if the listener is satisfied and not waiting for more, isnad is complete.

    7. Harf al-nida' (يا) stands in place of a fi'l — this resolves the apparent objection that يَا زَيْدُ violates the rule, since يا carries the meaning of أَدْعُو/أَطْلُبُ.

    8. The chapter on kalima (the three parts of speech) is now complete. The next session begins the detailed study of ism, which will continue for several months, followed by fi'l, then harf.
