    Nahw 201 - Study Notes

    Date: 2026-10-09

    Session Overview

    This session served as a review of the eight categories of Marfu'at (nominative nouns), followed by an introduction to the twelve categories of Mansubat (accusative nouns). The bulk of the class focused on two specific Mansubat in detail: Maf'ul Mutlaq (the absolute/cognate object) and Maf'ul Bihi (the direct object). Key topics included the purposes of each grammatical form, rules for dropping the governing verb (fi'l), and conditions for reordering sentence elements.

    Grammar Rules Covered

    Review: The Eight Marfu'at (Nominative Nouns)

    Nouns that take the raf' (nominative) case:

    # Category Example Notes
    1 Fa'il (Subject/Doer) قَرَأَ زَيْدٌ الْكِتَابَ — Zaidun is fa'il The one performing the action
    2 Na'ib al-Fa'il (Substitute of the Doer) قُرِئَ الْكِتَابُ — al-Kitabu is na'ib al-fa'il In passive voice, the object takes raf'
    3 Mubtada' (Subject of nominal sentence) الْكِتَابُ رَخِيصٌ The topic of a nominal sentence
    4 Khabar (Predicate of nominal sentence) الْكِتَابُ رَخِيصٌ — rakhisun is khabar
    5 Ism of Inna إِنَّ الْكِتَابَ جَمِيلٌ — Khabar jamilun is marfu' Ism of inna is actually mansoob; khabar is marfu'
    6 Ism of Kana كَانَ خَالِدٌ مَرِيضًا — Khalidun is marfu' Khabar of kana is mansoob
    7 Ism of Ma and La (resembling laysa) Also marfu'
    8 Khabar of Inna/Ma/La مَنْ جَدَّ وَجَدَ — relative pronoun man is marfu' Khabar can be a nominal clause

    The Twelve Mansubat (Accusative Nouns)

    Nouns that take the nasb (accusative) case — twelve in total, four of which were already covered in prior sessions:

    # Category
    1 Maf'ul Mutlaq (Absolute Object)
    2 Maf'ul Bihi (Direct Object)
    3 Maf'ul Fihi (Adverb of time/place)
    4 Maf'ul Lahu (Adverb of cause/reason)
    5 Maf'ul Ma'ahu (Adverb of accompaniment)
    6 Hal (Circumstantial qualifier)
    7 Tamyiz (Specification/Distinction)
    8 Mustathna (Exception)
    9 Khabar of Kana and its sisters
    10 Ism of Inna and its sisters
    11 Ism of La al-Nafiya lil-Jins (La that negates the genus)
    12 Khabar of Ma and La (resembling laysa)

    Note: Categories 9–12 were already covered in the Marfu'at/related sessions.

    Maf'ul Mutlaq (المَفْعُول المُطْلَق)

    Definition: A masdar (verbal noun) that carries the meaning of the verb mentioned before it.

    Three purposes:

    1. Taqid (Emphasis) — to emphasize the action

    2. Example: ضَرَبْتُهُ ضَرْبًا — I hit him (a real hitting)

    3. Bayan al-Naw' (Describing the type/manner of action)

    4. جَلَسْتُ جِلْسَةَ الْمَلِكِ — I sat like a king

    5. جَلَسْتُ جِلْسَةَ الطِّفْلِ — I sat like a child

    6. Bayan al-'Adad (Indicating the number of occurrences)

    7. جَلَسْتُ جَلْسَةً — I sat once
    8. جَلَسْتُ جَلْسَتَيْنِ — I sat twice
    9. جَلَسْتُ جَلَسَاتٍ — I sat multiple times

    Key rule — Maf'ul Mutlaq from a different root word: The masdar and the verb may come from different root words, provided the meaning is the same. - Example: أَنْبَتَهُ نَبَاتًا — He caused it to grow (a growing) - أَنْبَتَ is a fi'l mazid (augmented verb); نَبَات is from the fi'l mujarrad — different words, same core meaning.

    Dropping the governing verb (fi'l):

    The verb can be dropped either: - Jawāzan (optionally) — when context (qareena) makes the meaning clear - You see someone reading for a long time → you say: قِرَاءَةً طَوِيلَةً (the full sentence is implied) - When responding to a situation already understood: أَكْلًا كَثِيرًا - Saying خَيْرَ مَقْدَمٍ — actually means قَدِمْتَ قُدُومًا خَيْرًا; the verb and original masdar are dropped, and only the sifa (adjective) of the masdar remains

    • Wujūban (obligatorily) — in specific sam'i (transmitted) cases where Arabs established the usage without the verb:
    • سَقْيًا — May you be given to drink (rain/relief)
    • شُكْرًا — Thanks (from شَكَرْتُكَ شُكْرًا — the verb is always dropped in use)

    Maf'ul Bihi (المَفْعُول بِهِ)

    Definition: The noun upon which the action of the verb falls — the receiver of the action (the direct object).

    • Fa'il = the doer of the action
    • Maf'ul Bihi = the receiver of the action

    Example: ضَرَبَ زَيْدٌ عَمْرًا — Zaid hit Amr - زَيْدٌ = Fa'il (doer), مَرْفُوع - عَمْرًا = Maf'ul Bihi (receiver), مَنْصُوب

    Inversion of word order (Taqdim al-Maf'ul): Normally the order is: verb → fa'il → maf'ul bihi. It is permissible to place the maf'ul before the fa'il: - ضَرَبَ عَمْرًا زَيْدٌ (permissible)

    Condition: This is only allowed when there is clear i'rab marking on both nouns — so the reader/listener cannot be confused about who did what. If i'rab is absent or ambiguous, the inversion is not permitted.

    Reasons for inversion: - Emphasis on the receiver (the maf'ul is the more important information) - Natural flow of speech (the word came to tongue first)

    Dropping the governing verb (fi'l) for Maf'ul Bihi:

    Similar to Maf'ul Mutlaq, the verb can be dropped:

    • Jawāzan: In response to a question when context is clear
    • Question: مَنْ أَضْرِبُ؟ (Who should I hit?)

    • Response: زَيْدًا ([Hit] Zaid)

    • Wujūban (Obligatorily): Four specific situations (first two covered in this session):

    • Sam'i phrases — phrases transmitted from the Arabs where the verb is always absent:

      • امْرَأً وَنَفْسَهُ — Leave a man to himself (meaning: اتْرُكْ امْرَأً وَنَفْسَهُ; the verb اتْرُكْ is never stated)

    • Quranic/Transmitted usage:

      • وَانْتَهُوا خَيْرًا لَكُمْ — The full meaning is وَاقْصِدُوا خَيْرًا لَكُمْ; the verb وَاقْصِدُوا is dropped, and خَيْرًا is the maf'ul
      • خَيْرًا = tawḥid; meaning: opt for tawḥid, it is better for you

    3–4. (To be covered in the next session)

    Important Vocabulary

    Arabic Term Meaning
    مَرْفُوعَات (Marfu'at) Nominative nouns (those that take raf'/damma)
    مَنْصُوبَات (Mansubat) Accusative nouns (those that take nasb/fatha)
    فَاعِل (Fa'il) The doer/subject of a verb
    نَائِب الْفَاعِل (Na'ib al-Fa'il) Substitute of the doer (passive subject)
    مُبْتَدَأ (Mubtada') Subject of a nominal sentence
    خَبَر (Khabar) Predicate of a nominal sentence
    مَفْعُول مُطْلَق (Maf'ul Mutlaq) Absolute/cognate object (a masdar reinforcing its verb)
    مَفْعُول بِهِ (Maf'ul Bihi) Direct object (receiver of the action)
    مَصْدَر (Masdar) Verbal noun / infinitive form
    تَأْكِيد (Taqid) Emphasis/reinforcement
    بَيَان النَّوْع (Bayan al-Naw') Describing the type/manner of action
    بَيَان الْعَدَد (Bayan al-'Adad) Specifying the number of occurrences
    جَوَازًا (Jawāzan) Optionally / permissibly
    وُجُوبًا (Wujūban) Obligatorily / necessarily
    سَمَاعِي (Sam'i) Transmitted from Arab usage (not derived by rule)
    قَرِينَة (Qareena) Contextual indicator / circumstantial evidence
    تَقْدِيم (Taqdim) Bringing forward / fronting a word
    فِعْل مَزِيد (Fi'l Mazid) Augmented verb (with extra letters)
    فِعْل مُجَرَّد (Fi'l Mujarrad) Base/bare verb (root letters only)
    أَهْلًا وَسَهْلًا (Ahlan wa Sahlan) Welcome — lit. You have come to family and walked on soft ground
    شُكْرًا (Shukran) Thanks (a sam'i maf'ul mutlaq with dropped verb)
    سَقْيًا (Saqyan) May you be given to drink / may relief come to you

    I'rab (Sentence Analysis) Examples

    Example 1: إِنَّ الْكِتَابَ جَمِيلٌ - إِنَّ: Harf nasb wa tawkid (particle of emphasis), mabni - الْكِتَابَ: Ism of inna, mansoob, sign: fatha - جَمِيلٌ: Khabar of inna, marfu', sign: damma

    Example 2: كَانَ خَالِدٌ مَرِيضًا - كَانَ: Fi'l mad, mabni - خَالِدٌ: Ism of kana, marfu', sign: damma - مَرِيضًا: Khabar of kana, mansoob, sign: fatha

    Example 3: ضَرَبَ زَيْدٌ عَمْرًا - ضَرَبَ: Fi'l madi, mabni - زَيْدٌ: Fa'il, marfu', sign: damma - عَمْرًا: Maf'ul bihi, mansoob, sign: fatha

    Example 4: جَلَسْتُ جِلْسَةَ الْمَلِكِ - جَلَسْتُ: Fi'l + fa'il (conjugated) - جِلْسَةَ: Maf'ul mutlaq (bayan al-naw'), mansoob, sign: fatha (mudaf) - الْمَلِكِ: Mudaf ilayhi, majrur, sign: kasra

    Example 5: أَنْبَتَهُ اللَّهُ نَبَاتًا حَسَنًا (Quranic reference) - أَنْبَتَهُ: Verb + maf'ul bihi (pronoun) - اللَّهُ: Fa'il, marfu' - نَبَاتًا: Maf'ul mutlaq from a different root (أَنْبَتَ / نَبَات), mansoob - حَسَنًا: Sifa (adjective) of نَبَاتًا, mansoob

    Example 6: وَانْتَهُوا خَيْرًا لَكُمْ (Quran 4:171) - Full implied meaning: وَاقْصِدُوا خَيْرًا لَكُمْ - خَيْرًا: Maf'ul bihi with its governing verb (وَاقْصِدُوا) dropped — wujuban - Meaning: Seek what is better for you (i.e., tawḥid)

    Practice Examples

    1. Identify the maf'ul mutlaq and its purpose (taqid / bayan naw' / bayan 'adad):
    2. ضَرَبْتُهُ ضَرْبًا → Taqid (I hit him, a real hitting)
    3. جَلَسْتُ جِلْسَةَ الطِّفْلِ → Bayan al-Naw' (I sat like a child)

    4. زُرْتُ الْكَعْبَةَ زِيَارَتَيْنِ → Bayan al-'Adad (I visited the Ka'ba twice)

    5. Identify whether the dropped verb is jawāzan or wujūban:

    6. Someone is eating a lot; you remark: أَكْلًا كَثِيرًا → Jawāzan (context makes it clear)
    7. شُكْرًا → Wujūban Sam'i (Arabs never state the verb with this masdar)

    8. امْرَأً وَنَفْسَهُ → Wujūban Sam'i (the verb اتْرُكْ must be omitted)

    9. Is this inversion permissible? ضَرَبَ عَمْرًا زَيْدٌ

    10. Yes — both nouns have clear i'rab markers (عَمْرًا is mansoob = maf'ul; زَيْدٌ is marfu' = fa'il), so no ambiguity.

    11. Explain أَهْلًا وَسَهْلًا grammatically:

    12. Full meaning: أَتَيْتَ أَهْلًا وَبَاطَأْتَ سَهْلًا
    13. أَهْلًا = Maf'ul bihi (mansoob), verb أَتَيْتَ dropped — wujūban (sam'i phrase)
    14. سَهْلًا = Maf'ul bihi (mansoob), verb بَاطَأْتَ dropped
    15. Meaning: You have arrived among family, and you have walked on soft ground (ease)

    Key Takeaways

    1. All eight Marfu'at completed. Any noun in raf' belongs to one of eight categories: fa'il, na'ib al-fa'il, mubtada', khabar, ism of inna, ism of kana, ism of ma/la, or khabar of inna/ma/la.

    2. Twelve Mansubat to learn. Four were covered in earlier sessions; this session introduced Maf'ul Mutlaq and Maf'ul Bihi in detail. The remaining six will follow.

    3. Maf'ul Mutlaq is always a masdar. It can come from the same root as its verb or from a different root — the shared meaning is what matters. Its three functions are taqid, bayan al-naw', and bayan al-'adad.

    4. Dropping the fi'l follows a two-tier rule: jawāzan (when qareena is present) or wujūban (in specific sam'i constructions or Quranic patterns). Understanding which cases require the drop — and which merely allow it — is essential.

    5. Maf'ul Bihi word order can be inverted, but only when clear i'rab eliminates any ambiguity about who is the doer and who is the receiver.

    6. Sam'i phrases must be memorized as-is. Constructions like شُكْرًا, سَقْيًا, and امْرَأً وَنَفْسَهُ are transmitted usage — you cannot re-insert the dropped verb.

    7. Next session: Remaining two cases of wujubi hazf al-fi'l for Maf'ul Bihi, then onward through the remaining Mansubat.
