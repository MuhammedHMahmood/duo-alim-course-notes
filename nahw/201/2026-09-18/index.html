    Nahw 201 - Study Notes

    Date: 2026-09-18

    Session Overview

    This session covered the chapter on المرفوعات (al-Marfū'āt) — nouns in the nominative case. The class focused primarily on the first category: الفاعل (al-Fā'il, the doer/subject). Topics included the definition of Fā'il, conditions for a noun to function as Fā'il, the agreement rules between the verb (fi'l) and its Fā'il, cases where Fā'il must precede Maf'ūl, and cases where Fa'il and Fa'il can be dropped.

    Grammar Rules Covered

    1. The Eight Categories of Marfū'āt (أسماء المرفوعة)

    There are eight types of nouns that take the Raf' case (مرفوعات الأسماء):

    1. الفاعل (Al-Fā'il) — the doer
    2. مفعول ما لم يُسَمَّ فاعله (Maf'ūl mā lam yusamma fā'iluh) — passive subject (nā'ib al-fā'il)
    3. المبتدأ (Al-Mubtada') — the subject of a nominal sentence
    4. الخبر (Al-Khabar) — the predicate
    5. خبر إنَّ وأخواتها (Khabar inna wa akhawātihā)
    6. اسم كان وأخواتها (Ism kāna wa akhawātihā)
    7. اسم ما ولا المشبهتين بـ"ليس" (Ism mā wa lā al-mushabbahatayn bi-laysa)
    8. خبر أفعال التي رُفِعَ فيها الجنس (Khabar af'āl allatī rufi'a fīhā al-jins)

    2. Definition of الفاعل (Al-Fā'il)

    وَهُوَ كُلُّ اسْمٍ قَبْلَهُ فِعْلٌ أَوْ صِفَةٌ أُسْنِدَ إِلَيْهِ عَلَى مَعْنَى أَنَّهُ قَامَ بِهِ

    Fā'il is every noun before which a verb (fi'l) or a sifah (participial adjective) has come, and that verb/sifah is attributed to it, meaning the noun is the doer of the action.

    • The Fā'il is always Marfū' (in the nominative case).
    • Either a فِعْل (verb) or a صِفَة (sifah — i.e., ism al-fā'il, ism al-maf'ūl, sifah mushabbahah) must precede the Fā'il.

    Examples: | Arabic | Analysis | |---|---| | قَامَ زَيْدٌ | قَامَ = fi'l, زَيْدٌ = Fā'il (Marfū') | | زَيْدٌ ضَارِبٌ أَبُوهُ عَمْرًا | ضَارِبٌ = sifah, أَبُوهُ = Fā'il of the sifah | | ضَرَبَ زَيْدٌ عَمْرًا | Musbat (affirmative) | | مَا ضَرَبَ زَيْدٌ عَمْرًا | Manfi (negative) — Fā'il rule applies in both cases |

    Note: If the verb comes after the noun (e.g., زَيْدٌ قَامَ), then technically Zaid is Mubtada' and قَامَ is Khabar — not a Fā'il construction.

    3. Types of Fā'il

    The Fā'il can be:

    a) اسم ظاهر (Ism Zāhir) — an overt/explicit noun

    ذَهَبَ زَيْدٌ — Zaid is the explicit Fā'il

    b) ضمير (Damīr) — a pronoun

    ضَرَبْتُ زَيْدًا — the تُ is the Fā'il ضَرَبْتَ زَيْدًا — the تَ is the Fā'il ضَرَبْتِ زَيْدًا — the تِ is the Fā'il

    c) ضمير مستتر (Damīr Mustatir) — a hidden/implied pronoun

    زَيْدٌ ذَهَبَ — The verb ذَهَبَ contains a hidden هُوَ referring to Zaid; this hidden pronoun is the Fā'il, Zaid is technically the Mubtada'.

    The word مستتر comes from the root سَتَرَ (satara) — to conceal, to hide. (cf. the du'ā: اللهُمَّ اسْتُرْنَا بِسِتْرِكَ الجَمِيل)

    4. Lāzim vs. Muta'addī Verbs

    Type Definition Requires
    فعل لازم (Lāzim) Intransitive — action stops at the Fā'il Fā'il only
    فعل متعدٍّ (Muta'addī) Transitive — action exceeds/goes beyond the Fā'il Fā'il + Maf'ūl

    The word مُتَعَدٍّ comes from تَعَدَّى (to exceed, to go beyond), as in the Quranic verse:

    وَمَنْ يَتَعَدَّ حُدُودَ اللَّهِ فَأُولَٰئِكَ هُمُ الظَّالِمُونَ

    Every fi'l absolutely requires a Fā'il, because an action cannot come into existence without its doer.

    5. Agreement Between Fi'l and Fā'il

    Rule A: When Fā'il is Ism Zāhir (explicit noun) — Fi'l stays Wāhid (singular)

    The verb does not change according to the number or gender of the Fā'il — it stays singular:

    Sentence Fā'il Fi'l
    ضَرَبَ الزَّيْدَانِ Dual stays singular: ضَرَبَ
    ضَرَبَ الزَّيْدُونَ Plural stays singular: ضَرَبَ

    Rule B: When Fā'il is a Damīr (pronoun, i.e., fi'l comes after fā'il) — Fi'l changes

    If the noun comes first and the verb follows, the verb agrees with the noun in number:

    Sentence Number
    زَيْدٌ أَسْلَمَ Singular
    الزَّيْدَانِ أَسْلَمَا Dual
    الزَّيْدُونَ أَسْلَمُوا Plural

    (Technically, the leading noun is Mubtada' and the verb is Khabar; the Fā'il inside the verb is a hidden Damīr referring back to the noun as its Marja'.)

    Simplified rule: If verb comes before the fā'il → verb stays singular. If the noun comes before the verb → verb changes to match.

    6. Mu'annath (Feminine) Agreement Rules

    Definition of Mu'annath Haqīqī: a noun that has a genuine opposite gender (e.g., a woman has a male counterpart → genuinely feminine). Contrast with Mu'annath Ghayr Haqīqī (grammatically feminine, no biological opposite, e.g., الشَّمْس — the sun, or جَامِعَة — university).

    When Fā'il is Mu'annath Haqīqī:

    Condition Rule
    No separation (fasl) between fi'l and fā'il Tā' al-ta'nīth obligatory: قَامَتْ هِنْدٌ
    Separation (fasl) exists between fi'l and fā'il You have a choice (mukhayyar): either mukadhdhar or mu'annath

    Example with fasl:

    ضَرَبَ الْيَوْمَ هِنْدٌ (الْيَوْمَ is the fasl — choice allowed) ضَرَبَتِ الْيَوْمَ هِنْدٌ (both are valid)

    When Fā'il is Mu'annath Ghayr Haqīqī:

    You always have a choice between mudhdhakar and mu'annath forms, even without fasl:

    طَلَعَتِ الشَّمْسُ / طَلَعَ الشَّمْسُ (both valid)

    Exception — if fā'il (noun) precedes fi'l (verb): In this case (fā'il before fi'l), the verb must match the mu'annath noun:

    الشَّمْسُ طَلَعَتْ (must use ta'nīth here)

    Jam' Taksīr (Broken Plural):

    Jam' taksīr is treated like Mu'annath Ghayr Haqīqī for verb agreement purposes.

    7. Taqdimu al-Fā'il 'alā al-Maf'ūl (Precedence of Fā'il over Maf'ūl)

    The default order in Arabic: فِعْل → فَاعِل → مَفْعُول

    Normally it is permitted to swap the positions of Fā'il and Maf'ūl. Example:

    ضَرَبَ زَيْدًا خَالِدٌ (Maf'ūl brought forward — allowed)

    Exception — when swapping is FORBIDDEN:

    When both Fā'il and Maf'ūl are Ism Maqsūr (ending in alif maqsūrah, indeclinable — no i'rāb visible) AND there is fear of confusion (khawf al-labs):

    ضَرَبَ مُوسَى عِيسَى

    Here, Mūsā is the Fā'il and 'Īsā is the Maf'ūl. If you reverse them:

    ضَرَبَ عِيسَى مُوسَى

    The reader cannot tell from i'rāb alone which is Fā'il and which is Maf'ūl — both look identical. So swapping is forbidden when this confusion could arise.

    Exception to the exception — swapping IS allowed even with two Ism Maqsūr, if meaning prevents confusion:

    أَكَلَ الْكُمَّثْرَى يَحْيَى (A pear cannot eat Yahyā; meaning tells us Yahyā is Fā'il and pear is Maf'ūl, so no confusion arises.)

    Summary: Swapping is forbidden only when both i'rāb AND meaning fail to distinguish Fā'il from Maf'ūl.

    8. Dropping (Hazf) the Fā'il or Both Fi'l and Fā'il

    Dropping the Fā'il alone — permissible when context (qarīnah) makes it clear:

    مَنْ ضَرَبَكَ؟ → زَيْدٌ (The fi'l ضَرَبَ is dropped; only the Fā'il زَيْدٌ is mentioned because context supplies the rest.)

    Dropping both Fi'l and Fā'il — permissible when context is fully clear:

    أَقَامَ زَيْدٌ؟ → نَعَمْ (Both the fi'l أَقَامَ and the fā'il زَيْدٌ are dropped; نَعَمْ alone suffices because the question provides the context.)

    Important Vocabulary

    Arabic Term Meaning
    المرفوعات (al-Marfū'āt) Nouns in the nominative/Raf' case
    الفاعل (al-Fā'il) The doer; grammatical subject of a verbal sentence
    الفعل (al-Fi'l) The verb
    الصفة (al-Sifah) Participial adjective (ism fā'il, ism maf'ūl, sifah mushabbahah)
    مسند (Musnid) That which is attributed/predicated
    مسند إليه (Musnid Ilayh) That to which something is attributed
    اسم ظاهر (Ism Zāhir) An overt, explicit noun
    ضمير (Damīr) Pronoun
    ضمير مستتر (Damīr Mustatir) Hidden/implied pronoun (from سَتَرَ — to conceal)
    فعل لازم (Fi'l Lāzim) Intransitive verb
    فعل متعدٍّ (Fi'l Muta'addī) Transitive verb (from تَعَدَّى — to exceed/go beyond)
    مفعول (Maf'ūl) Object of a transitive verb
    مؤنث حقيقي (Mu'annath Haqīqī) Genuinely feminine (has a real male counterpart)
    مؤنث غير حقيقي (Mu'annath Ghayr Haqīqī) Grammatically feminine only (no real male counterpart)
    جمع تكسير (Jam' Taksīr) Broken plural
    فصل (Fasl) Separation between fi'l and fā'il
    اسم مقصور (Ism Maqsūr) Indeclinable noun ending in alif maqsūrah (no visible i'rāb)
    تقديم وتأخير (Taqdīm wa Ta'khīr) Bringing forward / sending back (word order shift)
    لَبْس (Labs) Confusion, ambiguity
    قرينة (Qarīnah) Contextual indicator/clue
    حذف (Hazf) Ellipsis; dropping a word
    مرجع (Marja') Antecedent of a pronoun
    واحد (Wāhid) Singular
    تثنية (Tathniyah) Dual
    جمع (Jam') Plural
    مُثبَت (Musbat) Affirmative statement
    منفي (Manfī) Negative statement

    I'rab (Sentence Analysis) Examples

    1. قَامَ زَيْدٌ - قَامَ: fi'l mādi, mabni 'alā al-fath - زَيْدٌ: Fā'il, marfū', 'alāmatu raf'ihi al-dammah

    2. زَيْدٌ ضَارِبٌ أَبُوهُ عَمْرًا - زَيْدٌ: Mubtada', marfū' - ضَارِبٌ: Khabar, marfū'; also a sifah acting like a fi'l - أَبُوهُ: Fā'il of the sifah ضَارِبٌ, marfū' (وَاو is sign of raf'), هُ = mudāf ilayh - عَمْرًا: Maf'ūl bih, mansūb

    3. ضَرَبَ الزَّيْدَانِ - ضَرَبَ: fi'l, stays singular (Fā'il is ism zāhir) - الزَّيْدَانِ: Fā'il, marfū', 'alāmatu raf'ihi al-alif (because dual)

    4. زَيْدٌ أَسْلَمَ - زَيْدٌ: Mubtada', marfū' - أَسْلَمَ: Khabar (jumlah fi'liyyah); Fā'il inside is a hidden هُوَ referring to Zaid as its marja'

    5. الزَّيْدَانِ أَسْلَمَا - الزَّيْدَانِ: Mubtada', marfū' - أَسْلَمَا: Khabar; fi'l changed to dual because noun (acting as marja') precedes it; Fā'il is the alif Damīr

    6. ضَرَبَ مُوسَى عِيسَى - ضَرَبَ: fi'l - مُوسَى: Fā'il, marfū' (indeclinable — ism maqsūr, no visible i'rāb marker) - عِيسَى: Maf'ūl bih, mansūb (indeclinable — ism maqsūr, no visible i'rāb marker) - Cannot swap their positions due to ambiguity

    7. أَكَلَ الْكُمَّثْرَى يَحْيَى - أَكَلَ: fi'l - الْكُمَّثْرَى: Maf'ūl bih muqaddam (brought forward), mansūb (ism maqsūr) - يَحْيَى: Fā'il mu'akhkhar, marfū' (ism maqsūr) - Swap allowed because meaning prevents confusion — a pear cannot eat a person

    Practice Examples

    Identify the Fā'il in each sentence:

    1. ذَهَبَ مُحَمَّدٌ إِلَى الْمَسْجِدِ → Fā'il: مُحَمَّدٌ (ism zāhir)

    2. ضَرَبْتُ زَيْدًا → Fā'il: تُ (damīr mutakallim)

    3. قَامَتْ هِنْدٌ → Fā'il: هِنْدٌ (mu'annath haqīqī, no fasl → tā' al-ta'nīth obligatory)

    4. ضَرَبَ الْيَوْمَ هِنْدٌ OR ضَرَبَتِ الْيَوْمَ هِنْدٌ → Both valid — الْيَوْمَ creates fasl, giving you a choice

    5. طَلَعَتِ الشَّمْسُ OR طَلَعَ الشَّمْسُ → Both valid — الشَّمْس is mu'annath ghayr haqīqī

    6. الشَّمْسُ طَلَعَتْ → Must use ta'nīth — noun precedes verb, verb must agree

    Practice identifying whether swapping Fā'il/Maf'ūl is permitted:

    Sentence Can you swap? Reason
    ضَرَبَ زَيْدًا خَالِدٌ Yes I'rāb distinguishes them
    ضَرَبَ مُوسَى عِيسَى No Both ism maqsūr, meaning doesn't help
    أَكَلَ الْكُمَّثْرَى يَحْيَى Yes Both ism maqsūr but meaning resolves ambiguity

    Key Takeaways

    1. Fā'il is always Marfū' — this is a fixed rule with no exceptions.

    2. Two triggers for Fā'il status: a verb (fi'l) OR a participial adjective (sifah — ism fā'il, ism maf'ūl, sifah mushabbahah) must precede the noun and the action must be attributed to that noun.

    3. Every fi'l requires a Fā'il — an action cannot exist without a doer. The Fā'il may be an overt noun (ism zāhir), a stated pronoun (damīr bāriz), or a hidden pronoun (damīr mustatir).

    4. When the Fā'il is an ism zāhir and precedes it, the fi'l stays singular regardless of whether the Fā'il is singular, dual, or plural. When the noun comes first (technically Mubtada'), the verb that follows as Khabar must agree in number.

    5. Tā' al-ta'nīth is obligatory when Fā'il is Mu'annath Haqīqī and there is no fasl (separation). With fasl, or with Mu'annath Ghayr Haqīqī, you have a choice.

    6. Swapping Fā'il and Maf'ūl is normally allowed in Arabic but becomes forbidden when both are Ism Maqsūr and neither i'rāb nor meaning can resolve which is which.

    7. Both fi'l and Fā'il may be dropped when a qarīnah (contextual indicator) makes the meaning clear to the listener.
