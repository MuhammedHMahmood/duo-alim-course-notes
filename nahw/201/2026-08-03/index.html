    Nahw 201 - Study Notes

    Date: 2026-08-03

    Session Overview

    This was the opening session of Nahw 201, covering the Muqaddimah (introduction) of Hidayat al-Nahw by its Musannif (author). The teacher began with motivational remarks about the importance of Nahw and Sarf as foundational sciences, then introduced the first Fasl (chapter) of the Muqaddimah: the definition and purpose of Nahw. The session also included Q&A about class preparation and an overview of what the book covers.

    Grammar Rules Covered

    Definition of Nahw (علم النحو)

    From the text of Hidayat al-Nahw:

    فَصِيعَةٌ: النَّحْوُ عِلْمٌ بِأُصُولٍ يُعْرَفُ بِهَا أَحْوَالُ أَوَاخِرِ الْكَلِمِ الثَّلَاثِ مِنْ حَيْثُ الْإِعْرَابِ وَالْبِنَاءِ وَكَيْفِيَّةُ تَرْكِيبِ بَعْضِهَا مَعَ بَعْضٍ

    Translation: Nahw is a science of foundational principles (usool/qawanin — grammatical rules) by which one knows: 1. The ahwal (states/conditions) of the endings (awakhir) of the three parts of speech 2. From the standpoint of i'rab and bina 3. And how words are constructed together (kayfiyyat al-tarkib)

    Key point: Nahw deals specifically with the last letter (harf akhir) of a word — not the first or middle letters. For example, in جَاءَ زَيْدٌ, Nahw determines what haraka goes on the Daal of Zaid.

    The Three Parts of Speech (الكلم الثلاث)

    1. الاسم (Ism) — Noun
    2. الفعل (Fi'l) — Verb
    3. الحرف (Harf) — Particle/Preposition

    Nahw governs the endings of all three.

    Purpose of Nahw (الغرض منه)

    وَالْغَرَضُ مِنْهُ صِيَانَةُ الذِّهْنِ عَنِ الْخَطَإِ اللَّفْظِيِّ

    Translation: The purpose of Nahw is to protect the mind from verbal/linguistic error.

    Important clarification from the teacher: The Musannif said "mind" (ذهن/zihan), not tongue. This is because errors originate in the mind before they reach the tongue. If the mind is protected from error, the tongue follows correctly.

    Practical example: - Without Nahw, one might say: جَاءَ زَيْدًا / جَاءَ زَيْدٍ / جَاءَ زَيْد (incorrect) - With Nahw, one says confidently: جَاءَ زَيْدٌ (correct — Zaid is the Fa'il, so Marfu', hence Dhamma)

    Mu'rab vs. Mabni — Introductory Distinction

    This was introduced through Q&A at the end of the session:

    Mabni (مبني): A word whose ending does not change regardless of its position in a sentence.

    • Example: هَذَا (hatha — this)
    • جَاءَ هَذَا (Hatha came)
    • رَأَيْتُ هَذَا (I saw Hatha)
    • مَرَرْتُ بِهَذَا (I passed by Hatha)
    • → The word هَذَا remains unchanged in all three cases.

    Mu'rab (معرب): A word whose ending changes according to its grammatical role.

    • Example: زَيْد
    • جَاءَ زَيْدٌ (Zaidun — Marfu', Dhamma — subject)
    • رَأَيْتُ زَيْدًا (Zaidan — Mansub, Fathah — object)
    • مَرَرْتُ بِزَيْدٍ (Zaidin — Majrur, Kasra — after preposition)
    • → The ending of Zaid changes in each case.

    I'rab — Primary vs. Secondary (Asl vs. Niyabi)

    Briefly introduced; to be covered in detail later:

    • Primary (Asl) I'rab: The word takes the actual haraka — e.g., جَاءَ زَيْدٌ (Dhamma directly on Daal)
    • Secondary/Representative (Niyabi) I'rab: A letter represents the haraka instead of an actual haraka:
    • Waw (و) represents Dhamma (Raf')
    • Alif (ا) represents Fathah or sometimes Dhamma (Nasb/Raf')
    • Ya (ي) represents Kasra (Jarr/Nasb)

    Book Structure — Muqaddimah Overview

    The Muqaddimah of Hidayat al-Nahw has three Fusool (chapters): 1. Fasl al-Awwal: Definition and purpose of Nahw (covered this session) 2. Fasl al-Thani: Al-Kalima wal-Kalam (next session) 3. Fasl al-Thalith: (to be covered)

    Important Vocabulary

    الْبَلَاغَة (Al-Balagha) Arabic rhetoric
    عُلُوم شَرْعِيَّة (Uloom Shar'iyya) Religious sciences (Tafsir, Hadith, Fiqh)
    عُلُوم آلِيَّة (Uloom Aliyya) Instrumental sciences (Nahw, Sarf, Balagha — tools for understanding)
    الْمُقَدِّمَة (Al-Muqaddimah) Foreword/introduction of a book
    الْفَصْل (Al-Fasl) / فُصُول (Fusool) Chapter(s)
    الْمُصَنِّف (Al-Musannif) Author of the book

    جَامِعَة (Jami'a) Comprehensive
    أُصُول (Usool) Principles/foundations; here: grammatical rules

    أَوَاخِر (Awakhir) Endings (of words)
    الْكَلِم الثَّلَاث (Al-Kalim al-Thalath) The three parts of speech (Ism, Fi'l, Harf)
    الِاسْم (Al-Ism) Noun
    الْفِعْل (Al-Fi'l) Verb
    الْحَرْف (Al-Harf) Particle/preposition

    الْبِنَاء (Al-Bina) Indeclinability — fixed endings that do not change
    التَّرْكِيب (Al-Tarkib) Construction/composition of sentences
    الْغَرَض (Al-Gharad) Purpose/aim
    صِيَانَة (Siyāna) Protection/preservation
    الذِّهْن (Al-Zihan) The mind/intellect
    الْخَطَأ اللَّفْظِيّ (Al-Khata al-Lafzi) Verbal/linguistic error
    مَبْنِي (Mabni) Indeclinable — word with fixed, unchanging ending
    مُعْرَب (Mu'rab) Declinable — word whose ending changes with grammatical role
    مَرْفُوع (Marfu') Nominative case — typically Dhamma (ـُ)
    مَنْصُوب (Mansub) Accusative case — typically Fathah (ـَ)
    مَجْرُور (Majrur) Genitive case — typically Kasra (ـِ)
    مَجْزُوم (Majzum) Jussive — typically Sukoon (ـْ), applies to verbs
    حَرَكَة (Haraka) / حَرَكَات (Harakat) Vowel diacritic(s)
    الضَّمَّة (Al-Dhamma) The "u" vowel (ـُ)
    الْفَتْحَة (Al-Fathah) The "a" vowel (ـَ)
    الْكَسْرَة (Al-Kasra) The "i" vowel (ـِ)
    السُّكُون (Al-Sukoon) Absence of vowel (ـْ)
    هَذَا (Hatha) This (demonstrative pronoun — example of Mabni)
    الْمُطَالَعَة (Al-Mutala'a) Pre-class preparation/reading
    الْمُذَاكَرَة / التَّكْرَار (Al-Mudhakara / Al-Takrar) Revision/review after class
    شُرُوحَات (Shuruhaat) Explanatory commentaries on texts

    I'rab (Sentence Analysis) Examples

    Example 1: جَاءَ زَيْدٌ

    Word I'rab Reason
    جَاءَ (Ja'a) Fi'l Madi, Mabni 'ala al-Fathah Verb — ends in Fathah, fixed
    زَيْدٌ (Zaidun) Ism, Fa'il, Marfu', 'alamatuhu al-Dhamma Subject of the verb; Mu'rab, takes Dhamma

    Example 2: رَأَيْتُ زَيْدًا

    Word I'rab Reason
    رَأَيْتُ (Ra'aytu) Fi'l Madi + Fa'il (Tu) Verb with attached subject pronoun
    زَيْدًا (Zaidan) Ism, Maf'ul bih, Mansub, 'alamatuhu al-Fathah Direct object; takes Fathah

    Example 3: مَرَرْتُ بِزَيْدٍ

    Word I'rab Reason
    مَرَرْتُ (Marartu) Fi'l Madi + Fa'il Verb with attached subject
    بِ (Bi) Harf Jarr Preposition
    زَيْدٍ (Zaidin) Ism, Majrur by Bi, 'alamatuhu al-Kasra Governed by preposition; takes Kasra

    Example 4: Mabni — هَذَا across positions

    Sentence Role of هَذَا Ending
    جَاءَ هَذَا Subject (Fa'il) هَذَا — unchanged
    رَأَيْتُ هَذَا Object (Maf'ul bih) هَذَا — unchanged
    مَرَرْتُ بِهَذَا Majrur (after preposition) هَذَا — unchanged

    Conclusion: هَذَا is Mabni — its ending never changes regardless of grammatical role.

    Practice Examples

    1. Identify Mabni vs. Mu'rab: Take the word زَيْد and place it in three sentences (as subject, object, and after a preposition). Note how the ending changes each time — this confirms it is Mu'rab.

    2. Identify the ending: In the sentence جَاءَ زَيْدٌ, point to the exact letter Nahw governs — the Daal of Zaid (the last letter). Ask: what haraka is on it, and why?

    3. Prepare pages 8–10 of Hidayat al-Nahw (upper portion of p.10 only; the lower portion is explanatory notes for reference): try to translate and assign proper I'rab before the next class.

    4. Self-test confidence: Read any Arabic text without vowel markings (Arabic without harakat). Assign the harakat yourself based on I'rab. This builds the confidence the teacher described — being unable to be "shaken" by a questioner.

    Key Takeaways

    1. Nahw and Sarf are Uloom Aliyya — instrumental sciences that are prerequisites for mastering Tafsir, Hadith, and Fiqh. Scholars equate their reward with studying the major Islamic sciences because without them, those sciences cannot be understood properly.

    2. Hidayat al-Nahw is both Mukhtasar (concise) and Jami'a (comprehensive) — this dual quality is rare and is why the book is widely used in madrasas, particularly in South Asia.

    3. The definition of Nahw encompasses three things: knowing the states of word endings, distinguishing Mu'rab from Mabni, and knowing how to construct words together in sentences.

    4. The purpose of Nahw is to protect the mind (and consequently the tongue) from committing errors in Arabic expression — enabling confident, grounded I'rab rather than coincidental correctness.

    5. Mabni vs. Mu'rab is the central axis of Nahw: Mabni words have fixed endings regardless of position; Mu'rab words change their endings based on their grammatical role (Raf', Nasb, Jarr, Jazm).

    6. Three habits are mandatory for this course:

    7. Mutala'a — prepare (translate + attempt I'rab) before class; prepare as if you will teach it
    8. Presence in class — attend live; do not let recordings pile up

    9. Takrar/Mudhakara — revise what was taught before the next session

    10. Dependency on Shuruhaat is a weakness — the goal is to reach a level where Arabic texts can be read and understood directly, without relying on Urdu or Arabic commentaries for basic translation.

    11. Next session covers: Al-Kalima wal-Kalam (Fasl al-Thani of the Muqaddimah) — pages 8–10 of Hidayat al-Nahw (upper portion).
