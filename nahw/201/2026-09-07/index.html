    Nahw 201 - Study Notes

    Date: 2026-09-07

    Session Overview

    This session continued the study of Ghayr Munsarif (words that do not accept tanwin and take fathah in place of kasrah in the majrur state). The teacher reviewed and expanded on the asbab (reasons/conditions) that render a word ghayr munsarif, focusing on: Ta'nīth with Alif Maqsūra or Alif Mamdūda, Alamiyyah, 'Ujmah, Jama' Muntaha al-Jumūm, and Tarkīb. The session also included a significant discussion on the distinction between alam and ma'rifah, and practical examples (Iblis vs. Shaytan, Abdullah, Ba'labakk). Two asbab remain — Alif Nun Za'idatan and Wazn al-Fi'l — to be covered in the next session.

    Grammar Rules Covered

    1. Review: Structure of Ghayr Munsarif

    A word is ghayr munsarif when either: - Two asbab (reasons) are present, OR - One sabab is present that is qā'im maqām as-sababayn (standing in place of two reasons)

    2. Ta'nīth with Alif Maqsūra or Alif Mamdūda — Qā'im Maqām as-Sababayn

    The alif of ta'nīth (femininity) — whether maqsūra (ى) or mamdūda (اء) — is so strong that it stands in place of two reasons. Therefore, a single word with this alif alone is ghayr munsarif — no additional sabab is required.

    Three signs of ta'nīth: | Sign | Example | |---|---| | Tā' Marbūtah (ة) | فَاطِمَة | | Alif Maqsūra (ى) | حُبْلَى | | Alif Mamdūda (اء) | حَمْرَاء |

    Only Alif Maqsūra and Alif Mamdūda serve as qā'im maqām as-sababayn. Tā' Marbūtah counts as only one sabab.

    3. Alamiyyah — Proper Noun as a Sabab

    Definition of Alam: A specific given name for a person, place, or thing.

    Critical distinction — Alam vs. Ma'rifah: - Every alam is ma'rifah (definite), but not every ma'rifah is alam. - Alam = a given name with no ambiguity (e.g., زَيْدٌ — one specific person named Zayd). - Ma'rifah (but not alam) = still carries potential confusion (e.g., الرَّجُلُ — "the man," but which man?). - Walad and Rajul are NOT alam even if used for a specific person; they are common nouns. - Al-Shaytan = ma'rifah but NOT alam → only one sabab → munsarif.

    Alamiyyah combined with another sabab makes a word ghayr munsarif.

    4. 'Ujmah — Non-Arabic Origin as a Sabab

    Definition: A word that is non-Arabic in its origin and was a proper name (alam) in its original language.

    Combined with Alamiyyah → Ghayr Munsarif:

    Examples — Names of Angels (all ghayr munsarif due to Alamiyyah + 'Ujmah): - جِبْرَائِيل، مِيكَائِيل — non-Arabic, proper names → غير منصرف - Exception (munsarif): مَالِك، مُنْكَر، نَكِير — these three are Arabic; they do not have 'ujmah, so only one sabab (alamiyyah) → munsarif

    Examples — Names of Prophets (all ghayr munsarif due to Alamiyyah + 'Ujmah): - إِبْرَاهِيم، إِسْمَاعِيل → غير منصرف - Exception — Seven munsarif Prophet names (Arabic, not 'ujmah → only alamiyyah = one sabab): مُحَمَّد، شُعَيْب، صَالِح، هُود، لُوط، نُوح، أَشِيس

    Critical condition for 'ujmah with sulāsī (3-letter) words: - If the foreign name is three letters (sulāsī), the middle letter must be sākin for it to be munsarif (since the combination of conditions fails to produce full ghayr munsarif status). - Example: نُوح — sulāsī, middle letter waw is sākin → munsarif - Example: لُوط — sulāsī, middle letter is sākin → munsarif - A sulāsī foreign word that is NOT alam → only 'ujmah without alamiyyah = one sabab → munsarif - A sulāsī foreign word that IS alam but middle letter is sākin → the word is treated as munsarif due to its lightness

    5. Jama' Muntaha al-Jumūm — "The Ultimate Plural" as a Sabab

    Definition: A broken plural (jama' taksīr) that is the final plural — meaning no further plural can be formed from it.

    Pattern condition: Must follow one of two patterns: | Pattern | Example | |---|---| | مَفَاعِل | مَسَاجِد (pl. of مَسْجِد)، مَدَارِس (pl. of مَدْرَسَة) | | مَفَاعِيل | مَصَابِيح (pl. of مِصْبَاح) |

    Rule for letters after the Alif of Jama': | Letters after alif | Condition | |---|---| | Two letters | Valid directly (e.g., مَسَاجِد: sīn + jīm + dāl = 2 after alif) | | Three letters | Middle one must be sākin (e.g., مَصَابِيح: bā' sākin) | | One letter | That letter must be mushaddad (doubled), like دَوَابّ |

    This sabab is also qā'im maqām as-sababayn — one sabab (jama' muntaha al-jumūm) is sufficient for ghayr munsarif status; no additional sabab is required.

    Counterexample — رِجَال: - Not on either of the two patterns (mafā'il/mafā'īl) - After the alif, only one letter follows (jīm + lām = not correct count for the pattern) - → Munsarif

    Counterexample — سَيَاكِلَة: - Resembles jama' muntaha al-jumūm, but: 1. The middle of the three post-alif letters is not sākin 2. Tā' marbūtah appears at the end - → Munsarif

    Important nuance — "Muntaha": - Some words form a plural of a plural (e.g., كَلْب → كِلَاب → أَكَالِب). أَكَالِب is not muntaha al-jumūm because another plural (from the first plural) was formed after it. - Jama' muntaha al-jumūm means no further plural can be generated from it — it is the final stage of pluralization.

    6. Tarkīb — Compound Word as a Sabab

    Definition: Two words combined into one, where neither a relation of Izāfah (possessive/genitive construction) nor a relation of Isnād (predicate-subject relation) exists between the two parts.

    Condition for ghayr munsarif via Tarkīb: - The compound must also be an Alam (proper name) - The two components must have NO izāfah and NO isnād between them - → Two asbab: Alamiyyah + Tarkīb → غير منصرف

    Example — بَعْلَبَكَّ: - بَعْل = name of an idol worshipped in ancient times - بَكَّ = name of a person who famously worshipped that idol - Combined: بَعْلَبَكَّ — now the name of a city in Lebanon - No izāfah, no isnād between the two parts → غير منصرف

    Counterexample — عَبْدُ اللَّهِ: - Also a compound/murakkab, but: عَبْد is mudāf, اللَّه is mudāf ilayhi - → Relation of Izāfah exists → condition for Tarkīb NOT met → munsarif

    Counterexample — شَابَ قَرْنَاهَا (a woman's laqab): - شَابَ = fi'l mādi (verb, musnad), قَرْنَاهَا = fā'il (musnad ilayhi) - → Relation of Isnād exists → condition for Tarkīb NOT met → munsarif

    7. Practical Case — إِبْلِيس vs. شَيْطَان

    Word Munsarif? Reason
    إِبْلِيس غير منصرف Alamiyyah (proper name of the original devil) + 'Ujmah (non-Arabic) → two asbab
    شَيْطَان منصرف NOT an alam (general term for any evil jinn) → only one possible sabab, insufficient

    Key rule: Al-Shaytan with ال is ma'rifah but not alam. Alam requires a given, specific name. Since شَيْطَان can refer to any evil jinn (like وَلَد refers to any boy), it cannot be alam.

    I'rab (Sentence Analysis) Examples

    Example 1 — جَاءَ زَيْدٌ - زَيْدٌ: fā'il, marfū', 'alāmah: dammah — it is alam, munsarif, takes tanwin normally

    Example 2 — زُرْتُ مَسَاجِدَ - مَسَاجِدَ: maf'ūl bih, mansūb, 'alāmah: fathah (not kasrah) — ghayr munsarif (jama' muntaha al-jumūm)

    Example 3 — ذَهَبْتُ إِلَى مَسَاجِدَ - مَسَاجِدَ: majrūr, 'alāmah: fathah instead of kasrah — because it is ghayr munsarif

    Example 4 — إِبْرَاهِيمُ رَسُولٌ - إِبْرَاهِيمُ: mubtada', marfū', 'alāmah: dammah — ghayr munsarif (alamiyyah + 'ujmah), but dammah still applies in raf'

    Example 5 — رَأَيْتُ إِبْرَاهِيمَ - إِبْرَاهِيمَ: maf'ūl bih, mansūb, 'alāmah: fathah (no tanwin — ghayr munsarif)

    Example 6 — عَبْدُ اللَّهِ عَالِمٌ - عَبْدُ: mubtada', marfū' — murakkab but has izāfah → munsarif, declines normally

    Example 7 — مَرَرْتُ بِبَعْلَبَكَّ - بَعْلَبَكَّ: majrūr, 'alāmah: fathah — ghayr munsarif (alamiyyah + tarkīb, no izāfah or isnād)

    Practice Examples

    Determine whether each word is munsarif or ghayr munsarif and state the reason:

    1. نُوحُ — Munsarif. It is an alam (alamiyyah = one sabab), but sulāsī with sākin middle letter; the 'ujmah condition for sulāsī requires middle sākin yet combined with a short form it remains too "light" — it is listed among the seven exceptions of Prophet names → munsarif.

    2. جِبْرَائِيلُ — Ghayr munsarif. Two asbab: Alamiyyah (name of an angel) + 'Ujmah (non-Arabic origin).

    3. مَدَارِسُ — Ghayr munsarif. Jama' muntaha al-jumūm on pattern مَفَاعِل; qā'im maqām as-sababayn.

    4. رِجَالٌ — Munsarif. Not on the mafā'il/mafā'īl pattern; after the alif only one letter follows without tashdīd → does not qualify as jama' muntaha al-jumūm.

    5. حَمْرَاءُ — Ghayr munsarif. Alif mamdūda = qā'im maqām as-sababayn; no additional sabab needed.

    6. شَيْطَانٌ — Munsarif. Not an alam (it is a common noun applicable to any evil jinn); without alamiyyah as a sabab, no two asbab can be formed.

    7. إِبْلِيسُ — Ghayr munsarif. Alamiyyah + 'Ujmah (non-Arabic proper name for the original Shaytan).

    8. دَوَابُّ — Ghayr munsarif. Jama' muntaha al-jumūm; one letter after alif but that letter is mushaddad (condition met).

    9. صَالِحٌ — Munsarif. Among the seven Prophet names that are Arabic (not 'ujmah) → only alamiyyah = one sabab → munsarif.

    10. مَالِكٌ (angel) — Munsarif. Arabic name, not 'ujmah → only alamiyyah = one sabab → munsarif. One of the three angel-name exceptions.

    Key Takeaways

    1. Two tiers of asbab: Most ghayr munsarif cases require two asbab combined. But certain asbab are so weighty they stand alone (qā'im maqām as-sababayn): Alif Maqsūra, Alif Mamdūda, and Jama' Muntaha al-Jumūm each singly make a word ghayr munsarif.

    2. Sulāsī exception for 'Ujmah: A non-Arabic proper name with only three letters (sulāsī) and a sākin middle letter is treated as munsarif — its lightness offsets the 'ujmah condition. This explains why نُوح and لُوط are munsarif despite being non-Arabic names of Prophets.

    3. Alam ≠ Ma'rifah: This distinction is critical. Every alam is ma'rifah, but not every ma'rifah is alam. For the sabab of alamiyyah to apply, the word must be a given name — not merely a definite or contextually specific noun. This is why شَيْطَان is munsarif but إِبْلِيس is not.

    4. Tarkīb requires no izāfah and no isnād: A compound word qualifies as a tarkīb sabab only when the two parts have no grammatical relationship (possessive or predicate) between them. عَبْدُ اللَّهِ fails this test (izāfah present); شَابَ قَرْنَاهَا fails it (isnād present). بَعْلَبَكَّ passes it.

    5. Jama' Muntaha al-Jumūm must be on the exact pattern: The word must actually be a plural on مَفَاعِل or مَفَاعِيل, with the post-alif letter count and voweling matching exactly. Superficial resemblance (like سَيَاكِلَة) does not qualify.

    6. Names of Angels and Prophets — default is ghayr munsarif: Because these names are generally non-Arabic proper nouns (alamiyyah + 'ujmah = two asbab). The exceptions are specifically Arabic names: three among angel names (مَالِك، مُنْكَر، نَكِير) and seven among Prophet names (مُحَمَّد، شُعَيْب، صَالِح، هُود، لُوط، نُوح، أَشِيس).

    7. Two asbab remain: Alif Nun Zā'idatān and Wazn al-Fi'l are to be covered in the next session — these will complete the full list of seven asbab for ghayr munsarif.
