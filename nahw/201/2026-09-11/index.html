    Nahw 201 - Study Notes

    Date: 2026-09-11

    Session Overview

    This session covered the ninth sabab (cause) for ghayr munsarif: Wazn al-Fi'l (being on the pattern of a verb). The class also revisited Alif Noon Zaidatan in detail, distinguishing between its occurrence in ism vs. wasf, and reviewed the exception rule that allows ghayr munsarif words to accept kasra when made muzaf or prefixed with alif-lam. The final portion addressed when a ghayr munsarif word becomes munsarif upon being made nakira.

    Grammar Rules Covered

    1. Alif Noon Zaidatan — Recap

    Definition: Alif and Noon at the end of a word that are extra (zaid) — not from the root letters (huruf asli) of the word.

    Two scenarios based on word type:

    A. If the word is an Ism (Noun):

    • The second sabab must be Alamiyya (being a proper noun/name).
    • Both conditions (Alif Noon Zaidatan + Alamiyya) = Ghayr Munsarif.
    • Example: Imran — root is ع-م-ر, so Alif Noon is zaid + it is a proper name → Ghayr Munsarif.
    • Example: Sa'dan — Alif Noon is zaid, but if it refers to a plant (not a person's name), it is Munsarif because the second condition (Alamiyya) is absent. If used as a person's name, then Ghayr Munsarif.

    B. If the word is a Wasf (Adjective/Descriptor):

    • The second sabab is the wasf itself (no need to find a separate sabab).
    • Condition: Its mu'annas (feminine form) must not come on the pattern of Fa'lana.
    • Sakran → Ghayr Munsarif: mu'annas is Sakra (on Fa'la, not Fa'lana).
    • Nadman → Munsarif: mu'annas is Nadmana (on Fa'lana) → second condition fails.
    • Rahman → Ghayr Munsarif: on pattern of Fa'lan, it is wasf, mu'annas is Rahma (not Fa'lana).
    • Ghazban → Ghayr Munsarif: same reasoning.

    Key clarification: The Alif Noon is added, not part of the root. If the Alif Noon is not zaid (i.e., it is from the root), the word is Munsarif regardless of whether it is an alam. Example: Shaytan — Alif Noon is from the root (ش-ط-ن), so it is Munsarif.

    2. Wazn al-Fi'l (Ninth Sabab for Ghayr Munsarif)

    Definition: An ism that falls on a pattern (wazan) that is exclusive to or derived from a verb (fi'l).

    The rule (from the matn):

    Its condition is that the pattern must be khas (exclusive) to the verb, or if common between ism and fi'l, the word must begin with one of the Huruf al-Mudara'a (أ ت ي ن) and must not end with a Ta Marbuta.

    Two categories of awzan:

    A. Awzan Khas with Fi'l (exclusive to verbs):

    If an ism falls on a pattern that belongs only to verbs, then just having Alamiyya is sufficient as the second sabab.

    Most common patterns in this category: - Fu'ila (فُعِلَ) — passive madi - Fa''ala (فَعَّلَ) — with shadda on 'ayn, bab taf'il

    Examples: - Zuriba (ضُرِبَ) → if used as a person's name: Alam + Wazn al-Fi'l → Ghayr Munsarif - Shammara (شَمَّرَ) → means "to prepare/ready oneself"; named after a horse, then became a proper name → Alam + Wazn al-Fi'l → Ghayr Munsarif - Du'ila (دُئِلَ) → name of a tribe, on pattern of Fu'ila → Ghayr Munsarif

    B. Awzan Common Between Ism and Fi'l:

    If a pattern is shared by both isms and fi'ls, then the ism must satisfy both of the following to be Ghayr Munsarif: 1. Alamiyya (proper noun) 2. Begin with one of the Huruf al-Mudara'a (أ ت ي ن — remembered by the word أَتَيْنَا) 3. No Ta Marbuta at the end

    Examples: - Ahmad (أَحْمَد) — Alam, begins with Hamza (من أتينا), no Ta Marbuta → Ghayr Munsarif - Yashkur (يَشْكُر) — used as Alam, begins with Ya → Ghayr Munsarif - Taglib (تَغْلِب) — Alam (name of tribe), begins with Ta → Ghayr Munsarif - Narjis (نَرْجِس) — Alam, begins with Noon → Ghayr Munsarif - Ya'mal (يَعْمَل) — Munsarif, because its feminine Naqah Ya'malah ends with Ta Marbuta → fails condition

    Important: The plural forms أَفْعَلُوا، يَفْعَلُوا، نَفْعَلُوا، تَفْعَلُوا are common awzan (not khas with fi'l), so they require the Huruf al-Mudara'a condition. Do not treat them as khas.

    Case Study — Yazid (يَزِيد):

    • Yazid is the fi'l mudari', first siga (يَفْعَل pattern) of the root ز-ي-د.
    • When used as an ism/alam (a person's name), it satisfies: Alam + Wazn al-Fi'l (yaf'al with Ya) → Ghayr Munsarif.
    • Note: The kasra under the Zai in Yazid is due to the natural demand of Ya (a harf 'illah) for kasra before it — this is a phonological smoothing rule and does not disqualify it from the pattern.

    3. Exception: Ghayr Munsarif Accepting Kasra

    By default: Ghayr Munsarif words do not accept kasra or tanwin in the jar case — they use fatha instead.

    Exception — two scenarios that allow kasra:

    1. Idafa (Muzaf): If a ghayr munsarif word is made muzaf (possessive construction), it accepts kasra.
    2. Example: مَرَرْتُ بِمَدَارِسِكُمْ (I passed by your madaris)

    3. Example: مَرَرْتُ بِمَسَاجِدِكُمْ

    4. Alif Lam: If alif-lam is added to a ghayr munsarif word, it accepts kasra.

    5. Example: مَرَرْتُ بِالْمَسَاجِدِ
    6. Example: مَرَرْتُ بِالْمَدَارِسِ
    7. Quranic example: وَلَا تُبَاشِرُوهُنَّ وَأَنْتُمْ عَاكِفُونَ فِي الْمَسَاجِدِ — كسرة on مَسَاجِد because of alif-lam.

    Matn reference: وَكُلُّ مَا لَا يَنصَرِفُ إِذَا أُضِيفَ — every word that is ghayr munsarif accepts kasra in the jar case when made muzaf or prefixed with alif-lam.

    4. Ghayr Munsarif Becoming Munsarif Upon Tankir (Indefinitization)

    Rule: A ghayr munsarif alam becomes munsarif when it is made nakira (indefinite), because it loses the sabab of Alamiyya.

    When is an alam made nakira? When it is used not as a reference to a specific known individual, but as a reference to a type or character.

    • Example: لِكُلِّ فِرْعَوْنَ مُوسَى
    • Here, Fir'aun does not refer to the historical Fir'aun specifically; it refers to the character/archetype of Fir'aun (i.e., oppressor, tyrant).
    • Similarly, Musa refers to the character of the one who fights for truth.

    • Therefore, both words are treated as nakira → they become Munsarif → accept tanwin and kasra.

    • Example: لِكُلِّ بَاطِلٍ حَقٌّ

    This applies to any ghayr munsarif word — not just those on Mafa'il/Mafa'il patterns.

    I'rab (Sentence Analysis) Examples

    1. مَرَرْتُ بِأَحْمَدَ - بِ: harf jarr - أَحْمَدَ: ism majrur, but with fatha instead of kasra because it is ghayr munsarif (Alam + Wazn al-Fi'l, on pattern Af'al with Hamza prefix)

    2. مَرَرْتُ بِمَدَارِسِكُمْ - مَدَارِسِ: ghayr munsarif (Jumu' Muntaha al-Jumu'), but accepts kasra here because it is muzaf - كُمْ: mudaf ilayhi

    3. وَأَنْتُمْ عَاكِفُونَ فِي الْمَسَاجِدِ - الْمَسَاجِدِ: ghayr munsarif (Jumu' Muntaha al-Jumu'), but accepts kasra because of alif-lam

    4. لِكُلِّ فِرْعَوْنَ مُوسَى - فِرْعَوْن: ghayr munsarif by origin (Alam + Ajam), but here used as nakira (archetype), so it becomes munsarif → takes tanwin in full usage

    5. يَزِيدُ (as a verb) vs. يَزِيد (as an alam) - As fi'l mudari': يَزِيدُ زَيْدٌ — verb, subject is Zayd - As alam: اسم شخص على وزن يَفْعَل → Alam + Wazn al-Fi'l → Ghayr Munsarif

    Key Takeaways

    1. Alif Noon Zaidatan requires confirmation that the Alif Noon are truly extra — always check the root letters first. If they are root letters (like Shaytan from ش-ط-ن), the word is Munsarif regardless.

    2. Two paths for Alif Noon Zaidatan: If in an ism, the second sabab must be Alamiyya. If in a wasf, the wasf itself is the second sabab — but only if the feminine form does not come on the pattern Fa'lana.

    3. Wazn al-Fi'l has two sub-rules: Awzan khas with the verb need only Alamiyya as a second sabab. Awzan common to both ism and fi'l additionally require the word to begin with one of أَتَيْنَا and not end with Ta Marbuta.

    4. The most common verb-exclusive patterns are Fu'ila and Fa''ala. The plural prefixed forms (أفعلوا، يفعلوا etc.) are not khas — they are common awzan.

    5. Ghayr Munsarif always accepts kasra (and tanwin) in two situations: when it is in an idafa construction, or when prefixed with alif-lam.

    6. A ghayr munsarif alam becomes munsarif when used as nakira — i.e., when it no longer refers to a specific individual but to a type or character (e.g., لِكُلِّ فِرْعَوْنَ مُوسَى).

    7. Huruf al-Mudara'a = أ ت ي ن — memorize these four as أَتَيْنَا. They are critical for determining whether a common-pattern word qualifies as Wazn al-Fi'l.
