    Nahw 201 - Study Notes

    Date: 2026-11-02

    Session Overview

    This session continued the study of Mansubat (منصوبات), covering two topics: a review and reinforcement of Hal (حال) with student practice exercises, and an introduction to Tamiz (تمييز). The teacher drilled Hal through live translation exercises with students, then transitioned to Tamiz, explaining its definition, purpose, types, and Quranic examples. The session ended with a preview of Mustasna as the next topic.

    Grammar Rules Covered

    1. Hal (حال) — Review and Reinforcement

    Definition: Hal is a Mansub (منصوب) word that describes the condition (هيئة, Hay'ah) of the Fa'il (فاعل) or Maf'ul (مفعول) at the time an action occurs.

    Two elements: - Zulhal (ذو الحال): The doer or object whose condition is being described - Hal (حال): The word describing that condition

    Key rules: - Hal is always Nakirah (نكرة) — indefinite - Zulhal is usually Ma'rifah (معرفة) — definite - If Zulhal is Nakirah, the order must be reversed — Hal comes first, Zulhal comes second - Reason: To avoid confusion with Mawsuf-Sifah (موصوف-صفة) construction - Example: جَاءَ رَاكِبًا رَجُلٌ (not رَجُلٌ رَاكِبًا, which would look like Mawsuf-Sifah) - Hal must agree with Zulhal in gender (muzakkar/mu'annas) and number (wahid, tathniyah, jama') - The 'Amil (عامل — the governing verb) may be dropped when a Qarinah (قرينة, contextual indicator) makes the meaning clear - Example: سَالِمًا غَانِمًا (said to a traveler, implying تَرْجِعُ — "May you return safe and victorious")

    Forms of Hal: - Mufrad (مفرد): A single word, e.g., جَاءَ زَيْدٌ رَاكِبًا - Jumlah Khabariyyah (جملة خبرية): A full declarative sentence used as Hal - Example: جَاءَنِي رَجُلٌ وَغُلَامُهُ رَاكِبٌ / يَرْكَبُ

    Agreement rules for Hal (student drill summary):

    Subject Hal Form Example
    Single male فاعل مفرد مذكر ذَكَرْتُ اللهَ جَالِسًا
    Single female فاعل مفرد مؤنث ذَكَرْتُ اللهَ جَالِسَةً
    Two males مثنى مذكر ذَكَرْنَا اللهَ جَالِسَيْنِ
    Two females مثنى مؤنث ذَكَرْنَا اللهَ جَالِسَتَيْنِ
    Group of women جمع مؤنث ذَكَرْنَا اللهَ جَالِسَاتٍ

    2. Tamiz (تمييز) — Introduction

    Definition: Tamiz is a Nakirah Mansub (نكرة منصوب) that comes to remove Ibham (إبهام) — ambiguity — regarding: - Adad (عدد): Number - Wazan (وزن): Weight - Kail (كيل): Volume/measure - Masahat (مساحة): Area/surface measure

    Key terms: - Mumayyaz (مُمَيَّز): The word (or sentence) containing the ambiguity — what is being clarified - Tamiz (تمييز): The clarifying word that removes the ambiguity

    Position of Tamiz: - Usually comes after the Mumayyaz - Can come after a single word (e.g., عِشْرُونَ — ambiguous number) or after a full Jumlah (e.g., طَابَ زَيْدٌ — ambiguous sentence)

    Rules on case: - When Tamiz is about Miqdar (مقدار) — weight, measure, number — it is Mansub (منصوب) - When Tamiz is about other than Miqdar (e.g., material or substance), it is more commonly used as Majrur (مجرور) — الخفض فيه أكثر - Example: هَذَا خَاتَمُ حَدِيدٍ (ring of iron — Majrur) vs. هَذَا خَاتَمٌ حَدِيدًا (Mansub form)

    Tamiz vs. Khabar: - Khabar also provides information and removes ambiguity in a broad sense - Tamiz is specifically focused on Miqdar (quantity, weight, volume, area, number) or specifying the nature/substance of something - Example: زَيْدٌ كَثِيرٌ alone is Khabar; زَيْدٌ كَثِيرٌ إِيمَانًا — here إِيمَانًا is Tamiz specifying in what way Zaid is abundant

    Arabic Term Meaning

    ذُو الْحَال (Zulhal) The person/thing whose condition is described; the subject of Hal

    I'rab (Sentence Analysis) Examples

    Example 1: جَاءَ زَيْدٌ رَاكِبًا - جَاءَ — Fi'l Madi (past verb) - زَيْدٌ — Fa'il, Marfu' - رَاكِبًا — Hal, Mansub. Zulhal is زَيْدٌ (Ma'rifah, so normal order maintained)

    Example 2: جَاءَنِي رَاكِبًا رَجُلٌ - رَاكِبًا — Hal, Mansub, comes first because Zulhal (رَجُلٌ) is Nakirah - رَجُلٌ — Zulhal, Fa'il, Marfu' (order reversed to avoid Mawsuf-Sifah confusion)

    Example 3: جَاءَنِي رَجُلٌ وَغُلَامُهُ رَاكِبٌ - وَغُلَامُهُ رَاكِبٌ — Hal as Jumlah Khabariyyah (غلامه = Mubtada, راكب = Khabar)

    Example 4: ذَكَرْتُ اللهَ جَالِسًا - ذَكَرْتُ — Fi'l + Fa'il - اللهَ — Maf'ul bihi, Mansub - جَالِسًا — Hal, Mansub, describes the condition of the Fa'il (the speaker, male singular)

    Example 5: عِنْدِي عِشْرُونَ دِرْهَمًا - عِنْدِي — Khabar (Jar-Majrur, in pre-verbal position) - عِشْرُونَ — Mubtada (Mumayyaz — contains Ibham: "20 of what?") - دِرْهَمًا — Tamiz, Mansub, removes Ibham (clarifies: 20 dirhams)

    Example 6: قَفِيزَانِ بُرًّا - قَفِيزَانِ — Mumayyaz (two Qafiz — of what?) - بُرًّا — Tamiz, Mansub (wheat — clarifies the two Qafiz are of wheat)

    Example 7: هَذَا خَاتَمٌ حَدِيدًا / خَاتَمُ حَدِيدٍ - Tamiz is not about Miqdar here (it specifies material) - Mansub form: حَدِيدًا — Tamiz - Majrur form: حَدِيدٍ — more common when Tamiz is non-Miqdar (خفض أكثر)

    Example 8: طَابَ زَيْدٌ نَفْسًا / عِلْمًا / إِيمَانًا - طَابَ زَيْدٌ — full Jumlah as Mumayyaz (whole sentence has Ibham: good in what way?) - نَفْسًا / عِلْمًا / إِيمَانًا — Tamiz after Jumlah, Mansub

    Example 9: وَفَجَّرْنَا الْأَرْضَ عُيُونًا (Surah Qamar) - الْأَرْضَ — Mumayyaz (Maf'ul bihi, Mansub) - عُيُونًا — Tamiz, Mansub — clarifies the nature of the outburst (springs)

    Key Takeaways

    1. Hal always agrees with Zulhal in gender and number — this is the most practically important rule for forming correct Hal constructions.

    2. Order rule: Normal order is Zulhal → Hal. If Zulhal is Nakirah, reverse the order (Hal first) to prevent confusion with Mawsuf-Sifah.

    3. Hal can be Mufrad or Jumlah Khabariyyah — both are valid; the choice depends on what the speaker wants to express.

    4. Tamiz exists to remove Ibham (ambiguity) — whenever a number, weight, volume, or area is mentioned without specifying what, Tamiz comes as a Nakirah Mansub to clarify it.

    5. Mumayyaz can be a single word or a full sentence — e.g., عِشْرُونَ (single word with Ibham) or طَابَ زَيْدٌ (full sentence with Ibham).

    6. Tamiz is usually Mansub when it follows Miqdar words; it tends to be Majrur when clarifying substance/material (non-Miqdar).

    7. Tamiz vs. Khabar: Khabar gives general information; Tamiz specifically resolves quantitative or material ambiguity. When you see a Nakirah Mansub following a word of quantity/measure, that is Tamiz.
