    Nahw 102 - Study Notes

    Date: 2026-06-29

    Session Overview

    This session covered section 3.10.3: البدل (Al-Badal) — the third major topic under التوابع (tawabi', the "followers" in Arabic grammar). Building on the previous class's coverage of تأكيد (ta'keed/emphasis), this session introduced the concept of badal (substitution), defined its four types with examples, and worked through a class exercise identifying each type. The next session will cover عطف النسق (atf al-nasq) and عطف البيان (atf al-bayan), sections 3.10.4.

    Grammar Rules Covered

    1. What is البدل (Al-Badal)?

    • Badal is a تابع (tabi') — the second word that follows another word
    • The word being followed is the مطبوع / مبدل منه (matboo'/mubdal minhu — "the substituted")
    • The badal is the intended word in the sentence; the mubdal minhu merely serves as an introduction to it

    Key distinction (two angles): - In terms of meaning: The mubdal minhu (first word) is secondary; the badal (second word) is the primary, intended meaning - In terms of I'rab: The badal follows the I'rab of the mubdal minhu — whatever case the first word is in, the badal must match it

    Terminology summary:

    Term Role
    تابع (Tabi') The badal — the second word; the intended one
    مطبوع / مبدل منه (Matboo' / Mubdal Minhu) The first word; serves as introduction

    2. How Badal Differs from صفة/موصوف (Sifah/Mawsoof)

    • Sifah (adjective) and mawsoof (the described noun) must agree in four things (gender, number, definiteness, case)
    • Badal and mubdal minhu do not need to agree in those four things (e.g., they can differ in gender)
    • Also, the badal is not a description of the mubdal minhu — it refers to the same entity, a part of it, or is otherwise related to it

    Example: زَيْدٌ أَخُوكَ — أَخُوك cannot be a sifah for زيد grammatically (because زَيْدٌ الأَخُ would be required for sifah), so it can only be badal

    3. The Four Types of Badal

    Type 1: بَدَلُ الكُلّ مِنَ الكُلّ (Badalul Kull — Full Substitution)

    • The badal refers to the exact same entity as the mubdal minhu — no difference whatsoever between them
    • Example:

    جَاءَ زَيْدٌ أَخُوكَ Zayd, your brother, came. - زيد = mubdal minhu (introduces the intended word) - أَخُوكَ = badal (the intended, primary meaning) - Both refer to the same person entirely

    Type 2: بَدَلُ البَعْض مِنَ الكُلّ (Badalul Ba'dh — Partial Substitution)

    • The badal refers to a part of the mubdal minhu
    • Rule: The badal must contain a ضمير (dhamir/pronoun) that refers back to the mubdal minhu, and that dhamir must agree in gender and number with the mubdal minhu
    • Example:

    ضَرَبْتُ زَيْدًا رَأْسَهُ I hit Zaid's head. - زيدًا = mubdal minhu - رَأْسَهُ = badal (رأس is part of Zaid; هُ refers back to زيد) - More concise equivalent: ضَرَبْتُ رَأْسَ زَيْدٍ

    Type 3: بَدَلُ الاشتِمَال (Badalul Ishtimal — Encompassing Substitution)

    • The badal is related to the mubdal minhu but is not a part of it (not a body part or physical portion)
    • Rule: Same as badalul ba'dh — the badal must contain a ضمير referring back to the mubdal minhu
    • اشتمال = to encompass/include → Zaid's "sphere" encompasses his shirt, his honesty, etc.
    • Example:

    سُرِقَ زَيْدٌ قَمِيصُهُ Zaid's shirt was stolen. - زَيْدٌ = na'ib al-fa'il (mubdal minhu); in rafa' case - قَمِيصُهُ = badal (also rafa' to match); هُ refers back to زيد - قميص is not a body part of Zaid — it is related to him (his property)

    Key distinction — ba'dh vs. ishtimal: - Ba'dh: badal is a physical part of the mubdal minhu (e.g., head, hand) - Ishtimal: badal is related to but not a physical part of the mubdal minhu (e.g., honesty, shirt, light)

    Type 4: بَدَلُ الغَلَط (Badalul Ghalat — Correction Substitution)

    • The badal is mentioned to correct an error made by the mubdal minhu — the speaker said one word and immediately corrects with another
    • No ضمير required; the two words refer to completely different entities
    • In Arabic, no لا or negation is needed — the correction word is simply added
    • This type is identified by elimination: if the badal is not kull, ba'dh, or ishtimal, it must be ghalat
    • Example:

    اشْتَرَيْتُ فَرَسًا حِمَارًا I bought a horse — (correction:) a donkey. - فَرَسًا = mubdal minhu (the erroneous word) - حِمَارًا = badal (the correction) - Both are maf'ul bih of اشتريت - حمار ≠ فرس in any way → cannot be kull, ba'dh, or ishtimal → must be ghalat

    4. General I'rab Rule for Badal

    Whatever I'rab case the مبدل منه carries, the بدل must carry the same case. - Mubdal minhu in رفع → Badal in رفع - Mubdal minhu in نصب → Badal in نصب - Mubdal minhu in جر → Badal in جر

    Important Vocabulary

    Arabic Term Meaning
    البَدَل Substitute / substitution
    المُبْدَل مِنْه / المُبْدَل مِنْهُ The substituted (first word; the one being replaced/introduced)
    التَّابِع The follower (the badal — second word)
    المَطْبُوع The followed / the one being followed (same as mubdal minhu)
    التَّوَابِع The followers — grammatical category including na't, ta'keed, badal, atf
    بَدَلُ الكُلّ مِنَ الكُلّ Full substitution — badal = same entity as mubdal minhu
    بَدَلُ البَعْض مِنَ الكُلّ Partial substitution — badal is a part of the mubdal minhu
    بَدَلُ الاشتِمَال Encompassing substitution — badal is related to (but not part of) mubdal minhu
    بَدَلُ الغَلَط Correction substitution — badal corrects an error in the mubdal minhu
    الضَّمِير (dhamir) Pronoun — required in badal al-ba'dh and badal al-ishtimal
    نَائِب الفَاعِل Substitute of the doer (subject of passive verb) — takes rafa' case
    الاشتِمَال Encompassing / including
    الغَلَط Error / mistake

    I'rab (Sentence Analysis) Examples

    Example 1: بَدَلُ الكُلّ

    جَاءَ زَيْدٌ أَخُوكَ

    Word I'rab
    جَاءَ فعل ماضٍ
    زَيْدٌ فاعل، مرفوع، علامته الضمة — also مبدل منه
    أَخُوكَ بدل كل من كل، مرفوع، علامته الواو (أخو) — تابع لزيد في الرفع

    Example 2: بَدَلُ البَعْض

    ضَرَبْتُ زَيْدًا رَأْسَهُ

    Word I'rab
    ضَرَبْتُ فعل وفاعل
    زَيْدًا مفعول به، منصوب — مبدل منه
    رَأْسَهُ بدل بعض من كل، منصوب (following زيدًا) — الهاء: ضمير يعود على زيد

    Example 3: بَدَلُ الاشتِمَال

    سُرِقَ زَيْدٌ قَمِيصُهُ

    Word I'rab
    سُرِقَ فعل ماضٍ مبني للمجهول
    زَيْدٌ نائب الفاعل، مرفوع — مبدل منه
    قَمِيصُهُ بدل اشتمال، مرفوع (following زيدٌ) — الهاء: ضمير يعود على زيد

    Example 4: بَدَلُ الغَلَط

    اشْتَرَيْتُ فَرَسًا حِمَارًا

    Word I'rab
    اشْتَرَيْتُ فعل وفاعل
    فَرَسًا مفعول به، منصوب — مبدل منه (erroneous word)
    حِمَارًا بدل غلط، منصوب (following فرسًا)

    Practice Examples

    The class worked through the following exercise, identifying the type of badal in each sentence:

    Sentence Translation Badal Type Reasoning
    أَدَّيْتُ دَيْنِي ثُلُثَهُ I paid off one-third of my debt بَدَلُ البَعْض ثلثه is a part of الدَّيْن; contains ه referring back to دَيْن
    أَعْجَبَنِي الخَادِمُ أَمَانَتُهُ The servant's honesty pleased me بَدَلُ الاشتِمَال أمانة is related to الخادم but not a physical part of him; contains ه
    ذَهَبَ السُّيَّاحُ أَكْثَرُهُمْ إِلَى الوَادِي Most of the tourists went to the valley بَدَلُ البَعْض أكثرهم is a part of السياح; contains هم
    سَطَعَ القَمَرُ نُورُهُ The moon's light shone بَدَلُ الاشتِمَال نور is related to القمر (not a physical part); contains ه
    سَرَّنَا الشَّوَارِعُ نَظَافَتُهَا The streets' cleanliness pleased us بَدَلُ الاشتِمَال نظافة is related to الشوارع; contains ها
    قَدِمَ الأَمِيرُ الوَزِيرُ The minister (not the commander) arrived بَدَلُ الغَلَط الوزير ≠ الأمير; not same, not part, not related → correction
    عَامَلْتُ التَّاجِرَ زَيْدًا I dealt with the merchant, Zaid بَدَلُ الكُلّ زيد and التاجر refer to the same person entirely
    تَمَزَّقَ الكِتَابُ غِلَافُهُ The book's cover tore بَدَلُ الاشتِمَال غلاف (fabric cover/sleeve) is related to but not a physical part of الكتاب; contains ه
    أَغْلَقَتْ عَائِشَةُ الحَدِيقَةَ بَابَهَا Aisha closed the garden gate بَدَلُ البَعْض الباب is a physical part of الحديقة; contains ها
    كَانَ أَبُو حَامِدٍ الغَزَالِيُّ مُجَدِّدًا Abu Hamid Al-Ghazali was a revivalist بَدَلُ الكُلّ الغزاليّ and أبو حامد refer to the same person; الغزاليّ follows I'rab of أبو (not حامد which is mudaf ilayhi)

    I'rab note on أبو حامد الغزاليّ: أبو is فاعل/اسم كان (in رفع), حامد is مضاف إليه (in جر). الغزاليّ is badal of أبو — so it takes the رفع case of أبو, not the جر case of حامد.

    Key Takeaways

    1. Badal is the intended word — the mubdal minhu is merely an introduction. This is the opposite of how sifah/mawsoof works, where the sifah describes and qualifies the mawsoof.

    2. I'rab always follows the mubdal minhu — whatever case the first word carries, the badal must match it.

    3. Ba'dh and Ishtimal both require a ضمير in the badal that refers back to the mubdal minhu, agreeing in gender and number.

    4. The distinction between ba'dh and ishtimal is physical vs. relational:

    5. Ba'dh: badal is a physical part (body part, fraction, portion)

    6. Ishtimal: badal is related to but not physically part of the mubdal minhu (qualities, possessions, attributes)

    7. Ghalat is identified by elimination — if none of the first three types apply, the badal is a correction substitution.

    8. Badal ≠ Sifah — unlike sifah/mawsoof which require agreement in four things (gender, number, definiteness, case), badal and mubdal minhu do not need to agree in gender or other grammatical features beyond I'rab case.

    9. Next session (3.10.4): عطف النسق (Atf al-Nasq) and عطف البيان (Atf al-Bayan) — scheduled to begin after Maghrib at 9:15 PM.
