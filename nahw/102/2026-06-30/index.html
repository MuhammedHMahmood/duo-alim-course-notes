    Nahw 102 - Study Notes

    Date: 2026-06-30

    Session Overview

    This session covered ʿAtf bi-Ḥarfin (عطف بحرف), also known as ʿAtfun Nasaq — conjunction using a particle. The lesson introduced the concept of grammatical conjunction in Arabic, its core terminology, and a detailed walkthrough of all major particles of conjunction (ḥurūf al-ʿatf) with examples and i'rāb analysis. A short oral exercise was conducted at the end to reinforce the lesson.

    Grammar Rules Covered

    1. Definition of ʿAtf (عطف)

    ʿAtf is a type of tābiʿ (a dependent/following word) that appears after a ḥarf al-ʿatf (particle of conjunction). The particle is inserted between the first word and the second word. The ʿāmil (governing factor) that applies to the first word also applies to the second, and both words share the same i'rāb (case ending).

    Key terms: - Maʿṭūf ʿalayh (مَعْطُوف عَلَيْه) — the first word (the matbūʿ, the one followed) - Maʿṭūf (مَعْطُوف) — the second word (the tābiʿ, the following word) - Ḥarf al-ʿatf (حَرْف العَطْف) — the particle of conjunction placed between them

    Rule: Whatever i'rāb the maʿṭūf ʿalayh has, the maʿṭūf must share the same i'rāb.

    2. The Particles of Conjunction (ḥurūf al-ʿatf)

    وَ (Wāw) — "and"

    • Used for simple conjunction (ishtirāk).
    • Does not indicate sequence (tartīb). Mentioning Zayd before Bakr does not mean Zayd acted before Bakr.
    • Example: جَاءَ زَيْدٌ وَعَمْرٌ — Zayd and ʿAmr came.

    فَ (Fāʾ) — "then" / "thus"

    • Indicates immediate sequence (tartīb with no delay): the first mentioned acted first, the second acted immediately after.
    • Also conveys causality: what comes before the fāʾ is the cause; what comes after is the effect.
    • Quranic example: أَنْزَلَ مِنَ السَّمَاءِ مَاءً فَتُصْبِحُ الْأَرْضُ مُخْضَرَّةً — He sends water from the sky, therefore the earth becomes green. (The water is the cause; the greening is the effect.)

    ثُمَّ (Thumma) — "then" (with delay)

    • Indicates sequence with a delay between the two actions.
    • Use thumma when the second action came some time after the first. If the second action was immediate, use fāʾ instead.
    • The distinction between fāʾ and thumma is linguistically significant and has implications in uṣūl al-fiqh (the ruling can change based on which particle is used in an āyah).

    حَتَّى (Ḥattā) — "until" / "till" / "even"

    • Conveys the meaning of an endpoint (ghāya).
    • The maʿṭūf introduced by ḥattā represents the furthest/most extreme point of what has been mentioned.
    • Example 1: قَرَأْتُ الْقُرْآنَ حَتَّى آخِرَهُ — I read the Quran until its end.
    • Example 2: قَدِمَ الْحُجَّاجُ حَتَّى الْمُشَاةُ — The pilgrims came, even those on foot.

    إِمَّا (Immā) — "either…or"

    • Used in pairs: the first إِمَّا means "either," the second means "or."
    • Structure: إِمَّا [maʿṭūf ʿalayh] وَإِمَّا [maʿṭūf]
    • Example: الثَّمَرُ إِمَّا حُلْوٌ وَإِمَّا مُرٌّ — The fruit is either sweet or bitter.

    أَوْ (Aw) — "or"

    • Used for doubt or choice in a non-interrogative context (general statements).
    • Example (from Sūrat al-Kahf): لَبِثْنَا يَوْمًا أَوْ بَعْضَ يَوْمٍ — We stayed for a day or part of a day.

    أَمْ (Am) — "or" (in questions)

    • Used for or specifically within interrogative (istifhām) sentences.
    • Wh en asking a question involving an alternative, use أَمْ not أَوْ.
    • Example: أَزَيْدٌ عِنْدَكَ أَمْ بَكْرٌ؟ — Is Zayd with you or Bakr?

    لَا (Lā) — "not"

    • Used to negate the ruling from the maʿṭūf and confine it to the maʿṭūf ʿalayh.
    • Example: جَاءَنِي زَيْدٌ لَا بَكْرٌ — Zayd came to me, not Bakr. (Zayd came; Bakr did not.)

    بَلْ (Bal) — "rather" / "but instead"

    • Used for iḍrāb — abandoning one notion and replacing it with another.
    • After a positive sentence or command: negates the ruling for what is before it and affirms it for what is after it.
    • Example: جَاءَنِي زَيْدٌ بَلْ بَكْرٌ — Zayd came to me — rather, Bakr (came). (Zayd did not come; Bakr did.)
    • After a negation (nafi) or prohibition (nahy): confirms the negation for what is before it and affirms the opposite (i.e., the positive) for what comes after.
    • Example: مَا قَرَأْتُ الْكِتَابَ كُلَّهُ بَلْ بَعْضَهُ — I did not read the whole book; rather, I read part of it. (Negates reading the whole; affirms reading a portion.)

    لَكِنْ (Lākin) — "but" / "however"

    • Used for istidrāk — rectification of a possible misunderstanding.
    • After making a statement that might lead the listener to a wrong conclusion, لَكِنْ corrects that assumption.
    • Example: مَا جَاءَ زَيْدٌ لَكِنْ بَكْرٌ — Zayd did not come; however, Bakr came. (Corrects the assumption that neither came.)

    Important Vocabulary

    Arabic Term Meaning
    عَطْف (ʿAtf) Conjunction; a grammatical category where two words are linked by a particle
    عَطْف بِحَرْفٍ / عَطْفُ نَسَق Conjunction by particle (the full technical term)
    حَرْف العَطْف (ḥarf al-ʿatf) Particle of conjunction
    مَعْطُوف عَلَيْه (maʿṭūf ʿalayh) The first word (the one being followed); the matbūʿ
    مَعْطُوف (maʿṭūf) The second word (the tābiʿ; the one that follows)
    تَابِع (tābiʿ) A dependent/following word that takes its i'rāb from another
    مَتْبُوع (matbūʿ) The word that is followed; the base word
    عَامِل (ʿāmil) The governing factor that assigns i'rāb to a word
    تَرْتِيب (tartīb) Sequence/order
    إِضْرَاب (iḍrāb) Abandoning one notion in favour of another (function of بَلْ)
    اسْتِدْرَاك (istidrāk) Rectification of a potential misunderstanding (function of لَكِنْ)
    اسْتِفْهَام (istifhām) Interrogation / asking a question
    نَافِيَة (nāfiya) Negating particle
    نَهْي (nahy) Prohibition
    غَايَة (ghāya) Endpoint; the furthest extent of something (function of حَتَّى)
    إِعْرَاب (i'rāb) Grammatical inflection / case-ending analysis
    فَاعِل (fāʿil) Subject (doer of a verb); in the rafʿ case
    مُبْتَدَأ (mubtadaʾ) Subject of a nominal sentence
    خَبَر (khabar) Predicate of a nominal sentence
    مَفْعُول فِيهِ / ظَرْف (maʿfūl fīhi / ẓarf) Adverb of time or place
    مُضَاف / مُضَاف إِلَيْه (muḍāf / muḍāf ilayh) First/second term of a genitive construct
    الْمُشَاة (al-mushāt) Those on foot; plural of مَاشٍ (māshī)
    الْحُجَّاج (al-ḥujjāj) The pilgrims

    I'rab (Sentence Analysis) Examples

    Example 1 — Wāw: جَاءَ زَيْدٌ وَعَمْرٌ

    Zayd and ʿAmr came.

    Word Term I'rāb
    جَاءَ فِعْل مَاضٍ —
    زَيْدٌ مَعْطُوف عَلَيْه / فَاعِل مَرْفُوع (ḍamma)
    وَ حَرْف عَطْف —
    عَمْرٌ مَعْطُوف مَرْفُوع (ḍamma) — follows i'rāb of maʿṭūf ʿalayh

    Example 2 — Ḥattā: قَدِمَ الْحُجَّاجُ حَتَّى الْمُشَاةُ

    The pilgrims came, even those on foot.

    Word Term I'rāb
    قَدِمَ فِعْل مَاضٍ —
    الْحُجَّاجُ مَعْطُوف عَلَيْه / فَاعِل مَرْفُوع
    حَتَّى حَرْف عَطْف —
    الْمُشَاةُ مَعْطُوف مَرْفُوع — both together form the fāʿil of qadima

    Example 3 — Immā: الثَّمَرُ إِمَّا حُلْوٌ وَإِمَّا مُرٌّ

    The fruit is either sweet or bitter.

    Word Term I'rāb
    الثَّمَرُ مُبْتَدَأ مَرْفُوع
    إِمَّا (first) حَرْف عَطْف (1st of pair) —
    حُلْوٌ مَعْطُوف عَلَيْه مَرْفُوع
    وَإِمَّا (second) حَرْف عَطْف (2nd of pair) —
    مُرٌّ مَعْطُوف مَرْفُوع
    حُلْوٌ + مُرٌّ (together) خَبَر of الثَّمَرُ مَرْفُوع

    Example 4 — Aw: لَبِثْنَا يَوْمًا أَوْ بَعْضَ يَوْمٍ (Sūrat al-Kahf)

    We stayed for a day or part of a day.

    Word Term I'rāb
    لَبِثْنَا فِعْل + فَاعِل —
    يَوْمًا مَعْطُوف عَلَيْه / ظَرْف مَنْصُوب
    أَوْ حَرْف عَطْف —
    بَعْضَ مَعْطُوف / مُضَاف مَنْصُوب
    يَوْمٍ مُضَاف إِلَيْه مَجْرُور
    يَوْمًا + بَعْضَ يَوْمٍ together = مَفْعُول فِيهِ (ظَرْف) of لَبِثْنَا —

    Example 5 — Am: أَزَيْدٌ عِنْدَكَ أَمْ بَكْرٌ؟

    Is Zayd with you or Bakr?

    Word Term I'rāb
    أَ حَرْف اسْتِفْهَام —
    زَيْدٌ مَعْطُوف عَلَيْه / مُبْتَدَأ مَرْفُوع
    عِنْدَكَ ظَرْف / خَبَر —
    أَمْ حَرْف عَطْف —
    بَكْرٌ مَعْطُوف مَرْفُوع

    (Note: عِنْدَكَ can appear anywhere in the sentence depending on what is being emphasized.)

    # Arabic English Translation Key particle used

    6 أَأَنْتَ فَعَلْتَ هَذَا أَمْ زُبَيْرٌ؟ Did you do this or Zubair? أَمْ (Am — interrogative)

    Key Takeaways

    1. ʿAtf (conjunction) always requires a ḥarf al-ʿatf placed between maʿṭūf ʿalayh and maʿṭūf. Both words share the same i'rāb — whatever case the first word has, the second must have the same.

    2. Wāw (وَ) does not imply sequence. It is pure conjunction. Do not assume the first-mentioned party acted first.

    3. Fāʾ (فَ) vs. Thumma (ثُمَّ): Both convey sequence, but fāʾ means immediately after, while thumma means after a delay. This distinction is legally significant in uṣūl al-fiqh — the same āyah can yield different rulings depending on which particle is used.

    4. Fāʾ also conveys causality: what precedes it is the cause; what follows is the effect.

    5. Aw (أَوْ) is for general "or" in non-interrogative sentences. Am (أَمْ) is for "or" inside questions (istifhām). They are not interchangeable.

    6. Immā (إِمَّا) is always used in pairs: إِمَّا...وَإِمَّا... ("either...or..."). The first occurrence = "either," the second = "or."

    7. Bal (بَلْ) is for iḍrāb (abandoning one claim for another). Its effect differs depending on whether it follows a positive statement or a negation:

    8. After positive: negates ruling from what precedes, affirms it for what follows.

    9. After negation: confirms the negation for what precedes, affirms the opposite (positive) for what follows.

    10. Lākin (لَكِنْ) is for istidrāk — correcting a likely misunderstanding the listener may have drawn from the previous statement.

    11. Practical relevance: These particles are studied extensively in uṣūl al-fiqh because subtle differences in their meaning directly impact how legal rulings are derived from Quranic āyāt and aḥādīth. Understanding them well now pays dividends in year three and beyond.
