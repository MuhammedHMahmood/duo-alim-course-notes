    Weekly Review — Week of Sep 21 - Sep 27, 2026

    Nahw 201

    Concept Questions

    1. When the Fa'il (subject) comes after the verb (i.e., it is Mu'akhkhar), which aspect of agreement does the verb maintain and which does it drop?

    2. What is the rule for Jama' Taksir Ghair 'Aqil in verb agreement — is it Lazim (obligatory) or Ikhtiyari (optional) to treat it as a singular feminine noun? How does this differ for Jama' Taksir 'Aqil?

    3. Define Naib al-Fa'il. What are the two reasons mentioned in the text for why the Fa'il might be dropped from a sentence?

    4. What role does a Fasl (intervening word) play between a verb and its Fa'il or Naib al-Fa'il? Give one example sentence.

    5. Mubtada and Khabar are both Marfu', yet their 'Amil differs from that of Fa'il and Naib al-Fa'il. Explain this difference using the terms Lafzi 'Amil and Ma'nawi 'Amil.

    6. The default rule is that Mubtada must be Ma'rifa. Name any three of the six conditions under which a Nakira noun may serve as Mubtada.

    Answer Key

    Nahw 201

    1. When the Fa'il is Mu'akhkhar, the verb drops number agreement entirely — it is always Wahid (singular) regardless of whether the Fa'il is singular, dual, or plural. However, it retains gender agreement: the verb must be Mudhakkar if the Fa'il is Mudhakkar, and Mu'annas if the Fa'il is Mu'annas.

    2. Jama' Taksir Ghair 'Aqil (non-rational, e.g., الكُتُب): it is Lazim (obligatory) to treat it as singular feminine — no choice. Jama' Taksir 'Aqil (rational, e.g., الرِّجَال): it is Ikhtiyari (optional) — the speaker may treat it as singular feminine or as plural masculine; both are correct.

    3. Naib al-Fa'il is the Maf'ul (object) that is raised to take the place of the Fa'il when the verb becomes passive (Majhul). It is always Marfu'. Two reasons for dropping the Fa'il: (1) the doer is unknown, or (2) you deliberately wish to conceal the doer — for example, to avoid announcing your own good deed (بُنِيَ الْمَسْجِدُ instead of بَنَيْتُ الْمَسْجِدَ).

    4. A Fasl is any word appearing between the verb and its Fa'il or Naib al-Fa'il. When a Fasl is present, the verb is permitted to be Mudhakkar even if the Fa'il or Naib al-Fa'il is Mu'annas, because the Fasl removes their direct adjacency. Example: أَسْلَمَ الْيَوْمَ سَارَةُ — الْيَوْمَ is the Fasl, permitting the Mudhakkar verb even though سَارَةُ is Mu'annas.

    5. Fa'il and Naib al-Fa'il take Raf' due to a Lafzi 'Amil — an explicit, visible grammatical agent, namely the verb itself. Mubtada and Khabar have no such explicit agent; their cause of Raf' is Ma'nawi — it is the act of Al-Ibtida' (beginning a nominal sentence), which is a conceptual/implied agent, not a physical word in the sentence.

    6. Any three of: (1) the Nakira has a Sifa (adjective), making it Nakira Mukhassasa; (2) it comes between Hamza Istifham and Am; (3) it comes after Ma Nafiya; (4) it has a hidden/implied Sifat (Mahdhuf); (5) the Khabar is a Muqaddam Zarf and the Mubtada is Nakira — the Khabar must come first; (6) the Nakira is Mudhaf to a Ma'rifa (implied genitive construction).

    Vocabulary:

    نَائِبُ الْفَاعِل — the subject of a passive sentence, substituting for the dropped doer; it always takes Raf' (nominative case)

    فِعْلُ مَجْهُول — the passive verb; signaled by changing the vowel pattern of the active verb (e.g., ضَرَبَ → ضُرِبَ: first syllable Damma, middle Kasra); the original Fa'il is dropped entirely

    جَمْعُ التَّكْسِير — for verb agreement purposes it falls under the category of Mu'annas Ghair Haqiqi (non-real feminine)

    فَصْل — permits the verb to be Mudhakkar even when the Fa'il is Mu'annas, because the intervening word removes their direct adjacency

    نَكِرَة مُخَصَّصَة — a Nakira "specified" by a Sifa or qualifying element; the specification moves it closer to Ma'rifa, making it eligible to function as Mubtada

    عَامِل مَعْنَوِي — its specific name is Al-Ibtida' (the act of beginning a nominal sentence); it is called Ma'nawi because it is conceptual/implicit — not a visible word

    لَازِم — indicates the rule is obligatory — the speaker has no choice; e.g., Jama' Taksir Ghair 'Aqil must be treated as singular feminine

    مُقَدَّم — when the Khabar is a Zarf and the Mubtada is Nakira, the Khabar must come first (Muqaddam) and the Mubtada comes last (Mu'akhkhar) — this is the required word order

    Tafseer 201

    1. Allah charges the Ahbar with commanding people to believe in Prophet Muhammad ﷺ — as his coming was described in the Torah — while they themselves refused to believe in him. It is called irrational because they recited those very descriptions in the Torah yet still rejected him; they preached belief they themselves did not hold, which is an incoherent and self-contradicting position.

    2. In this context, Sabr does not mean general patience in hardship. It specifically means bearing the burden of Shari'ah obligations (takalif shar'iyyah) — enduring what the self finds difficult when fulfilling religious duties.

    3. The scholars interpret yadhunnun here as expressing firm, certain belief — not supposition. The technical phrase used is اعْتِقَادًا جَازِمًا لَا يُخَالِجُهُ شَكّ (a decisive belief without any trace of doubt). The context (the Khashi'in's certainty about meeting Allah on the Day of Judgment) functions as a qarinah (contextual indicator) that elevates dhann to the level of yaqeen.

    4. The three categories through which Bani Israel were preferred: (1) Allah sent many Prophets and Messengers from among them; (2) He revealed divine books to them; (3) He made some of them rulers and kings — Dawud and Sulayman ﷺ combined prophethood with kingship.

    5. The four conditions of the Day of Judgment for disbelievers: (1) no soul shall help or avail another soul anything; (2) no intercession shall be accepted for them; (3) no ransom or compensation shall be taken from them; (4) no helper — no one can protect them from Allah's punishment.

    6. The prescribed tawbah required the innocent (bari') among Bani Israel to kill the guilty (mujrim). It was a literal divine command specific to that sin — not a general ruling in Shari'ah. Willingness to submit to this difficult command was itself rida' bi-hukm Allah, and when they carried it out Allah accepted their repentance.

    7. وَإِذْ creates a structure of specific enumeration — recounting each blessing individually rather than speaking of them in general, which makes the reminder more rhetorically powerful and more conducive to gratitude. The implied understood verb before each وَإِذْ is أُذْكُرُوا — "Remember (My blessing) at the time when..."

    Vocabulary:

    تَقْرِيع — pointed/formal rebuke; the tone of Ayah 44 toward the Ahbar; verbal root: كَرَّعَ يُقَرِّعُ (Bab Taf'eel)

    الْخَاشِعِين — those whose inner souls and hearts are pure, devoted solely to Allah ﷻ; Salah is easy for them because their hearts are already oriented toward Allah, making the outward act a natural expression of their inner state

    الْفُرْقَان — the criterion; that which distinguishes between haqq (truth) and batil (falsehood) — a descriptive title for the Tawrat (and similarly the Quran) based on its function

    بَلَاء — divine trial/test; the stated purpose was to distinguish the righteous (salih) from the wicked (talih/fajir)

    بَارِئ — the Creator; one who creates and is simultaneously free from all fault and deficiency; from the root بَرَأَ يَبْرَأُ; one of the Asma' al-Husna

    اعْتِقَادًا جَازِمًا — firm, decisive belief without doubt; significant because it shows that ظَنّ in Ayah 46 carries the weight of certainty (yaqeen), not mere conjecture — a contextually elevated usage

    آلُ فِرْعَوْن — covers Fir'aun's family, close companions, followers, and army; the word آل derives from أَهْل; in this ayah it refers specifically to his tyrannical forces

    تَوَّاب — the intensive مُبَالَغَة form (pattern فَعَّال) indicates Allah accepts repentance abundantly, repeatedly, and for grave sins — His acceptance of tawbah is vast and without limit

    Fiqh 201

    1. The Hanafi reasoning for Isfar: delaying Fajr until daylight spreads ensures there is enough remaining time that if something invalidates the salah (making it Fasid), the person can repeat it with Tiwal al-Mufassal (the longer surahs from Surah al-Hujurat onward) before Fajr time expires. Praying too early may not allow this safety buffer.

    2. Imam Abu Hanifa: Asr begins when the shadow of every object reaches twice (2×) its original length beyond the base shadow at zawal. Imam Muhammad, Imam Abu Yusuf, and the three other major Imams (Shafi'i, Maliki, Hanbali): Asr begins when the shadow reaches once (1×) the original length.

    3. Tarjih = saying the Shahadatayn softly first, then repeating them loudly in the Adhan. Ahnaf: no Tarjih — the Adhan proceeds at consistent pace and volume throughout; Dalil: the lifelong practice of Bilal (RA), who never used Tarjih. Shafi'i: Tarjih is Masnoon; Dalil: the Hadith of Abu Mahzura in Tirmizi. The Hanafi counter-argument: the Prophet's instruction to Abu Mahzura to repeat was understood as a command to raise his voice, not to institute Tarjih as a permanent feature.

    4. Adhan without Wudu: not ideal and should be avoided, but not Makruh — the Adhan need not be repeated. Iqamah without Wudu: Makruh — must be in a state of Wudu. The Iqamah ruling is stricter because if the Mu'azzin calls Iqamah and then leaves to make Wudu, it creates a gap between Iqamah and the start of salah and disrupts the congregation. In both cases the call is not repeated.

    5. Practical Hanafi method: delay Zuhr until near the end of its time; pray Asr at its earliest — ideally with the same Wudu. Similarly, delay Maghrib and pray Isha at its earliest. If even this is genuinely impossible (e.g., on a flight): the dominant Muftabih opinion is to let the salah become Qada and make up both prayers as Qasr upon reaching the destination. Temporarily adopting another madhab's ruling for convenience is not valid.

    6. The One-Fourth Rule: "one-fourth equals the whole" (الرُّبْع كَالْكُلّ) — if one-fourth of something is affected, the entire thing is treated as affected. Two applications in Shurut al-Salah: (1) Najasa on clothing — less than ¼ is treated as clean; ¼ or more renders salah invalid; (2) 'Awra exposure — less than ¼ of an 'awra part exposed does not invalidate salah; ¼ or more does. (Also applies to Masah of the head in Wudu: wiping ¼ counts as the whole.)

    7. Mid-prayer: must perform Istidarah (turn immediately toward the correct Qibla) and continue the prayer. After prayer: if ijtihad (tahharri) was performed before the prayer, the salah is valid and need not be repeated. If no ijtihad was made at all, the salah must be repeated. Imam Shafi'i adds an additional condition: if one faced exactly 180° opposite the Qibla, repetition is required even after ijtihad — the Ahnaf do not make this distinction.

    Vocabulary:

    إِسْفَار — when daylight has visibly spread across the sky (not full darkness, not merely true dawn) — the Hanafi preferred time for Fajr

    تَرَسُّل — measured pausing between words; it is Masnoon specifically in the Adhan (the Iqamah uses Hadar — quick, continuous speech — which is the opposite)

    تَثْوِيب — the phrase "الصَّلَاةُ خَيْرٌ مِنَ النَّوْم" (Prayer is better than sleep); inserted after "Hayya 'alal-falah" in the Fajr Adhan only

    تَقْرَار — repeating the phrases of the Iqamah twice; derived from the practice of Abu Mahzura (RA) — not Bilal (RA); Bilal's Iqamah had no Taqrar, which is what Imam Shafi'i and Imam Ahmad followed

    نَجَاسَة مُخَفَّفَة — light/minor filth, e.g., the urine of animals whose meat is permissible; the One-Fourth Rule applies to it the same as Ghaleeza for the purposes of salah validity, though it is a lighter legal category

    سَتْر الْعَوْرَة — for a free woman (hurra) in salah, only the face and the hands up to the wrists are NOT 'awra; the entire rest of the body must be covered (some fuqaha also include the feet as 'awra in salah)

    تَحَرِّي — diligent effort to determine the correct Qibla; if performed and the person was wrong, the prayer is valid and does not need to be repeated; if no tahharri was made, the prayer must be repeated

    نِيَّة — verbalization is recommended (sunnah) but not obligatory; if Takbir al-Tahrimah is made before the niyyah is formed, the salah is invalid per Zahir al-Riwayah
