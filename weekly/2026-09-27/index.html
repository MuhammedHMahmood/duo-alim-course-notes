    Nahw 201

    Sessions: Sep 21 & Sep 25

    This week in a nutshell: The first session completed the rules of Fa'il (verb-subject agreement, Jama' Taksir treatment, and Fasl) and introduced Naib al-Fa'il — the passive subject — as the second category of Marfu'at. The second session drilled Fi'l Majhul conversion exercises and then opened the new chapter on the nominal sentence, introducing Mubtada and Khabar with all the conditions under which an indefinite noun may serve as Mubtada.

    Key Points

    • Verb number when Fa'il is Mu'akhkhar: The verb is always singular (wahid) regardless of whether the Fa'il is dual or plural — only gender agreement applies, not number agreement.
    • Jama' Taksir treatment: All broken plurals are treated like non-real feminine (Mu'annas Ghair Haqiqi). For non-rational (Ghair 'Aqil) broken plurals this is obligatory (lazim); for rational ('Aqil) broken plurals it is optional (ikhtiyari) — both masculine plural and singular feminine verb forms are valid.
    • Fasl rule: Any word placed between the verb and its Fa'il (or Naib al-Fa'il) permits the verb to take the masculine form even when the Fa'il is feminine. Without Fasl, the verb must match the gender of the Fa'il.
    • Naib al-Fa'il: When a verb is made passive (Fi'l Majhul), the Fa'il is dropped and the Maf'ul takes its place in Raf', now called Naib al-Fa'il. It is the second of the Marfu'at. All rules of the Fa'il apply equally to it — including the Fasl rule.
    • Reasons to use the passive: The doer is unknown, or the speaker deliberately conceals themselves (e.g., بُنِيَ الْمَسْجِدُ instead of بَنَيْتُ الْمَسْجِدَ to avoid self-praise).
    • Mubtada and Khabar: The subject and predicate of a nominal sentence are both Marfu'. Their cause of Raf' is Ma'nawi — Al-Ibtida' (the act of beginning a nominal sentence) — not a verb. The default rule: Mubtada is Ma'rifa (definite), Khabar is Nakira (indefinite).
    • Nakira as Mubtada: An indefinite noun may serve as Mubtada under six conditions: it has a Sifat (adjective); it falls between Hamza Istifham and Am; it comes after Ma Nafiya; it has a hidden (Mahdhuf) Sifat; the Khabar is a Muqaddam Zarf (fronted adverb) — in which case the Nakira Mubtada must come last (Mu'akhkhar); or it is Mudhaf to a Ma'rifa.
    • When both nouns are Ma'rifa: Either may be Mubtada — the speaker chooses based on what they intend to speak about.

    Vocabulary to Know

    Arabic Term Meaning
    نَائِبُ الْفَاعِل (Naib al-Fa'il) Substitute for the doer; subject of a passive verb
    فِعْل مَجْهُول (Fi'l Majhul) Passive verb; doer unknown or omitted
    فِعْل مَعْلُوم (Fi'l Ma'lum) Active verb; doer known
    فَصْل (Fasl) An intervening word between verb and subject
    جَمْعُ التَّكْسِير (Jama' Taksir) Broken plural
    عَاقِل ('Aqil) Rational/living (humans)
    غَيْرُ عَاقِل (Ghair 'Aqil) Non-rational/non-living
    لَازِم (Lazim) Obligatory; no choice
    اخْتِيَارِي (Ikhtiyari) Optional
    مُبْتَدَأ (Mubtada) Subject of a nominal sentence
    خَبَر (Khabar) Predicate of a nominal sentence
    نَكِرَة مُخَصَّصَة (Nakira Mukhassasa) Specified indefinite noun; eligible for Mubtada
    عَامِل مَعْنَوِي (Amil Ma'nawi) Implicit grammatical agent (e.g., Al-Ibtida')
    ظَرْف (Zarf) Adverb of time or place
    مَا نَافِيَة (Ma Nafiya) Negating Ma

    Easy to Mix Up

    • Lazim vs. Ikhtiyari for Jama' Taksir: Treating a broken plural as feminine singular is lazim (no choice) for non-rational things like الْكُتُبُ, but ikhtiyari (optional) for rational beings like الرِّجَالُ — both اِنْطَلَقَتِ الرِّجَالُ and اِنْطَلَقَ الرِّجَالُ are valid.
    • Mubtada Ma'rifa vs. Nakira: When one noun is definite and one is indefinite, the choice is fixed — the Ma'rifa is always the Mubtada. Only when both are Ma'rifa does the speaker have a choice.
    • Fi'l Majhul gender agreement: The passive verb agrees in gender with the Naib al-Fa'il (the new subject), not with the original Fa'il that was dropped — so the verb's gender may change from the active form.

    Tafseer 201

    Sessions: Sep 22 & Sep 26

    This week in a nutshell: The first session covered Surah Al-Baqarah, Ayat 44–48, rebuking the Jewish scholars (Ahbar) for preaching to others while not following their own scripture, then prescribing Sabr and Salah as the antidote to worldly desires. The second session moved to Ayat 49–54, where Allah enumerates His specific blessings upon Bani Israel one by one — salvation from Fir'aun, the parting of the sea, the giving of the Tawrat, the pardon after calf worship — building a cumulative case for gratitude that Bani Israel repeatedly failed to meet.

    Key Points

    • Hypocrisy condemned (Ayah 44): The Ahbar commanded people to believe in Prophet Muhammad ﷺ (whose descriptions appeared in the Torah) while themselves refusing to believe — they recited the Torah without acting on it. Allah rebukes: "Do you not understand?" (Afala ta'qilun).
    • Sabr and Salah as remedy (Ayah 45): To overcome desires and fulfill Shari'ah obligations, Allah prescribes Sabr (bearing the burden of religious duties) and Salah. Salah is described as easy only for the Khashi'in — those whose hearts are pure and directed solely to Allah.
    • Yaqeen in the Akhirah (Ayah 46): The word yadhunnun (normally "to suppose") here carries the meaning of firm, certain belief (I'tiqad Jazim) — a contextual usage elevated by the subject matter (meeting Allah). The Khashi'in are certain they will meet their Lord and be held accountable.
    • Blessing upon father = blessing upon son: Allah addresses the Bani Israel at the time of the Prophet ﷺ about blessings that occurred to their forefathers — establishing the principle that ancestral honor and the debt of gratitude pass down through generations.
    • Parting of the sea (Ayah 50): Bani Israel crossed on dry land; Fir'aun and his army drowned while Bani Israel watched firsthand — an overwhelming sign (Ayah Bahirah) of Allah's power, saving His awliya and destroying His enemies.
    • Calf worship and tawbah (Ayat 51–54): Bani Israel worshipped the calf during Musa's (AS) 40-night absence — described as mutanahi fi al-qubh (extreme ugliness). The prescribed tawbah was that the innocent kill the guilty among them. When they submitted to this command, Allah pardoned them: إِنَّهُ هُوَ التَّوَّابُ الرَّحِيمُ.
    • The Tawrat as Furqan (Ayah 53): The Tawrat was given as a Furqan — a criterion distinguishing haqq from batil. The correct way to receive such a scripture is deep contemplation (tadabbur) and acting upon its rulings, not mere recitation.
    • Day of Judgment — no recourse (Ayah 48): Four things will not exist for disbelievers: no soul will avail another, no intercession will be accepted, no ransom taken, and no helper to protect from Allah's punishment.

    Arabic Term Meaning
    تَقْرِيع (Taqri'a) Pointed rebuke
    خَاشِعِين (Khashi'in) The humble and submissive; those whose hearts are pure for Allah
    اعْتِقَاد جَازِم (I'tiqad Jazim) Firm, decisive belief without doubt
    الْفُرْقَان (Al-Furqan) The criterion distinguishing truth from falsehood
    بَلَاء (Bala') Trial, test — here, the severe oppression of Fir'aun as a divine test
    يَسُومُونَكُمْ سُوءَ الْعَذَابِ They subjected you to the worst punishment
    يَسْتَحْيُونَ (Yastahyun) They kept alive (Bab Istif'al); females were spared
    اسْتَبْقَى (Istabqa) To spare/keep alive for service
    مُتَنَاهٍ فِي الْقُبْح (Mutanahi fil-Qubh) In the extreme of ugliness/evil
    بَارِئ (Bari') Creator; one free from all fault — a Divine Name
    تَوَّاب (Tawwab) The One who greatly and repeatedly accepts repentance (intensive form)
    عَفَا يَعْفُو (Afa ya'fu) To pardon, overlook (follows pattern of da'a yad'u)

    Easy to Mix Up

    • يَظُنُّونَ (yadhunnun) here vs. its ordinary meaning: The root ظَنَّ normally means "to suppose/think" (implying uncertainty). In Ayah 46, context (certainty of meeting Allah) elevates it to yaqeen — firm belief. This contextual shift (qarīnah) is a recognized Arabic usage; do not read it as mere supposition.
    • آل (Al) vs. أَهْل (Ahl): آل derives from أَهْل but in use it means the family, close companions, followers, and army of a ruler — not just blood relatives. In Aali Fir'awn it refers primarily to Fir'aun's forces and tyrannical regime.

    Fiqh 201

    Sessions: Sep 23 & Sep 26

    This week in a nutshell: The first session completed the chapter on prayer timings — recommended (mustahab) times for each of the five prayers and Witr, with detailed madhab differences — and then covered the Adhan and Iqamah in full: their origin, legal status, structural differences between schools, and related masail. The second session opened the new chapter of Shurut al-Salah (conditions of prayer), covering all seven prerequisites, the detailed 'awra rulings for men and women, the One-Fourth Rule, niyyah, facing the Qibla, and introduced the six internal Fara'id of salah.

    Key Points

    • Mustahab Fajr timing — Hanafi vs. Shafi'i: Ahnaf prefer Isfar (delay until daylight spreads) so that if salah becomes fasid, there is time to repeat with Tiwal al-Mufassal Surahs. Shafi'i prefers Ghalas (complete darkness). Both positions rest on authentic Hadith — neither should be disparaged.
    • Adhan is Sunnah, established for the five daily Fard prayers and Jumu'ah only. No Adhan for Witr, Eid, Janazah, Tarawih, or any Nafl. Collective abandonment of Adhan is a grave communal sin — scholars say the ruler must fight or imprison those who abandon it.
    • Tarjih: Ahnaf do not use Tarjih (saying the Shahadatayn softly then loudly) in Adhan — they follow the practice of Bilal (RA). Shafi'i holds Tarjih is Masnoon based on the Hadith of Abu Mahzura (RA).
    • Iqamah — Tarassul vs. Hadar: Adhan is said slowly with measured pausing (Tarassul); Iqamah is said quickly and continuously (Hadar). Ahnaf derive the Iqamah wording from Abu Mahzura (RA) — phrases repeated twice (Taqrar). Shafi'i follows Bilal's Iqamah (phrases once).
    • Adhan before time: If called before the prayer time has entered, it must be repeated once the time arrives. Exception per Imam Abu Yusuf and Shafi'i: Fajr Adhan may precede time (two-Adhan system of Madinah); Hanafi Muftabih requires repetition even for Fajr.
    • Shurut al-Salah (seven conditions): Time, purity of body (from Hadath and Najas), purity of clothing, purity of place, Satr al-'Awra, Niyyah, and facing the Qibla. These are all external prerequisites — they must be met before Takbirat al-Ihram.
    • 'Awra — Hanafi rulings: Free man: navel (excluded) to below the kneecap (knee included). Free woman: entire body except face and hands to the wrist. Slave woman: same as a man plus stomach and back. The One-Fourth Rule applies: less than ¼ of the 'awra exposed → salah valid; ¼ or more → invalid.
    • Niyyah: An act of the heart; verbal utterance is recommended but not fard. It must coincide with or precede Takbirat al-Ihram — no gap. If Takbir precedes niyyah, salah is invalid per Zahir al-Riwayah.
    • Qibla ijtihad: One who performs tahharri (best effort to determine direction) and is wrong is excused — no repetition required. One who prays without any ijtihad must repeat. If the error is discovered mid-prayer, turn (Istidarah) and continue.

    Vocabulary to Know

    Arabic Term Meaning
    شُرُوط الصَّلاة (Shurut al-Salah) Conditions of prayer (prerequisites external to the prayer)
    فَرَائِض الصَّلاة (Fara'id al-Salah) Obligatory pillars performed within the prayer
    سَتْر الْعَوْرَة (Satr al-'Awra) Covering of the nakedness
    نَجَاسَة غَلِيظَة (Najasa Ghaleeza) Heavy/severe filth (e.g., urine, stool)
    نَجَاسَة مُخَفَّفَة (Najasa Mukhaffafa) Light/minor filth (e.g., urine of halal animals)
    إِسْفَار (Isfar) Praying Fajr when daylight has spread (Hanafi preferred)
    غَلَس (Ghalas) Praying Fajr in full darkness (Shafi'i preferred)
    تَرَسُّل (Tarassul) Measured pausing in Adhan
    حَدَر (Hadar) Speaking quickly and continuously; used in Iqamah
    تَثْوِيب (Tasweeb) Adding "al-Salatu khayrun min al-nawm" in Fajr Adhan
    تَكْبِيرَة الإِحْرَام (Takbirat al-Ihram) The opening Allahu Akbar that begins prayer
    تَحَرِّي (Tahharri) Diligent effort to determine Qibla direction
    اسْتِدَارَة (Istidarah) Turning toward the correct Qibla mid-prayer
    مُفْتَى بِه (Muftabih) The relied-upon opinion in the madhab

    Easy to Mix Up

    • Shurut vs. Arkan/Fara'id: Conditions (Shurut) are met before Takbirat al-Ihram; pillars (Arkan/Fara'id) are performed within the prayer. Confusing these leads to errors about what invalidates salah.
    • 'Awra of the knee — Ahnaf vs. Shafi'i: Ahnaf hold the knee is part of the 'awra and must be fully covered; Shafi'i holds the knee is not 'awra. Practically, covering well below the knee avoids the dispute entirely.
    • Adhan without Wudu vs. Iqamah without Wudu: Adhan without Wudu is merely undesirable and the Adhan need not be repeated. Iqamah without Wudu is Makruh (stronger) — because the Mu'azzin calling Iqamah must then leave for Wudu and delay the prayer.
