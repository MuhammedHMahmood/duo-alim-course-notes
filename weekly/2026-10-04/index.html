    Weekly Review — Week of Sep 28 - Oct 04, 2026

    Self-test quiz covering everything taught this week across all active classes. Answer each question yourself before checking the Answer Key at the end — don't peek early.

    Tafseer 201

    Concept Questions

    1. The 70 chosen men of Bani Israel went to Mount Tur to apologize for a specific sin. What was that sin, and what demand did they make upon arriving at Tur — and what was the precise wording of that demand in Arabic?

    2. After the 70 were struck down by the thunderbolt, Musa عليه السلام wept and supplicated at length. What was the substance of his supplication, and what was the outcome? What does Allah state was the purpose of their resurrection?

    3. Why were Bani Israel confined to the field of Tih for 40 years? What was the exact nature of their disobedience, and what strange experience did they have while wandering in the Tih?

    4. What three blessings did Allah provide to Bani Israel during their 40-year punishment in the Tih? For each, describe what it was and how it reached them.

    5. Bani Israel were given three specific instructions when commanded to enter Bayt al-Maqdis. List all three. What did the wrongdoers among them do instead — both in word and in action — and what punishment followed?

    6. According to Ayah 62, what three conditions must be met for a person to receive their reward from Allah, have no fear, and have no grief on the Day of Judgment? To which four religious communities does this ayah address itself?

    Vocabulary

    Arabic Term Question
    تَأَجُّب (ta'ajjub) This grammatical construction has two patterns. Name both patterns and give one example of each.
    جَهْرَةً (jahratan) Bani Israel used this word to describe how they wanted to see Allah — what does it mean, and what is its opposite?
    تِيه (Tīh) What is the linguistic root of this word, what does that root verb mean, and why is this name fitting for the place?
    الْمَنُّ (al-mann) Describe what this substance actually was, how it reached Bani Israel, and how they consumed it.

    Fiqh 201

    Concept Questions

    1. Imam al-Quduri lists exactly six Fara'id of Salah. Name all six in order. Which pillar do some scholars count as a seventh, and why does Imam al-Quduri leave it out (what will be addressed in a future session)?

    2. Regarding Takbir al-Tahreemah: Imam Abu Hanifah and Imam Abu Yusuf classify it as a Shart rather than a Rukn. What is the distinction? Which wordings of the Takbir are valid according to Imam Abu Hanifah, and which does Imam Abu Yusuf restrict it to?

    3. Surah al-Fatihah and the addition of a Surah after it are both classified as Wajib by the Ahnaf — not Fard. What is the practical consequence of this classification? What is the minimum amount of Quran that satisfies the requirement to add a Surah after Fatihah?

    4. Explain the ruling on Ta'deel al-Arkan. What exactly does it require, when does it apply, and how do the Ahnaf, Sahibayn, and Imam Shafi'i differ on its status? Why does the teacher flag this as especially important for Imams?

    5. According to the Hadith of Ibn Abbas (r.a.) in Bukhari, a person must prostrate on seven bones. List all seven. What is the difference of opinion among Imam Abu Hanifah, the Sahibayn, and Imam Shafi'i on whether both the forehead and nose must touch the ground?

    6. Sajdah on a turban (amama) or prayer mat is permissible under what condition? What renders it impermissible? What Hadith establishes this ruling, and from whom is it narrated?

    Nahw 201

    Concept Questions

    2. When the Khabar is a Jumlah (any type), a specific element must be present inside it. What is that element, what is its grammatical function, and what would happen to the sentence's meaning without it? Illustrate with an example.

    3. A Sifat (e.g., an Ism Fa'il) does not normally function as a Mubtada. Under what three conditions is it permitted to become Mubtada? Write out the conditions precisely, then provide two example sentences — one where the Sifat is Mubtada and one where it is Khabar.

    4. What is the diagnostic test for determining whether a Sifat is acting as Mubtada or as Khabar? Explain the analogy that connects this rule to Fi'l behavior. Apply the test to the sentence: مَا قَائِمَانِ الزَّيْدَانِ.

    5. How does Inna change the I'rab of the Mubtada and Khabar when it enters a Mubtada-Khabar sentence? Using إِنَّ زَيْدًا قَائِمٌ, give a full I'rab breakdown of each word.

    6. The rule for Taqdim (fronting) of Khabar differs between a regular Mubtada-Khabar sentence and a sentence with Inna. State the rule for each case, and give an example of a Khabar that can be fronted even in the Inna construction and one that cannot.

    Answer Key

    Tafseer 201

    1. The sin was worshipping the Golden Calf (al-'ijl) during Musa's 40-night absence. At Tur, they said: "لَن نُّؤْمِنَ لَكَ حَتَّىٰ نَرَى اللَّهَ جَهْرَةً" — "We will never believe in you until we see Allah openly." They had already heard Allah's speech but refused to accept it as sufficient.

    2. Musa wept intensely and said: "O Lord, what shall I say to Bani Israel? I selected the best 70 among them and You have caused them to die." He supplicated without ceasing. Allah revived the 70 after a full day and night, stating the purpose: لَعَلَّكُمْ تَشْكُرُونَ — "that you may be grateful."

    3. They refused the divine command to enter Palestine and fight the jabbarīn (tyrants), saying: "Go, you and your Lord, and fight — we will not go." As punishment, they were confined to the Tih for 40 years. Despite walking all morning to cross the field, they would find themselves back at the starting point by evening — unable to exit.

    4. (1) Shade from clouds — Allah covered them with clouds like a canopy protecting them from the intense desert heat. (2) Al-Mann — a honey-like liquid that descended onto tree leaves; they mixed it with water and drank it, receiving sustenance without effort. (3) As-Salwā — a quail-like bird of delicious taste, sent down for food. All three came without effort or exertion.

    5. The three instructions: (1) Eat abundantly from wherever you wish (raghadan); (2) Enter the gate prostrating (sujjadan); (3) Say "Ḥiṭṭah" (seeking forgiveness). The wrongdoers changed the word from ḥiṭṭah to ḥabbatun fī sha'īratin ("a grain in barley") — a mocking substitution — and entered crawling on their backsides instead of prostrating. The punishment was a plague (ṭā'ūn) that killed 70,000 in a single hour.

    6. The three conditions: (1) Sincere belief in Allah (īmān ṣādiq); (2) Belief in the Last Day/Hereafter; (3) Righteous deeds ('amal ṣāliḥ). The four communities addressed: Muslims (followers of Muhammad ﷺ), Jews (alladhīna hādū), Christians (Naṣārā), and Sabians (Ṣābi'ūn).

    Vocabulary:

    • تَأَجُّب — Expressing wonder/astonishment. Two patterns: (1) Mā af'alahu — e.g., mā akramahu ("How honourable he is!"); (2) Af'il bihi — e.g., akrim bihi ("How honourable he is!"). In context they express: "How wicked/disgraceful they are!"
    • جَهْرَةً — Openly, manifestly. Opposite: something hidden. It describes their demand to see Allah directly and openly, as opposed to hearing His speech.
    • تِيه — From the verb tāha yatīhu, meaning to wander aimlessly. The name is fitting because Bani Israel literally wandered the field for 40 years without being able to leave, making it "the field of aimless wandering."
    • الْمَنُّ — A honey-like liquid that descended upon the leaves of trees. Bani Israel mixed it with water and drank it. It required no labor to obtain or prepare — pure divine provision.
    • السَّلْوَى — A quail-like bird sent as food. It was distinguished by its delicious taste — described as exceptional in flavor compared to ordinary quail.
    • حِطَّة — Means "Remove [our sins] / Forgive us." The wrongdoers substituted it with ḥabbatun fī sha'īratin ("a grain in barley") — a meaningless, mocking phrase — as an act of outright ridicule of Allah's command.
    • رَغَدًا — Indicates abundance combined with ease and happiness — the nuance is enjoying plentiful blessings in a pleasant, carefree manner, not merely having a large quantity.
    • ذُلّ and مَسْكَنَة — Humiliation (dhull) and wretchedness/abject poverty (maskanah). Imposed permanently. Three causes: (1) disbelieving in Allah's signs out of juḥūd (denial) and arrogance; (2) killing the prophets unjustly and aggressively; (3) disobedience ('iṣyān) and transgression (i'tidā').
    • الصَّابِئُون — A group who broke away from Judaism and Christianity and began worshipping the angels. Mentioned three times in the Quran (Al-Baqarah 62, Al-Ma'idah 69, Al-Hajj 17).
    • بَعَثَ — Has two distinct meanings: (1) to resurrect/revive the dead; (2) to send/dispatch (e.g., a prophet). Context determines which meaning applies — in Ayah 56 it means resurrection; in prophetic contexts it means being sent with a mission.

    Fiqh 201

    1. The six Fara'id: (1) Takbir al-Tahreemah; (2) Qiyam (standing); (3) Qira'at (recitation); (4) Ruku; (5) Sujood; (6) Al-Qad'ah al-Akhirah for the duration of Tashahud. Some count Salam as a seventh — Imam al-Quduri's omission of it will be addressed in a future session.

    2. A Shart is a precondition that connects what is outside Salah to the Arkan within it; a Rukn is an internal pillar. Imam Abu Hanifah and Imam Abu Yusuf classify Takbir as a Shart; Imam al-Quduri classifies it as a Rukn. Imam Abu Hanifah accepts: Allahu Akbar, Allahu Ajall, Allahu A'zam, and Ar-Rahmanu Akbar. Imam Abu Yusuf restricts it to only Allahu Akbar or Allahu Kabir (must contain the root k-b-r).

    3. Because Surah al-Fatihah is Wajib (not Fard), its omission can be compensated by Sajdah Sahu — the Salah is not automatically invalid. If a Fard were missed, the Salah would have to be repeated. Minimum for the additional Surah: three short ayahs OR one long ayah from any Surah of the Quran.

    4. Ta'deel al-Arkan requires pausing in stillness between postures: (1) standing fully upright after rising from Ruku before going into Sajdah; (2) sitting with Itminan between the two Sajdahs. For the Ahnaf it is Wajib (missing it may require Sajdah Sahu); for the Sahibayn and Imam Shafi'i it is Fard (missing it invalidates the Salah). Important for Imams because if the Imam skips this pause, the validity of the entire congregation's Salah may be affected.

    5. The seven bones: forehead (along with tip of nose), both hands, both knees, toes of both feet. Imam Abu Hanifah: either forehead or nose is sufficient. Sahibayn (Mufta bihi): both must touch the ground without a valid excuse. Imam Shafi'i: both are Fard and must touch the ground.

    6. Permissible condition: the forehead must still be able to feel the hardness of the ground and make firm contact with the surface. Impermissible when: the turban/cloth is wrapped so thickly that the forehead cannot make contact at all. Hadith: narrated by Anas ibn Malik (r.a.) in Bukhari — the Sahaba placed the ends of their clothing at the place of prostration due to scorching heat and the Prophet ﷺ did not object (Muqarrar Hadith).

    Vocabulary:

    • فَرَائِضُ / فَرْض — If a Fard is missed mid-Salah and remembered in a later rak'ah, it must be performed immediately; the musalli continues from where they left off and performs Sajdah Sahu at the end. If only remembered after Salah, the Salah must be repeated entirely.
    • وَاجِب — Missing a Wajib does not invalidate Salah outright; Sajdah Sahu at the end compensates. If deliberately omitted without Sajdah Sahu, the Salah must be repeated. Missing a Fard, by contrast, invalidates the Salah.
    • تَكْبِيرَةُ التَّحْرِيمَة — According to the Ahnaf, hands are raised first, then "Allahu Akbar" is said. Dalil: Hadith in Muslim.
    • تَعْدِيلُ الْأَرْكَان — Applies at two points in each rak'ah: (1) after rising from Ruku, before going into the first Sajdah; (2) between the first and second Sajdahs (the Jalsa).
    • سَجْدَةُ السَّهْو — One set compensates for multiple missed Wajibs, up to approximately three. Beyond a certain number of missed Wajibs the Salah must be repeated (exact limit to be confirmed in a future session).
    • مُفْتَى بِهِ — The operative fatwa position acted upon in the school. It is important when the Hanafi Imams differ because it tells the student which position to follow in practice, even when other valid scholarly opinions exist within the same school.
    • سَمِعَ اللَّهُ لِمَنْ حَمِدَهُ — According to Imam Abu Hanifah (Mufta bihi): the Imam says only this phrase while rising from Ruku. The Muqtadi says Rabbana lakal hamd and does not say Sami'allahu liman hamidah. A person praying alone says both.
    • اِطْمِئْنَان — Required in the Jalsa (sitting between the two Sajdahs). Minimum duration: enough to say "Alhamdulillah" two or three times — i.e., a genuine, settled pause, not a momentary touch.

    Nahw 201

    1. Four types of Khabar beyond Mufrad:
    2. Jumlah Ismiyya (nominal sentence): زَيْدٌ أَبُوهُ قَائِمٌ
    3. Jumlah Fi'liyya (verbal sentence): زَيْدٌ قَامَ أَبُوهُ
    4. Jumlah Shartiyya (conditional sentence): زَيْدٌ إِنْ جَاءَنِي فَأَكْرَمْتُهُ

    5. Zarfiyya / Jar-Majrur (adverbial): عَمْرٌو فِي الدَّارِ

    6. A Damir (pronoun) must be present inside the Khabar-Jumlah, referring back to the Mubtada. Without it there is no logical link between the subject and the predicate sentence — the Khabar would describe nothing in particular. Example: in زَيْدٌ قَامَ أَبُوهُ, the هُ in أَبُوهُ refers back to زَيْد and establishes that it is Zaid's father who stood.

    7. Three conditions for Sifat to become Mubtada: (1) It must be a Sighat Sifat (Ism Fa'il, Ism Maf'ul, Sifat Mushabbaha, or Ism Tafdhil); (2) It must come after a Harf Nafi or Harf Istifham (e.g., مَا, هَلْ); (3) It must give Rafa' to the following Ism Zahir. Sifat as Mubtada: مَا قَائِمٌ زَيْدٌ — qā'imun = Mubtada, zaydun = Khabar. Sifat as Khabar: مَا قَائِمَتَانِ الْبِنْتَانِ — al-bintān = Mubtada, qā'imatāni = Khabar (matches in Tasniyah).

    8. The diagnostic test: if the Sifat matches the following noun in number and gender, it is Khabar. If it stays Wahid (singular) regardless of the noun's number, it is Mubtada acting as Amil. The analogy: a Fi'l stays Wahid even when its Fa'il is Tasniyah or Jam' (e.g., ذَهَبَ الزَّيْدَانِ) — Sifat acting as Amil behaves the same way. Applied to مَا قَائِمَانِ الزَّيْدَانِ: qā'imāni matches al-zaydāni in Tasniyah → therefore qā'imāni = Khabar and al-zaydāni = Mubtada.

    9. Inna gives Nasb to the Mubtada (now called Ism Inna) and leaves the Khabar Marfoo'. Full I'rab of إِنَّ زَيْدًا قَائِمٌ: inna = Harf from Inna's group, Mabni; zaydan = Ism Inna, Mansoob; qā'imun = Khabar Inna, Marfoo'.

    10. Regular Mubtada-Khabar: Khabar can be freely fronted (Muqaddam) — e.g., فِي الدَّارِ زَيْدٌ is fully permissible. Inna construction: Khabar of Inna cannot be Muqaddam over the Ism Inna unless it is a Zarf or Jar-Majrur. Example that can be fronted: إِنَّ فِي الدَّارِ زَيْدًا (Zarf Khabar → permitted). Example that cannot be fronted: ❌ إِنَّ قَائِمًا زَيْدًا (non-Zarf Khabar → not allowed).

    Vocabulary:

    • مَحْذُوف — The implied (omitted) Fi'l is اسْتَقَرَّ, meaning "to be present / to reside." Full sentence: عَمْرٌو اسْتَقَرَّ فِي الدَّارِ — "Amr is (present/residing) in the house." It is Mahzoov because Arabic habitually omits it while the meaning remains understood.
    • مُتَعَلِّق — "Linked/connected to." A Zarf is Muta'alliq because it cannot stand alone grammatically — it must be connected to a Fi'l or something functioning as a Fi'l (here the implied istaqarra) to complete the predication.
    • النَّوَاسِخ — Abrogators. They "abrogate" (override) the default I'rab of the Mubtada-Khabar structure — changing what was Marfoo' Mubtada into Mansoob (with Inna), or changing both elements (with Kāna). They enter upon an existing Mubtada-Khabar structure and alter it.
    • اسم إِنَّ — Before Inna entered, this word was the Mubtada (Marfoo'). After Inna enters, it becomes the Ism Inna and is Mansoob. The Khabar retains its Rafa'.
    • عَامِل and مَعْمُول — In مَا قَائِمٌ زَيْدٌ: the Amil is qā'imun (the Sifat acting as Mubtada), and the Ma'mul is zaydun. The Amil causes Rafa' in the Ma'mul — zaydun is Marfoo' because qā'imun governs it as its Khabar/Fa'il-in-meaning.
    • صِفَة مُشَبَّهَة — The four qualifying Sighat Sifat are: Ism Fa'il, Ism Maf'ul, Sifat Mushabbaha, and Ism Tafdhil. All four are eligible to become Mubtada when the three conditions are met.
    • مُقَدَّم and مُؤَخَّر — In فِي الدَّارِ زَيْدٌ: fī al-dār (the Jar-Majrur) is Muqaddam (fronted Khabar); zaydun is Mu'akhkhar (delayed Mubtada). Yes, this arrangement is Ja'iz — Khabar may precede Mubtada in a regular Mubtada-Khabar sentence.
