## Nahw 201

*Sessions: Aug 24 & Aug 28*

**This week in a nutshell:** The first session focused on Fi'il-Fa'il gender and number agreement, the unusual I'rab of Jama' Mu'annath Salim, and Ism al-Manqus. The second session was an error-correction review of the nine categories of I'rab, sharpening the distinction between Taqdeeri I'rab and Mabni, and practicing the noon-drop rule in Mudaaf constructions.

### Key Points

- **Jama' Mu'annath Salim (JMS) in Nasb** never takes Fathah — it takes Kasrah as a substitute. So Dammah in Rafa', Kasrah in both Jarr and Nasb are all it can receive.
- **Fi'il-Fa'il gender agreement is absolute** regardless of word order. Number agreement depends on order: when the Fa'il comes *after* the Fi'il (standard), both gender and number match; when the Fa'il comes *before* the Fi'il (inverted), the Fi'il stays singular (Wahid) — only gender still matches.
- **Ism al-Manqus** (noun ending in Ya preceded by Kasrah, e.g. قَاضٍ) shows its I'rab only in Nasb, where Fathah appears explicitly on the Ya (الْقَاضِيَ). In Rafa' and Jarr the Harakah is Taqdiri — the word appears unchanged.
- **Taqdeeri ≠ Mabni**: Taqdeeri is a sub-type of Ma'rab — I'rab exists but cannot appear on the word. Mabni words (pronouns, demonstratives, relative nouns) receive no I'rab at all. مُوسَى is Ma'rab with Taqdeeri I'rab; هَؤُلَاءِ is Mabni.
- **Noon drops in Mudaaf**: whether it is the Noon of Tasniyah (وَلَدَانِ → وَلَدَا حَامِدٍ) or the Noon of Jama' Mudhakkar Salim (مُسْلِمُونَ → مُسْلِمُو بَاكِسْتَانَ), the Noon is always dropped when the word is Mudaaf.
- **Khabar of Inna is always Marfu'**: إِنَّ makes its Ism Mansub; the following Khabar remains in Rafa'. Do not confuse Khabar with Maf'ul Bih.
- **Sahih in Nahw vs. Sarf**: Nahw only checks whether the *last* letter is a Harf 'Illah; Sarf checks *all* letters. A word can be Sahih in Nahw but Mu'tall in Sarf.

| Arabic Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| فَاعِل (Fa'il) | Subject / doer of the verb |
| مُقَدَّم / مُؤَخَّر (Muqaddam / Mu'akhkhar) | Fronted (before Fi'il) / Deferred (after Fi'il) |
| اسم مَنْقُوص (Ism Manqus) | Noun ending in Ya preceded by Kasrah |

### Easy to Mix Up

- **Taqdeeri vs. Mabni**: both cause the word's ending to *look* unchanged, but for opposite reasons. Taqdeeri = I'rab is there but hidden (Ma'rab). Mabni = no I'rab whatsoever. Test: can the word's category (pronoun, demonstrative, etc.) be found in the fixed Mabni list? If yes, Mabni. If it is a regular noun like مُوسَى or كِتَابِي, it is Ma'rab with Taqdeeri.
- **JMS Nasb takes Kasrah, not Fathah**: unlike virtually every other noun, the sound feminine plural uses Kasrah in the accusative. إِنَّ الْمُسْلِمَاتِ (Mansub, Kasrah) vs. الْمُسْلِمَاتُ جَاءَتْ (Marfu', Dammah).

## Tafseer 201

*Sessions: Aug 25 & Aug 27*

**This week in a nutshell:** Both sessions worked through the munafiqeen passages of Surah Al-Baqarah. The first session covered Ayat 12–18: the grammatical emphasis on the munafiqeen as true corruptors, their mockery of the Sahabah, Allah's retributive "mocking" of them through respite before punishment, the profitless trade metaphor, and the fire parable. The second session completed the storm parable (the second munafiqeen example), then opened the new section (Ayat 21–25) with the address to all humanity, its rhetorical shift (iltifat), and the enumeration of Allah's blessings.

### Key Points

- **"Inna Humul Mufsidoon" (Ayah 12)** uses three layers of restriction: إِنَّ (emphasis) + هُمْ (dameer fasl) + الْمُفْسِدُونَ with the definite article = it is they — and none other — who are the corruptors. They cannot perceive this because the light of Iman (noor al-iman) has been extinguished in their hearts.
- **Hamzat al-Istifham lil-Inkaar**: when the munafiqeen said أَنُؤْمِنُ كَمَا آمَنَ السُّفَهَاءُ, the hamza is not a genuine question — it is an interrogative of mockery and contempt. Allah's response (أَلَا إِنَّهُمْ هُمُ السُّفَهَاءُ) uses hasr to restrict foolishness exclusively to the munafiqeen themselves.
- **"Allahu Yastahzi'u bihim" (Ayah 15)** uses the same root as the munafiqeen's mockery, but the meanings differ entirely: their istihza' = words of mockery; Allah's istihza' = actual punishment through imla' (giving respite) followed by a severe final reckoning. The Quran repeatedly uses one word for an evil act and its just punishment (جَزَاءُ سَيِّئَةٍ سَيِّئَةٌ مِثْلُهَا) — the moral status of each is opposite.
- **The fire parable (Ayah 17)**: a person kindles a fire, feels warmth and security, then Allah extinguishes it and leaves the person in total darkness and fear. Applied to the munafiqeen: the fire = their Iman when they first entered Islam; the extinguishing = their return to kufr; the darkness = the blindness of doubt and hypocrisy. Misguidance chosen *after* insight is worse than never having known.
- **The storm parable (Ayah 19–20)**: the munafiqeen are like people trapped in a violent storm — they walk when lightning flashes and freeze when it goes dark. This mirrors their behavior: aligning with whoever appeared stronger at any given time, wavering between iman and kufr (مُذَبْذَبِينَ بَيْنَ ذَٰلِكَ). The parenthetical sentence "Wallahu muhitun bil-kafirin" inserted mid-parable warns that Allah encircles them entirely — no escape is possible.
- **Iltifat (Ayah 21)**: the shift from third-person description of the three groups to the direct address "يَا أَيُّهَا النَّاسُ" is a rhetorical technique that activates the listener and signals a matter of utmost importance — used precisely because people are heedless (ghafilun) of it.
- **The blessings of creation (Ayat 21–22)**: creation from non-existence, the earth as a stable resting place (firash) despite being spherical, the sky as a canopy, rain bringing provision — all presented as rational grounds for worshipping Allah alone, with لَعَلَّكُمْ تَتَّقُونَ as the goal.

### Easy to Mix Up

- **Summun / Bukmun / Umyun** are plural forms (وزن فُعْل); the singulars are أَصَمّ / أَبْكَم / أَعْمَى (وزن أَفْعَل). Do not treat the plural form as singular.
- **Allah's "istihza'" vs. the munafiqeen's**: same word, completely different realities — their istihza' is a sinful act; Allah's is a just punishment. The Quran uses this device deliberately to show that the recompense matches the crime in form, while being its opposite in moral status.

## Fiqh 201

*Sessions: Aug 26 & Aug 29*

**This week in a nutshell:** The first session completed the chapter on wells (bi'r) — covering the water-removal quantities for different animal categories, the rule on repeating salah after unknowing use of contaminated water, and the concession for small dried droppings. The second session closed the chapter on water by completing the five categories of animal leftover water (su'r), then opened the chapter of Tayammum with its definition, historical origin, valid substances, obligatory elements, sunnahs, and what breaks it.

### Key Points

- **Dabaghat (tanning) purifies all animal hides** — except swine (najis in essence) and human skin (impermissible out of respect for human dignity). Once tanned, leather may be used for salah, wudu vessels, and water containers.
- **Eight parts of a dead animal are inherently pure (Hanafi)**: hair, bone, hoof, horn, wool, nail/claw, feather, beak — because they contain no life (no blood, no sensation). Exception: none of these are permissible from swine. Imam al-Shafi'i holds all eight najis.
- **Well contamination — water to remove by animal size**: small mouse/bird → 20–30 buckets; pigeon/cat-sized → 40–50 buckets; dog/goat/human or any swollen+decomposed animal → entire well emptied. The mu'tamad position for a ma'in (constantly replenishing) well is 200–300 buckets.
- **Repeating salah after contaminated well water**: if the animal was found dead but not yet swollen — repeat 5 prayers (1 day/night). If found swollen and cracked — Imam Abu Hanifa: repeat 15 prayers (3 days/nights); the two Sahibaan say repeat only the confirmed period.
- **Su'r (leftover water) — five categories**: (1) humans and halal-meat animals = pure; (2) dogs and swine = najis; (3) wild hunting animals = najis (Ahnaf) / pure (Shafi'i); (4) domestic free-roaming animals (cat, chicken) = makruh (Ahnaf) / pure (Shafi'i and Abu Yusuf); (5) donkeys and mules = mashkuk (doubtful) — perform both wudu and tayammum.
- **Tayammum** is exclusive to this ummah, revealed during Ghazwah Zat al-Riqa' when Aisha (RA)'s necklace went missing and water ran out by Fajr. Its two arkan: (1) striking the hands on earth; (2) wiping the face then both hands to the elbows. Niyyah is fard in tayammum (unlike wudu where it is mustahab).
- **Valid substances for Tayammum**: Imam Abu Hanifa (mu'tamad/operative fatwa) — anything from the genus of earth, including rock and stone. Imam Abu Yusuf — only soil or sand. The earth must be free of najasah.
- **Tayammum is broken** by everything that breaks wudu, plus one additional breaker: **sighting water one is able to use** invalidates tayammum immediately. If water is seen mid-salah, both tayammum and salah are invalidated and must be redone. If water is sighted after salam, the completed salah stands.

### Easy to Mix Up

- **Makruh vs. Mashkuk su'r**: cat and domestic animals = makruh (disliked but valid for wudu). Donkey/mule = mashkuk (genuinely doubtful) — wudu alone is not sufficient; both wudu and tayammum must be performed.
- **Niyyah in wudu vs. tayammum**: in wudu (Hanafi) niyyah is mustahab (recommended); in tayammum it is fard (obligatory). Missing it in tayammum invalidates it entirely.
