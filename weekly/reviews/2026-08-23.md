## Nahw 201

*Sessions: Aug 17 & Aug 21*

**This week in a nutshell:** The class launched a new chapter on the nine categories of i'rāb for nouns, covering all nine by the end of the week. The sessions moved from the standard primary i'rāb (Category 1) through deviations using substitute harakāt (Categories 2–3), letters in place of harakāt (Category 4), and then the remaining five categories including dual, sound plurals, and nouns with hidden i'rāb (Categories 5–9).

### Key Points

- **The nine categories** arise because the three base i'rāb states (Raf', Naṣb, Jarr) can be expressed by primary harakāt, substitute harakāt, letters (ḥurūf), or not visibly at all (taqdiree) — each combination produces a distinct category.
- **Category 1 (default):** Ḍamma / Fatḥa / Kasra — applies to mufrad, munsarif, ṣaḥīḥ nouns, as well as jārī majrā l-ṣaḥīḥ (e.g., *dalw*, *ẓaby*) and jam' mukassar munsarif (e.g., *rijāl*). In naḥw, ṣaḥīḥ means only the **final letter** is free of ḥarf 'illah — unlike in ṣarf, where any ḥarf 'illah anywhere makes it ma'tall.
- **Category 2 (jam' mu'annas sālim):** Ḍamma / **Kasra** / Kasra — kasra substitutes for fatḥa in naṣb; the kasra on *muslimāt* after *inna* is NOT jarr, it stands for naṣb.
- **Category 3 (ghayr munsarif):** Ḍamma / Fatḥa / **Fatḥa** — fatḥa substitutes for kasra in jarr; ghayr munsarif rejects both tanwīn and kasra entirely. When parsing i'rāb, state: *alāmatu jarrihi l-fatḥa niyābatan 'an il-kasra*.
- **Category 4 (asmā' al-sitta):** Wāw / Alif / Yā' — applies only when the noun is one of the six (أَبٌ، أَخٌ، حَمٌ، فَمٌ، هَنٌ، ذُو), is mukabbara (not diminutive), muwāḥada (singular), and muḍāf to **other than** Yā' al-Mutakallim. If muḍāf to Yā' al-Mutakallim, all three cases are identical (ـِي) and a different rule applies.
- **Category 5 (tasniyah, kilā/kiltā, ithnan/ithnatān):** Alif in Raf', Yā with fatḥa before it in Naṣb/Jarr. The Noon of tasniyah is always **maksūr (kasra)**; the Noon of jam' muzakkar sālim is always **maftūḥ (fatḥa)** — confusing these changes the meaning entirely.
- **Category 6 (jam' muzakkar sālim, ulu, 'ishrun wa akhawātuhā):** Wāw in Raf', Yā in Naṣb/Jarr. Both tasniyah and jam' muzakkar sālim **lose their Noon** when made muḍāf.
- **Categories 7 & 8 (ism maqṣūr; muḍāf to Yā' al-Mutakallim):** All three i'rābs are **hidden (taqdiree)** — the word's form never changes. Ism manqūṣ (Category 8) has hidden Raf' and Jarr but a **visible fatḥa in Naṣb** — the only case it shows.
- **Category 9 (jam' muzakkar sālim + Yā' al-Mutakallim):** The morphological process: Noon drops → Wāw + Yā' → Wāw converts to Yā' (ta'līl) → two Yā's merge with shaddah → preceding ḍamma changes to kasra (since Yā' requires kasra before it) → result: **مُسْلِمِيَّ**. Raf' is hidden; Naṣb/Jarr use visible Yā'.

### Easy to Mix Up

- **Ṣaḥīḥ in naḥw vs. ṣaḥīḥ in ṣarf:** In naḥw, only the final letter matters; in ṣarf, any ḥarf 'illah anywhere makes the word ma'tall. Do not use the ṣarf definition when categorizing nouns for i'rāb.
- **Kasra on jam' mu'annas sālim after *inna*** is not jarr — it is kasra standing for fatḥa of naṣb. Similarly, fatḥa on ghayr munsarif after a jarr particle is not naṣb — it is fatḥa standing for kasra of jarr.
- **Tasniyah vs. jam' muzakkar sālim in naṣb/jarr:** Both use yā', but the harakah before it differs — *muslimayni* (fatḥa, dual) vs. *muslimīna* (kasra, plural). Mixing them changes number and meaning.

## Tafseer 201

*Sessions: Aug 18 & Aug 20*

**This week in a nutshell:** The two sessions covered Surah Al-Baqarah Ayahs 7–12, moving from the sealing of the hearts (Ayah 7) into the detailed opening of the Munafiqeen section (Ayahs 8–12). The classes established the definition and types of nifāq, the historical occasion of revelation, and then worked through each of the first five Munafiqeen ayahs with both tafseer and rhetorical analysis.

### Key Points

- **Khatm al-qulūb (sealing of hearts):** Allah seals the hearts, hearing, and sight of persistent disbelievers as a direct consequence of their kufr and accumulated sins. This is a spiritual condition, not a physical disease — these people are physically fully functional. The sealing locks kufr in and Iman out, with no remaining mâslik (pathway) for guidance.
- **Three categories in the surah's opening:** Ayahs 1–5 describe the Mu'mineen, 6–7 the Kāfirūn, and 8–20 the Munāfiqūn — Allah devotes thirteen full ayahs to the hypocrites to highlight their exceptional danger and harm to the Muslim community from within.
- **Definition of nifāq (Ibn Kathir):** *Iẓhār al-khayr wa-isrār al-sharr* — displaying goodness outwardly while concealing evil inwardly. **Nifāq i'tiqādī** (creedal, inward kufr with outward Islam) causes eternal punishment in the Fire. **Nifāq 'amalī** (behavioral, e.g., lying, breaking promises, betraying trust) is among the gravest sins but does not make one a permanent kāfir if the aqeedah is sound.
- **Nifāq is a Madinan phenomenon:** In Makkah, opposition was open. Nifāq emerged only in Madinah when Islam became dominant and some joined for social/political benefit — which is why all detailed descriptions of munāfiqūn appear in Madani surahs.
- **Sabab al-nuzūl:** Ibn Abbas reports these ayahs were revealed about hypocrites from Ahl al-Kitāb, notably **'Abdullāh ibn Ubayy ibn Salūl** (the foremost leader of the munāfiqūn in Madinah) and **Mu'tab ibn Qushayr** — who would claim before Muslims that they found the Prophet's description in their own scriptures while concealing disbelief in their hearts.
- **Ayah 9 — yukhādi'ūn Allāh:** The munāfiqūn attempt to deceive Allah and the believers by displaying Iman while insisting on kufr internally. In reality (*wamā yakhda'ūna illā anfusahum*), they deceive only themselves — the evil consequence (*wabāl*) of their actions returns to them — yet they do not perceive this due to their excessive heedlessness (*ghafla*) and foolishness (*hamāqa*).
- **Ayah 10 — fī qulūbihim maraḍ:** The disease is spiritual — doubt (*shakk*) and hypocrisy, not a physical illness. Allah increases their disease as a consequence of their persistence in nifāq — *rijs fawqa rijs, ḍalāl fawqa ḍalāl*. The painful punishment awaits for their lying about Iman and mocking Allah's verses.
- **Ayah 12 — alā innahum hum al-mufsidūn:** Allah's emphatic refutation uses four stacked rhetorical devices: *alā* (particle of alerting), *inna* (particle of confirmation), *hum* (ḍamīr al-faṣl, separating pronoun), and *al-mufsidūn* with the definite article — together the most emphatic possible repudiation in Arabic style.

### Easy to Mix Up

- **إِسْرَار (concealing) vs. إِصْرَار (persisting):** isrār (with sīn) comes from sirr (secret) — to hide something; iṣrār (with ṣād) comes from aṣarra — to insist on or persist in something. In the context of nifāq, isrār al-sharr = concealing evil; iṣrār 'ala al-kufr = persisting in disbelief.
- **رِجْس (rijs) vs. نَجَاسَة (najāsa):** Rijs is moral/spiritual filth — used for idols, kufr, and nifāq. Najāsa is physical ritual impurity affecting worship. The two are distinct categories.
- **Kasra on *muslimāt* after *inna* (Ayah 7, previous session)** is a naṣb marker, not a jarr marker — the jam' mu'annas sālim rule (kasra substitutes for fatḥa in naṣb) applies in tafseer as in grammar.

## Fiqh 201

*Sessions: Aug 22*

**This week in a nutshell:** This session completed the categories of ghusl and the three bodily fluids (mani, madhi, wadi), then covered the chapter on water — what makes water permissible or impermissible for tahara, the rules for stagnant and flowing water, animals dying in water, and the status and uses of maa' musta'mal. Evidences from Quran, hadith, and the four madhhabs were examined throughout.

### Key Points

- **Categories of ghusl:** Fard (janaba, menstruation, postnatal bleeding), Sunnah/Masnoon (Jumu'ah, both Eids), Wājib (one who gives ghusl to a deceased person must perform ghusl themselves), Mustaḥab (upon accepting Islam, reaching puberty, regaining sanity). The wājib and mustaḥab categories are supplemental — not in Imam Quduri's text directly.
- **Three bodily fluids:** **Manī** (seminal discharge with desire) requires ghusl. **Madhī** (pre-seminal fluid during foreplay) breaks wuḍū only — wash and make wuḍū, no ghusl. **Wadī** (thick fluid sometimes appearing after urination) breaks wuḍū only. The distinction between madhī and manī determines whether ghusl is obligatory.
- **Permissible water for tahara:** Rain, valleys (*awdiya*), springs (*'uyūn*), wells (*abār*), and seas (*biḥār*) — all traced back to rainwater (Surah al-Furqan 25:48: *mā'an ṭahūrā*). Water becomes impermissible when a foreign substance **dominates** (*ghalaba*) and alters its qualities; if only one of three qualities (color, taste, smell) is altered by a permissible additive (soap, *ishnān*, saffron), it remains usable. The Ahnaf argue that naming it "saffron water" does not strip it of its essence, just as "well water" remains *ṭāhur*.
- **Stagnant water — small body (*ma-e-kalīl*):** Any amount of *najāsa* entering a small stagnant body (less than 10×10 hand-spans, per Imam Muhammad's opinion adopted by the madhhab) renders it entirely impure. Imam Malik disagrees — if none of the three qualities change, it remains pure; the Ahnaf respond that *Bi'r Buḍā'a* (his dalil) had an underground stream, making it effectively flowing water.
- **Stagnant water — large body (*ma-e-kathīr*):** *Najāsa* entering a large body (10×10 hand-spans with sufficient depth) does NOT render it impure unless one of the three qualities visibly changes. The **ripple test**: if movement on one side causes no perceptible movement on the other, it is a large body (Imam Muhammad's definition: scooping water on one side; Shaykhain: performing ghusl on one side).
- **Flowing water (*mā' jārī*):** *Najāsa* does not render it impure as long as no *athar* (visible trace/effect) of the *najāsa* remains — the flow disperses it. Water is considered flowing if leaves/grass placed on it are carried away, or if scooping immediately draws new water from upstream.
- **Animals without flowing blood dying in water:** Fish, frogs, flies, mosquitoes, scorpions — their death in water does not introduce *najāsa*, since the Quran declares flowing blood as *najis* (Surah al-An'am). A fly falling into food may be removed and the food remains permissible.
- ***Maa' musta'mal* (used water):** Water used to remove a *ḥadath* becomes *musta'mal* the moment it **leaves the body** (drips off). The operative fatwa of the Ahnaf (aligned with Imam Muhammad and one opinion of Imam Abu Hanifa): it is ***ṭāhir* but not *muṭahhar*** — pure in itself, but cannot be used for wuḍū or ghusl. It may be used for istinji', irrigation, cooking. Dalil: the Prophet ﷺ gave leftover wuḍū water to the sick for *baraka*; if it were najis, he would not have done so.

### Easy to Mix Up

- ***Ṭāhir* vs. *ṭahūr*:** *Ṭahūr* is both pure AND purifying (e.g., rain, well water). *Ṭāhir* is pure but cannot purify others (e.g., *maa' musta'mal* per the operative fatwa). Using *ṭāhir* water for wuḍū is not valid even though it is not impure.
- **Kasra on naṣb of *jam' mu'annas sālim*** (Nahw overlap): just as in grammar, the kasra on *muslimāt* in naṣb is not a jarr marker — the same logic applies when encountering this in Fiqh texts (e.g., in *inna al-muslimāt*).
- **Imam Malik's position on small stagnant water with unaltered qualities** (water remains pure) is the opposite of the Ahnaf position (water is impure regardless of whether qualities change). The Ahnaf maintain the strict rule; Malik requires visible change. Do not conflate the two.
