## Nahw 201

*Sessions: Aug 31 & Sep 04*

**This week in a nutshell:** The class covered the framework of Munsarif vs. Ghair Munsarif nouns and examined three of the nine asbab that trigger Ghair Munsarif status. Aug 31 focused on Adl (both Tahqiqi and Taqdiri sub-types) and Wasf (Asli vs. Ardi). Sep 04 introduced the third sabab, Ta'nith, with its two types (Lafzi and Ma'nawi) and the layered sub-conditions for three-letter words.

### Key Points

- **Ghair Munsarif** requires two of the nine asbab (or one equivalent to two); its hukm is rejection of both Kasra and Tanwin — Fatha substitutes for Kasra in the Jarr position (e.g., مَرَرْتُ بِأَحْمَدَ not بِأَحْمَدٍ).
- **Adl Tahqiqi** is deviation with evidence: the meaning of ثُلَاث/مَثْلَث ("three and three") implies repetition in the original form (سَلَاسَةٌ سَلَاسَةٌ), proving it was deviated. Paired with Wasf, it gives two asbab.
- **Adl Taqdiri** is assumed deviation: grammarians inferred an original form (عَامِر → عُمَر; زَافِر → زُفَر) to account for Arab usage. Only 15 words fall in this category; Adl Taqdiri pairs with Alamiyya.
- **Adl never pairs with Wazn Fi'l** as its second sabab — when seeking the pairing sabab for Adl, Wazn Fi'l is excluded.
- **Wasf must be Asli (permanent)** to function as a sabab. Wasf Ardi — adjectival only within a specific sentence structure — does not qualify. A word born as a descriptor retains Wasf Asliyya permanently even if it later becomes a common noun (e.g., أَسْوَد becoming a name for black snakes remains Ghair Munsarif).
- **Wasf and Alamiyya never combine** as the two asbab. Wasf pairs with other asbab (e.g., Adl), never with Alam.
- **Ta'nith Lafzi** (visible marker: ة / ى / اء) + Alam = Ghair Munsarif, with no further conditions. If the marker is present but the word is not an Alam (e.g., مَدْرَسَةٌ), it remains Munsarif.
- **Ta'nith Ma'nawi** (no visible marker; exclusively female by usage, e.g., زَيْنَب، هِنْد) + Alam = Ghair Munsarif if the word has more than three letters. For three-letter words, check the middle letter: if voweled (mutaharrik) → Ghair Munsarif; if vowelless (sakin) and non-Arabic ('Ajami) → Ghair Munsarif; if vowelless and Arabic → both Munsarif and Ghair Munsarif are valid (e.g., هِنْد).

### Vocabulary to Know

| Arabic Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| مُنْصَرِف (Munsarif) | Fully declinable noun; accepts all harakaat + tanwin |
| غَيْرُ مُنْصَرِف (Ghayru Munsarif) | Partially declinable noun; rejects Kasra and Tanwin |
| عَدْل (Adl) | Deviation of a noun from its original pattern (sigha) |
| عَدْل تَحْقِيقِي (Adl Tahqiqi) | Verified deviation — evidence for the original form exists |
| عَدْل تَقْدِيرِي (Adl Taqdiri) | Assumed deviation — grammarians infer the original form |
| وَصْف أَصْلِي (Wasf Asli) | Permanent descriptor — a word coined originally as an adjective |
| وَصْف عَرَضِي (Wasf Ardi) | Temporary descriptor — only an adjective within a specific syntactic context |
| تَأْنِيث لَفْظِي (Ta'nith Lafzi) | Femininity marked by a visible sign (ة / ى / اء) |
| تَأْنِيث مَعْنَوِي (Ta'nith Ma'nawi) | Femininity established by meaning/usage without a visible marker |
| مُتَحَرِّك الأَوْسَط (Mutaharrik al-Awsat) | Three-letter word whose middle letter carries a vowel |
| سَاكِن الأَوْسَط (Sakin al-Awsat) | Three-letter word whose middle letter is vowelless |
| عَجَمِي (Ajami) | Non-Arabic; foreign in origin |
| عَلَمِيَّة (Alamiyya) | The quality of being a proper noun |

### Easy to Mix Up

- **Wasf Asli vs. Wasf Ardi:** Only Asli qualifies as a sabab for Ghair Munsarif. The test: does the word function as a descriptor outside of any particular sentence structure? If yes, it is Asli. If it only describes within a specific grammatical context, it is Ardi and does not count (e.g., أَرْبَعٍ in نِسْوَةٍ أَرْبَعٍ is Ardi → Munsarif).
- **Ta'nith Ma'nawi + Thulathi:** Students often assume any female name is automatically Ghair Munsarif. The three-letter female name requires an additional check — middle letter (mutaharrik or sakin) and then origin (Arabic or Ajami). هِنْد being Arabic with a sakin middle letter is the exception where both readings are valid.

## Tafseer 201

*Sessions: Sep 01, Sep 03 & Sep 05*

**This week in a nutshell:** Three sessions traced a continuous arc in Surah Al-Baqarah: Sep 01 covered the ayat establishing Tawheed al-Uluhiyyah through Allah's blessings and the prohibition of Shirk, then opened the Tahhaddi (challenge) verses; Sep 03 deepened the Tahhaddi with the role of لَن, the description of Hellfire, and its fuel; Sep 05 completed the arc with glad tidings for the believers, detailing the four supreme blessings of Jannah. Substantial grammatical work ran across all three sessions.

### Key Points

- **Tawheed al-Uluhiyyah** is established through Allah's acts of creation and sustenance: bringing humanity from non-existence, sending rain from السَّحاب (clouds) at precisely the moment of need, and producing crops and fruits as rizq for people and their livestock. Because He alone creates and sustains, He alone deserves worship — setting up أَنْدَاد (equals/partners) alongside Him contradicts this acknowledged reality.
- **The Tahhaddi (challenge)** of Surah Al-Baqarah: if you doubt the Quran sent to عَبْدِنَا (the Prophet ﷺ), produce a single surah like it — calling on all of humanity, jinn, and idols for help. The challenge targets the Quran's بَلَاغَة (rhetoric), بَيَان (clarity), نَظْم (composition), words, meanings, and legislation simultaneously.
- **لَن (Lan)** negates permanently and eternally into the future. وَلَن تَفْعَلُوا is a parenthetical clause (جُمْلَة اعْتِرَاضِيَّة) stating with divine certainty that the challenge has never been and will never be met — not a guess, but a qati' jazim (decisive, conclusive) declaration confirmed by history.
- **Hellfire's fuel** (وَقُودُهَا النَّاسُ وَالْحِجَارَة) is people and stones; Mujahid identified the stones as sulfur (كِبْرِيت) — more foul-smelling than carrion (الجِيفَة). Both the disbelievers and their idols will be fuel (Surah Al-Anbiya cross-reference). The fire is described as أُعِدَّتْ — passive past tense (Madhi Majhul): it was prepared and is already awaiting the disbelievers.
- **Targheeb and Tarheeb** is the Quranic method of pairing hope and fear: the description of Jannah immediately follows Jahannam to move the heart in both directions.
- **Jannah's four supreme blessings** map to the four deepest human desires: (1) best residence — gardens with rivers flowing beneath; (2) best food — identical-looking fruits with entirely different tastes each time; (3) best companions — أَزْوَاجٌ مُطَهَّرَة (purified wives), free from all physical and moral impurity; (4) eternal abiding — خَالِدُون, the blessings never interrupted, constituting تَمَامُ السَّعَادَة (complete happiness).
- **Fruits of Jannah:** Ibn Jareer: similar in color and appearance, different in taste. Ibn Abbas: only the names are shared; everything else — color, appearance, taste — is entirely unlike the dunya.
- **I'jaz al-Quran** has both apparent (ظَاهِرَة) and hidden (بَاطِنَة) dimensions; some miracles only became manifest as history unfolded. The entire Quran is at the highest level of فَصَاحَة and بَيَان, recognizable as such by anyone with command of Arabic.

| Arabic Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| الأُلُوهِيَّة (al-Uluhiyyah) | Divinity; Allah's exclusive right to be worshipped |
| أَنْدَاد (Andaad) | Equals, rivals, partners (pl. of نِدّ) set alongside Allah |
| إِعْجَاز الْقُرْآن (I'jaz al-Quran) | The miraculous inimitability of the Quran |
| الرَّيْب (al-Rayb) | Doubt, suspicion |

| جُمْلَة اعْتِرَاضِيَّة (Jumla I'tiradiyya) | Parenthetical/interjected clause |
| وَقُود (Waqud) | Fuel; the substance that kindles a fire |
| أُعِدَّت (U'iddat) | Was prepared (passive past tense / Madhi Majhul) |
| كِبْرِيت (Kibrit) | Sulfur |

### Easy to Mix Up

- **لَن vs. لَا / لَمْ:** لَمْ negates in the past; لَا negates in the present; لَن negates in the future permanently. In وَلَن تَفْعَلُوا, the emphasis is that the incapacity extends into the infinite future — it is not just "you did not" but "you never will."
- **Ism of Anna vs. Khabar of Anna:** The ism is always mansoob and the khabar is always marfoo', but their order can invert (khabar muqaddam, ism mu'akhkhar). In أَنَّ لَهُمْ جَنَّاتٍ, لَهُمْ is the pre-posed khabar (jar + majroor / shibhu jumla); جَنَّاتٍ is the post-posed ism — mansoob with kasra because it is a sound feminine plural (Jam' Mu'annath Salim), not fatha.

## Fiqh 201

*Sessions: Sep 02 & Sep 05*

**This week in a nutshell:** Sep 02 completed the chapter on Tayammum (conditions, distances, sickness rulings, invalidators, performing multiple prayers) and opened Masa al-Khuffayn (dalil, conditions, duration, method, invalidators). Sep 05 concluded Masa by covering Jorabayn, Jabair, and head coverings, then opened the chapter on Haydh — its definition, durations per the four madhabs, blood colors, and the rulings it activates.

### Key Points

- **Tayammum is a complete, independent Tahara** in the Hanafi view — not tied to a specific prayer or time window. Multiple prayers with one Tayammum are valid; the dalil is the hadith that the earth serves as Tahara for Muslims even for ten years. Imam Shafi'i limits each Tayammum to one prayer.
- **What breaks Tayammum:** everything that breaks Wudhu, plus finding water sufficient for Wudhu. If found water is only enough to drink, Tayammum remains valid. If Tayammum was for Ghusl and the water found is only enough for Wudhu, Tayammum for Ghusl also remains valid.
- **Masa al-Khuffayn is Hadith Mutawatirah** — narrated by 40–70 Sahabah. Imam Abu Hanifa warned of Kufr upon anyone who denies its permissibility. Condition: socks must be worn while in complete Wudhu; Masa applies only to Hadas (minor impurity), not Janabah. Duration: 24 hours for resident (Muqeem), 72 hours for traveller (Musafir), counting from the first Hadas after wearing — not from when the socks were put on. Method: three wet fingers, top surface only, drawn from toes upward to the shin.
- **Regular Jorabayn (socks) require three conditions** for valid Masa per the Hanafi mainstream: (1) water cannot seep through, (2) they stay up on the shin unaided, (3) they can withstand walking one mile without tearing. Thin professional socks worn today typically fail all three. Sahibayn (Abu Yusuf and Muhammad) permit Masa if socks are simply thick enough to repel water.
- **Masa on Jabair (bandages/casts) has four key differences from Masa on socks:** (1) no prior Wudhu is required when applying the bandage; (2) no time limit — valid as long as medically necessary; (3) if the bandage falls and the wound is healed, Wudhu breaks; if the wound is still unhealed, simply retie and Wudhu is intact; (4) if one sock is removed, both feet must be washed — with Jabair, only the exposed area is addressed.
- **Masa on turbans (Amamah), caps, face veils, and gloves is not valid** in the Hanafi madhab. Valid Masa of the head requires the hand to reach actual hair — slide the hand beneath the Amamah to wipe at least one-quarter of the head.
- **Haydh (minimum/maximum per Ahnaf):** 3 days minimum, 10 days maximum. Blood outside this window is Istihada, not Haydh, and does not carry Haydh's rulings. Imam Shafi'i: 1 day / 15 days. Imam Malik: no minimum / 10 days.
- **End of Haydh** is confirmed only by completely white/clear discharge with no trace of color. Sayyidah Aisha's fatwa: do not hurry — wait for complete whiteness.
- **Rulings of Haydh:** Salah is سَاقِط (permanently dropped — no qada). Sawm (Ramadan) is haram during Haydh but **qada is required**. Entering the Masjid and all forms of Tawaf are absolutely prohibited. Marital relations are haram. Recitation of the Quran is prohibited (unlike a person who merely lacks Wudhu, who may recite from memory — Haydh and Janabah prohibit recitation because the impurity encompasses the entire body including the mouth). Touching the written Quran without Wudhu is prohibited; a cloth/cover barrier is permissible.

### Easy to Mix Up

- **Salah vs. Sawm during Haydh:** Salah is saqit — permanently dropped, no qada ever. Sawm (Ramadan fasting) is haram during Haydh but qada is obligatory afterward. Confusing these is common and leads to incorrect practice.
- **Person lacking Wudhu (Muhdith) vs. Ha'idah/Junub regarding Quran:** A person who only lacks Wudhu (hadas asghar) may recite the Quran from memory but may not touch the written text. A Ha'idah or Junub may neither recite nor touch the written text — the impurity of Haydh and Janabah is of the entire body, including the mouth.
- **When the Masa duration clock starts:** It begins at the first Hadas after wearing the socks — not when the socks were put on. If socks were worn after Wudhu at 6:20 PM and Wudhu broke at 7:30 PM, the 24-hour window starts at 7:30 PM, not 6:20 PM.
