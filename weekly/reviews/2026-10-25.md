## Nahw 201

**In a nutshell:** This week introduced مَفْعُولٌ مَعَهُ, the fourth type of Maf'ool, and the rules governing when it is obligatory versus when 'Atf is permissible instead.

### Key Points

- **مَفْعُولٌ مَعَهُ** is a منصوب noun after a وَاوُ الْمَعِيَّة ("with"), not a Harf 'Atf ("and"), indicating the Fa'il and this noun co-occurred.
- When the Fa'il is present *lafzan* and 'Atf is *jaa'iz*, **two options** are valid: treat the noun as Maf'ool Ma'ahu (منصوب) or as Ma'toof (following Ma'toof 'Alayh in i'raab).
- **نَصْبٌ مُتَعَيَّن** (Nasb is obligatory) when 'Atf is not permissible — the most common reason being that the Ma'toof 'Alayh is a **ضَمِيرٌ مُتَّصِل** with no **ضَمِيرٌ مُنْفَصِل** present (e.g., جِئْتُ وَزَيْدًا).
- Inserting a matching **ضَمِيرٌ مُنْفَصِل** (e.g., أَنَا for تُ) restores the permissibility of 'Atf, giving two valid options again (e.g., جِئْتُ أَنَا وَزَيْدًا/زَيْدٌ).
- **مَعْطُوف** has no independent i'raab — it always mirrors its **مَعْطُوفٌ عَلَيْه**, whether مرفوع, منصوب, or مجرور.

### Vocabulary

| Arabic Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| مَفْعُولٌ مَعَهُ | Object of accompaniment; منصوب noun after Waaw of "with" |
| وَاوُ الْمَعِيَّة | The Waaw meaning "along with," not "and" |
| مُصَاحَبَة | Companionship; co-occurrence of Fa'il and noun |
| مُتَعَيَّن | Obligatory; only one grammatical option is valid |
| ضَمِيرٌ مُنْفَصِل | Detached pronoun (أَنَا، أَنْتَ) that permits 'Atf upon a Zameer Muttasil |
| مَعْطُوفٌ عَلَيْه | The noun the Ma'toof is conjoined to; determines Ma'toof's i'raab |

### Easy to Mix Up

- **وَاوُ الْمَعِيَّة vs. حَرْفُ عَطْف**: both are written وَ, but Waaw of Ma'iyyah means "along with" and makes what follows منصوب (Maf'ool Ma'ahu), while Harf 'Atf means "and" and makes what follows take the i'raab of the Ma'toof 'Alayh.

## Fiqh 201

**In a nutshell:** This week covered the Ḥanafī ruling against nawāfil before Maghrib, then opened Bāb al-Nawāfil with the daily Sunnah ṣalāhs, their classifications, and the rules on how many rakʿāt may be prayed per salām by day and night.

### Key Points

- **Nawāfil before Maghrib are not permitted** in the Ḥanafī madhhab — the farḍ must be prayed immediately; Imām al-Shāfiʿī permits two rakʿāt, based on a ḥadīth in Bukhārī.
- The **12 Sunnah Muʾakkadah rakʿāt** (2 Fajr + 4+2 Ẓuhr + 2 Maghrib + 2 ʿIshāʾ) carry a ḥadīth promise of a house in Jannah and should be prioritized over general nawāfil.
- The **ʿAṣr sunnah** (4 rakʿāt before) is **مستحب**, not Sunnah Muʾakkadah — missing it is less grave than missing the emphasized sunnahs.
- Imām Abū Ḥanīfah permits up to **8 rakʿāt per salām** at night; the Ṣāḥibayn and Imām al-Shāfiʿī restrict night nawāfil to **2 rakʿāt per salām**.
- **Duʿāʾ in ṣalāh** must be in Arabic from transmitted supplications; the best moments are in **سجود** and before the closing salām — personal needs may be held in the heart without verbalizing.

### Vocabulary

| Arabic Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| سُنَّةٌ مُؤَكَّدَة | Emphasized sunnah; Prophet ﷺ was rarely absent from it |
| مستحب | Recommended; rewarding but no sin for omission |
| أَوَّابِين | Six voluntary rakʿāt after Maghrib farḍ |
| تَهَجُّد | Voluntary night prayer performed after sleeping |
| قِيَامُ اللَّيْل | Night worship; broadly synonymous with tahajjud |
| دُعَاءٌ مَأْثُورَة | Supplications transmitted from the Prophet ﷺ |

### Easy to Mix Up

- **Sunnah Muʾakkadah vs. Mustaḥab**: both are non-obligatory, but Sunnah Muʾakkadah reflects the Prophet's ﷺ near-constant practice and carries greater weight — habitually missing it is blameworthy, whereas missing a Mustaḥab carries no blame.
