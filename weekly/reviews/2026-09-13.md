## Nahw 201

*Sessions: Sep 07 & Sep 11*

**This week in a nutshell:** Both sessions focused on the causes (*asbab*) that make a word *ghayr munsarif* (diptote). The first session covered five asbab — Ta'nith with Alif Maqsura/Mamduda, Alamiyya, 'Ujma, Jama' Muntaha al-Jumu', and Tarkib — while the second session completed the list with Alif Nun Za'idatan and Wazn al-Fi'l, then covered the two exceptions that restore kasra to ghayr munsarif words.

### Key Points

- **Ghayr munsarif** requires either two asbab together, or one sabab that is *qa'im maqam as-sababayn* (standing in place of two). The three asbab strong enough to stand alone are: **Alif Maqsura, Alif Mamduda**, and **Jama' Muntaha al-Jumu'**.
- **Alamiyya vs. Ma'rifa:** Every alam is definite, but not every definite noun is an alam. An alam is a specific *given name* (زَيْد, إِبْلِيس). شَيْطَان is definite but not an alam — it can refer to any evil jinn — so it is munsarif despite sounding like a proper name.
- **'Ujma (non-Arabic origin)** only serves as a sabab when the word is also an alam. Sulathi (three-letter) foreign names with a sakin middle letter are treated as munsarif due to their lightness (نُوح, لُوط).
- **Jama' Muntaha al-Jumu'** must be on pattern مَفَاعِل or مَفَاعِيل. After the alif: two letters directly, three letters with a sakin middle, or one mushaddad letter. رِجَال fails this pattern and is munsarif.
- **Tarkib** (compounding) is a sabab only when the compound is an alam AND has no idafa or isnad between its parts. عَبْدُ اللَّهِ has idafa → munsarif. بَعْلَبَكَّ has neither → ghayr munsarif.
- **Alif Nun Za'idatan in an ism** requires Alamiyya as the second sabab. In a *wasf* (adjective), the wasf itself is the second sabab — but only if the feminine form does not come on the pattern فَعْلَانَة (e.g., نَدْمَان → feminine نَدْمَانَة → munsarif; سَكْرَان → feminine سَكْرَى → ghayr munsarif). Check the root letters first: if the Alif Nun is *from* the root (like شَيْطَان from ش-ط-ن), it is not za'id and the word is munsarif.
- **Wazn al-Fi'l** has two tracks: patterns *exclusive* to verbs (like فُعِلَ or فَعَّلَ) need only Alamiyya as the second sabab. Patterns *shared* between ism and fi'l additionally require the word to begin with one of the **Huruf al-Mudara'a (أ ت ي ن)** and to have no Ta Marbuta at the end (أَحْمَد, يَزِيد, تَغْلِب).
- **Two exceptions restore kasra** to ghayr munsarif words: (1) when the word is in an **idafa** construction, and (2) when it is prefixed with **alif-lam**. Example: مَرَرْتُ بِالْمَسَاجِدِ (kasra because of alif-lam); مَرَرْتُ بِمَسَاجِدِكُمْ (kasra because muzaf).
- A ghayr munsarif alam becomes **munsarif when used as nakira** (no longer referring to a specific individual). In لِكُلِّ فِرْعَوْنَ مُوسَى, Fir'aun and Musa refer to *archetypes*, not specific people → both become munsarif and accept tanwin.

### Vocabulary to Know

| Arabic Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| غَيْرُ مُنصَرِف | Diptote; does not accept tanwin and takes fatha instead of kasra when majrur |

### Easy to Mix Up

- **Alif Nun za'id vs. Alif Nun as a root letter:** Always check the root first. شَيْطَان has Alif Nun from the root ش-ط-ن → munsarif. عِمْرَان has Alif Nun added to root ع-م-ر → ghayr munsarif (with Alamiyya).
- **Khas vs. common awzan for Wazn al-Fi'l:** Patterns like فُعِلَ and فَعَّلَ belong exclusively to verbs → Alamiyya alone is sufficient. Patterns like يَفْعَل are shared → must also begin with أتينا and have no Ta Marbuta. Do not apply the Huruf al-Mudara'a condition to khas patterns.

## Tafseer 201

*Sessions: Sep 08*

**This week in a nutshell:** The session completed the passage on the Fasiqeen in Surah Al-Baqarah, enumerating their three defining qualities and their ultimate loss. It then moved into Ayat 28–29, where Allah presents the stages of human existence and the creation of the universe as proofs of His power and the certainty of resurrection.

### Key Points

- **Ghayr munsarif in context — the Fasiqeen are those who have exited Allah's obedience** (*al-kharijeen 'an ta'at Allah*) and who deny His signs. The Quran's parables do not mislead anyone; it is the Fasiq's own rejection that causes misguidance.
- **Three qualities of the Fasiqeen:** (1) Breaking Allah's covenant (*Naqd al-'Ahd*) — specific to Ahl al-Kitab, who were bound by the Torah and Injeel to believe in the final Prophet ﷺ. (2) Severing what Allah commanded to be joined (*Qat' ma amarallahu bihi an yusala*) — includes cutting family ties, rejecting some prophets while accepting others, and abandoning loyalty to believers; applies broadly. (3) Spreading corruption on earth (*Ifsad fil-Ard*) — through sins, kufr, shirk, and raising doubts about the Quran; applies to all groups.
- **"Al-Fitnatu ashaddu min al-Qatl"** — "Fitnah is worse than killing" (Al-Baqarah). All Mufassireen explain that Fitnah here means spreading kufr and shirk — the gravest form of corruption.
- The Fasiqeen are called **the true losers** because they exchanged guidance for misguidance and forgiveness for punishment. The emphasis "هُمُ" (they *themselves*) marks them as the real losers — those who lose their akhira, not merely worldly things.
- **Ayah 28 — "Kayfa" as an interrogative of rebuke:** The question "How can you disbelieve in Allah?" is not a real question but an *Inkaar* (rebuke and denial). Allah then lists five stages of human existence — non-existence, life, death, resurrection, return to Allah — as proof that He who gave life once can give it again.
- **Ayah 29 — Creation as daleel for resurrection:** Allah created the earth for humanity's benefit, then formed seven heavens. The logical proof: the One capable of creating the entire universe can certainly recreate a single human being. The greater act has already been done; resurrection is lesser by comparison.
- **"Istawa ila al-sama'"** — Allah rose/directed His will toward the heavens, understood as befits His majesty (no likening to creation).

| نَقْضُ الْعَهْدِ (Naqd al-'Ahd) | Breaking the covenant |
| مِيثَاق (Mithaq) | A binding, affirmed, witnessed covenant — stronger than a basic 'ahd |
| قَطْعُ الأَرْحَام (Qat' al-Arham) | Severing ties of kinship |

### Easy to Mix Up

- **Alam vs. Ma'rifa (carried over from Nahw):** The Tafseer session reinforces this: the Fasiqeen are defined by *leaving* obedience — just as an alam must be a *specific given name*, not merely any definite term. Shaytan is definite but not a specific proper name — similarly, a Fasiq is not just any definite sinner but one who has made a deliberate exit from submission.
- **Naqd al-'Ahd applies to Ahl al-Kitab only** — not to the Mushrikeen, who had no revealed book with such covenants. The Ifsad characteristic, by contrast, applies to all groups.

## Fiqh 201

*Sessions: Sep 09 & Sep 12*

**This week in a nutshell:** The first session finished the eleven rulings of hayd and introduced istihada with its four intra-Hanafi opinions and the three categories of women. The second session completed nifas and then opened Babu'l-Anjas (Chapter on Impurities), covering the two types of najasa and the methods of purifying the body, clothing, and various surfaces.

### Key Points

- **No qada' for salah during hayd, but qada' is required for fasting.** The rationale is ḥaraj: a woman with a 10-day habit misses ~600 salawat per year — an unmanageable burden. Missed fasting days (max 10) can be spread across the remaining 11 months without reaching the same threshold. Evidence: hadith in Sahih Muslim from 'A'isha (RA).
- **Masjid vs. musalla distinction:** A ḥa'idha may not enter the main prayer hall of a masjid (land permanently declared waqf). A musalla — a rented or temporary prayer space — does not carry this ruling. The criterion is legal designation (*intention and waqf*), not physical features like a dome or minaret.
- **Marital relations during hayd:** If a husband or wife considers this prohibition halal (denies it is haram), this constitutes **kufr** (per Fath al-Qadir), because it is an explicit Quranic ruling. Committing the act while knowing it is haram is a sin requiring tawba — not kufr.
- **Istihada** is any vaginal bleeding lasting fewer than 3 days or more than 10 days. It carries the ruling of a nosebleed: salah, fasting, and conjugal relations are all permissible. The woman performs ghusl when hayd ends (day 10), then makes fresh wudu for each obligatory prayer.
- **Nifas:** Post-natal bleeding with no minimum and a maximum of 40 days. Bleeding before the child is born = istihada. If bleeding exceeds 40 days, the woman reverts to her established adat (habit from previous births); if no consistent adat exists, 40 days = nifas, everything beyond = istihada.
- **Twin births disagreement:** The Shaykhain (Abu Hanifa and Abu Yusuf) hold that nifas begins from the first birth. Imam Muhammad and Imam Zufar hold it begins from the second. This matters practically when the two births are far apart.
- **Domain-based weighting of Hanafi imams:** Imam Abu Hanifa's opinion is primary in **ibadah**. Imam Abu Yusuf — who served 12 years as Qadi al-Qudat — governs **judicial matters**. Imam Muhammad — who mastered ilm al-miras and compiled the Zahir al-Riwaya — governs **inheritance**.
- **Two types of najasa:** *Najasat-e-Hukmi* (ritual impurity requiring wudu or ghusl, no visible filth) and *Najasat-e-Hakiki/Khabath* (physical filth — blood, urine, feces, mani — removed by washing, wiping, or rubbing).
- **Purification of surfaces:** A hard, non-absorbent surface (glass, mirror) is purified by wiping with a wet cloth. A leather sock with dried najasa is purified by rubbing firmly on the ground until no residue remains (per Abu Hanifa and Abu Yusuf). Ground soiled with najasa becomes pure — and valid for salah and tayammum — if the earth fully absorbs it and the sun completely dries it.
- **Mani:** Najis in the Hanafi school. If wet, it must be washed. If dry, rubbing/scratching off all residue is sufficient; washing is not required.

### Easy to Mix Up

- **Masjid vs. musalla:** A masjid is permanently waqf land designated for prayer — a ha'idha cannot enter its prayer hall. A musalla is a rented or multi-purpose space used for prayer — a ha'idha may enter it. The physical appearance (dome, minaret) is irrelevant; the legal designation is what counts.
- **Nifas adat vs. no adat when bleeding exceeds 40 days:** If the woman has a consistent previous habit (e.g., always 35 days), that number applies as her nifas; the rest is istihada. If there is no consistent habit (e.g., 35, 39, 43 days across births — no stable pattern), the default maximum of 40 days applies as nifas.
