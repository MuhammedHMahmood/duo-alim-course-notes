## Answer Key

### Nahw 201

1. A word is *ghayr munsarif* when either (a) **two *asbāb*** are simultaneously present, or (b) **one *sabab*** is present that is *qā'im maqām as-sababayn* — so weighty it stands in place of two reasons on its own.

2. Alif Maqsūra (ى) and Alif Mamdūda (اء) each act as *qā'im maqām as-sababayn*. Tā' Marbūtah (ة) does not because it counts as only one *sabab* — it requires a second *sabab* alongside it to produce *ghayr munsarif* status.

3. Every *alam* is *ma'rifah*, but not every *ma'rifah* is *alam*. An *alam* is a **given, specific name** with no ambiguity (e.g., زَيْدٌ — one named individual). A *ma'rifah* may still refer to an unspecified individual (e.g., الرَّجُلُ — "the man," but which man?). شَيْطَان is a common noun applicable to any evil jinn (like وَلَد for any boy) — it is *ma'rifah* with ال but not *alam* — so it has no *sabab* of *alamiyyah* and is *munsarif*. إِبْلِيس is a given proper name for a specific entity (alamiyyah) *and* is non-Arabic in origin (ʿujmah) — two *asbāb* → *ghayr munsarif*.

4. It is treated as **munsarif**. The principle: a sulāsī (three-letter) non-Arabic proper name with a *sākin* middle letter is too "light" — its brevity offsets the *ʿujmah* condition, so it does not achieve full *ghayr munsarif* status. This is why نُوح and لُوط are *munsarif* despite being non-Arabic Prophets' names.

5. The two patterns are **مَفَاعِل** (e.g., مَسَاجِد) and **مَفَاعِيل** (e.g., مَصَابِيح). For letters after the alif of the plural: (a) **two letters** — valid as-is with no additional condition; (b) **three letters** — the middle one must be *sākin*; (c) **one letter** — it must be *mushaddad* (doubled), as in دَوَابّ.

6. The two conditions beyond being an *alam* are: (1) **no *iḍāfah*** (possessive construction) between the two parts, and (2) **no *isnād*** (predicate-subject relation) between them. Example satisfying both: بَعْلَبَكَّ (بَعْل + بَكَّ — two names joined, no grammatical relationship → *ghayr munsarif*). Example failing due to *iḍāfah*: عَبْدُ اللَّهِ — عَبْد is *muḍāf* and اللَّه is *muḍāf ilayhi*, so the *iḍāfah* relationship is present → *munsarif*.

**Vocabulary:**

**غَيْرُ مُنْصَرِف** — A diptote; it does not accept *tanwīn*, and takes *fatḥah* instead of *kasrah* in the *majrūr* state.

**قَائِمٌ مَقَامَ السَّبَبَيْن** — The three *asbāb* that carry this weight: Alif Maqsūra, Alif Mamdūda, and Jama' Muntaha al-Jumūm.

**عَلَمِيَّة** — Every *alam* is *ma'rifah*, but not every *ma'rifah* is *alam*. The relationship is one-directional: *alam* → *ma'rifah*, not the reverse.

**عُجْمَة** — *ʿUjmah* alone provides only one *sabab*. It must be paired with **alamiyyah** (the word must also be a proper noun in its original language) to produce *ghayr munsarif* status.

**جَمْعُ مُنْتَهَى الْجُمُوع** — It is "terminal" because **no further plural can be formed from it** — it is the final stage of pluralization beyond which the word cannot be pluralized again.

**أَلِف مَقْصُورَة** — Written as ى at the end of a word; example: حُبْلَى.

**أَلِف مَمْدُودَة** — An alif followed by hamzah at the end; example: حَمْرَاء.

**تَرْكِيب** — The two disqualifying relationships are **iḍāfah** (possessive/genitive construction) and **isnād** (predicate-subject relation).

**إِضَافَة** — عَبْدُ اللَّهِ has an *iḍāfah* relationship (عَبْد is *muḍāf*, اللَّه is *muḍāf ilayhi*), which disqualifies it from the *tarkīb sabab* → *munsarif*.

**مُشَدَّد** — It becomes legally significant in *jama' muntaha al-jumūm* when only **one letter** follows the alif of the plural — that single letter must be *mushaddad* for the pattern to be valid (e.g., دَوَابّ).

### Tafseer 201

1. The Fasiqeen are those who have stepped outside Allah's obedience (*al-khārijīn ʿan ṭāʿat Allah*) and those who deny and reject Allah's signs (*al-jāḥidīn bi-āyātihi*).

2. (a) The covenants taken from Ahl al-Kitāb included belief in Prophet Muhammad ﷺ (explicitly commanded in the Torah and Injeel), as well as belief in One Allah, all messengers, and adherence to the Sharīʿah. (b) The Mushrikeen never received a divine book containing such formal covenants — they had no *mīthāq* of this kind to break.

3. Three examples: (1) **Severing ties of kinship** (*qat' al-arḥām*) — general, applies to all; (2) **Rejecting some prophets while accepting others** (e.g., accepting Mūsā but rejecting Muḥammad ﷺ) — specific to Ahl al-Kitāb; (3) **Abandoning bonds of friendship and loyalty with believers** (*tark muwālāt al-muʾminīn*) — general.

4. The acts of ifsād include: committing sins and transgressions, spreading *fitnah* (temptations), preventing people from accepting Īmān and Islam, raising doubts about the Qurʾān, and spreading *kufr and shirk*. The worst form is spreading **kufr and shirk**. The supporting phrase: **"الْفِتْنَةُ أَشَدُّ مِنَ الْقَتْلِ"** (Fitnah is worse than killing), from Surah Al-Baqarah itself.

5. *Kayfa* here is an **interrogative of rebuke and denial (*inkār*)** — it scolds and rejects the disbelievers' stance; it is **not** a genuine request for information. The five stages: (1) Death/non-existence (أَمْوَاتًا — in your fathers' loins, in your mothers' wombs); (2) Life in this world (فَأَحْيَاكُمْ); (3) Death at the appointed time (ثُمَّ يُمِيتُكُمْ); (4) Resurrection from the graves (ثُمَّ يُحْيِيكُمْ); (5) Return to Allah for reckoning (ثُمَّ إِلَيْهِ تُرْجَعُونَ).

6. The argument: Allah created the entire universe — the earth, seven heavens, all planets and galaxies. This is an act of incomparably greater magnitude than resurrecting a single human being. If Allah accomplished the greater, the lesser (resurrection) is even more easily within His power. Furthermore, the framework of creation already exists; resurrection is simply re-assembling what was already made once.

**Vocabulary:**

**الْفَاسِقِينَ** — They are going outside the **obedience of Allah** (*ṭāʿat Allāh*).

**نَقْضُ الْعَهْدِ** — An *ʿahd* is a general covenant or agreement; a *mīthāq* is an **affirmed, witnessed, and binding** covenant backed by proofs and evidences. The ayah uses *mīthāq* to stress that the covenant was not a vague promise but a solemn, well-established agreement — making its violation all the more grave.

**قَطْعُ الأَرْحَام** — Allah commands that family ties (*arḥām*) be **maintained and upheld**. This command appears in multiple places in the Qurʾān, and severing these ties is listed here as a defining characteristic of the Fasiqeen.

**إِفْسَادٌ فِي الْأَرْضِ** — In this passage, *fitnah* specifically means the **spreading of kufr and shirk** — not temptation generally, but active propagation of disbelief and polytheism.

**الْفِتْنَةُ أَشَدُّ مِنَ الْقَتْلِ** — From **Surah Al-Baqarah**. All *mufassirūn* agree that *fitnah* here means the spreading of **kufr and shirk**.

**بَعْث** — Stage four in Ayah 28: **resurrection** — Allah reviving the dead from their graves (*baʿth min al-qubūr*).

**اسْتَوَى** — Used in Ayah 29 to mean Allah **rose/directed His will toward** the heavens. It must be understood **as it befits Allah's majesty** — without likening it to the rising of a created being.

**أَمَاتَ / أَحْيَا** — Both are Form IV (bāb ifʿāl). Form IV conveys **causativity**: *amāta* = "to cause to die"; *aḥyā* = "to cause to live / to give life."

**دَلِيلُ الْقُدْرَةِ الْبَاهِرَةِ** — The *dalīl* is **the creation of the earth and seven heavens** (Ayah 29). It proves **Allah's overwhelming, absolute power** — and by extension, that resurrection is entirely within His ability.

**بَلَاغَة** — The teacher declined to cover it because the students have not yet studied a *balāghah* textbook; introducing it now would consume too much time and divert from the required tafseer curriculum.

### Fiqh 201

1. The principle is ***ḥaraj*** — recognized hardship that Sharīʿah relieves through concession. Ṣalāh: if a woman's habit is 10 days, she misses 50 ṣalawāt per month, or approximately **600 per year** — a cumulative burden that constitutes *ḥaraj*, so qaḍāʾ is waived. Fasting: she misses at most **10 days** per month; spread across the remaining 11 months, even one makeup day per month closes the gap — the burden does not reach *ḥaraj* level, so qaḍāʾ is required.

2. A **masjid** is land permanently designated as *waqf* for the purpose of a masjid — irreversible, single-purpose, full ḥukm applies (ḥāʾiḍah may not enter the prayer hall). A **muṣallā** is a rented or temporary space used for prayer, not *waqf*, can serve multiple purposes — ḥāʾiḍah may enter. The determining criterion is **intention and formal legal designation** (*waqf*), not architectural features. A dome and minaret are historical symbols, not juridical markers — the Tāj Maḥal has both yet is not a masjid; the Prophet's first masjid had neither minaret nor dome.

3. *Istiḥāḍah* is vaginal bleeding that is **less than 3 days** or **more than 10 days** in duration. It carries the same *ḥukm* as a nosebleed (*ḥukm al-ruʿāf*). It does **not** prevent: (1) ṣalāh, (2) fasting, (3) conjugal relations (*waṭʾ*).

4. Days 1–7: **ḥayḍ** (within her established habit). Days 8–10: still counted as **ḥayḍ** (within the 10-day maximum). On **day 10**: she performs **ghusl** (the ḥayḍ ends at the 10-day cap). Days 11 onward: classified as **istiḥāḍah** — she makes fresh **wuḍūʾ** for each farḍ ṣalāh time and prays normally throughout.

5. **Ṭalāq sunnī**: one ṭalāq is pronounced in each separate period of *ṭuhr* (purity), and no ṭalāq is ever given during ḥayḍ. **Ṭalāq bidʿī**: pronouncing three ṭalāqs simultaneously, or giving ṭalāq while the wife is in ḥayḍ — both are impermissible. *ʿAqīdah* consequence: if a husband **or** wife considers marital relations during ḥayḍ to be *ḥalāl* (denying the prohibition), this constitutes **kufr**, because it is an explicit Qurʾānic ruling. If they know it is *ḥarām* but commit it anyway, that is a grave sin requiring *tawbah* and *istighfār* — but not kufr.

6. **Imām Abū Ḥanīfah** (and Abū Yūsuf): wuḍūʾ remains valid until the **next ṣalāh time begins** (e.g., wuḍūʾ for Ẓuhr lasts until the Aṣr adhān). **Imām Muḥammad**: wuḍūʾ is valid until the **current ṣalāh time exits** (e.g., valid only within Ẓuhr time). **Imām al-Shāfiʿī**: wuḍūʾ is valid only **for that single farḍ ṣalāh**; nāfil after it requires a new wuḍūʾ. Practical difference for nāfil ṣalāh: under the Ḥanafī position (Abū Ḥanīfah/Abū Yūsuf), the *maʿdhūr* may pray nāfil, recite Qurʾān, and perform any worship freely until the next ṣalāh time begins — all with the same wuḍūʾ. Under the Shāfiʿī position, each act after the farḍ would require a fresh wuḍūʾ.

**Vocabulary:**

**istiḥāḍah** — It shares the *ḥukm* of a **nosebleed** (*ḥukm al-ruʿāf*): the bleeding is treated as a minor impurity that does not prevent ṣalāh, fasting, or conjugal relations — worship continues normally.

**maʿdhūr** — Three examples: (1) a woman with continuous istiḥāḍah (irregular bleeding beyond 10 days); (2) a person with chronic urinary incontinence; (3) a person with a continuously bleeding wound.

**mubtadaʾah** — Days 1–10 = ḥayḍ; days 11–12 = istiḥāḍah. Her *ʿādah* becomes **10 days** (the maximum, since that is what her first cycle produced within the legal limit).

**muʿtādah** — All 9 days are classified as **ḥayḍ** (it is within the 10-day maximum). Her *ʿādah* **changes** — 9 days becomes her updated established habit.

**mustaribah** — She should track her cycles carefully, look for seasonal patterns, and consult family history. A general ruling cannot be applied directly because her situation is a "moving target" — she needs **individualized guidance from a qualified scholar (*muftī/ʿālim*)** who can issue a *fatwā* specific to her condition.

**ṭuhr** — Minimum: **15 days**. If spotting occurs before the 15-day minimum has elapsed, it is classified as **istiḥāḍah**, not a new ḥayḍ.

**ḥaraj** — Two other Sharīʿah examples: (1) the **sick person** is permitted to pray seated or lying down (removing the hardship of standing); (2) the **traveler** is permitted to shorten (*qaṣr*) the four-rakʿah prayers to two.

**ṭalāq sunnī** — The husband pronounces **one ṭalāq** during each separate **period of ṭuhr** (purity, not during ḥayḍ) — never more than one at a time, never during menstruation.

**ṭawāf al-ziyārah** — It is a **rukn** of Ḥajj — its omission invalidates the Ḥajj entirely; it cannot be compensated by *dam* alone and must ultimately be performed. *Ṭawāf al-qudūm* is **not** a rukn — missing it does not invalidate Ḥajj and carries no comparable consequence.

**rukn** — Omitting a *rukn* **invalidates the entire act of worship** — it cannot be compensated by *dam* or any substitute. Omitting a *wājib* (obligatory but not a pillar) makes the act deficient and requires *dam* as compensation, but the act itself remains valid.
