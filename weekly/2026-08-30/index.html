    Weekly Review — Week of Aug 24 – Aug 30, 2026

    Self-test quiz covering everything taught this week across all active classes. Answer each question yourself before checking the Answer Key at the end — don't peek early.

    Nahw 201

    Concept Questions

    1. What case ending does Jama' Mu'annath Salim take in the Mansub position, and why is it considered unusual compared to most nouns?

    2. You see two nouns, both ending in Ya: الْفَائِزَيْن and الْمُسْلِمِين. Without counting the letters, how do you determine which is Tasniyah and which is Jama' Mudhakkar Salim?

    3. A student writes: سَأَلُوا الطُّلَّابُ أُسْتَاذَهُمُ. The teacher marks it wrong. Explain exactly why, and provide the correct form.

    4. Decline قَاضٍ in all three cases (Marfu', Mansub, Majrur) with and without ال, and state in which case(s) the I'rab is Taqdiri versus visible.

    5. A word appears identical in Rafa', Nasb, and Jarr positions. A student claims it must be Mabni. Explain why they could be wrong, and describe what additional information you would need to decide.

    6. How does the Nahw definition of Sahih differ from the Sarf definition? Give one example of a word that is Sahih by one definition but not the other.

    Tafseer 201

    Concept Questions

    1. The phrase "Inna humul mufsidoon" (Ayah 12) uses three grammatical devices stacked together to restrict and emphasize. Identify each device and explain what the combination achieves rhetorically.

    2. In Ayah 13, the munafiqeen say: أَنُؤْمِنُ كَمَا آمَنَ السُّفَهَاءُ. What rhetorical function does the hamzah serve here — is it a genuine question? Who were the "sufaha'" they were referring to, and what was their motive in using that word?

    3. Allah says "Allahu yastahzi'u bihim" using the same root as the munafiqeen's own claim of istihza'. How do the classical commentators (Ibn Abbas and Ibn Katheer) explain what Allah's "istihza'" actually means? What Quranic principle of using the same word for an act and its just consequence does this illustrate?

    4. In the second parable (Ayat 19–20), a traveler stops when it is dark and walks when lightning flashes. To what specific aspect of the munafiqeen's behavior does this apply? Quote or paraphrase the ayah elsewhere in the Qur'an that uses the word مُذَبْذَبِين to describe the same characteristic.

    5. What is iltifat, and why do commentators say Allah employs it when He shifts from third-person description of the three groups to the direct address "Ya Ayyuhan-Nas" in Ayah 21?

    6. The parable of the fire (Ayah 17) is said by Ibn Katheer to indicate something specific about the munafiqeen's prior history with iman. What does the parable imply happened to them — and why does that make their darkness worse than if they had never had light at all?

    Answer Key

    Nahw 201

    1. Jama' Mu'annath Salim takes Kasrah in the Mansub position instead of the expected Fathah. This is unusual because Fathah is the standard Nasb marker for most nouns; Kasrah here acts as a substitute (na'ib) for Fathah, making JMS one of the nouns that uses Kasrah for two distinct cases (Jarr and Nasb).

    2. Look at the vowel immediately before the Ya: if the vowel before ي is Fathah (ـَيْن), the word is Tasniyah (dual). If the vowel before ي is Kasrah (ـِيْن), the word is Jama' Mudhakkar Salim (plural). الْفَائِزَيْن = dual (Fathah before Ya); الْمُسْلِمِين = plural (Kasrah before Ya).

    3. The error is that سَأَلُوا is a plural Fi'il, but the Fa'il (الطُّلَّابُ) comes after the Fi'il (it is Mu'akhkhar). When the Fa'il is Mu'akhkhar, the Fi'il must be Wahid (singular), not plural — only gender agreement is retained. The correct form is: سَأَلَ الطُّلَّابُ أُسْتَاذَهُمُ (Wahid Muzakkar Fi'il, because Fa'il is Muzakkar).

    4. Without ال (Tanween/Indefinite):

    5. Marfu': قَاضٍ — Taqdiri Dammah (hidden)
    6. Mansub: قَاضِيًا — visible Fathah on Ya
    7. Majrur: قَاضٍ — Taqdiri Kasrah (hidden) With ال (Definite):
    8. Marfu': الْقَاضِي — Taqdiri Dammah
    9. Mansub: الْقَاضِيَ — visible Fathah

    10. Majrur: الْقَاضِي — Taqdiri Kasrah I'rab is Taqdiri in Rafa' and Jarr; visible (Zaahir) only in Nasb.

    11. The student could be wrong because Taqdeeri I'rab also causes the word to appear unchanged, yet the word is still Ma'rab (declinable) — it receives I'rab that is simply hidden. Mabni means the word receives no I'rab at all and belongs to a fixed grammatical category (pronouns, demonstratives, relative nouns, etc.). To determine which it is: check whether the word belongs to a known Mabni category. If not, it is likely Ma'rab with Taqdeeri I'rab (e.g., مُوسَى is Ma'rab, not Mabni).

    12. Nahw definition of Sahih: the word's final letter (Lam al-Kalimah) is not a Harf 'Illah (ا، و، ي). Sarf definition: none of the letters throughout the word is a Harf 'Illah. Example: زَيْد — Sahih in Nahw (last letter is Dal, not a Harf 'Illah), but could be examined in Sarf for the Ya in the middle. عَلِي — Mu'tall in both (ends in Ya, a Harf 'Illah).

    Vocabulary:

    مُقَدَّم — placed before the Fi'il (fronted Fa'il); when Muqaddam, the Fi'il must agree in gender but stays Wahid (singular) regardless of the Fa'il's number.

    اسم مَنْقُوص — a noun ending in Ya preceded by Kasrah; shows visible I'rab only in the Mansub case (Fathah appears on the Ya).

    تَقْدِيرِي — it does not mean no I'rab; it means I'rab is present but hidden/unable to appear on the word's surface. The word is still Ma'rab.

    مَبْنِي — examples include: Zamaa'ir (pronouns) such as هُوَ، هِيَ; Asmaa' ul-Ishaara (demonstratives) such as هَذَا، هَؤُلَاءِ; Asmaa' ul-Mawsoola (relative nouns) such as الَّذِي، الَّتِي.

    يَاء الْمُتَكَلِّم — when a noun is Mudaaf toward the Yaa Mutakallim, its I'rab becomes Taqdeeri in all three cases; the word appears unchanged regardless of its grammatical position.

    نُون التَّثْنِيَة — the Noon is dropped (mahjoof) when the Tasniya noun is Mudaaf; وَلَدَانِ becomes وَلَدَا before the Mudaaf Ilayh.

    حَرْف عِلَّة — the three are: Alif (ا), Waw (و), and Ya (ي). Sarf requires that none of them appear anywhere in the word for it to be called Sahih; Nahw only checks the last letter.

    عَامِل — the Amil is the grammatical operator that determines the case of a Ma'rab word. It is irrelevant to Mabni words, which do not accept I'rab regardless of which Amil precedes them.

    Tafseer 201

    1. Three devices: (1) إِنَّ — particle of affirmation and emphasis; (2) هُمْ — a Dameer Fasl (separating pronoun) that creates a Mubtada-Khabar structure; (3) الْمُفْسِدُونَ — the definite article (ال) on the predicate creates Hasr (restriction). Together they achieve complete restriction and maximum emphasis: it is they — and none other — who are the corruptors.

    2. The hamzah in "Anu'minu" is not a genuine question — it is a Hamzat al-Istihza' wal-Inkaar (interrogative of mockery and contempt). The "sufaha'" they referred to were the early Sahabah — specifically Bilal ibn Rabah, Suhaib al-Roomi, and Ammar ibn Yasir (RA), who were poor, former slaves, or of low social standing. Their motive was tahqir shanhum — to belittle and publicly degrade the social standing of the Muslims.

    3. Ibn Abbas (RA): Allah's "yastahzi'u" means He takes revenge (nikma) by giving the munafiqeen respite (imla') before a severe punishment. Ibn Katheer (رحمه الله): Allah will retribute them with the punishment of deception (uqubat al-khida') because they deceived the believers. The Quranic principle: the same word is used for the evil act and its just recompense — the first is Zulm (injustice), the second is Adl (justice). Another example: جَزَاءُ سَيِّئَةٍ سَيِّئَةٌ مِثْلُهَا — the two uses of سَيِّئَة have entirely different moral statuses.

    4. The traveler in the lightning storm walks when light flashes and freezes in darkness — this mirrors how the munafiqeen would side with the Muslims when Islam appeared strong, and incline toward the kuffar when they appeared stronger. The ayah elsewhere: مُذَبْذَبِينَ بَيْنَ ذَٰلِكَ لَا إِلَىٰ هَٰؤُلَاءِ وَلَا إِلَىٰ هَٰؤُلَاءِ — "Wavering between this and that, belonging neither to these nor to those."

    5. Iltifat is a rhetorical shift from third-person description to direct second-person address, literally "turning toward." Its effect: it activates and startles the listener, drawing their complete attention, and signals that what follows is of the utmost importance. Allah uses it because most people are heedless (ghafiloon) of the most critical truths; the direct address forces engagement that third-person description does not.

    6. Ibn Katheer says the parable indicates annahum amanu thumma kafaru — they believed, then they disbelieved. The fire they lit represents their initial iman; the light and warmth represent the benefits and security of living among Muslims. Allah extinguishing the light represents their return to kufr. This makes their darkness worse than that of someone who never had light: misguidance chosen after insight becomes absolute blindness, far harder to escape.

    Vocabulary:

    صَيِّب — a very heavy, torrential rain shower; not a gentle rain.

    جُمْلَة مُعْتَرِضَة — a parenthetical sentence inserted between two parts of a continuous statement to add a warning or emphasis without breaking the grammatical flow. "Wallahu muhitun bil-kafirin" is inserted mid-parable to warn: the disbelievers cannot escape Allah — He surrounds them entirely, as an enemy surrounds from all sides.

    إِمْلَاء / يُمْلِي — to give respite; to delay punishment. Being given imla' is not divine approval — it is itself a form of punishment, extending the transgressor's rope before a final, severe reckoning.

    اسْتَبْدَلَ — "They purchased misguidance at the price of guidance" (اشْتَرَوُا الضَّلَالَةَ بِالْهُدَى); they gave up guidance (huda) and received misguidance (dalalah) in return — a transaction that earned no profit in either world.

    مُذَبْذَبِين — the wavering ones; those who sway between two positions with no firm stance. Describes the munafiqeen, who belonged fully to neither the believers nor the disbelievers.

    إِلْتِفَات — a rhetorical "turning toward" the audience with direct address. It signals a command or reminder of the highest importance, used whenever Allah addresses people who tend to be heedless of a critical matter.

    يَعْمَهُونَ — wandering blindly in confusion; floundering without direction. It conveys a lostness more severe than simply not knowing the path — the person has no capacity even to orient themselves.

    مُنَاسَبَة — the logical/rhetorical connection between passages. Having presented the three groups and given parables, Allah transitions to proofs of tawheed (Ayat 21–25): the logical connection is that after exposing the munafiqeen's false reasoning, Allah now lays before all humanity the clear evidences and blessings that make worship of Him the rational and grateful response.

    Fiqh 201

    1. The two permanent exceptions to tanning are: (1) Pig skin (khinzir) — swine is najis in its very essence (najasah 'ayniyyah); no process can purify it. Dalil: Surah al-An'am 6:145. (2) Human skin (adami) — not because it is najis, but out of respect for human dignity (karamat/azmat al-adam); using it would be a violation of that dignity.

    2. The eight parts: (1) hair (sha'r), (2) bone (azm), (3) hoof (hafir), (4) horn (qarn), (5) wool (suf), (6) nail/claw (zufr), (7) feather (rish), (8) beak (minqar). Hanafi principle: any part of an animal that contains no life — no blood, no sensation — remains pure even after the animal's death. Imam al-Shafi'i's position: all eight parts are najis and none may be used from a dead animal.

    3. (a) Small bird — remove 20 buckets (sufficient); 30 is mustahab. (b) Cat — cat falls in the pigeon/chicken category: remove 40 buckets (sufficient); 50 is mustahab. (c) Dog — the entire well must be drained. Additional condition triggering full drainage regardless of animal size: if the dead body has swollen and begun to crack (tafassukh/intifakh), the entire well must be emptied no matter how small the animal was.

    4. The mu'tamad position is that of Imam Muhammad (al-Shaybani): remove 200 to 300 buckets of average local size. This is agreed upon as the operative Hanafi fatwa and is practical without requiring experts or physical entry into the well.

    5. Donkey and mule su'r is mashkuk (doubtful). The practical ruling: perform both wudu and tayammum (in any order), then proceed with salah. The rationale: since the ruling is uncertain, combining both forms of purification covers all possibilities; the salah is valid.

    6. Two arkan: (1) Striking the hands on the earth; (2) Wiping the face and both hands up to and including the elbows. Four sunnahs (any four of eight): saying Bismillah before starting; striking with the soft inner portion of the palms; keeping fingers spread and palms flat on the surface; shaking off excess dust; observing tarteeb (face first, then right hand, then left hand); continuity between wiping face and hands; moving hands slightly forward and then back after striking. What breaks tayammum but not wudu: sighting water that one is able to use — tayammum is immediately invalidated upon seeing accessible water.

    Vocabulary:

    الدِّبَاغَة الحُكُمِيَّة — constructive/constructive tanning: the hide is purified by natural drying in open air, sun, and soil with no substance applied. Yes, it is sufficient for purification under Hanafi fiqh even without a chemical or physical agent.

    سُؤْر — leftover water or remnant liquid remaining after an animal or person has drunk from a vessel. The Ahnaf divide it into five ruling categories: tahir (pure), najis (impure), makruh (disliked), mashkuk (doubtful), and the muttafaq 'alayh category of humans and halal animals (pure, agreed by all four madhabs).

    مَشْكُوك — doubtful. Donkey su'r is mashkuk because Sahabah disagreed about the permissibility of donkey meat (and thus its su'r), creating genuine evidential uncertainty. Practical ruling: combine wudu and tayammum.

    صَعِيد — clean earth or ground used for tayammum. The Hanafi position (Imam Abu Hanifa and Imam Muhammad — the muftaa bihi) extends it to all earth-genus substances: soil, sand, stone, limestone, antimony, arsenic minerals — anything from the earth's genus. Imam Abu Yusuf restricts it to loose soil or sand only.

    الرُّخْصَة — a Shari'ah concession. Example from this week: small quantities of dried animal droppings blown by wind into a well — if they do not visibly alter the water, the Hanafi school grants a rukhsa permitting use of the water. Usul principle: al-mashaqqah tajlib al-taysir (hardship brings ease).

    التَّفَسُّخ — decomposition; the stage when the dead body begins to crack and break open. If an animal in a well reaches this stage, the entire well must be emptied regardless of the animal's size.

    تَرْتِيب — the required sequence in tayammum: wipe the face first, then the right hand, then the left hand. Tarteeb is listed among the eight sunnah acts of tayammum.

    الْمَعِين — a well with a constantly flowing, self-replenishing underground water source. The fiqhi problem: draining it entirely is impractical since water continuously returns; hence the separate set of six scholarly opinions on how to determine sufficient purification, with 200–300 buckets as the mu'tamad.
