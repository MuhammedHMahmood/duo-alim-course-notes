    Weekly Review — Week of Aug 16 - Aug 22, 2026

    Self-test quiz covering everything taught this week across all active classes. Answer each question yourself before checking the Answer Key at the end — don't peek early.

    Nahw 201

    Concept Questions

    1. What is the difference between كَلَامٌ تَامٌّ and كَلَامٌ نَاقِصٌ? What is the alāma used to identify كَلَامٌ تَامٌّ?

    2. Of the nine categories of noun i'rāb, what are the three conditions a noun must meet to fall under Category 1 (Raf' with ḍamma, Naṣb with fatḥa, Jarr with kasra)?

    3. How does the naḥw definition of ṣaḥīḥ differ from the ṣarf definition? Give an example of a word that would be ṣaḥīḥ in naḥw but ma'tall in ṣarf.

    4. In Category 2 (Jam' Mu'annas Sālim), kasra substitutes for fatḥa in the naṣb case. Using إِنَّ الْمُسْلِمَاتِ as your example, write out the full i'rāb of الْمُسْلِمَاتِ and explain why the kasra is not a kasra of jarr.

    5. What are the four conditions required for a noun to take the wāw/alif/yā' i'rāb of Asmā' al-Sitta (Category 4)? What happens when the noun is muḍāf to Yā' al-Mutakallim?

    6. Decline عُمَرُ through all three i'rāb cases and explain what substitution occurs in the jarr case and why.

    Tafseer 201

    Concept Questions

    1. What do the mufassireen mean by خَتَمَ in Ayah 7? Name the two Arabic terms they use to explain it. Is this sealing a physical affliction? What causes it according to the Quran?

    2. Three faculties are mentioned as sealed in Ayah 7. Name all three, and explain what each is sealed from in terms of its spiritual function.

    3. What is the structural logic (al-munasabah) of Surah Al-Baqarah's opening twenty ayahs? Which group receives the most verses, and what does that signal about Allah's purpose?

    4. According to Ibn Abbas, who were the Munafiqeen about whom Ayahs 8–20 were revealed? Name at least two specific individuals and describe their outward tactic toward Muslims.

    5. Ayah 8 states وَمَا هُم بِمُؤْمِنِينَ. According to the tafseer, what is the essential distinction between what the Munafiqeen say and what they actually are? Use the terms اعْتِقَاد and تَصْدِيق in your answer.

    6. How does Imam al-Baydawi describe the Munafiqeen relative to other disbelievers? What two qualities make them أَخْبَثُ الْكَفَرَة and أَبْغَضُهُمْ إِلَى اللَّه?

    Answer Key

    Nahw 201

    1. كَلَامٌ نَاقِصٌ is incomplete speech that does not convey a full meaning on its own (e.g., زَيْدٌ الْبَاعِدُ). كَلَامٌ تَامٌّ is complete speech on which it is valid to stop — the listener has no remaining questions (e.g., زَيْدٌ فِي الْبَيْتِ). Its alāma: يَصِحُّ السُّكُوتُ عَلَيْهَا — "it is permissible to pause/stop on it."

    2. The noun must be: (1) مُفْرَد (singular), (2) مُنْصَرِف (accepts tanwīn and kasra), and (3) صَحِيح (its final letter is not a ḥarf 'illah — ا، و، ي). Also included under Category 1 are nouns جَارٍ مَجْرَى الصَّحِيحِ and broken plurals that are munsarif.

    3. In naḥw, a noun is ṣaḥīḥ if its final letter is not a ḥarf 'illah — the rest of the word is irrelevant. In ṣarf, a noun is ma'tall if a ḥarf 'illah appears anywhere in the word (beginning, middle, or end). Example: دَلْوٌ — it ends in wāw but the letter before the wāw carries sukūn, making it جَارٍ مَجْرَى الصَّحِيحِ in naḥw, yet it contains a ḥarf 'illah and would be ma'tall in ṣarf.

    4. الْمُسْلِمَاتِ is the ism of إِنَّ, and إِنَّ normally causes naṣb. Its i'rāb: مَنْصُوبٌ، وَعَلَامَةُ نَصْبِهِ الْكَسْرَةُ نِيَابَةً عَنِ الْفَتْحَةِ لِأَنَّهُ جَمْعُ مُؤَنَّثٍ سَالِمٌ. The kasra is not a kasra of jarr — it stands in for the expected fatḥa of naṣb because jam' mu'annas sālim does not accept fatḥa.

    5. The four conditions: (1) the noun must be one of the six nouns (أَسْمَاءُ السِّتَّة), (2) مُكَبَّرَة (base form, not diminutive), (3) مُوَحَّدَة (singular, not dual or plural), (4) muḍāf to other than Yā' al-Mutakallim. When muḍāf to Yā' al-Mutakallim, all three i'rāb cases look identical (e.g., أَخِي in raf', naṣb, and jarr), so i'rāb cannot be distinguished and a different rule governs.

    6. Raf': جَاءَنِي عُمَرُ (ḍamma, no tanwīn). Naṣb: رَأَيْتُ عُمَرَ (fatḥa). Jarr: مَرَرْتُ بِعُمَرَ (fatḥa, not kasra). In the jarr case, fatḥa substitutes for kasra because عُمَرُ is ghayr munsarif — it rejects both tanwīn and kasra.

    Vocabulary:

    يَصِحُّ السُّكُوتُ عَلَيْهَا — The alāma of كَلَامٌ تَامٌّ; it means "it is permissible to stop/pause on it," indicating that the speech conveys complete meaning.

    جَارٍ مَجْرَى الصَّحِيحِ — "Functioning like a ṣaḥīḥ noun" — a purely morphological classification, unrelated to jār-majrūr. It applies to a noun whose final letter is wāw or yā' and the letter immediately before it carries sukūn (e.g., دَلْوٌ, ظَبْيٌ).

    غَيْرُ مُنْصَرِفٌ — A noun that rejects tanwīn and kasra. In the jarr case, fatḥa substitutes for kasra (فَتْحَة نِيَابَةً عَنِ الْكَسْرَة).

    أَسْبَابُ مَنْعِ الصَّرْفِ — There are nine causes; any two of them present in a single noun make it ghayr munsarif.

    أَسْمَاءُ السِّتَّة — أَبٌ، أَخٌ، حَمٌ، فَمٌ، هَنٌ، ذُو.

    مُكَبَّرٌ — The base/regular form of a noun, as opposed to مُصَغَّر (diminutive). The distinction matters because Category 4 i'rāb (wāw/alif/yā') only applies when the noun is in its مُكَبَّر form; the diminutive follows different i'rāb.

    يَاءُ الْمُتَكَلِّمِ — All three cases: جَاءَ أَخِي / رَأَيْتُ أَخِي / مَرَرْتُ بِأَخِي. All forms are identical, illustrating that when muḍāf to يَاءُ الْمُتَكَلِّمِ, the wāw/alif/yā' i'rāb of Category 4 does not apply.

    إِعْرَابٌ فَرْعِيٌّ — Secondary i'rāb uses ḥurūf (letters: wāw, alif, yā') instead of ḥarakāt, or substitutes one ḥaraka for another. Asmā' al-Sitta (Category 4) is one example from this week's notes.

    جَمْعُ مُكَسَّرٌ — Munsarif example: رِجَالٌ (Category 1). Ghayr munsarif example: مَدَارِسُ (Category 3).

    مُضَافٌ إِلَيْهِ — In أَخُو حَامِدٍ: أَخُو is the muḍāf (fā'il, marfū', wāw niyābatan 'an al-ḍamma, asmā' al-sitta); حَامِدٍ is the muḍāf ilayhi (majrūr, kasra ẓāhira).

    Tafseer 201

    1. خَتَمَ means التَّغْطِيَة (covering) and الطَّبْع (sealing/imprinting). It is not a physical affliction — these people are physically healthy and fully functional. The cause is their كُفْر (disbelief): accumulated sins obscure the نُور الْبَصِيرَة in the heart, and Allah seals it in response to their willful, sustained rejection.

    2. The three sealed faculties: (1) الْقُلُوب (hearts) — sealed from following the truth; (2) السَّمْع (hearing) — sealed from truly perceiving دَاعِي الْفَلَاح, the call to guidance; (3) الْأَبْصَار (eyesight) — sealed from perceiving نُورُ الْهِدَايَة, the light of guidance.

    3. Ayahs 1–5: qualities of the Mu'mineen. Ayahs 6–7: qualities of the Kafireen. Ayahs 8–20: qualities of the Munafiqeen. The Munafiqeen receive the most verses (13), signaling the great danger (خَطَر عَظِيم) and great harm (ضَرَر كَبِير) they pose to the Muslim community from within.

    4. According to Ibn Abbas, the verses were revealed about hypocrites from Ahl al-Kitab. Named individuals: Abdullah ibn Ubayy ibn Salul (the foremost leader of the Munafiqeen in Madinah) and Mu'tab ibn Qushayr. Their tactic: when meeting Muslims they openly declared belief in the Prophet ﷺ, in Allah, and in the Quran — even claiming to find the Prophet's description in their own scriptures — while concealing kufr inwardly.

    5. The Munafiqeen say with their tongues: آمَنَّا بِاللَّه وَبِالْيَوْمِ الْآخِر. But Allah declares they are not true believers: they utter these words as mere speech (كَلَام) without اعْتِقَاد (inner conviction held in the heart) and without تَصْدِيق (sincere affirmation of the truth). The tongue acts without the heart.

    6. Imam al-Baydawi describes the Munafiqeen as أَخْبَثُ الْكَفَرَة (the most vile of all disbelievers) and أَبْغَضُهُمْ إِلَى اللَّه (the most hateful to Allah). The two qualities that make them so: (1) خِدَاع — deception (mixing outward Iman with inward kufr), and (2) مَكْر — scheming and mockery of the believers, making their disbelief more harmful and reprehensible than open kufr.

    Vocabulary:

    الطَّبْع — Sealing/stamping; used alongside التَّغْطِيَة (covering) by the mufassireen to explain the meaning of خَتَمَ on the hearts.

    نُورُ الْبَصِيرَة — When sins accumulate on the heart, this light of inner insight and Iman becomes obscured and darkened, leaving no مَسْلَك for guidance to enter.

    مَسْلَك — Pathway or opening. Its absence means kufr is locked in and Iman is locked out — nothing can enter and nothing can exit, like a sealed vessel.

    النِّفَاق — A مُنَافِق is one who أَظْهَرَ الْإِيمَان (outwardly displays faith) while أَبْطَنَ الْكُفْر (inwardly conceals disbelief).

    الْمُزَبْزَب — Confused and wavering between two states. Imam al-Baydawi uses it to describe the Munafiqeen as the third category, wavering between the Mu'mineen and the Kafireen — neither fully one nor the other.

    خَادَعَ — يُخَادِعُ — Form III verb (mufā'alah), indicating a mutual or attempted action between two parties. In Ayah 9, the Munafiqeen attempt to deceive Allah and those who believe (وَالَّذِينَ آمَنُوا).

    مَآل — From آلَ يَؤُولُ, meaning "to lead to / result in." The مَآل of the Munafiqeen is هَلَاك وَدَمَار — destruction and ruin, which is the inevitable outcome their hypocrisy leads them toward.

    سَبَب النُّزُول — The occasion or reason of revelation. For Ayahs 8–20: reported by Ibn Abbas that these verses were revealed about the hypocrites from Ahl al-Kitab — those who outwardly joined the Muslim community while concealing their kufr, led by Abdullah ibn Ubayy ibn Salul and Mu'tab ibn Qushayr.

    اعْتِقَاد — Inner conviction held in the heart. The Munafiqeen's verbal declarations of faith lack اعْتِقَاد entirely — their words are disconnected from any real inner belief.

    الْمُنَاسَبَة — The contextual connection between ayahs. Here it describes the surah's structural transition: from the qualities of the Mu'mineen (Ayahs 1–5) and Kafireen (Ayahs 6–7) into the qualities of the Munafiqeen (Ayah 8 onward), completing the three-category framework introduced at the surah's opening.
