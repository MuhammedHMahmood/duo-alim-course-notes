    Nahw 201

    Sessions: Sep 14 & Sep 18

    This week in a nutshell: The first session was a practice and review class on Ghayr Munsarif, drilling the asbab through student-identified examples and correcting common errors in majrur position. The second session opened a new chapter — al-Marfu'at — covering the first and most important category: al-Fa'il (the grammatical subject), its definition, types, agreement rules with the verb, and restrictions on swapping word order.

    Key Points

    • Wazan al-Fi'l as a sabab requires the pattern to be exclusive to verbs. If the pattern is shared with nouns, two additional conditions must both hold simultaneously: the word begins with a harf mudara'ah (أ/ي/ت/ن) AND does not accept ta' marbuta. If either condition fails, the wazan does not count as a sabab.
    • Alif-lam (ال) removes Ghayr Munsarif status entirely — any Ghayr Munsarif word to which ال is attached reverts to full declension and takes kasrah normally in majrur position (e.g., بِالْمَدَارِسِ ✓, not بِالْمَدَارِسَ).
    • Colors and adjectives on the pattern أَفْعَل (e.g., أَبْيَض, أَحْمَر) are Ghayr Munsarif by two asbab: Wasf (adjective status) + Wazan Af'al. These are wasf da'im (permanent qualities) and never take tanwin.
    • Jam' Muntahal Jumu' (patterns like مَفَاعِل / مَفَاعِيل) is a single sabab that counts as two — no second cause is needed. Quranic proof: بِمَصَابِيحَ (Surat al-Mulk) takes fatḥah, not kasrah.
    • Alif-Nun Za'idatan in a proper noun (e.g., سَلْمَان — root: سلم) pairs with 'Alamiyyah to produce two asbab. In majrur position the word takes fatḥah, not kasrah: بِسَلْمَانَ ✓ (not بِسَلْمَانِ ✗).
    • Ta'nith lafzi counts as a sabab for Ghayr Munsarif even when the meaning is mudhakkar. A word like طَلْحَة has ta' marbuta (ta'nith lafzi) but refers to a male. The verb follows the meaning (جَاءَ طَلْحَةُ — mudhakkar verb), but for the purpose of Ghayr Munsarif, the form decides.
    • Al-Fa'il is always marfu' — no exceptions. It requires a preceding verb (fi'l) or participial adjective (sifah) attributing the action to it. When the fa'il is an explicit noun (ism zahir) and follows the verb, the verb stays singular regardless of whether the fa'il is dual or plural: ضَرَبَ الزَّيْدَانِ ✓.
    • Swapping fa'il and maf'ul is normally permitted but becomes forbidden when both are ism maqsur (indeclinable, ending in alif maqsura) and neither i'rab nor meaning can resolve which is the subject — e.g., ضَرَبَ مُوسَى عِيسَى cannot be reversed. Exception: if meaning removes the ambiguity (أَكَلَ الْكُمَّثْرَى يَحْيَى — a pear cannot eat a person), swapping is permitted.

    Vocabulary to Know

    Arabic Term Meaning
    غير منصرف (Ghayr Munsarif) Non-declinable noun; does not accept tanwin or kasrah in standard declension
    الأسباب (al-asbab) The causes/reasons for non-declension
    وزن الفعل (wazan al-fi'l) Verbal pattern — when exclusive to verbs, a sabab for Ghayr Munsarif
    جمع منتهى الجموع (Jam' Muntahal Jumu') The "ultimate broken plural" — a single sabab equivalent to two
    ألف نون زائدتان (alif nun za'idatan) Extra alif-nun at end of a proper noun — one of the asbab
    تأنيث لفظي (ta'nith lafzi) Femininity by form (word carries ta' marbuta or alif maqsura)
    تأنيث معنوي (ta'nith ma'nawi) Femininity by meaning (referent is female)
    الفاعل (al-Fa'il) The doer; grammatical subject of a verbal sentence; always marfu'
    ضمير مستتر (Damir Mustatir) Hidden/implied pronoun (from سَتَرَ — to conceal)
    فعل لازم (Fi'l Lazim) Intransitive verb — action stops at the fa'il
    فعل متعدٍّ (Fi'l Muta'addi) Transitive verb — action goes beyond the fa'il to a maf'ul
    اسم مقصور (Ism Maqsur) Indeclinable noun ending in alif maqsura; no visible i'rab change

    Easy to Mix Up

    • Ta'nith lafzi vs. ta'nith ma'nawi: Lafzi is determined by the word's form (does it carry ta' marbuta or alif maqsura?); ma'nawi is determined by the meaning of the referent (is the person/being female?). Both count as a sabab for Ghayr Munsarif. However, verb agreement always follows ma'nawi (meaning) — so جَاءَ طَلْحَةُ uses a mudhakkar verb despite the ta' marbuta on طلحة.
    • Verb before fa'il vs. noun before verb: If the verb comes first, it stays singular regardless of the fa'il's number (ضَرَبَ الزَّيْدُونَ). If the noun comes first, it is technically a mubtada' and the following verb as khabar must agree with it in number (الزَّيْدُونَ أَسْلَمُوا).

    Tafseer 201

    Sessions: Sep 15, Sep 17 & Sep 19

    This week in a nutshell: Three sessions advanced through Surah Al-Baqarah, covering two major Quranic episodes and then a series of commands. The first two sessions traced the story of Adam (AS): his appointment as khalifah, his superiority in knowledge over the angels, Iblis's arrogance and fall, life and temptation in Jannah, the descent to earth, and Adam's accepted tawbah. The third session turned to the divine commands addressed to Bani Israel — fulfilling the covenant, believing in the Quran, not selling or concealing truth, and establishing salah and zakah.

    Key Points

    • Adam's appointment as khalifah has a dual meaning: Adam (AS) specifically as the first vicegerent, and all of humanity as a continuous succession of generations implementing Allah's rulings on earth. Blessing the asl (Adam) is a blessing to the far' (all his descendants).
    • Allah taught Adam الأسماء كلها — the names of all things in existence, extending to everything that would exist until the Day of Resurrection. This God-given capacity is why human knowledge and science continually expand; it is encoded in human fitrah since Adam.
    • The angels' prostration to Adam was sujud tahiyyah (greeting and respect), not sujud 'ibadah (worship). Prostration of worship is exclusively for Allah and is haram toward any other being.
    • Iblis was from the jinn, not the angels — Surah Al-Kahf confirms this explicitly. He had elevated himself through worship to dwell with the angels until Allah's test at Adam's creation exposed his arrogance (istikbar) and refusal (iba'), which made him a kafir.
    • Both Adam AND Hawa ate from the forbidden tree — the Quran consistently uses the dual form throughout (fa-azallahuma, fa-aghwahuma); Surah Ta-Ha explicitly states Adam disobeyed his Lord. Attributing the sin solely to Hawa is a baseless interpretation with no Quranic or hadith support.
    • Adam's words of tawbah were inspired by Allah — He revealed to Adam specific words (identified by the mufassirun as the du'a in Surah Al-A'raf 7:23: Rabbana zalamna anfusana wa in lam taghfir lana wa tarhamna la-nakunanna minal-khasirin). Allah then accepted his repentance: fa-taba 'alayhi — using 'ala to indicate acceptance, an act attributed only to Allah.
    • The Jewish scholars exchanged Tawrah verses for worldly payment — people would approach them wanting rulings changed, and they would alter the Tawrah text accordingly. What they concealed specifically were the descriptions and prophecies of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ that existed in the original Tawrah.
    • The command وَارْكَعُوا مَعَ الرَّاكِعِينَ was addressed to the Jews of Madinah, commanding them to pray with the Muslim congregation — an outward affirmation of faith. From this command (amr), some scholars derive that salah with jama'ah is wajib.

    Vocabulary to Know

    Arabic Term Meaning
    خَلِيفَة (khalifah) Vicegerent; Allah's representative on earth
    سجود تحية (sujud tahiyyah) Prostration of greeting/respect — not worship
    أَبَى (aba) To refuse, to reject
    اسْتَكْبَرَ (istakbara) To become arrogant
    أَزَلَّهُمَا (azallahuma) Caused them both to slip/fall; tempted them (dual)
    تَلَقَّى (talaqqā) To receive (Adam received words of supplication from his Lord)
    تَابَ إِلَى (taba ila) To repent to Allah — the servant's act
    تَابَ عَلَى (taba 'ala) To accept repentance — attributed only to Allah
    تَوَّاب (tawwab) Abundantly/intensely accepting of repentance (ism al-mubalagha)
    مُصَدِّق (musaddiq) Confirming/attesting to the truth of something
    كَتَمَ (katama) To conceal, to hide
    تَحْرِيف (tahrif) Distortion, alteration of scripture

    Easy to Mix Up

    • تَابَ إِلَى vs. تَابَ عَلَى: The servant repents to Allah (taba ila — the act of turning back). Allah accepts repentance upon the servant (taba 'ala — acceptance). Only acceptance uses 'ala, and this verb with 'ala is never attributed to the servant.
    • Sujud tahiyyah vs. sujud 'ibadah: The angels' prostration to Adam was tahiyyah (honor/greeting) — permissible at that time by divine command. Sujud 'ibadah (worship) is haram toward anyone other than Allah, in all times and for all creation.

    Fiqh 201

    Sessions: Sep 16 & Sep 19

    This week in a nutshell: The first session was a comprehensive Q&A review of Kitab al-Tahara, addressing irregular haid, Quran recitation during haid, tanning and animal by-products, masa on socks, najasa thresholds, and the three male discharges. The second session completed Kitab al-Tahara with a full treatment of istinja — its ruling, correct method, impermissible materials, and the dirham threshold — then opened Kitab al-Salah with the definition of salah and the detailed timings of all five daily prayers according to Imam Abu Hanifa and the Sahibayn.

    Key Points

    • Irregular haid and the 15-day rule: If the gap between two episodes of bleeding is less than 15 days, the entire stretch (both bleedings plus the gap) is treated as one continuous Haid. If the gap is 15 days or more, it is a valid tuhar and the second episode begins a new Haid.
    • Tanning (dabaghat) purifies any animal hide regardless of how the animal was killed — zabiha is not a condition. The only exceptions are pig and human skin, which cannot be purified by tanning. Leather from regular stores is pure and valid for salah.
    • Masa on fabric socks (jorabayn) requires all three conditions simultaneously: (1) covers above the ankle bone and does not slide down on its own; (2) wet hands placed on the sock do not cause water to seep through within approximately 2–3 seconds; (3) durable enough to walk approximately one mile without tearing at the bottom. The muftabi opinion requires all three; a minority view requires only the waterproof condition.
    • Najasat-e-Mughalaza threshold for salah: Up to the size of a dirham (~3.5cm diameter) or 3.5 Masha by weight is pardoned — salah is valid with makruh tanzihi. Above this, salah is impermissible without washing. If the stain is discovered after salah, the salah is valid and need not be repeated.
    • The three male discharges: Mani (semen) requires full Ghusl; if wet it must be washed off, if dry it may be scraped off. Madhi (pre-seminal fluid from arousal) and Wadi (post-urinary thick discharge) break Wudu only — no Ghusl is required for either.
    • Istinja is sunnah per Ahnaf (wajib per Imam Shafi'i). The correct method for defecation uses three strokes: front-to-back, back-to-front, front-to-back. Impermissible materials include animal bones (no absorptive capacity), dried dung (itself najasa), and food items (ihana/disrespect toward food). Using the right hand is prohibited except by necessity.
    • The dirham threshold also applies to istinja: If najasa spreads beyond the makharaj more than the size of a dirham, water becomes obligatory. Within the dirham size, dry materials suffice. (Shaykhayn measure the dirham excluding the makharaj itself; Imam Muhammad includes it.)
    • Zuhr and Asr timing disagreement: Zuhr time ends — and Asr begins — when the shadow of an object equals its own height plus the shadow at zawal (Sahibayn), or twice its height plus the shadow at zawal (Imam Abu Hanifa). The Hanafi Asr time is therefore later.
    • Maghrib and Isha timing (Shafaq disagreement): Imam Abu Hanifa holds that Maghrib ends at the disappearance of Shafaq al-Abyad (white twilight) — this is the muftabi opinion, giving a longer Maghrib window. The Sahibayn hold it ends at Shafaq al-Ahmar (red twilight), meaning Isha begins earlier in their calculation.

    Vocabulary to Know

    Arabic Term Meaning
    الاستنجاء (istinja) Purification of the place of exit after relieving oneself
    الطُّهر (tuhar) Period of purity between bleedings; minimum 15 days
    الدرهم (dirham) Coin used as size reference (~3.5cm diameter) for najasa threshold
    نجاسة مغلظة (Najasat-e-Mughalaza) Heavy impurity: blood, human urine, feces, khamar, pus
    نجاسة مخففة (Najasat-e-Mukhafaffa) Light impurity: urine of halal animals; threshold is ¼ of the garment
    مَنِي (mani) Semen — requires Ghusl
    مَذْي (madhi) Pre-seminal fluid — breaks Wudu only, no Ghusl
    وَدْي (wadi) Post-urinary discharge — breaks Wudu only, no Ghusl
    الزوال (zawal) The sun's declination from its zenith; marks the start of Zuhr time
    الشفق الأحمر (Shafaq al-Ahmar) Red twilight — Sahibayn's marker for end of Maghrib
    الشفق الأبيض (Shafaq al-Abyad) White twilight — Imam Abu Hanifa's marker for end of Maghrib (muftabi)
    الفجر الصادق (Fajr al-Sadiq) True dawn; horizontal white light spreading across horizon; marks Fajr time

    Easy to Mix Up

    • Mani vs. Madhi: Mani is full ejaculation and requires Ghusl regardless of quantity; Madhi is pre-seminal fluid released from arousal (without ejaculation) and only breaks Wudu. The hadith of Ali (RA) establishes explicitly that Madhi does not require Ghusl.
    • Shafaq al-Ahmar vs. Shafaq al-Abyad: Red twilight appears first on the horizon after sunset and fades earlier; white twilight persists afterward and fades later. Sahibayn use the red (earlier end of Maghrib, earlier start of Isha); Imam Abu Hanifa uses the white (later end of Maghrib, later start of Isha). The muftabi Hanafi opinion follows Imam Abu Hanifa — this affects the correct Isha start time on prayer apps.
