    Nahw 201

    Sessions: Oct 05 & Oct 09

    This week in a nutshell: The first session completed the topic of Marfu'at by covering ism Kana, khabar Inna, Ma and La Mushabbeha to Laisa, the special Mubtada formed from a Siga Sifat, and the two types of La. The second session reviewed all eight Marfu'at and introduced the twelve Mansubat, covering Maf'ul Mutlaq and Maf'ul Bihi in detail, including when the governing verb must be dropped.

    Key Points

    • Ism Kana is always Marfu'; khabar of Inna is always Marfu' — these are the two symmetrical rules for the two major groups (أفعال ناقصة and inna's sisters).
    • Khabar Muqaddam (advancing the khabar before the ism) is permitted for the first nine أفعال ناقصة, prohibited for those beginning with ما (e.g., مَا زَالَ، مَا دَامَ), and a matter of scholarly disagreement (ikhtilaf) for لَيْسَ.
    • ما and لا Mushabbeha to Laisa both function like لَيْسَ — mubtada stays marfu' (as ism) and the khabar becomes mansoob. ما takes both ma'rifa and nakira; لا Mushabbeha takes only nakira.
    • The special Mubtada rule: when a Siga Sifat (ism fa'il, ism maf'ul, sifa mushabbeha, or ism tafdil) appears after ما نافية or istifham and gives rafa' to an ism zahir, it becomes a special Mubtada — not a khabar. The diagnostic test: if the sifa is mufrad but the following noun is tasniya or jama', the sifa is the amil (active and singular, like ذَهَبَ الزَّيْدَانِ), making it the Mubtada. If the sifa matches the following noun in number, it is Khabar Muqaddam.
    • لا Nafil Jins (ism mabni ala al-fath, no tanwin) negates the entire genus — بَل cannot follow it. لا Mushabbeha (ism marfu' with tanwin) negates one individual — بَل can follow to name an alternative.
    • Maf'ul Mutlaq is always a masdar and serves three purposes: taqid (emphasis: ضَرَبْتُهُ ضَرْبًا), bayan al-naw' (manner: جَلَسْتُ جِلْسَةَ الْمَلِكِ), and bayan al-'adad (number: جَلَسْتُ جَلْسَتَيْنِ). The masdar may come from a different root than the verb if the meaning is the same.
    • Maf'ul Bihi word order can be inverted (maf'ul before fa'il) only when clear i'rab markings eliminate any ambiguity about who is the doer and who is the receiver. The governing verb may be dropped jawāzan (when context is clear) or wujūban (in specific sam'i constructions like شُكْرًا, سَقْيًا, امْرَأً وَنَفْسَهُ).

    Vocabulary to Know

    Arabic Term Meaning
    أَفْعَالٌ نَاقِصَةٌ Incomplete verbs (Kana and sisters)
    خَبَرٌ مُقَدَّمٌ Khabar advanced before its ism
    نَفْيُ الْجِنْسِ Negation of the entire genus
    صِيغَةُ صِفَةٍ Form of a describing word (ism fa'il, ism maf'ul, sifa mushabbeha, ism tafdil)
    مُطَابَقَةٌ Agreement (in gender, number, case)
    عَامِلٌ / مَعْمُولٌ Governing word / governed word
    مَفْعُولٌ مُطْلَقٌ Absolute/cognate object (a masdar reinforcing its verb)
    مَفْعُولٌ بِهِ Direct object (receiver of the action)
    تَأْكِيدٌ / بَيَانُ النَّوْعِ / بَيَانُ الْعَدَدِ Emphasis / describing the type / specifying the number
    جَوَازًا / وُجُوبًا Optionally / obligatorily

    Easy to Mix Up

    • Special Mubtada vs. Khabar Muqaddam: both involve a Siga Sifat before an ism zahir after ما or istifham. If the sifa is mufrad and does not match the following noun → sifa is Mubtada (active amil). If the sifa matches in number → sifa is Khabar Muqaddam, and the following noun is Mubtada Mu'akhkhar.
    • لا Nafil Jins vs. لا Mushabbeha: the marker is the ism's vowel — fathah without tanwin (مَبْنِيٌّ عَلَى الْفَتْحِ) = Nafil Jins (whole genus denied, no بَل possible); damma with tanwin (marfu') = Mushabbeha (one individual denied, بَل valid).

    Tafseer 201

    Sessions: Oct 06 & Oct 08

    This week in a nutshell: The first session concluded the story of the slaughtered cow (Ayat 71–74), covering the murder mystery, the miraculous revival of the victim, and the condemnation of Bani Israel's spiritual hardness. The second session moved into Ayat 76–77, analyzing the deliberate distortion of the Torah by Jewish scholars and their hypocritical double-talk with Muslims — speaking belief outwardly while privately suppressing knowledge of the Prophet ﷺ.

    Key Points

    • Final description of the cow (Ayah 71): not trained for plowing or irrigation, مُسَلَّمَةٌ (sound, without defect), لَا شِيَةَ فِيهَا (completely uniform in color, no markings). After this complete description, Bani Israel finally slaughtered it — but وَمَا كَادُوا يَفْعَلُونَ (they were on the verge of not doing so), due to the cow's high price and fear of scandal.
    • The murder mystery (Ayat 72–73): a murder had occurred and each group pushed blame onto others (فَادَّارَأْتُمْ — from دَرَأَ, to push away). Allah commanded striking the dead body with a piece of the slaughtered cow; the victim was briefly revived and named his killer. This miracle demonstrated كَذَلِكَ يُحْيِي اللَّهُ الْمَوْتَى — just as He revived this man, He will resurrect all the dead on the Day of Judgment.
    • Hearts harder than stones (Ayah 74): after witnessing these miracles, Bani Israel's hearts still hardened — فَهِيَ كَالْحِجَارَةِ أَوْ أَشَدُّ قَسْوَةً. Even rocks respond to Allah: some burst with rivers, some crack from His awe, some crumble from mountains. وَمَا اللَّهُ بِغَافِلٍ عَمَّا تَعْمَلُونَ — the بَ is emphatic after negation: Allah is absolutely not unaware.
    • Hidden wisdom in the command: Allah commanded slaughter of the very animal Bani Israel venerated (they had been cow-worshippers before Tawheed). The command was itself a test of submission — their delay revealed the residual attachment.
    • Deliberate distortion (Ayah 76): Jewish scholars heard Allah's words clearly, عَقَلُوهُ (grasped them with full intellect), then ثُمَّ يُحَرِّفُونَهُ — deliberately distorted them عَلَى بَصِيرَة (with full awareness, not by mistake). Distortion took two forms: تَبْدِيل (outright substitution of words) and تَأْوِيل (false interpretation).
    • Hypocritical double-talk (Ayah 76): a sub-group among the Jews told Muslims "we believe" and acknowledged the Prophet ﷺ's coming being foretold. But when alone among fellow Jews, others rebuked them: "Do you tell the Muslims what Allah revealed to you, so that on the Day of Judgment they will have an argument (حُجَّة) against you before your Lord?" Their fear was that acknowledging the Prophet ﷺ's truthfulness would be used as evidence against them in the Akhirah.
    • Allah's total knowledge (Ayah 77): أَوَلَا يَعْلَمُونَ أَنَّ اللَّهَ يَعْلَمُ مَا يُسِرُّونَ وَمَا يُعْلِنُونَ — whether they conceal or openly declare, Allah knows all. The rebuke exposes the contradiction: claiming faith while behaving as though Allah is unaware.

    Vocabulary to Know

    Arabic Term Meaning
    مُسَلَّمَةٌ Sound, free from defect
    لَا شِيَةَ فِيهَا No markings differing from its color; without spots
    وَمَا كَادُوا يَفْعَلُونَ They were on the verge of not doing it (كَادَ = verb of nearness)
    فَادَّارَأْتُمْ You disputed / pushed blame from one to another (from دَرَأَ: to push away)
    قَسَتْ / قَسْوَةٌ Became hard / hardness (of the heart)
    تَفَجَّرَ / يَشَّقَّقُ / يَهْبِطُ Bursts forth / splits apart / crumbles down (the three responses of rocks)
    تَحْرِيفٌ Distortion of Allah's words
    تَبْدِيلٌ Outright substitution (one form of distortion)
    تَأْوِيلٌ False interpretation (another form of distortion)
    عَلَى بَصِيرَةٍ With full insight — deliberately, not by mistake
    يُسِرُّونَ / يُعْلِنُونَ They conceal / they declare openly
    حُجَّةٌ Argument, proof (used against someone on the Day of Judgment)
    أَمَانِيٌّ كَاذِبَةٌ False hopes/desires (inherited beliefs without scriptural basis)

    Easy to Mix Up

    • تَبْدِيل vs. تَأْوِيل: both are forms of تَحْرِيف. تَبْدِيل = changing the actual words of the text; تَأْوِيل = keeping the words but distorting their meaning through false interpretation.
    • لَعَلَّكُمْ تَعْقِلُونَ at the end of Ayah 73 is not addressed only to Bani Israel — the teacher stressed it is an address to all Quran readers until the Day of Judgment; every Quranic story carries a lesson we must contemplate.

    Fiqh 201

    Sessions: Oct 07 & Oct 10

    This week in a nutshell: The first session covered Salatul Witr (its wajib status per Imam Abu Hanifa, its three-rakah structure, Dua al-Qunut and its distinction from Qunut al-Nazila) and rules of recitation in salah, including the five categories and the prohibition on reciting behind an imam. The second session covered the full rules of congregational prayer: selecting an imam, proper saf formation, when a woman standing in a men's row invalidates the prayer, women attending the masjid, and permissible vs. impermissible forms of iqtida — anchored by the central principle that the imam is Damin (guarantor) for the congregation.

    Key Points

    • Witr is Wajib according to Imam Abu Hanifa (muftabih fatwa), not fard (no Quranic dalil) and not mere sunnah (has taqeed-laden ahadith). Practical consequence: qadha is required if Witr is missed, unlike a sunnah prayer. Sahibain and Imam Shafi'i hold it is sunnah.
    • Witr structure (Ahnaf): three continuous rakahs without an intervening salam, resembling Maghrib — but the imam recites aloud in all three rakahs, a surah must be added after Fatihah in every rakah, and Dua al-Qunut is recited in the third rakah before ruku (after reciting the surah, raise hands with takbir, recite Qunut, then go to ruku). Imam Shafi'i and Imam Malik structure Witr as 2+1 with a salam between.
    • Qunut is year-round in Witr per Ahnaf (dalil: Ibn Majah). Imam Shafi'i restricts it to the last 15 nights of Ramadan. Qunut al-Nazila (supplication during calamity) is distinct — it is recited after rising from ruku in any salah during actual hardship, not as a permanent fixture. Ahnaf do not permanently perform Qunut in Fajr — the Prophet ﷺ did so for only one month for a specific necessity, then stopped.
    • Muqtadi recites nothing behind the imam — not Fatihah, not any surah, no silent lip movement — in any salah, jahri or sirri. Quranic basis: Surah al-A'raf 204 (listen and be silent). Two intentions are required: niyyat al-salah (the specific prayer) and niyyat al-mutaba'ah (the intention to follow this imam).
    • Imam selection order: most knowledgeable in fiqh → best recitation → highest in Wara' (avoiding doubtful matters, beyond mere Taqwa) → oldest → better in manners → lineage → appearance. These criteria apply when no imam is designated; do not contest an appointed masjid imam.
    • Five conditions for a woman standing in a men's row to make their prayer Fasid: (1) same imam and prayer, (2) no barrier between her and the men's row, (3) prayer has Ruku and Sujud (Janazah is excluded), (4) she is Ahli Shahwa (of mature age), (5) the imam intended to lead both men and women. If all five are met, the men's prayer is Fasid; the woman's prayer remains valid. Imam Shafi'i holds it is only makruh for both.
    • The Imam is Damin (guarantor): if the imam's prayer is valid, the congregation's prayer is valid even if individual muqtadis made minor errors. If the imam's prayer is invalid (e.g., he had no wudu) — per Ahnaf — the entire congregation must repeat. Imam Shafi'i holds each person is independently Damin for their own prayer.
    • Impermissible iqtida: a fard muqtadi behind a nafil imam; a qari behind an ummi; a muqtadi doing full ruku/sujud behind an imam praying with ishara (gestures only); praying a different fard than the imam. Permissible: tayammum imam leading those with wudu (dalil: Amr ibn al-As رضي الله عنه), nafil muqtadi behind fard imam, standing muqtadis behind a sitting imam (when the imam has a valid excuse — later Sunnah of Rasulullah ﷺ).

    Vocabulary to Know

    Arabic Term Meaning
    وِتْرٌ The odd-numbered night prayer; three continuous rakahs per Ahnaf
    قُنُوتٌ Supplication in the third rakah of Witr before ruku (year-round per Ahnaf)
    قُنُوتُ النَّازِلَةِ Supplication recited during times of calamity; not permanent in any salah
    وَرَعٌ Avoidance of doubtful matters (beyond Taqwa, which is avoiding Haram/Makruh Tahrimi)
    فَاسِقٌ Open sinner; leads to Makruh Tanzih if appointed as imam
    مُقْتَدِي Follower praying behind an imam
    ضَامِنٌ Guarantor/answerable — the imam is Damin for the congregation's prayer
    اقْتِدَاءٌ Following the imam; making one's prayer contingent on the imam's
    أَهْلُ الشَّهْوَةِ Of mature age (relevant to the five conditions for fasid)
    مَكْرُوهٌ تَنْزِيهِي Disliked but not sinful; prayer remains valid
    تَيَمُّمٌ Dry ablution (valid for imam leading those with wudu)
    مُؤِمٌّ / إِشَارَةٌ One who prays with gestures due to inability to do ruku/sujud

    Easy to Mix Up

    • Wara' vs. Taqwa: Taqwa = avoiding Haram and Makruh Tahrimi. Wara' = going further and avoiding anything doubtful (shubuhat). The hadith of the king's sanctuary illustrates Wara': those who linger near the boundary risk crossing it.
    • Qunut in Witr vs. Qunut al-Nazila: Witr Qunut is recited before ruku in the third rakah, year-round, as a fixed act of worship. Qunut al-Nazila is recited after rising from ruku in any salah, only during actual calamity, for a limited period — not permanent. Ahnaf do not perform permanent Qunut in Fajr; that practice is specific to Imam Shafi'i.
    • Woman standing in men's row: all five conditions must be met for the men's prayer to become Fasid — if even one condition is absent (e.g., a barrier exists, or the imam did not intend to lead women), no fasid occurs. The woman's prayer is never made Fasid by this — only the men's.
